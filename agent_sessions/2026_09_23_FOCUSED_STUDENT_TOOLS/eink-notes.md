# Separate e-note hardware project: bounded engineering review

Checked **2026-09-23**. This project fits the proposed family of focused student tools as a dedicated reader and handwriting surface, with its own hardware validation.

1. **Display and pen are distinct subsystems.** An E Ink panel renders pages but does not, by itself, locate a stylus. [E Ink's own digital-paper device](https://www.eink.com/product/detail/digital-paper-device) specifies an e-paper display, capacitive touch panel, and electromagnetic-resonance (EMR) pen sensor separately. [Wacom explains](https://www.wacom.com/en-gb/about-wacom/technologies) that a battery-free EMR pen is powered and tracked by a sensor *under* the display. The MVP therefore needs a matched display, digitizer/pen, display controller, and mechanical stack; a loose e-paper panel plus stylus is insufficient.
2. **Static reading power is different from writing power and responsiveness.** [E Ink's bistability explanation](https://www.eink.com/tech/detail/Benefits) says the image persists without display power between updates. Its [digital-paper product page](https://www.eink.com/product/detail/digital-paper-device) warns that extensive handwriting or continuous wireless connection draws more power. The pen experience must be measured for stroke latency, refresh artifacts, palm rejection, and battery endurance during actual annotation, rather than inferred from a static-reader battery claim.
3. **Notes are a document workflow, not a mini tablet feature list.** E Ink describes student eNotes as reading textbooks and marking them with handwritten notes or highlights in its [education use case](https://www.eink.com/application/detail/education_enote). Define file import, local save, export with annotations intact, and recovery after power loss as core behaviors. Search/handwriting recognition and Hivemind assistance can come later; those are software and synchronization features whose failure should not prevent local reading or writing.

**Scope recommendation:** Prototype one monochrome, pen-enabled reader around an available matched display/digitizer platform. Ship only PDF reading, freehand annotation, notebooks, local autosave, and straightforward export in the first trial. Test with real student lecture slides and textbooks before choosing custom industrial design. Keep this as a separate project from the small voice wearable, while sharing only account/sync conventions if useful.
