# One bare payment-capable NFC IC with a public one-unit price

Checked **2026-09-23**. [PhoneLCDParts advertises one 72-pin 100VB27 NFC IC for $2.14, in stock](https://www.phonelcdparts.com/iphone-xr-xs-xs-max-ipad-pro-11-1st-gen-2018-ipad-pro-11-2rd-gen-2020-ipad-7-2019-ic-100vb27-xr). The quantity selector starts at one; shipping and tax are extra. This is an independent phone-repair seller, not an authorized NXP production distributor. [TechInsights' original teardown analysis identifies the 100VB27 marking as NXP SN100U](https://www.techinsights.com/blog/google-pixel-3-xl-teardown), integrating NFC, a secure element, and eSIM in one die. [NXP lists the SN100 family among its digital-wallet products](https://www.nxp.com/products/rfid-nfc/nfc-hf/nfc-enabled-digital-wallet:NFC-ENABLED-DIGITAL-WALLET) and describes it as a single-die NFC controller plus embedded secure element. Thus the advertised chip is materially different from a generic NFC tag or reader-only IC.

The **$2.14 is the price of a physical repair IC**, not a functioning payment wallet or production procurement price. The listing does not establish whether the particular part is authentic, new, configured, locked, supplied with usable firmware or documentation, or eligible for payment credential provisioning. A custom wearable would still need the antenna and RF design, host integration, wallet software, issuer/network relationships, provisioning, and applicable approvals. In particular, a salvaged or repair-channel part should not be assumed to carry transferable payment credentials or accessible keys.

Second one-unit datapoint: [AK Info Tools lists an “NXP100TB2D IC” at ₹57 per piece, in stock, with quantity one selectable](https://akinfotools.com/product/details/nxp100tb2d-ic). It says the IC may be **new or refurbished** and offers **no warranty or guarantee**. The page itself does not explain the exact silicon or wallet configuration. The checked NXP primary material did not independently tie the **100TB2D** marking to SN100T, so this is a corroborating low-cost repair listing, **not** the best-verified named-chip example. [NXP's SN100T security target](https://www.commoncriteriaportal.org/files/epfiles/SecurityTarget-Lite_SN100T_v1.1_20190418.pdf) establishes that SN100T integrates a secure element with an NFC controller and identifies mobile payment as a use case; it does not prove that AK Info Tools' 100TB2D item is an initialized SN100T.

Suggested document line: **“Bare integrated NFC + secure-element IC: NXP SN100U (100VB27 phone-repair part), $2.14 for one advertised chip; independent repair source, provisioning and custom-device usability unverified.”**
