# Existing-phone cellular service through a custom Bluetooth accessory

Checked **2026-09-23**. A phone-companion design uses the *phone's* cellular subscription and radio; a Bluetooth accessory does not become a cellular endpoint by pairing.

1. **Calls:** The [Bluetooth SIG Hands-Free Profile (HFP)](https://www.bluetooth.com/specifications/specs/hands-free-profile-1-10/) explicitly covers a wearable/headset remotely controlling a mobile phone and carrying the voice connection. A build intended to function like a normal call headset needs a phone-compatible HFP implementation. [Nordic says the nRF5340 does not support Bluetooth Classic BR/EDR HFP](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/89716/hfp-ag-audio-gateway-dongle-function); [its product page](https://www.nordicsemi.com/Products/nRF5340) lists Bluetooth LE and LE Audio. Therefore an nRF5340-only BLE design must not be presented as an ordinary HFP phone headset. A separate BR/EDR HFP-capable radio/module is one route; a phone-specific LE Audio route needs separate compatibility validation.
2. **Incoming notifications:** [Apple's ANCS](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/CoreBluetooth/Reference/AppleNotificationCenterServiceSpecification/Introduction/Introduction.html) can expose iOS notification information to a BLE accessory. This is notification access, not blanket Messages database access or permission to send texts. Apple's [ANCS service specification](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/CoreBluetooth/Reference/AppleNotificationCenterServiceSpecification/Specification/Specification.html) says the service is not guaranteed always to be present and its characteristics require authorization.
3. **Sending SMS:** On iPhone, [Apple's `MFMessageComposeViewController`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/messageui/mfmessagecomposeviewcontroller) can prefill a message, but presents the standard composer for a person to approve or cancel; it does not silently send a text from an accessory/companion app. On Android, [Google's default-handler guidance](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/permissions/default-handlers) says Google Play access to sensitive SMS and call-log permissions generally requires the app to be the user's default SMS/Phone handler, unless a policy exception applies. Avoid promising arbitrary background read/send access for a basic companion app.

Suggested design wording: **“Pair with a phone to use its existing mobile plan. BLE can carry device controls and permitted notifications; phone call audio/control requires a compatible hands-free audio profile, which the nRF5340 alone does not provide. Text workflows depend on phone OS permissions and may require the user to approve sending on the phone.”**
