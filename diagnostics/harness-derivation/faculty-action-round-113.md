## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Start this, and keep control of it even if I walk away.” Give me one action baton that can move between the pendant, relay, Mac, and browser: begin a reversible task, show/announce who currently owns it, let me pause or cancel from the pendant, and return a durable proof of exactly what happened—even across a dropped connection."
- **useful because:** Today judgement can choose an action but action execution is fragmented: browser may be offline, the Mac may be busy, and a receipt can exist without a way to stop an in-flight job. A durable, owner-visible baton makes long-running work safe and comprehensible: the relay stays awake, the Mac/browser performs the step that has reach, and the pendant remains the immediate cancel/status surface.
- **path:** faculty-judgement → relay-realtime → relay → mac-planner → mac-vision → browser-extension → pendant → dashboard
- **model tier:** gpt-5.6-luna for planning/recovery decisions; deterministic relay/local executors for leases, retries, receipts, and cancellation; gpt-realtime-2.1 only for the short spoken update.
- **latency:** Immediate spoken acknowledgement under 500 ms; lease/state updates under 2 s; execution may run for minutes without holding the voice turn open. Reconnect reconciliation within 10 s.
- **cost:** About $0.01–$0.08 per multi-step task depending on planner retries; deterministic state/receipt traffic dominates neither tokens nor compute. No model call for cancel, status, lease expiry, or reconciliation.
- **security:** The relay must never receive private page contents or credentials; owner-private browser legs remain on the authenticated Safari/Chrome bridge. Use a short-lived, audience-bound lease and idempotency key; reject stale workers and duplicate commits. Pendant cancel should be available without speech. Require explicit confirmation before irreversible browser/Mac actions; retain only hashes, field-level before/after evidence, and configurable audio/job retention.
- **missing:** A single durable action-baton state machine shared by relay, Mac agent, browser bridge, and dashboard (queued/claimed/running/paused/cancel-requested/cancelled/committed/failed/expired).; Lease claim/renew/revoke and exactly-once commit endpoints with worker identity, fencing token, idempotency key, and heartbeat timeout.; Pendant event/push mapping for status, pause, and cancel, including offline event spool and replay (the requested offline_intent_spool and physical_action_confirmation skills are still absent).; A cancellation checkpoint contract in mac_run_actions/browser_run_actions/mac_delegate and a receipt schema that distinguishes acknowledged, partially applied, and committed effects.; A reconnect reconciler and dashboard timeline that can prove which surface performed each step and expose safe undo where available.

### "“That partly failed—repair it without repeating anything that already happened.” After a Mac, browser, or relay action is interrupted, inspect the real state across the relevant surfaces, identify the exact boundary reached, and safely complete or roll back only the missing reversible work. Return a proof of the repaired final state, not merely a retry log."
- **useful because:** Today a failed multi-surface task leaves the owner to guess whether anything happened. Retrying can duplicate a message, appointment, file, or browser submission; undo is often unavailable once the worker or connection disappears. State-based repair would turn ambiguous failures into safe recovery, especially when the pendant goes offline after the Mac has already acted.
- **path:** faculty-judgement → faculty-perception → faculty-action → relay → mac-planner → mac-vision → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use a cheaper background planner for state diff and repair-plan generation; deterministic typed probes and action receipts establish facts; reserve realtime for the short owner-facing explanation and confirmation when a repair could change external state.
- **latency:** Initial failure classification under 2 seconds; read-only reconciliation under 10 seconds; any repair requiring approval waits indefinitely for the owner rather than timing out into execution.
- **cost:** Usually $0.01–$0.05 per incident, dominated by one background planning call and surface reads; deterministic retries and receipt comparison are negligible.
- **security:** Never infer completion from a timeout. Read private browser state only through the authenticated bridge and keep credentials on-device. Repairs that send, submit, delete, purchase, or alter external records require fresh confirmation with a precise diff. Store hashes and provenance rather than page contents; expire sensitive evidence.
- **missing:** A state-invariant and reconciliation protocol: each action declares its expected postcondition and an idempotent observation method.; Cross-surface read probes that can verify those postconditions after worker loss, including authenticated browser state and Mac filesystem/app state.; A repair planner that produces a minimal diff from observed state, with explicit safe-to-repeat and must-not-repeat steps.; A durable incident record linking the original intent, every receipt, observed state snapshots, repair approval, and final proof.; Per-action compensation adapters for effects that are reversible but lack a current undo implementation.

### "“Tell me the private part only when it is safe.” Route sensitive results and confirmations to the owner’s pendant only when the paired device proves it is present; otherwise leave a redacted notification on the Mac/dashboard and wait. This should cover logged-in browser findings, account alerts, and action receipts without exposing their contents to nearby people or an unattended speaker."
- **useful because:** The system can reach private browser sessions and produce speech, but it has no owner-presence boundary: a spoken account balance, message preview, or approval payload can be overheard when the pendant is elsewhere. Presence-bound disclosure lets the owner use the hive in shared spaces without choosing between privacy and usefulness.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → relay → mac-planner → browser-extension → dashboard → faculty-perception → faculty-judgement → faculty-action
- **model tier:** No model call for presence, pairing, sensitivity labels, or redaction. Use a background model only to summarize after the policy gate; realtime speaks only after an authenticated presence grant.
- **latency:** Presence decision under 200 ms from a valid pendant heartbeat/button nonce; redacted fallback under 1 second. Never speak sensitive content while presence is uncertain.
- **cost:** Negligible model cost for gating; roughly a few hundred bytes of authenticated telemetry per heartbeat and event. Optional background summarization costs less than $0.01 per briefing.
- **security:** Use mutually authenticated device keys and short-lived proximity/session proofs, not Bluetooth name or network address alone. Treat browser contents, messages, financial data, and action diffs as sensitive by default. Do not send raw private content to the relay when a local redacted route is possible. Require explicit owner policy for categories and provide an immediate pendant mute/lock gesture.
- **missing:** A paired-device cryptographic presence protocol with freshness and replay protection.; Sensitivity labels propagated from browser extraction through planner, receipt, TTS, and dashboard rendering.; A relay policy gate that refuses sensitive TTS/upload when the presence proof is absent or stale.; Local redaction templates and a dashboard review queue for withheld content.; Pendant firmware support for secure presence nonce, lock/mute gesture, and offline policy cache.


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `hardware` — Add a tiny coin/LRA haptic actuator with a dedicated low-side driver and a protected, debounced action button to the pendant; expose three firmware patterns (short acknowledgement, repeating pending lease, urgent cancel/failure). Keep the existing LED as a redundant visual channel and make haptic intensity/pattern configurable. Log button-edge timestamps and pattern IDs so an action receipt can prove local acknowledgement without recording audio.
- **owner gets:** The owner can feel that a requested Mac/browser action was accepted, is waiting, or failed while walking, in a noisy room, or with audio muted. A deliberate tactile cancel/confirm gesture remains usable when the network or speech path is unavailable; it prevents the dangerous ambiguity of “did it actually send?”
- effort: Pendant PCB/enclosure revision, one GPIO/PWM or I2C driver, firmware event queue and battery tests, plus relay-to-device event mapping and dashboard settings. Prototype validation should measure vibration start latency and false button presses during wear.  ·  risk: Vibration can be annoying or drain the small battery; mitigate with short bounded patterns, quiet hours, and a hardware/software kill switch. A stuck button must not repeatedly cancel actions. No hardware should be flashed until an actual pendant is present and separately approved; current inventory has no pendant.
- cost: Roughly $2–$6 BOM increase (LRA/ERM, driver, mechanical mounting) and <10–25 mW while vibrating, zero steady-state if load-switched. No meaningful API cost; telemetry adds a few bytes per event.  ·  latency: Local acknowledgement under 50 ms; network state remains best-effort and must not block the tactile response.
- security: Button events are sensitive action signals: authenticate and bind them to a short-lived action nonce, reject stale/replayed events, and avoid putting raw event history in speech logs. Haptic patterns must not reveal private page content to bystanders.
- depends on: A durable action receipt/lease contract between relay, Mac, browser, and pendant; A firmware event spool for button events while offline (requested offline_intent_spool remains unavailable); Pendant hardware actually connected for measurement; currently none is online
