## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Handle this across my Mac and logged-in browser, keep going if a device disconnects, and tell me exactly what was done, what was skipped, and what I can undo.”"
- **useful because:** Today the mind can decide a multi-step outcome but cannot safely carry it through a partial Mac/browser outage: it may leave queued browser commands stale, mistake an untrusted UI receipt for success, or lose its place after a disconnect. A durable saga makes partial completion explicit, resumes only from fresh witnesses, and gives the owner a truthful per-step receipt plus compensation where available.
- **path:** faculty-judgement emits a typed step graph and risk/approval policy → relay persists saga and step state, leases, idempotency keys, retry/compensation records, and wakes the job after disconnects → faculty-action executes Mac steps through mac_run_actions/mac_delegate and browser steps through browser_run_actions plus /browser/poll and /browser/result/:commandId → faculty-perception supplies fresh /observe inputReachability and browser heartbeat/session witnessIds before every UI step → pendant receives concise progress, blocked, completed, and undo status; owner can confirm irreversible steps or request rollback
- **model tier:** Use the cheaper background model for planning/reconciliation and retry classification; reserve realtime only for the owner's live confirmation and urgent interruption. Deterministic route/step validation and idempotency should be code, not model calls.
- **latency:** First acknowledgment under 1 second; reversible steps normally under 10 seconds each. A disconnected bridge should become “paused/unknown” immediately, never “failed/succeeded”; resume on a fresh heartbeat, with a bounded retry window. Progress can arrive asynchronously while the owner leaves.
- **cost:** Roughly 1–3 background model calls per saga (plan, reconcile, final summary), plus one realtime turn only if approval is needed; dominant cost is context for private page evidence and repeated receipts, so pass compact witness IDs/hashes rather than full transcripts.
- **security:** Private page content and action payloads stay on the authenticated browser/Mac path; relay stores hashes, minimal metadata, and encrypted short-lived payloads. Never retry an irreversible step without a fresh proof and explicit approval. A stale browser session, changed URL/tab identity, missing Accessibility/Screen Recording, or inputReachability != ready must quarantine the step. Unknown outcomes require inspection or owner confirmation, not automatic replay. Every step exposes before/after evidence, idempotency key, and compensation availability.
- **missing:** Durable saga/step state machine and lease/heartbeat protocol spanning relay, Mac, and browser; A shared witness schema: observe inputReachability, browser extension heartbeat/session epoch, tab identity, and action receipt provenance; Quarantine/resume semantics for pending browser commands and unknown outcomes; Typed compensation registry and owner-facing partial-completion/undo view; A route/tool to inspect or approve one blocked saga step without replaying earlier steps

### "“Take the document or secret I’m looking at in my logged-in browser and put it into the right Mac app or folder, without showing its contents to the AI; show me the destination and a fingerprint on the pendant, then let me approve the transfer.”"
- **useful because:** The owner can currently ask agents to read a private page or type data into a Mac app, but cannot safely move sensitive material between those trust domains without exposing it to model context, logs, clipboard history, or a broad browser command. This creates a sealed, owner-approved handoff: the browser produces an encrypted one-time payload, the Mac receives it directly, and the pendant verifies destination plus content fingerprint without displaying the secret.
- **path:** browser-extension extracts the selected download/document/field value and encrypts it locally under a one-time transfer key; it binds the payload to tabId, URL origin, and a short expiry → relay stores only ciphertext and transfer metadata, brokers the one-time rendezvous, and records delivery/expiry receipts without plaintext → faculty-judgement selects the destination app/path and sensitivity policy; faculty-action validates the destination and requests delivery → mac-planner/mac-terminal receives the ciphertext through a local authenticated handoff, decrypts only inside the target app or protected file write, and returns a hash/receipt → pendant displays destination, origin, byte count, and truncated fingerprint; a physical button confirmation authorizes release and a long press cancels/zeroizes the transfer
- **model tier:** Cheaper background model for destination classification and metadata-only reconciliation; no model should receive the payload. Realtime is used only for the owner's live confirmation, if they ask conversationally.
- **latency:** Prepare metadata in under 2 seconds; after button approval, local delivery in under 5 seconds for ordinary documents. Expire an unapproved transfer after 60 seconds and make cancellation immediate.
- **cost:** Usually one cheap metadata classification call or none when destination is explicit; relay storage and bandwidth dominate, with no token cost for document contents. A small encrypted payload incurs ordinary relay egress/storage cost.
- **security:** Plaintext must never transit model prompts, action logs, clipboard, or durable relay storage. Use authenticated encryption, one-time keys held only by browser and Mac, origin/tab binding, destination allowlists, expiry, replay protection, and zeroization on cancel/failure. Require physical pendant approval for secrets and external destinations; show a warning for uploads or sharing. Recovery must leave the source untouched and report whether delivery is unconfirmed, delivered, or verified by destination hash.
- **missing:** A browser-to-Mac encrypted transfer protocol with one-time key exchange and origin/tab binding; A local Mac receiver that writes/imports directly without clipboard or model exposure, plus destination verification; Relay ciphertext rendezvous with short TTL, replay protection, and no-plaintext receipts; Pendant firmware/UI for fingerprint display, physical approve/cancel, and offline-safe zeroization signal; Typed transfer receipts and an owner-facing audit view distinguishing prepared, approved, delivered, and verified


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Add a cross-surface Action Witness Contract and saga coordinator between existing /plan→/execute→/jobs routes and the Mac/browser bridges. Each step must declare target surface, idempotency key, precondition witness requirements, effect class (reversible/irreversible), timeout, and compensation. Before dispatch, coordinator validates a fresh witness bundle: Mac /observe inputReachability=ready plus stateWitnessId, and browser heartbeat/session epoch plus tabId. It atomically leases the step, records dispatch, and transitions outcomes to succeeded, failed, blocked, or unknown. Unknown (disconnect after dispatch) is never retried automatically; it triggers read-only reconciliation against receipt/evidence, then either resumes or asks the owner. Browser commands with stale epochs are quarantined rather than consumed. Expose saga status, step evidence, and an approval/reconcile endpoint, reusing existing job receipts and undo routes.
- **owner gets:** When the Mac or browser drops during a real task, the owner gets an honest paused state and a safe way to continue or undo instead of duplicate sends, phantom success, or lost work. It turns the existing action pieces into dependable hands across devices.
- effort: Medium-high: relay state machine + durable storage, Mac witness emission, browser epoch/quarantine changes, typed receipt adapters, and pendant progress/approval rendering; integration tests for crash/disconnect at every transition.  ·  risk: A strict witness gate may pause work more often until permissions are fixed; stale queued commands could appear delayed. Recover by retaining all evidence, allowing explicit owner-approved override for low-risk reversible steps, and providing cancellation/compensation. Never mark success from transport acknowledgment alone.
- cost: Negligible relay storage/CPU; 1–3 cheap background reconciliation calls per interrupted saga. No additional per-step realtime calls.  ·  latency: Adds tens to hundreds of milliseconds for witness validation and lease persistence; avoids costly duplicate actions and can run long work asynchronously.
- security: Reduces replay and confused-deputy risk; stores only hashes/IDs in relay where possible. Requires strict binding of witness to tab/session and expiry, and approval for irreversible effects.
- depends on: Mac local state witness and browser reconnect quarantine (mac-planner contribution); A durable relay job/saga store rather than in-memory /execute orchestration; Typed action receipts with provenance and compensation metadata; Pendant progress and approval UI/event channel
