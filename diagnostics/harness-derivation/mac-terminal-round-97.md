## Capabilities it proposed

### "If something I asked you to do on my Mac fails or gets stuck, diagnose it, try the safest available alternate route, and tell me exactly what happened without making me ask again."
- **useful because:** Today the system can record a failed job, but repeated browser failures are indistinguishable from a transient outage and can be retried blindly. This would turn a dead Mac/browser bridge into a useful result: detect the failure class, avoid duplicate retries, fall back to relay/public web or a pendant-spoken explanation, and preserve the exact evidence for later continuation.
- **path:** mac-planner → browser-extension → relay-realtime → pendant → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use a cheap background classifier for failure fingerprints and routing; reserve realtime only for the short spoken status or a genuinely ambiguous recovery decision.
- **latency:** Classify within 1 second of a terminal failure; attempt one alternate route within 10 seconds; speak a concise status immediately and continue durable work asynchronously.
- **cost:** Usually <$0.01 per failure using a small classifier; dominant cost is any alternate browser/model call, not the receipt or fingerprint computation.
- **security:** Private page text, URLs, and shell stderr must remain on the Mac/relay authorization boundary; never upload authenticated page contents to a public fallback. Alternate actions must inherit the original job's intent and idempotency key, and only reversible/read operations may be attempted automatically; mutations remain as the owner's existing trusted full-control policy but must be clearly reported.
- **missing:** A durable failure-fingerprint and retry-budget record keyed by action type, normalized error, extension/permission state, and idempotency key.; A route selector that can distinguish browser-offline, permission-missing, timeout, command failure, and ambiguous planner errors and choose Mac, authenticated browser, relay, or spoken handoff.; A cross-surface completion event so relay-realtime can announce terminal failure/fallback without polling and dashboard can show the causal chain.; A safe private/public data-boundary marker for deciding when public fallback is legal.

### "When I ask “what did you do for that?”, show me one trustworthy, chronological answer covering the pendant, relay, Mac shell, and browser: what was attempted, what actually changed, what failed, and the evidence—without rerunning anything."
- **useful because:** Today status is fragmented: relay job status, Mac jobs, browser receipts, and journal entries can disagree or omit tier attribution. The owner cannot obtain a single verifiable postmortem for a task, especially after a browser timeout or unrestricted shell command. A trace-backed answer would make the system accountable without adding approval gates or reducing capability.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use deterministic aggregation and a small background summarizer; use the realtime tier only to speak the already-computed concise answer.
- **latency:** Return the structured trace in under 1 second from local/relay stores; generate the spoken summary in under 2 seconds. Never wait for a new Mac or browser round trip.
- **cost:** Near-zero for aggregation; typically under $0.01 for optional summarization. Storage and redaction are the dominant costs, not inference.
- **security:** The trace may contain private URLs, shell commands, file paths, and snippets. Keep raw evidence on the paired Mac, expose redacted hashes and bounded excerpts by default, require explicit owner request for sensitive detail, and never rerun a command merely to gather evidence. Preserve the owner's deliberate full-control policy; this is observability, not a gate.
- **missing:** A durable cross-surface trace ID propagated from the original pendant request through relay, Mac jobs, browser commands, and receipts.; An immutable event schema distinguishing planned, dispatched, started, completed, failed, retried, and actually-mutated effects.; A join/normalization service that correlates local job IDs, browser command IDs, receipt IDs, and relay IDs, including gaps and stale/offline periods.; A read-only owner-facing trace endpoint and dashboard/pendant renderer with evidence links, redaction, and retention/export controls.

### "When I say “stop that” to the pendant, cancel every still-running part of that request across the relay, Mac, and browser, leave completed changes untouched, and tell me exactly what was stopped and what could not be stopped."
- **useful because:** The system exposes cancellation for individual Mac jobs, but the owner cannot reliably revoke one conversational request across multiple surfaces. Without a causal cancellation group, a queued browser command or relay continuation may still run after the owner has changed their mind. This gives the wearable a real emergency brake while preserving an honest boundary around already-completed effects.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Deterministic cancellation propagation and state reconciliation; no expensive model call is needed except a short realtime spoken response when the owner says the phrase.
- **latency:** Acknowledge locally in under 300 ms, propagate cancellation within 1 second, and reconcile receipts within 3 seconds. Do not wait for an unavailable Mac to acknowledge; report it as unconfirmed.
- **cost:** Negligible API/model cost; state fan-out and durable event writes dominate.
- **security:** Cancellation must be scoped to the authenticated request trace, not a broad global kill switch. It must never pretend to undo completed shell, browser, or external mutations. Use signed cancellation tokens, monotonic sequence numbers, idempotent delivery, and an explicit distinction between cancelled, completed, and cancellation-unconfirmed.
- **missing:** A request-level cancellation group and signed cancellation token created at pendant/relay ingress.; Cancellation propagation and acknowledgement handlers in the Mac executor and browser command queue, including removal or tombstoning of pending commands.; A reconciliation state machine that merges late completions with cancellation, preserving receipts and never reversing completed effects automatically.; A pendant phrase/gesture handler and dashboard timeline showing stopped, completed, and unconfirmed branches.


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `mac-harness` — Add a tamper-evident causal execution ledger for every Mac action, including raw action type, normalized argv/command hash, cwd, environment-key allowlist, start/end timestamps, parent planner/relay request ID, retry lineage, exit code/signal, stdout/stderr hashes plus bounded redacted excerpts, and the exact receipt/undoability verdict. Link browser commands and relay announcements to the same trace ID, and expose a read-only replay bundle endpoint that can reconstruct why a job succeeded or failed without rerunning it.
- **owner gets:** When the Mac says “failed” or “done,” the owner can see what actually ran, which attempt caused it, and whether recovery is possible—especially important when shell is intentionally unrestricted and browser retries currently repeat the same failing navigate. It also makes the pendant's later “what happened?” answer trustworthy even after the 120-job store rolls over.
- effort: Medium: schema and append-only writer in local-agent, trace propagation through executor/browser bridge/relay, redaction and retention tests, plus dashboard timeline rendering.  ·  risk: Ledger leakage of secrets through command lines or stderr, and storage growth. Mitigate with explicit secret-pattern redaction, hashes instead of full output by default, short retention with owner export, and atomic append/recovery after crashes. This is observation-only and must never block execution.
- cost: Negligible compute/storage cost (roughly a few KB per action, bounded and compacted); no model cost unless the dashboard asks for a natural-language explanation.  ·  latency: Under 5 ms synchronous metadata write; full output hashing/redaction can finish asynchronously without delaying the action result.
- security: Improves accountability but creates a sensitive local artifact. Encrypt at rest where available, keep it Mac-local by default, and send relay only trace IDs and redacted summaries unless the owner explicitly requests detail.
- depends on: Existing action receipts and /journal need a stable trace/parent ID field.; Existing job retention needs compaction/export so causal records survive beyond the 120-job window.; Relay and browser bridge need to echo the trace ID in completion events.

### `integration` — Introduce a cross-surface readiness lease and wake/resume protocol. The Mac agent publishes signed capability leases (shell, accessibility, screen recording, browser extension, relay link) with freshness and reason codes; the relay expires stale leases instead of treating a last-seen browser as online, parks dependent jobs with a single causal blocker, and resumes exactly once on a verified heartbeat. The pendant receives a one-sentence blocker only when the owner asks or a configured job becomes actionable, while the dashboard shows pending work grouped by blocker rather than nine opaque browser commands.
- **owner gets:** The current Mac reports browser offline, 9 pending commands, missing Accessibility/Screen Recording, and ready=false, yet callers can still spend 45 seconds retrying the same navigation. The owner gets truthful “waiting for Safari bridge” state, no duplicate work, and automatic continuation when the bridge or permission becomes available—without changing the deliberate unrestricted shell policy.
- effort: Medium-high: define lease schema and signatures, heartbeat/expiry handling in local agent and relay, job dependency state machine, one-shot resume deduplication, pendant and dashboard status rendering.  ·  risk: A stale or incorrect lease could delay work or resume at the wrong time. Use short TTLs, monotonic attempt IDs, explicit blocker categories, and a manual “resume now” endpoint; expiry must park rather than discard jobs.
- cost: Tiny D1/local state and heartbeat traffic; no additional model calls. One extra relay heartbeat per surface (seconds-scale cadence, configurable for battery).  ·  latency: No impact on normal actions; failed jobs become parked immediately, and resume starts within one heartbeat interval (target 5–15 seconds).
- security: Lease contents reveal device capability state but no page data; sign leases per paired Mac/relay and never include tokens, URLs, shell commands, or authenticated content.
- depends on: GET /ops/status and GET /browser/status must expose machine-readable capability reasons and timestamps.; Existing /jobs and /journal need a parked/blocked state and dependency/attempt fields.; Browser heartbeat/poll and relay mac-bridge presence must carry a shared trace or lease ID.; A small relay-to-pendant status event path is needed for actionable resume announcements.
