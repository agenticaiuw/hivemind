## Capabilities it proposed

### "“I have 15 minutes—what should I do right now?” (or “I’m overloaded; what can wait?”)"
- **useful because:** The owner currently gets briefs and individual task execution, but not a grounded decision about the best next action under time, attention, and interruption constraints. This turns scattered commitments into one small, explainable choice, with a deferrable alternative—not another noisy list.
- **path:** pendant: capture the spoken time/energy constraint and deliver one short spoken recommendation → relay-realtime: clarify only if the constraint is ambiguous, then issue a signed triage request → mac-planner: inspect local reminders, notes, calendar gaps, battery/connectivity, and current work context → browser-extension: read already-open authenticated task/project tabs for urgent unresolved items, without opening new sites → relay: merge evidence, rank by deadline, consequence, effort, and interruption cost, and persist the decision plus expiry → pendant: play the recommendation and accept 'do it', 'defer', or 'show why' → mac-planner/browser-extension: execute only reversible preparation (open the item, draft a note, set a reminder); require confirmation for sending, deleting, purchasing, or submitting
- **model tier:** Background planner (cheap model) gathers and ranks evidence; realtime is used only for the spoken clarification and final 1–2 sentence response. A deterministic policy layer enforces permissions and expiry; no expensive model is needed for routine ranking.
- **latency:** Under 8 seconds when Mac and browser are already connected; up to 20 seconds if authenticated tabs must be reattached. If a surface is unavailable, say which evidence was omitted rather than pretending completeness. Decision expires after the stated time window or a major new event.
- **cost:** Roughly $0.01–$0.05 per invocation depending on how many page extracts are needed; most cost is the planner context, so send normalized snippets and provenance rather than whole pages. Realtime audio generation is the small fixed tail.
- **security:** Private calendar, reminders, notes, and authenticated pages leave their surfaces only as minimized excerpts and hashes. Do not expose secrets in the spoken rationale. Browser reads are allowed by owner policy, but any external side effect remains behind explicit confirmation and an auditable receipt.
- **missing:** A durable cross-surface decision record with constraints, evidence, chosen action, expiry, and dismissal reason; A normalized urgency/effort/interruption-cost projection across calendar, reminders, notes, and browser tabs; A single preflight that reports stale or unavailable surfaces before ranking; The requested local interruption gate and pendant audio queue/action primitives; 24 kHz superwideband pendant audio end-to-end, so the recommendation is intelligible in noisy environments


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `firmware` — Make 24 kHz playback the negotiated end-to-end contract rather than a decode-only mode: add a capability handshake (sample rate, frame size, jitter-buffer depth), a timestamped adaptive jitter buffer, and a CPU-aware half-duplex scheduler. During playback, pause/downsample uplink unless speech activity is detected; during capture, use the existing 16 kHz/16 kbps path. Add a measured fallback ladder (24 kHz → 16 kHz) and expose underrun, decode-time, and resampler telemetry in the receipt. Validate against the real I2S clocks and the ESP32 bridge instead of assuming the prototype wire rate is stable.
- **owner gets:** The owner hears a clear, continuous answer instead of clipped or delayed speech, while the pendant avoids spending nearly all of its CPU on simultaneous Opus encode/decode. It also makes the promised 24 kHz audio honest: when conditions cannot sustain it, the device degrades cleanly and says why rather than silently breaking.
- effort: Medium-high: firmware audio state machine and telemetry, relay negotiation/transcoding changes, bridge clock validation, and an automated noisy-link/interruption test matrix. Requires an actual pendant/bridge bench run, not just simulator tests.  ·  risk: A scheduler bug could drop a user utterance or leave the device in playback mode. Recover with a watchdog timeout to capture-only, a physical-button abort, sequence-numbered frames, and an always-supported 16 kHz fallback. Roll out behind a negotiated feature flag and retain current behavior for unknown firmware.
- cost: No per-request API increase; modest extra relay metadata. Engineering/test cost is the main cost. On current hardware no component cost, but a product revision may need a better I2S clock/amp (roughly $2–$8 BOM, negligible incremental power relative to the radio).  ·  latency: Adds one short capability exchange at session start; adaptive buffering targets 60–120 ms and should reduce retransmission-induced stalls. Half-duplex gating may delay uplink by a speech frame during playback, which must be surfaced in UX.
- security: Telemetry contains timing and quality counters only, not audio content. Capability negotiation must be authenticated to prevent a downgrade or malicious frame-size request; never log raw PCM or Opus payloads.
- depends on: End-to-end 24 kHz acceptance criteria and a connected pendant/ESP32 bench; Relay Opus transcode path updated to preserve negotiated rate and timestamps; A firmware build/flash/test harness that can collect UART audio telemetry

### `hardware` — Replace the prototype’s single-button interaction with a sealed, low-profile two-way tactile control: a three-position momentary rocker (left/neutral/right) plus a small haptic actuator, retaining the existing button as press-to-talk. Firmware should expose it as a signed, debounced event stream with press, hold, and release semantics; the relay maps those events to accept/defer/more-info, focus-mode changes, and safe transaction approval. Mac and browser surfaces consume the same event IDs and return a short haptic acknowledgement, so the control remains useful even when the voice link is unavailable.
- **owner gets:** The owner can make consequential choices quietly—in a meeting, on a walk, or when speech is inconvenient—instead of having to speak to the pendant or reach for a phone. A single left/right gesture can accept a prepared reminder, defer a decision, or reject an action, while haptic feedback confirms what happened. This gives the wearable a reliable control channel for the entire hive, not just a microphone and speaker.
- effort: High: product enclosure and input redesign, electrical debouncing, low-power firmware event protocol, relay event persistence and replay, and Mac/browser handlers with receipts. Prototype first with an external wired rocker, then validate ingress protection, accidental activation, and glove use.  ·  risk: The owner could trigger an action accidentally or misunderstand haptic patterns. Default every gesture to reversible/no-side-effect operations; require a deliberate hold plus distinct double-pulse acknowledgement for irreversible approvals; provide a physical lockout gesture and a voice escape. Event IDs, timeout expiry, and receipts make duplicate delivery recoverable.
- cost: Prototype hardware roughly $5–$15 in components and enclosure changes, with well under 1 mA average added draw for the actuator/input when idle and brief higher current during haptic pulses. No additional model/API cost for local gestures; relay persistence adds negligible storage cost.  ·  latency: Local acknowledgement should be under 100 ms. A remote action depends on the relay/Mac or browser, but the pendant can acknowledge receipt immediately and later signal completion or failure.
- security: Treat the rocker as an authenticated intent signal, not proof of identity: an unlocked or unattended pendant must not authorize purchases, sending, deletion, or publication. Bind approvals to a displayed/spoken summary, short expiry, action hash, and one-time event ID; retain only the event metadata, never ambient audio.
- depends on: A pendant firmware event protocol that survives dropped links and replays safely; Cross-surface action receipts and expiry-aware approval handling; A compact haptic vocabulary documented for the owner; A future product enclosure/board revision; the current Nordic development kit is explicitly provisional
