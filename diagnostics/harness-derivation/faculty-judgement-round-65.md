## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Give me one useful update, not five separate briefings—and if I miss it, catch me up without repeating what I already heard.”"
- **useful because:** The owner currently has several successful daily routines at overlapping times (morning brief, calendar/mail/files, news/research) but no shared attention ledger. This would merge discoveries across Mac, authenticated browser, relay, and pendant into one expiring, prioritized stream; suppress duplicates, remember spoken/acknowledged items, and replay only still-actionable items after a missed connection.
- **path:** relay-realtime receives a live spoken item or a one-button request for catch-up and plays the selected short item → mac-planner runs scheduled collection via /routines/:routineId/run, /briefing, /research and reads machine state through /machine-context → browser-extension reads owner-private tabs through browser_run_actions and POST /research {mode:"page"} without sending or submitting → relay persists a compact attention ledger and delivery receipts, while the Mac leaves a cited review queue/audio artifact for later
- **model tier:** Background/scheduled collection uses a cheaper non-realtime model; realtime is used only to choose and phrase the next item during a live pendant interaction. Deduplication and expiry should be deterministic before model summarization.
- **latency:** Scheduled merge under 30 seconds after source jobs complete; live catch-up decision under 1 second from the compact ledger, with audio beginning within 2 seconds. If the Mac or browser is offline, deliver the last trusted pending item and mark it stale rather than inventing freshness.
- **cost:** About $0.01–$0.05 per daily merge, dominated by summarizing genuinely new source text; live selection is a few hundred tokens on the realtime tier. Deterministic hashing, TTL, and receipt checks should cost effectively zero.
- **security:** Private browser extracts and calendar/mail content leave the Mac only as compact fact snippets and citations; redact secrets and preserve source sensitivity. Never auto-send mail, submit forms, buy, or delete. A spoken 'skip' or button press should acknowledge only the current item, not erase its source. Require confirmation for any suggested destructive or external action.
- **missing:** A durable cross-surface attention-item schema with source, semantic fingerprint, urgency, expiry, and delivery/acknowledgement receipts; A timezone/quiet-hours authority: current owner preference says America/Chicago while machine-context reports America/New_York; A pendant-local queue/ack marker that survives LTE-M drops and prevents replay storms; A merge endpoint or worker job that can consume existing briefing/research/routine outputs without re-running expensive source collection

### "“When you do something for me, don’t just say it ran—tell me whether the outside world actually reflects the result, and warn me if it didn’t.”"
- **useful because:** Today an action can produce a local execution receipt while the real service remains pending, rejects it, changes it, or silently fails. The owner should receive a plain-language outcome such as confirmed, awaiting external confirmation, or could not verify—with the evidence and the next safe step—rather than false closure.
- **path:** relay-realtime asks for approval when a corrective or retry action could have external side effects and speaks the short outcome → mac-planner executes reversible preparation and reads local receipts, mail, calendar, and notifications through the Mac agent → browser-extension revisits the exact authenticated tab/session and checks the service’s postcondition (confirmation number, changed status, sent-state, or error) → relay remains the durable coordinator that correlates intent, execution receipt, and independent postcondition evidence across the Mac and browser
- **model tier:** Use deterministic postcondition checks and typed evidence first; use the cheaper background model to map a goal to likely confirmation signals. Reserve realtime for the owner-facing explanation or an approval question, never for background polling.
- **latency:** Immediate local result in under 2 seconds; verify fast services within 10 seconds and slow services by scheduled follow-up. If verification is unavailable, say so explicitly and do not claim success.
- **cost:** Roughly $0.005–$0.03 per completed action, dominated by one authenticated page revisit or notification read; deterministic checks and receipt correlation are negligible. Long-running checks should be scheduled/background, not realtime.
- **security:** Revisiting authenticated pages exposes only the minimum needed fields to the coordinator; retain hashes/snippets rather than whole pages. Never retry a potentially duplicate purchase, message, deletion, or submission automatically. Require confirmation before any retry or compensating action, and show the evidence source and timestamp.
- **missing:** A typed intent-to-postcondition contract (expected state, acceptable variants, timeout, and whether duplicate retry is dangerous); A durable verifier that can reattach to the original browser tab/session or read the relevant Mac notification/email without confusing an old confirmation; A state machine separating locally executed, externally accepted, externally completed, and unverifiable; A user-visible escalation path on the pendant for failed or ambiguous outcomes


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

Normalize each result into {itemId, semanticFingerprint, sourceRefs, sensitivity, urgency, expiresAt, state}, merge equivalent items across jobs, and record delivery/ack/skip receipts per surface. Use deterministic fingerprinting first, then a cheap background model only for borderline semantic duplicates. Expose GET/POST attention endpoints plus a pendant-safe last-delivered cursor; do not treat playback completion as acknowledgement unless the owner presses the button or says 'got it'.
- **owner gets:** The owner gets one calm, current briefing instead of repeated calendar/news/work alerts, and can say “what did I miss?” after a dropped connection without hearing stale items or losing an urgent one.
- effort: Medium: D1 schema and worker, adapters for /briefing and /research records, relay delivery cursor, and a small Mac/pendant acknowledgement protocol; 1–2 weeks including replay and timezone tests.  ·  risk: A bad fingerprint could collapse distinct urgent items or repeat an item. Recover by retaining sourceRefs, keeping originals queryable, supporting 'show all', and using conservative duplicate thresholds. Clock conflicts could deliver at the wrong time until the owner timezone is explicitly selected.
- cost: Low ongoing storage (small D1 rows plus source IDs); roughly <$0.01/day for deterministic merges, with occasional cheap-model calls for ambiguous duplicates. No additional audio retention is required.  ·  latency: No impact on source collection; merge is asynchronous. Live catch-up adds one D1 read and should stay below 200 ms before realtime speech.
- security: Ledger stores summaries and source references, not raw private page content by default; sensitivity labels gate spoken delivery. External actions remain confirmation-gated and receipts must not contain secrets.
- depends on: A durable schema for cross-surface attention items and delivery receipts; An authoritative owner timezone and quiet-hours policy; A small offline pendant cursor/ack marker (or server-side idempotency key if firmware work is deferred)

### `integration` — Add a postcondition-verification state machine that compiles every approved action into an intent contract before execution: expected external state, evidence sources, timeout, freshness bound, and duplicate-risk. After /execute returns, automatically verify through the originating authenticated browser session or Mac notification/mail/calendar state, attach timestamped evidence, and transition the job to executed_locally, accepted_externally, completed, failed, or unverifiable. For duplicate-risk actions, never auto-retry; surface a pendant approval request with the evidence and safe alternatives.
- **owner gets:** The owner can trust the difference between “my computer ran the command” and “the appointment was actually booked,” without manually checking websites or guessing whether a retry might double-send or double-buy.
- effort: High: define contracts and typed evidence, add browser-session reattachment and Mac read adapters, implement timeout/recheck scheduling, and add failure/duplicate-risk tests across mail, calendar, forms, and reminders.  ·  risk: A stale email or cached page could be mistaken for fresh confirmation, or a verifier could misclassify a legitimate variant. Mitigate with source timestamps, session/tab affinity, freshness limits, conservative unverifiable states, and a visible evidence trail. Recovery is a human-reviewed retry or correction, never silent automation.
- cost: Low storage and deterministic processing; approximately one extra authenticated read per action. Ambiguous cases may cost a cheap model call. No new hardware required.  ·  latency: Adds a fast local receipt immediately, then verification asynchronously (typically seconds, potentially minutes for external systems). The owner is never blocked waiting for a slow confirmation.
- security: The verifier handles sensitive authenticated state, so scope reads to the minimum confirmation fields, redact secrets from receipts, encrypt retained evidence, and preserve existing confirmation gates for external side effects.
- depends on: A typed intent/postcondition contract and duplicate-risk classifier; Authenticated browser tab/session reattachment with reliable freshness; Mac adapters for reading relevant notifications, mail, and calendar state; A durable follow-up scheduler and pendant notification/approval path
