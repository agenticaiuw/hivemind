## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Carry this through everywhere: update the source on my Mac, reflect it in the logged-in site, and tell me only when all sides agree it is done.”"
- **useful because:** Today actions are handed to one surface and receipts describe individual attempts; a Mac change, private-browser mutation, and pendant notification can diverge. A cross-surface transaction gives the owner one coherent outcome: staged reversible work, explicit commit at the irreversible boundary, authoritative checks on each surface, compensation for completed reversible steps, and a concise spoken explanation of any partial failure.
- **path:** faculty-judgement creates a typed plan with dependencies, risk class, preconditions, and irreversible boundary → relay holds a durable transaction/lease, dispatches idempotent steps, survives a dropped pendant link, and speaks progress/final status → mac-planner/mac-terminal apply and verify filesystem/app actions through AppleScript or allowlisted shell, returning typed before/after evidence → browser-extension executes authenticated tab actions only when online, pinned to a session/tab and returning field-level before/after evidence → faculty-perception runs read-only authoritative probes after every commit and classifies each step and the whole saga as verified, contradicted, pending, or unverifiable → pendant presents a short progress state and asks for confirmation exactly once at the irreversible boundary; it can report partial completion offline when the link drops → dashboard exposes the transaction graph, evidence, compensation attempts, and a one-tap retry/undo for still-reversible steps
- **model tier:** Use the realtime tier only for the live confirmation and short status utterances. Use a cheaper background model for plan decomposition, retry/compensation selection, evidence normalization, and final briefing.
- **latency:** Immediate acknowledgement under 1 second; staged local actions within 5 seconds when surfaces are online; authenticated browser waits may take up to 60 seconds per step. Never hide a pending state behind a success phrase.
- **cost:** Roughly one realtime turn for confirmation/status plus 2–6 background calls for planning and verification; typically <$0.05 excluding any browser/computer-use metering. Dominant costs are screenshots/page extraction and repeatedly re-sending large evidence; use hashes, snippets, and typed deltas instead.
- **security:** A transaction could touch multiple accounts or leave a partially committed state. Require explicit recipient/account identity and owner confirmation for irreversible sends/deletes/purchases; redact secrets from evidence; bind each step to a session, tab, machine, and short-lived lease; never treat a receipt as proof. Data leaving the Mac is limited to requested fields, hashes, and redacted evidence unless the owner opts in.
- **missing:** A durable cross-surface saga/lease coordinator (not just per-job execution); Step dependency, idempotency, compensation, and commit-boundary schema shared by Mac and browser harnesses; Authoritative postcondition probes and a verifier that returns verified/contradicted/pending/unverifiable; Evidence envelopes linking receipts to source, pre/post snapshots, and transaction/step IDs; Pendant-visible transaction state and exactly-once irreversible confirmation

### "“Stop everything right now.”"
- **useful because:** The owner cannot currently revoke an in-flight action everywhere with one unmistakable command. A browser command may be queued while a Mac job is running and the relay may still be speaking progress. A pendant-triggered global stop would immediately halt queued and dispatchable work, prevent new mutations, and return a bounded inventory of what completed, what was cancelled, and what needs inspection.
- **path:** pendant detects the dedicated stop gesture or spoken stop phrase locally and emits a high-priority signed stop signal → relay is the always-awake revocation authority: marks the owner's action epoch revoked, rejects new mutations carrying the old epoch, and broadcasts cancellation to Mac and browser workers → Mac agent cancels active cancellable jobs and stops dispatching queued actions, using existing job cancellation where possible → browser harness deletes pending commands and refuses results for revoked command epochs, preserving the tab/session evidence for inspection → faculty-perception performs read-only reconciliation after the stop and reports the last known state of each surface → dashboard shows a red global-stop state, cancellation acknowledgements, and any mutation that could not be interrupted
- **model tier:** No expensive model is needed to stop or revoke work: use signed protocol messages and deterministic cancellation. Use a cheaper background model only to summarize the resulting partial-state inventory; use realtime solely if the owner is actively asking what stopped.
- **latency:** Pendant-to-relay revocation under 500 ms when connected; relay-to-Mac/browser acknowledgement within 2 seconds. If the pendant link is down, the local stop indicator must still suppress queued local actions and retry revocation when connectivity returns.
- **cost:** Near-zero model cost for the stop path; small durable relay writes and cancellation requests. Optional post-stop summarization is one cheap background call, dominated by evidence collection rather than inference.
- **security:** The stop signal must be authenticated, replay-protected, and scoped to this owner and device pair. It must fail closed for new mutations but never pretend an already-completed irreversible action was undone. Preserve tamper-evident cancellation and completion records while applying existing retention/deletion policy.
- **missing:** A global owner action epoch/revocation protocol shared by relay, Mac, browser, and pendant; A high-priority cancellation channel that outranks normal queued work; Pendant-local stop gesture/state that works during a dropped network link; A deterministic post-stop reconciliation report


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `relay` — Add a durable cross-surface transaction ledger and saga coordinator between the existing plan/execute/jobs/browser/pipeline paths. A transaction record contains ordered steps with dependency edges, idempotency keys, target surface/session/tab, precondition and postcondition descriptors, reversibility, compensation action, commit boundary, lease expiry, and evidence references. The relay dispatches one step at a time, persists acknowledgements before advancing, pauses at irreversible steps for a single owner confirmation, retries only idempotent steps, and runs compensations on reversible partial failure. Extend job receipts rather than replacing them, but require transactionId/stepId and evidence-envelope linkage; emit pipeline events and pendant audio for state transitions. Keep authoritative verification as a separate read-only worker so execution never self-certifies.
- **owner gets:** A request spanning the Mac and a private logged-in tab either finishes coherently or clearly tells the owner what remains; dropped links, browser delays, or a restarted Mac no longer create silent half-completed work. The owner gets one spoken pending/verified/partial result instead of guessing from unrelated receipts.
- effort: Medium-high: schema/migrations, relay state machine, idempotent dispatch adapters, compensation policy, and integration tests for crash/retry at every boundary.  ·  risk: Duplicate mutations if idempotency is missing, or harmful compensation if a supposedly reversible action has side effects. Default unknown actions to pause; use short leases, durable transition logs, and operator-visible recovery. Existing per-job undo remains the fallback.
- cost: Negligible storage/queue cost; background verification adds 2–6 cheap model or rule-based calls per multi-step transaction. Evidence should be hashes/deltas by default to avoid repeated token and storage cost.  ·  latency: Adds tens to hundreds of milliseconds for ledger writes per step and intentional waits at browser/verification boundaries; immediate spoken acknowledgement remains under one second.
- security: Improves isolation by binding every command to an authenticated machine and browser session, redacting evidence, and making account identity/irreversible confirmation explicit. The ledger itself contains sensitive metadata and needs encryption, retention limits, and deletion controls.
- depends on: Existing receipt schema must accept transactionId/stepId/evidence references; A verified read-only postcondition probe (the requested verify_action_proof capability); Durable action lease and pendant confirmation primitives (already requested, not yet observed as granted); Browser extension must be online for private-tab steps; otherwise the saga must remain pending rather than claim success
