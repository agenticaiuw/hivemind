## Capabilities it proposed

### "“I’m going into a meeting. For the next hour, watch this thread and handle only routine, reversible follow-ups; tell me if anything important changes, and let me stop it from the pendant.”"
- **useful because:** Today the owner must either micromanage every step or grant an unbounded instruction. This creates a bounded, expiring delegation that spans the logged-in browser, Mac apps, always-on relay, and the pendant’s physical stop control. It can triage incoming changes, prepare or perform explicitly reversible housekeeping, pause on ambiguity or any irreversible consequence, and return one auditable completion/stop receipt.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → unified → faculty-perception → faculty-judgement → faculty-action
- **model tier:** Use the expensive realtime model only to establish the delegation contract and speak urgent exceptions. Use a cheaper background model for polling, change classification, and receipt summarization; use the local Mac planner for concrete app/browser steps.
- **latency:** Contract confirmation and pendant stop acknowledgement under 1 second when connected; ordinary checks every 2–5 minutes; urgent-change speech within 10 seconds of detection. If the pendant or browser goes offline, stop claiming completion and queue a clearly marked degraded-state receipt.
- **cost:** Low-to-moderate: one realtime turn to define/confirm the lease, then inexpensive background classification per observed change; Mac/browser execution dominates operational overhead, not tokens. Avoid sending unchanged page contents by storing hashes and sending only deltas.
- **security:** The lease must contain an exact scope, allowed action types/apps/sites, maximum duration, and an action count/budget; it must expire by default and pause on uncertainty, login/permission changes, new recipients, money, deletion, or sending. The pendant gets a local stop latch and audible confirmation; stop must win over queued work. Private browser content stays on the Mac/browser bridge where possible, with only minimal deltas and receipts sent through the relay. Every action needs provenance, before/after evidence, and undo where available; owner confirmation remains mandatory for mail sends, purchases, deletion, and other destructive actions.
- **missing:** A durable delegation-lease record shared by relay, Mac, and browser, with expiry, scope, budget, and pause reasons; A single cross-surface preflight/health snapshot that verifies browser session, Mac reachability, relay queue, and pendant delivery before claiming the lease is active; A pendant-local stop latch that works offline and is checked before every queued action; Delta-based authenticated page/thread watchers that can feed the lease without copying full private pages; A unified receipt stream that correlates observation, decision, action, pause, stop, and undo across surfaces

### "“During this conversation, quietly tell me if I’m about to promise something that conflicts with my calendar, existing commitments, or a private account deadline—and save only the commitments I approve.”"
- **useful because:** The owner currently has to remember every promise while simultaneously talking. This would make the pendant a private second set of eyes: it detects proposed commitments in the live conversation, checks them against the owner’s actual calendar/tasks/email/browser deadlines, and gives a discreet warning before the promise is made. It also prevents unwanted recording or memory capture by requiring explicit approval for each saved commitment.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → faculty-perception → faculty-judgement → mac-planner → browser-extension → unified
- **model tier:** Realtime is justified only for low-latency speech segmentation and urgent conflict warnings. A cheaper background model reconciles extracted candidate commitments against calendar, mail, task systems, and authenticated browser pages. The Mac and browser surfaces perform deterministic lookups and create approved reminders or drafts.
- **latency:** Warning within roughly 1–2 seconds of a candidate promise; ordinary account/calendar checks within 5 seconds. If the network or private browser is unavailable, the pendant says that it cannot verify rather than implying there is no conflict.
- **cost:** Moderate during an active conversation because speech segmentation and conflict checks are repeated; minimize cost by sending only candidate commitment spans, not the whole audio stream, and cache unchanged calendar/account facts. Background reconciliation should use the cheaper model.
- **security:** Audio and candidate commitments are highly sensitive. Keep raw audio on the pendant/relay only for the short processing window, discard it by default, and transmit minimal text spans. Never inspect private accounts without an active user-started session. Do not write to memory, calendar, reminders, or drafts without per-item approval. Warnings must cite the conflicting item and source without exposing unrelated private content. A physical pendant control must immediately suspend listening.
- **missing:** A live candidate-commitment extractor that emits short, uncertainty-scored spans without retaining raw audio; A cross-surface obligation/deadline reconciliation service that can compare a candidate promise with calendar, reminders, mail, and authenticated browser state; A low-distraction private warning channel on the pendant distinct from normal spoken responses; An approval queue that lets the owner accept, edit, defer, or discard each candidate before persistence or reminder creation; A strict short-lived audio retention and disclosure policy enforced at the relay


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Add a durable Delegation Lease Coordinator between /plan, /execute, the browser command queue, Mac jobs, and the pendant event/audio pipeline. A lease is a signed record containing owner intent, allowed surfaces/actions, expiry, maximum actions/cost, current revision, and pause/stop state. Every observation and action must acquire the current revision, revalidate reachability and policy immediately before execution, append a typed receipt, and release/advance the lease. A pendant stop event invalidates the revision atomically; stale queued jobs become no-ops with an explicit stopped receipt. On expiry or any precondition failure, the coordinator pauses rather than guessing, and relay_job_status can render one chronological owner-facing receipt.
- **owner gets:** The owner can safely say “handle this while I’m away” without losing control or wondering whether an offline browser or stale Mac command acted later. A single pendant tap stops work everywhere, and the owner gets an honest account of what was observed, changed, skipped, and undone.
- effort: Medium-high: shared durable schema, compare-and-swap lease revisions, adapters for Mac/browser/job runners, event ordering, crash recovery, and integration tests for duplicate delivery and offline transitions.  ·  risk: A coordinator bug could pause legitimate work or, worse, allow a stale action. Default deny on missing lease/revision, idempotency keys on every action, append-only receipts, automatic expiry, and a recovery scanner that marks unknown outcomes for review rather than retrying blindly.
- cost: Small background storage/compute cost and a few extra receipt writes per action; no additional model call for the state machine. Classification remains on the cheaper background tier.  ·  latency: Adds one local durable read/write and reachability check before each action (typically tens of milliseconds); urgent stop propagation is prioritized over normal work.
- security: Improves security by making authority narrow, time-bounded, auditable, and revocable. Lease contents must exclude secret values; private page payloads remain surface-local, with hashes/deltas in receipts.
- depends on: The pending cross-surface preflight and universal-stop/local-interruption primitives, or equivalent implementations; A durable cross-surface event/receipt store with monotonic sequence numbers; Browser and Mac adapters exposing idempotency keys and explicit queued-job cancellation; Pendant event uplink plus an offline local stop latch

### `context` — Add a privacy-preserving Live Commitment Firewall: the audio pipeline emits only tentative, short-lived candidate commitment spans with confidence and conversation timestamp; a reconciliation service resolves referenced people, dates, and obligations against typed calendar/mail/task/browser facts, computes conflicts and uncertainty, and sends either a discreet warning or silence. Nothing becomes memory or an action until the owner explicitly approves it. Candidate spans expire automatically, and a pendant suspend event cancels extraction immediately.
- **owner gets:** The owner gets timely protection against accidental overpromising without turning every conversation into a transcript or an automatic task list. They can remain present in the conversation while the system catches conflicts they would otherwise notice too late.
- effort: High: streaming segmentation, entity/date resolution, cross-surface fact projection, low-latency warning delivery, approval UX, and extensive false-positive/privacy testing.  ·  risk: False warnings could distract the owner; missed conflicts could create false confidence. Use uncertainty thresholds, say “I couldn’t verify” when sources are unavailable, allow one-tap mute, and never silently claim coverage. Raw audio and rejected candidates must be deleted on a short TTL.
- cost: Realtime speech processing during active sessions is the dominant API cost; candidate-only processing and local/VAD filtering reduce it. Storage cost is negligible with aggressive expiry.  ·  latency: Adds a streaming inference and lookup path targeting 1–2 seconds; warning delivery gets priority over ordinary briefing or background jobs.
- security: Reduces data exposure compared with transcription by forwarding only candidate spans. Requires explicit disclosure that the session is listening, per-session activation, local suspend control, source-level access restrictions, and auditable approval records.
- depends on: A short-lived audio buffer with enforced deletion; Typed, provenance-aware projections of calendar, reminders, mail, and authenticated browser facts; An attention arbitration path for discreet pendant alerts; An owner approval queue for persistence and reminder creation
