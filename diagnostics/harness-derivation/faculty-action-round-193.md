## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Do this, but don’t tell me it’s done until the result is independently verified; if it is unknown, stop and ask me rather than retrying.”"
- **useful because:** This is the system’s most important end-to-end behavior: the Mac or browser can perform an action, but only faculty-perception can establish that the intended postcondition actually holds. It prevents false 'sent/saved/booked' claims and prevents duplicate side effects after an ambiguous timeout.
- **path:** unified → faculty-judgement → faculty-action → mac-planner → mac-vision → browser-extension → faculty-perception → relay-realtime
- **model tier:** Use the realtime tier only to clarify the owner’s command and render the immediate status; use the cheaper background/local planner for action decomposition, executor calls, and postcondition verification. No model should infer success from an executor receipt alone.
- **latency:** Stage approval immediately; execute within 2–10 seconds; verify within 2 seconds after each mutating step. On unknown, stop within 1 second and present a retry/inspect choice.
- **cost:** Usually one planner call plus one verifier call per mutating step; roughly $0.01–$0.08 depending on model and number of steps. The dominant cost is repeated page/app evidence, not the pendant signal.
- **security:** The verifier is read-only and must receive only declared postconditions and bounded evidence. Never send form secrets or page contents to the pendant. A timeout or missing evidence is UNKNOWN, never SUCCESS and never an automatic retry. Irreversible actions require the existing physical transaction approval latch.
- **missing:** A first-class transaction controller that binds operation_id, step_id, attempt_id, executor receipt, verifier provenance, and final state into one state machine; An explicit policy that distinguishes safe-to-retry from unknown for each action class; Verifier provenance attached to the user-visible receipt and the existing tactile outcome beacon

### "“When I hand you a task on my Mac and walk away, keep it alive, tell me exactly what is waiting on me, and let me resume it from the pendant later without repeating the whole request.”"
- **useful because:** Today a multi-step Mac/browser task is tied to a live session and becomes opaque when the owner leaves. A durable, resumable task capsule would let the owner use the pendant as the remote control for work already underway, while preserving the exact step, required approval, and evidence state instead of restarting or guessing.
- **path:** unified → faculty-judgement → faculty-action → mac-planner → mac-vision → browser-extension → relay-realtime → faculty-perception
- **model tier:** Use a cheap background/local planner to checkpoint and resume; use realtime only for the owner’s short resume/cancel conversation and urgent status.
- **latency:** Checkpoint after each mutating step in under 500 ms; pendant status under 2 s when requested; resume execution within 5 s after a fresh approval and reachable surface.
- **cost:** Usually one small planner call per checkpoint and one verifier call on resume; approximately $0.01–$0.05 per resumed task. Storage and transport dominate only for long workflows.
- **security:** Persist opaque task handles, step summaries, deadlines, and evidence hashes—not page secrets or form contents. Re-authenticate physical approval after expiry or context change. A resumed task must never silently use a stale browser session or stale Mac state; mark it blocked and ask the owner.
- **missing:** A durable task-capsule schema shared by relay, Mac planner, browser bridge, action ledger, and perception; A resume protocol that revalidates host/browser session identity and postconditions before continuing; Pendant-visible compact states for waiting-for-owner, blocked/stale, expired, and resumable


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `firmware` — Amend physical_transaction_approval_latch so an approval is accepted only after a deliberate sw1 gesture plus a short, physically plausible motion signature from the owned LSM6DSOX (for example: pendant is worn/moving, then becomes still, then button hold-and-release). Enable i2c2 and integrate the IMU with a low-rate interrupt/poll path; store only a compact motion-result bit and sensor timestamp in the signed decision envelope, never raw motion data. If the IMU is absent, stale, or noisy, fall back to the existing conservative gesture rule and mark the decision as legacy-input rather than silently claiming motion qualification.
- **owner gets:** A pocket bump, bag compression, or accidental button contact cannot approve a consequential browser or Mac action. The owner gets stronger physical proof without adding a conspicuous LED or requiring a new device; the already-owned accelerometer and haptic controller fit the jewellery form factor.
- effort: Medium: enable the existing i2c2 bus, add LSM6DSOX driver/configuration and calibration, define a 1–2 second motion classifier, then amend the signed envelope and test false accepts/rejects on the bench. No procurement.  ·  risk: A bad classifier could reject legitimate approvals or drain power. Keep the old deliberate hold as a safe fallback, expose the qualification bit for audit, and provide a firmware feature flag that defaults to conservative legacy behavior until measured. Never let an IMU error turn into approval.
- cost: No API cost; roughly 1–3 mA while sampling briefly and negligible storage (a few bytes per decision). Existing LSM6DSOX, nRF9160 DK, and I2C wiring are the hardware cost; wiring/configuration must be confirmed.  ·  latency: Adds at most 1–2 seconds to the approval gesture and a few milliseconds of I2C work; no impact on ordinary conversation capture.
- security: Improves resistance to accidental approvals but is not a cryptographic factor. Sign the motion-qualified/legacy-input status with the existing nonce and monotonic counter; retain refusal of expired, consumed, or digest-mismatched envelopes.
- depends on: physical_transaction_approval_latch (s10-j9l4); LSM6DSOX physically wired to i2c2; a firmware calibration/test fixture; the existing tactile_action_outcome_beacon for rejection feedback

### `model-routing` — Add an evidence-escalation router for action verification: every step starts with hash_only provenance; if the verifier cannot decide, automatically retry verification (not the mutation) once with a minimal bounded snippet; only a user-approved diagnostic path may request full evidence. Route hash-only and minimal-snippet checks to a cheap background model or deterministic comparator, reserving the realtime model for explaining UNKNOWN to the owner.
- **owner gets:** Actions finish faster and leak less private screen/page content. Most saves, sent states, URLs, and file checks can be proven by hashes or structured fields; the owner sees a useful explanation only when evidence is genuinely ambiguous, instead of a confident guess or an unnecessary dump of private content.
- effort: Medium: extend verify_operation_step result metadata with evidence_level, escalation_reason, and provenance hash; implement a bounded one-step escalation policy in faculty-action and a final UNKNOWN state. Add tests for redaction and no escalation on secret locators.  ·  risk: A hash can prove equality to the wrong baseline, and a snippet can contain sensitive text. Require the expected locator and baseline to be bound to operation_id/step_id, redact secret sensitivity entirely, cap snippet bytes, and never escalate automatically to full evidence. If escalation fails, remain UNKNOWN.
- cost: Reduces average model/context cost; occasional ambiguous steps cost one extra small verification call. No hardware cost.  ·  latency: Adds near-zero latency on decisive hash checks; ambiguity takes one bounded extra verification round, typically under 2 seconds.
- security: Strongly reduces data leaving the Mac/browser. Full evidence is opt-in and should require the existing approval/policy path; provenance hashes are opaque and must not encode content.
- depends on: verify_operation_step; the proposed operation_id/step_id/attempt_id correlation in action receipts; GET /observe; GET /browser/status; existing actionRisk.js and policyRouter.js

### `integration` — Add a resumable, owner-visible task capsule protocol across relay, Mac planner, browser bridge, and pendant: each capsule contains an opaque task ID, ordered step descriptors, current checkpoint, expiry, required approval class, executor receipt IDs, verifier provenance hashes, and a single next-action reason. On disconnect or sleep, atomically checkpoint before any new mutation; on return, revalidate app/browser identity and the last postcondition, then either continue, pause for the owner, or expire. The pendant receives only compact state codes and human-readable summaries, never secrets.
- **owner gets:** They can start a real task, walk away, and later ask “where did that get to?” or “continue it” from the pendant without losing work or accidentally repeating a side effect. It turns a fragile live computer-use session into something the owner can trust over a day.
- effort: High: shared schema and migration across job records, action ledger, browser session binding, relay retention, and pendant inbox; crash-injection tests are mandatory.  ·  risk: A stale capsule could resume in the wrong account or duplicate a side effect. Default to blocked on identity mismatch, expired approval, missing verifier evidence, or changed page/app state; require a new physical approval for any mutating continuation.
- cost: Small durable metadata cost; no hardware cost. Background checkpoint/verifier calls add modest API usage but reduce expensive full-task restarts.  ·  latency: Sub-500 ms checkpoint overhead per step; resume requires one host/browser freshness check and one verifier pass, usually 1–3 s.
- security: Improves privacy by sending only opaque IDs and bounded summaries to the pendant. Capsules need encryption at rest, per-task authorization, expiry, and deletion; never persist passwords, page bodies, or message contents.
- depends on: GET /jobs/:jobId/receipts; GET /journal/:jobId; GET /browser/status; GET /browser/sessions; verify_operation_step; physical_transaction_approval_latch; existing actionLedger.js and policyRouter.js

### `hardware` — Add a small secure element (for example, an I2C secure authenticator) to the production pendant and bind it to the physical-transaction approval latch. Generate the device signing key inside the element at provisioning; require a monotonic counter and challenge digest for every approval, with the nRF9160 firmware unable to export the private key. The relay verifies the certificate chain and rejects envelopes from an unprovisioned or rolled-back device. Keep the bench DK path software-compatible, but make production approvals hardware-backed.
- **owner gets:** A deliberate press on the pendant becomes a trustworthy authorization, not merely a message emitted by firmware that could be replaced or cloned. The owner can approve a high-impact Mac/browser action while knowing a copied relay token or duplicated device cannot approve it later.
- effort: High: select a supported secure-element part, add I2C wiring and provisioning fixtures, implement challenge/counter signing and certificate rotation, and update relay verification and recovery procedures. Requires a production pendant PCB/enclosure revision.  ·  risk: Lost or replaced jewellery could make approvals unavailable; bad provisioning could brick trust. Provide a documented replacement/revocation ceremony, bounded recovery key held offline, and retain safe cancel/unknown behavior. Do not put user passwords or action contents in the element.
- cost: Roughly $1–$5 per production unit plus PCB/provisioning engineering; a few mA only during signing and negligible persistent storage beyond the chip.  ·  latency: Typically tens to hundreds of milliseconds per approval, hidden inside the deliberate hold gesture; no impact on audio streaming.
- security: Substantially raises the bar against firmware cloning, replay, and relay-side forged approvals. It does not replace owner intent or server authorization: bind signatures to opaque transaction digest, expiry, monotonic counter, and risk class.
- depends on: physical_transaction_approval_latch; existing tactile_action_outcome_beacon; relay-side certificate/revocation store; production pendant PCB with an available I2C bus; a secure provisioning and device-replacement process

## Its own summary

Recorded three distinct advances: (1) a verified end-to-end transaction controller that refuses to claim success or retry after UNKNOWN, joining executor receipts to independent faculty-perception evidence and the pendant outcome beacon; (2) an IMU-qualified physical approval amendment using the already-owned LSM6DSOX, with conservative fallback and signed qualification state; and (3) an evidence-escalation router that starts hash-only, escalates once to bounded minimal evidence, and never leaks full content automatically. I still need the owner’s explicit policy for which action classes may auto-run versus stage, plus a bench-confirmed map of the LSM6DSOX wiring/calibration and measured false accept/reject rates before enabling motion qualification. The missing Mac lock/wake source also remains an honest blocker for any deferred action that depends on unlock state.

**Biggest unknown:** Whether the LSM6DSOX is physically wired to the DK’s i2c2 pins and what motion pattern is reliable on the actual pendant enclosure; the prompt establishes ownership but not wiring or measurements.
