## Capabilities it proposed

### "“If you start something on my Mac and the browser or connection drops, keep track of it and continue safely when it comes back—tell me what finished, what did not, and what you need from me.”"
- **useful because:** Today a browser command can sit in a pending queue or fail after a 45-second timeout while the owner receives no durable, cross-device explanation. This capability turns a disconnected multi-surface task into a resumable handoff: the relay/pendant can acknowledge the interruption, the Mac can preserve receipts and checkpoints, and the browser can resume only the uncompleted steps once its extension is healthy. It is beyond a generic durable job runner because it reconciles Mac receipts, browser tab/session identity, and pendant-delivery state, preventing duplicate submissions after reconnect.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-planner → browser-extension
- **model tier:** Use a cheap background worker/state machine for heartbeat monitoring, checkpoint reconciliation, retry classification, and delivery; use the realtime tier only for the owner's short spoken request and an urgent interruption notice. Escalate to the stronger planner only when a failed step is ambiguous or the page has materially changed.
- **latency:** A reconnect should be noticed within 10–20 seconds of browser heartbeat recovery; deterministic reconciliation should take under 2 seconds. Pendant interruption/complete notices should be queued for offline delivery and spoken on next contact. No polling or model call is needed while all surfaces are healthy.
- **cost:** Usually <$0.01 per task, dominated by occasional background model recovery for ambiguous page changes; healthy tasks use only D1/R2/worker operations. TTS/audio delivery is the secondary cost when a notice is actually spoken.
- **security:** Persist only task IDs, step receipts, tab/session IDs, URL hashes, and minimal redacted evidence; never copy page secrets into the pendant queue. Resume must require an idempotency key and verify the same tab/session plus a precondition (URL/title/semantic fingerprint) before any browser write. A changed page becomes paused and asks the owner; no automatic retries of submit/send/purchase steps. The owner can ask the pendant for status and cancel, while existing maximum-control policy remains unchanged.
- **missing:** A durable cross-surface checkpoint record linking each Mac action receipt to its browser command and pendant delivery acknowledgment.; A reconnect coordinator that consumes browser heartbeat changes, reconciles pending commands against receipts, and distinguishes safe read/retry steps from non-repeatable writes.; A compact offline status envelope on the relay/pendant (queued, resumed, paused-needs-owner, completed) with deduplication and expiry.; An explicit browser precondition/fingerprint check and idempotency key carried through /browser/poll and /browser/result before resuming.

### "“When I’m in a focus block or meeting, keep interruptions out of my way, but collect anything important and give me one short catch-up when it ends.”"
- **useful because:** The pendant is the one surface that can start or end a focus block without the owner touching the Mac, while the relay can hold alerts during a dropped link, the Mac can change local notification state and collect Calendar/Mail context, and the browser can stop surfacing non-urgent page-watch changes. Existing routines and briefings do not provide a shared interruption contract or a single end-of-block digest.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-planner → browser-extension
- **model tier:** Use deterministic rules and a small background model to classify queued events by urgency and deduplicate them. Use the realtime tier only for the pendant's start/end acknowledgment and when the owner explicitly asks for an interruption. Generate the end digest with a cheaper batch model, grounded in collected source snippets.
- **latency:** Start/stop acknowledgment under 1 second when connected; if offline, persist the state locally and replay it on reconnect. Suppression decisions are immediate and rule-based. End-of-block digest in under 10 seconds, with no need to wait on every source if one is unavailable.
- **cost:** Typically <$0.01 per focus block: mostly event storage and one small digest call. TTS costs only for the final spoken catch-up. No model call is made for routine low-priority events.
- **security:** The relay stores event metadata and redacted snippets with a user-configurable retention window; sensitive mail/browser bodies stay on the Mac unless explicitly included. Urgency classification must show source links and why an item was held. Emergency/allowlisted senders, alarms, and explicit owner interrupts bypass suppression. Never alter or send messages; this is notification control and summarization only.
- **missing:** A shared focus-state lease with owner/device origin, start/end time, timezone, and offline replay semantics.; A relay-side held-event queue with urgency/allowlist rules and deduplication across Mail, Calendar, browser watches, and Mac jobs.; Mac/browser adapters that apply and restore notification suppression without losing the prior user state.; An end-of-block reconciliation job that cites every surfaced item and reports anything that could not be inspected.

### "“Keep a private list of promises and follow-ups I make across email, meetings, and logged-in sites; remind me only when one is genuinely at risk, and let me close it with evidence.”"
- **useful because:** Today the system can read sources or produce briefings, but it cannot maintain a single, provenance-backed lifecycle for commitments that begin in one surface and are completed in another. The owner should be able to say this once on the pendant and have the relay monitor deadlines, Mac inspect Calendar/Mail, and the browser verify a completed ticket/order/form without turning every unchanged update into noise.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-planner → browser-extension
- **model tier:** Use a cheap background extraction/classification model on new or changed source snippets, with deterministic deadline and status rules. Use the realtime tier only when the owner asks about a commitment or when an urgent deadline needs a spoken alert. Use a stronger model only to resolve ambiguous people, dates, or evidence links.
- **latency:** Capture an explicitly spoken promise immediately into a pending record. Process new source deltas within a few minutes; deadline-risk alerts should be delivered within the configured window. A status question should answer in under 3 seconds from the ledger, with source inspection performed asynchronously when needed.
- **cost:** Usually under $0.02 per day for a normal owner: event-driven extraction over short redacted snippets and one compact risk digest. TTS is incurred only for spoken reminders. No continuous realtime inference.
- **security:** Keep the ledger private and minimize content: store commitment text, parties, due window, source IDs, sensitivity, and hashes rather than full mail/page bodies. Require explicit opt-in source scopes and quiet hours. Never mark a promise complete solely from a model guess; completion needs an owner utterance or cited evidence from the same account/context. Alerts must include why they fired and an easy dismiss/snooze action.
- **missing:** A durable commitment schema with states (captured, clarified, active, at-risk, done, dismissed), due-window uncertainty, owner/source provenance, and evidence links.; Cross-surface entity resolution for person, project, and account without copying secrets into relay memory.; Delta-driven extractors for Mail/Calendar and authenticated browser changes, plus a deduplicating risk scheduler.; A pendant action protocol for confirm, clarify, snooze, and mark-done-with-evidence, including offline replay.


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Add a cross-surface event ledger and focus-state lease in the relay. Each incoming alert or completion event gets a stable event ID, source (pendant/Mac/browser), sensitivity class, urgency, focus-block ID, and delivery acknowledgments. The relay exposes a compact delta feed; Mac and browser adapters report applied notification state and restore tokens. On focus end, a worker joins undelivered deltas with Calendar/Mail/browser citations, emits one digest, and marks each item delivered or unavailable. Expire leases and held payloads automatically, and make replay idempotent.
- **owner gets:** The owner can press one pendant control to get uninterrupted work without losing important things, even if the Mac or browser disconnects. They get one trustworthy catch-up instead of duplicate alerts, silent loss, or a permanently altered notification setting.
- effort: Medium-high: new D1 schema/state machine, relay endpoints, Mac notification adapter, browser event adapter, and pendant start/stop/replay protocol; add fault-injection tests for disconnects and duplicate delivery.  ·  risk: A stale lease could suppress an urgent event or fail to restore the prior notification state. Mitigate with hard lease expiry, emergency bypass rules, visible active-state feedback on the pendant, restore tokens, and a recovery sweep that replays anything unacknowledged. If the digest worker fails, retain the queue and report its unavailable state rather than dropping data.
- cost: Low storage and worker cost; one inexpensive digest inference per focus block, plus optional TTS. No continuous realtime model use.  ·  latency: State changes and suppression deltas are sub-second when online; reconnect replay is bounded by the next heartbeat. Digest generation is asynchronous and should complete within 10 seconds.
- security: Sensitive content remains on the Mac by default; the relay holds IDs and redacted snippets only, encrypted with short retention. Every digest item carries provenance and a reason for inclusion. Focus state is scoped to the owner's authenticated devices.
- depends on: A pendant-side focus start/stop event and offline persistence skill; A relay-held alert queue with TTL and urgency policy; Mac notification-state read/restore adapter (AppleScript where possible); Browser watch/event adapter that can pause and resume cleanly
