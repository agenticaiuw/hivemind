## Capabilities it proposed

### "Finish this across my Mac and logged-in browser tabs—even if I leave: carry out the dependent steps, resume after a device or network drops, and tell me exactly which steps happened, which did not, and what was undone."
- **useful because:** Today a decision can be made but execution is fragmented: Mac actions, private Safari state, public web work, and relay persistence have separate lifecycles. A cross-surface saga lets one spoken goal actually complete, or recover cleanly, instead of silently stopping halfway or duplicating an action after a retry.
- **path:** relay-realtime → faculty-judgement → faculty-action → mac-planner → mac-terminal → mac-vision → browser-extension → unified
- **model tier:** Use the realtime tier only to parse/confirm the spoken goal and report concise progress; use a cheaper background planner to compile the dependency graph, and deterministic action adapters for Mac/browser/relay steps. Escalate only ambiguous failures to the planner, not every step to the expensive model.
- **latency:** Start acknowledgement under 2 seconds; first executable step under 5 seconds; background continuation may run for hours. Progress is event-driven, with a final spoken or queued result rather than polling every turn.
- **cost:** About $0.01–$0.08 per ordinary multi-step job depending on planner retries; most cost is background model calls for recovery/ambiguity, while deterministic Mac/browser actions dominate wall-clock time. Storage/event overhead is negligible.
- **security:** Private page contents and action evidence remain on the Mac/browser bridge unless explicitly summarized to relay. Persist only redacted step receipts and hashes by default. Idempotency keys prevent duplicate sends or purchases. The owner policy currently has no approval gates, so this must not invent prompts; it should obey existing action policy and clearly mark irreversible steps and failures.
- **missing:** A durable cross-surface saga/job schema with dependency edges, leases, idempotency keys, and resumable checkpoints; Adapters that expose Mac and authenticated-browser actions as typed steps under one job, plus relay/public-browser fan-out; Compensation/undo handlers and a final causal receipt linking each action to its evidence; A job event stream to the planner/pendant, and durable persistence across Mac agent and relay restarts

### "Watch for the exact condition I described, and then carry out the whole prepared action automatically—for example, when the authenticated portal shows the approved invoice and the Mac is online, download it, file it, and tell me; do not act while any precondition is false."
- **useful because:** The owner can ask the system to do a one-shot action, but cannot leave behind a precise, durable conditional instruction that waits for facts across private browser state, Mac state, and relay availability. This turns an intention into an execution that happens at the right moment rather than failing early or requiring repeated voice commands.
- **path:** faculty-judgement → faculty-action → relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → mac-terminal → unified
- **model tier:** Use a cheap background model only to compile the owner's natural-language condition into typed predicates and resolve ambiguity at setup; use deterministic polling/event adapters for predicates and actions. Realtime is only for setup and final notification.
- **latency:** Register the watch in under 3 seconds. Evaluate event-driven predicates within 30 seconds where a source supports events, otherwise at a bounded cadence (for example 5–15 minutes); execute the action within one evaluation cycle and report immediately.
- **cost:** Roughly $0.01–$0.05 to compile and recover an instruction; ongoing cost is mostly lightweight relay storage and source polling, with no realtime-model spend while waiting.
- **security:** Private browser content stays at the browser bridge; relay receives only predicate outcomes and redacted evidence. Conditions must be immutable and inspectable after registration, with expiry, owner-scoped credentials, replay protection, and a complete receipt. It must never broaden a predicate after a near-match or act on stale cached content; existing owner action policy governs whether a resulting action is allowed.
- **missing:** A durable conditional-intent record containing typed predicates, freshness limits, expiry, and action graph; Cross-surface event adapters for authenticated browser changes, Mac reachability/files/apps, and relay wake/retry; A predicate evaluator that requires all preconditions from the same fresh observation window; A wakeable scheduler/event subscription layer and a cancellation/status interface exposed to the pendant; Evidence binding from the triggering observation to each executed action


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `relay` — Implement a cross-surface saga coordinator rather than another surface-specific queue. Accept a typed plan whose nodes target Mac actions, authenticated browser commands, or relay/public-browser work; persist job graph, dependency state, idempotency key, lease/heartbeat, evidence references, and compensation status in relay storage. Dispatch only ready nodes through existing adapters, resume leased nodes after restart, emit progress events, and expose one causal receipt plus cancel/undo endpoints. Keep private page payloads on the Mac; relay stores redacted hashes and metadata. This fills the missing connective layer between existing /plan, /execute, browser queue, Mac job receipts, and relay status.
- **owner gets:** A single request that spans a logged-in tab and the Mac will finish reliably after leaving the house or losing a connection, without duplicate actions or an unexplained half-complete state. The owner gets one truthful result showing each step, evidence, retries, and any compensation.
- effort: Medium-high: shared schema and storage, adapter contracts for three executors, lease recovery, event stream, and integration tests for restart/duplicate/failure scenarios.  ·  risk: A coordinator bug could run a later step after an earlier step only appeared successful, or compensation could be incomplete. Mitigate with idempotency keys, explicit typed terminal states (never infer success from timeout), per-node evidence, bounded retries, and conservative dependency semantics. Recover by canceling the saga and exposing manual-repair steps; do not claim rollback when it did not occur.
- cost: Small persistent metadata and event storage; roughly $0.01–$0.08 in background model usage per complex job, with deterministic action calls dominating no API cost.  ·  latency: Adds roughly 100–500 ms dispatch overhead per node; restart recovery is seconds, while long browser/Mac work remains asynchronous.
- security: Reduces data movement by keeping authenticated content at the browser/Mac edge. Requires signed job/node identities, least-privilege adapter tokens, encrypted receipts, redaction of page text, and retention limits.
- depends on: Durable browser runner (chg-16bc5dee); Browser progress/resumable polling (chg-14accc01 remaining gap); Existing Mac job receipts/undo and relay job records; A typed action contract shared by faculty-judgement and faculty-action

### `routines` — Add a conditional-intent engine distinct from ordinary routines and durable jobs. Store a typed predicate DAG (source, expression, freshness window, required confidence, expiry) plus a typed action DAG and an evidence-binding rule. Subscribe to browser-bridge changes and Mac/relay state events where available; use bounded polling only as fallback. Evaluate all predicates against one observation epoch, acquire a short execution lease, then dispatch the action graph through existing Mac/browser adapters. Persist trigger evidence, suppress duplicate firings with a content-addressed observation key, and expose pause/resume/cancel plus a concise pendant notification.
- **owner gets:** The owner can leave a precise intention such as “when the approved invoice appears and the Mac is online, file it,” and trust that it will happen once at the correct moment—even after sleep, disconnects, or a stale near-match—rather than having to remember to ask again.
- effort: High: predicate DSL and compiler, event/poll adapters, durable evaluation state, observation-epoch consistency, leases, deduplication, and tests for stale data, source races, expiry, and restart.  ·  risk: A false predicate could trigger an unwanted action; a race could combine facts from different moments. Require explicit freshness/confidence bounds, same-epoch evaluation, immutable compiled predicates, dry-run inspection, expiry defaults, and a terminal receipt containing the exact evidence. If a source is unavailable, remain pending rather than infer false or true.
- cost: Low storage/event cost; polling authenticated pages can consume browser budget and Mac power, so prefer bridge events and adaptive backoff. Background model cost is limited to initial compilation and exceptional ambiguity.  ·  latency: Event-backed conditions trigger within seconds; fallback polling adds the configured cadence. Setup adds a few seconds, but no realtime model is needed while waiting.
- security: Predicate evaluation of private page content remains on the Mac/browser edge; relay stores hashes and redacted outcomes. Encrypt intent definitions and bind execution to the owner's device/session. Never transmit credentials or raw page contents to the relay.
- depends on: A durable event/predicate store and wakeable scheduler; Browser bridge change/event feed (currently offline); Mac state/file/app event adapters that do not require Accessibility for supported AppleScript intents; Typed action/receipt contracts shared by faculty-judgement and faculty-action
