## Capabilities it proposed

### "“If I walk away while you’re working, finish the job and tell me in the least disruptive way—haptic if I’m wearing the pendant, spoken if I’m actively listening, and a cited Mac workbench if neither is available.”"
- **useful because:** Existing background jobs can finish, but completion is currently a generic receipt rather than a coordinated handoff. This makes the hive feel continuous: the relay knows the job state, the pendant knows whether the owner is present and muted, the Mac has durable evidence, and the browser can attach private-page citations without exposing them to the voice stream.
- **path:** relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → relay-realtime → unified
- **model tier:** Use deterministic state transitions and receipt formatting for every job; use the background tier (gpt-4.1-mini) to summarize completed evidence. Escalate to the expensive planner only when the job result is ambiguous or a user decision is needed; never use realtime for unattended work.
- **latency:** Local presence/attention decision under 200 ms; haptic/voice selection under 1 s after completion. Background summary can arrive within 1–3 minutes. If the pendant is offline, persist the receipt and deliver it on reconnect rather than retrying model calls.
- **cost:** Low: deterministic routing and receipt persistence dominate no model cost; one short background summary per completed job, roughly 2k–4k input tokens. Browser citations and screenshots, not generation, dominate storage and relay bandwidth.
- **security:** Private browser evidence stays in the authenticated browser/Mac workbench and is represented to the relay by opaque receipt references plus minimal summary. Never speak sensitive snippets automatically when the privacy latch is active or when presence is uncertain. Sending mail, buying, deleting, or submitting still requires explicit confirmation. Need signed receipt IDs to prevent stale or forged completion cues.
- **missing:** A shared job-receipt schema with evidence references, sensitivity, expiry, and delivery status; Pendant presence/attention telemetry and local haptic/LED receipt patterns; Relay-to-Mac durable delivery queue with reconnect and deduplication; Browser extraction results bound to the originating tab/session and citation anchors

### "“Forget what I just told you everywhere.”"
- **useful because:** Today, deleting a remembered fact does not guarantee that copies in job receipts, Mac notes, browser evidence, audio queues, or cached context disappear. The owner should be able to revoke a spoken fact across the entire hive and receive proof of what was removed, what was retained only as an anonymized audit record, and any surface that could not yet be reached.
- **path:** relay-realtime → mac-planner → mac-terminal → browser-extension → unified
- **model tier:** Use deterministic matching, sensitivity labels, and deletion propagation for the actual purge. Use the background tier only to resolve ambiguous references such as “the thing from yesterday”; use realtime solely to ask a brief clarification when two facts collide. Never use the expensive planner for a clear exact-key revocation.
- **latency:** Acknowledge the request on the pendant in under 500 ms. Start relay deletion immediately, fan out to Mac and browser within 2 seconds, and report convergence or unreachable surfaces within 10 seconds. Retry offline surfaces durably and notify the owner when they reconnect.
- **cost:** Near-zero model cost for exact-key purges; occasional short background disambiguation, roughly 1k–2k input tokens. Storage and engineering cost dominate because every derived artifact needs a retention index and deletion receipt.
- **security:** Deletion must be fail-closed: do not claim success until every indexed copy is gone or explicitly marked unreachable. Preserve only a non-reconstructable audit hash, timestamp, and actor. Browser credentials and unrelated page content must never be exported to perform the purge. Require confirmation only for broad ambiguous scopes; an exact “forget the bike lock code” should be immediately actionable.
- **missing:** A cross-surface provenance/retention index linking spoken facts to derived prompts, notes, receipts, audio, and browser evidence; Authenticated deletion APIs on relay, Mac workspace, and browser cache/session storage with idempotency keys; A cryptographic deletion receipt and reconnect queue for offline surfaces; A policy defining which operational logs may retain irreversible hashes and for how long


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `hardware` — Replace the prototype nRF9160 DK wearable I/O with a product pendant that adds a fuel-gauge IC over the currently unused I2C bus, a low-power haptic motor with a driver, and a hardware mute/privacy switch with an input visible to firmware. Expose battery percentage/health and privacy state in the pendant heartbeat; let the relay and Mac job runner use them to decide whether to start/continue audio, defer nonurgent work, and deliver completion/failure cues as haptic patterns when speech is unavailable.
- **owner gets:** The owner can wear it all day without discovering too late that a long call or queued job killed the battery, and can tell—without looking at a screen—whether a background task finished or needs attention. A physical privacy switch gives immediate confidence that the microphone cannot be used while muted, even if the network or server is misbehaving.
- effort: Product-board revision, battery-fuel-gauge driver and calibration, haptic driver/pattern table, privacy input and fail-safe audio gating, heartbeat schema, relay power/attention policy, and Mac job receipt integration; prototype validation across LTE transmit bursts and brownout/reconnect tests.  ·  risk: Added BOM and enclosure complexity; false battery estimates under LTE bursts could cause premature deferral, so use voltage/current calibration and conservative thresholds. Privacy switch must default to muted on boot/fault and be tested electrically, not inferred from a server flag. Recovery is fallback to LED/button and existing spoken output if the gauge or haptic driver fails.
- cost: Roughly $4–10 incremental prototype BOM (fuel gauge, motor, driver, switch, passives), plus small standby and haptic current; negligible API cost. Battery calibration and RF coexistence testing dominate engineering cost.  ·  latency: Heartbeat adds a few bytes and no perceptible conversational latency; haptic receipt cues are local and immediate. Power-policy decisions may defer background audio by seconds when charge is low.
- security: Improves privacy by making mute local and fail-closed. Battery and privacy telemetry should be treated as device metadata, not mixed into user content; never upload microphone data while the hardware mute is active.
- depends on: Define the production pendant power/battery design rather than the current DK; Add a typed device-heartbeat schema consumed by relay and Mac job runner; Implement local privacy latch and attention/receipt cue patterns

### `model-routing` — Add a typed intent-normalization front door before planner selection. Normalize paraphrases such as time/battery/status, completion-receipt requests, and browser read requests into canonical intents with required parameters; run deterministic handlers when confidence is high, and return a structured clarification when a required device/session is offline instead of escalating an empty or failed command to the planner. Log match confidence, fallback reason, and tokens avoided per request.
- **owner gets:** Simple things the owner says naturally (“what time is it right now?” or “check my battery”) should work every time and answer quickly, even when a browser is offline. The owner gets fewer unexplained failures and the system spends its expensive reasoning budget on genuinely ambiguous work.
- effort: Build a versioned utterance-to-intent grammar from the failed owner commands, typed parameter validation, offline-aware handler responses, regression tests against voice transcripts, and routing telemetry. Keep planner fallback for low-confidence utterances and never silently execute destructive intents.  ·  risk: An over-broad synonym could route a request incorrectly; require confidence thresholds, read-only allowlists, and a one-sentence confirmation for near matches. Revert by disabling individual grammar rules via feature flags.
- cost: Reduces planner calls and repeated context costs; current routing already shows 60% of requests saved from planner and ~8.6k-token planner baselines, so each additional deterministic match has meaningful savings. Engineering cost is modest; no new API spend.  ·  latency: Successful common intents should complete in roughly 0.5–2 seconds, though Mac/device status may still depend on bridge RTT. Low-confidence requests incur one extra normalization step, ideally under 100 ms.
- security: Improves safety by constraining common commands to typed, read-only operations and making destructive actions ineligible for automatic normalization. Preserve audit logs with canonical intent and original utterance hash, not raw sensitive audio.
- depends on: Expose a stable typed intent registry shared by relay and Mac agent; Add explicit offline/error result types for each device and browser surface; Keep planner escalation and receipt logging compatible with normalized request IDs

### `memory` — Create a cross-surface revocation ledger rather than treating memory as isolated records. Every sensitive utterance-derived artifact receives a non-content lineage ID and retention class at creation; relay, Mac notes/receipts, audio queues, and browser evidence register only that ID. A revoke operation fans out idempotent tombstones, securely erases content, and emits a signed convergence receipt listing deleted, already absent, and temporarily unreachable surfaces. Expired tombstones remain long enough to prevent resurrection from offline queues, then collapse to irreversible hashes.
- **owner gets:** When the owner says “forget that,” it would actually stay forgotten—even if a Mac job finishes later, a browser reconnects, or an old audio queue is replayed. They get a trustworthy answer instead of having to guess where their private words may have been copied.
- effort: Define lineage and retention schemas; instrument relay transcript handling, planner context projection, Mac journals/notes, browser extraction storage, and audio queues; implement authenticated tombstone fan-out, offline replay suppression, secure erase, and convergence tests including crash/reconnect scenarios.  ·  risk: Incomplete instrumentation could create false confidence, so unknown/unindexed artifacts must make the receipt say “not proven deleted.” Aggressive erasure could remove useful non-sensitive work products; require explicit retention classes and keep only non-reconstructable audit hashes. Recovery is via tombstone replay, not content restoration.
- cost: Small persistent metadata overhead per artifact and modest index storage; negligible model/API spend. The main cost is lifecycle instrumentation and security testing.  ·  latency: Exact revocation acknowledgement is local and immediate; full convergence typically under seconds online and deferred until offline nodes reconnect.
- security: Strongly improves privacy and limits secret propagation. Requires authenticated device identity, encrypted transport, key separation for content versus lineage, and careful prevention of deletion receipts leaking the deleted value.
- depends on: A shared provenance/retention index across all surfaces; Authenticated deletion and reconnect APIs; A defined privacy policy for operational audit logs
