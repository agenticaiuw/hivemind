## Capabilities it proposed

### "Finish this checkout/order for me, but make me approve only the final commitment on the pendant."
- **useful because:** This turns the current live Safari bridge into a trustworthy hands-off workflow: the Mac/browser can fill and navigate, while the owner sees a terse merchant/item/total summary on the pendant and makes the one deliberate physical approval that commits the purchase. The model never needs to receive card numbers or page secrets, and completion is not claimed until an independent verifier confirms the confirmation page or order record.
- **path:** relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → mac-vision → faculty-judgement → faculty-action → faculty-perception → pendant → relay
- **model tier:** Realtime for the spoken request and concise approval summary; background/local planner for browser navigation and retries; no expensive model call for the final verification.
- **latency:** 2–5 s to stage the checkout summary, under 1 s for each browser action, under 3 s after approval to verify the result. If the browser disconnects, hold the staged operation rather than guessing.
- **cost:** Usually <$0.03 in model/API cost: one realtime turn plus local/browser actions; verification is mostly deterministic state inspection. Vision is only used when DOM/extension evidence is insufficient.
- **security:** Never transmit form secrets, card data, passwords, or full page text to the pendant or relay. Bind approval to an opaque operation nonce, merchant, total, currency, and expiry; refuse if any of those change. Treat a missing or contradictory postcondition as unknown, not success. Require explicit approval for irreversible purchase/submit, and retain a redacted receipt only.
- **missing:** A typed browser transaction-summary envelope (merchant, item summary, total, currency, destination, expiry, digest) shared by browser, relay, pendant approval latch, and verifier.; A browser postcondition adapter that can identify the order confirmation using URL/DOM evidence without exposing sensitive fields.; A durable cross-surface operation state machine that survives a Mac/browser disconnect between staging, approval, submission, and verification.

### "I started a long task and lost the connection — tell me exactly where it stopped and let me resume or cancel it from the pendant without starting over."
- **useful because:** Long browser and Mac tasks currently risk ending in an ambiguous middle state when the bridge, browser session, or relay drops. A resumable checkpoint lets the owner recover safely: the system reports the last verified step, preserves the operation's idempotency key, and offers resume, retry-safe, or cancel rather than replaying an order, message, or file mutation.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → relay → mac-planner → browser-extension → faculty-perception → faculty-action → faculty-judgement
- **model tier:** Cheap background/local state machine for checkpointing and reconciliation; realtime only to explain the checkpoint and accept the owner's short choice.
- **latency:** Checkpoint write is synchronous within 250 ms of every mutating step. After reconnection, a status summary in under 2 s; resume begins only after fresh verification of the saved precondition.
- **cost:** Negligible API cost when connected; one short realtime turn only when the owner asks. Storage is small metadata per operation, not duplicated page contents or audio.
- **security:** Every step must carry an idempotency key and a precondition digest. Never replay an unknown mutation automatically. Resume must fail closed if the browser session, target identity, or relevant file state has changed. The pendant receives only redacted step labels and status, never credentials or page contents. Expire abandoned checkpoints and make cancellation auditable.
- **missing:** A durable operation journal with explicit states staged, executing, interrupted, verified, unknown, cancelled, and expired.; Idempotency-key propagation through Mac actions and browser commands so a reconnect cannot double-submit.; A pendant resume/cancel protocol that extends the existing approval latch without treating a short acknowledgement as approval.; A reconciliation worker that asks faculty-perception for fresh postconditions before allowing resume.

### "Before you send, upload, or paste anything outside my devices, show me exactly what data is leaving, who receives it, and what was redacted; let me approve the transfer from the pendant."
- **useful because:** Today an action can be approved as a whole while the owner cannot inspect its actual outbound payload. This gives the owner a practical privacy boundary for browser forms, email/messages, uploads, and relay calls: a compact semantic manifest and content digest expose the transfer without putting the sensitive content into the pendant or model context.
- **path:** faculty-judgement → faculty-action → faculty-perception → mac-planner → browser-extension → relay-realtime → relay → pendant
- **model tier:** Local deterministic classifier and hashing for ordinary payloads; a slower background model may produce a human-readable field summary. Realtime is used only to answer the owner's spoken question and read back the compact manifest.
- **latency:** Under 500 ms for hash/field inventory and under 2 s for a staged summary. Never delay low-risk local-only actions; external transfer stays staged until the owner approves or it expires.
- **cost:** Usually pennies or less: hashing and schema inspection are local; model cost only applies when a payload needs semantic labeling. No payload bytes need leave the Mac for the normal path.
- **security:** The manifest must not copy secrets, full message bodies, passwords, card numbers, or private page content to the relay or pendant. Use typed categories, byte counts, destination/origin, redaction counts, and a digest. Approval binds to the exact payload digest, destination, action, and expiry; any mutation invalidates it. Unknown classification is a hard stop, not permission.
- **missing:** A data-flow interception seam covering browser commands, POST /execute actions, relay uploads, and message/mail automation.; A typed redaction-and-manifest format with sensitivity enums and stable payload digests.; A policy engine that classifies local-only, private, and external transfers and routes the manifest to the physical approval latch.; A receipt verifier that proves the committed payload matched the approved digest without retaining its contents.


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `interaction` — Add an owner-visible cross-surface circuit breaker keyed to the Mac's observed foreground app and secure-input state. Before each irreversible Mac/browser step, faculty-action must re-read GET /observe and GET /browser/status; if the foreground app, browser tab/session, secure-input state, or browser freshness differs from the staged operation, atomically pause it and send a compact pending/changed-context pattern to the pendant. A deliberate resume gesture re-stages the operation and demands fresh verification; no automatic retry is allowed. Record the reason and both observations in the operation journal.
- **owner gets:** The system stops doing the wrong thing merely because the owner switched windows, opened a different tab, entered a password field, or let the browser session go stale. They get a clear 'paused because context changed' signal instead of an accidental send, purchase, or edit.
- effort: Medium: add a pre-step guard to the action executor, bind browser session/tab identity and observation freshness to the operation digest, and add a small pendant status event. Existing /observe and /browser/status supply the facts.  ·  risk: False pauses when harmless background apps or tab IDs change; recover by showing the exact changed field and letting the owner deliberately resume. A stale observation must be treated as unknown and pause, never as permission. No action is undone automatically.
- cost: Near-zero API cost; two local GETs per mutating step and a few hundred bytes of journal metadata. No new hardware required.  ·  latency: Adds roughly 50–200 ms per mutating step locally; approval/resume may take human time, intentionally.
- security: Improves safety by binding execution to the context the owner approved and by treating secure input and stale browser state as a hard stop. The observation payload must be redacted before relay/pendant delivery.
- depends on: An operation ID and staged context digest; POST /execute action receipts joined to a journal; verify_operation_step for re-verification after resume; The accepted tactile_action_outcome_beacon and physical approval latch; A typed policy deciding which context changes are harmless versus approval-requiring

### `interaction` — Add a pendant-originated global revoke epoch, distinct from per-operation cancel. A deliberate two-button gesture (or the forthcoming wheel plus button) increments a monotonic revoke counter locally and signs it; relay, Mac planner, browser bridge, and queued jobs reject every staged or not-yet-committed external action with an older epoch. When the link is down, the pendant stores the revoke event in the existing typed outbox and renders a persistent revoked state; on reconnection the relay fans it out before accepting any queued work. Already-verified irreversible results remain reported honestly as completed, while unknown operations are quarantined for perception rather than silently retried.
- **owner gets:** One physical gesture gives the owner an emergency stop for the entire system—useful if the pendant is lost, a browser session looks wrong, or they suddenly realize several queued actions should not happen. They do not need to find and cancel jobs one at a time, and a disconnected pendant still records the intent safely.
- effort: High: signed epoch storage in pendant firmware, relay fan-out and ordering, Mac/browser admission checks, and a durable quarantine state. It needs careful race tests around commit versus revoke.  ·  risk: A mistaken gesture can pause useful work; recovery is an explicit new epoch and fresh approval, never automatic replay. A revoke racing with a commit may yield unknown, which must be surfaced rather than falsely labeled cancelled. Protect the gesture from accidental presses and rate-limit repeated epochs.
- cost: Low API/storage cost: one small monotonic record per revoke and a few bytes on each queued operation; implementation and race testing dominate.  ·  latency: No cost on local-only actions. External action admission adds a local epoch lookup; propagation after reconnection is sub-second when relay and bridge are online.
- security: Creates a system-wide emergency containment boundary that does not depend on Mac Accessibility, browser page content, or a live microphone. It must use anti-replay counters and never expose secrets to the pendant.
- depends on: Existing typed OUTBOX/INBOX durability on the pendant; physical_transaction_approval_latch as the gesture/nonce boundary; Relay durable jobs and browser command queue; An operation journal carrying the current revoke epoch; A single admission check shared by POST /execute and browser command dispatch

### `hardware` — Add a soldered secure element (for example, an ATECC608-class device) to the production pendant and use it as the non-exportable signing root for approval, global-revoke epochs, and device pairing. Provision an owner-controlled recovery key on the Mac/relay during enrollment; the relay stores only the public key and key version. Firmware must reject software-only fallback keys, expose attestation/key-version metadata, and support rotation by an explicit physical ceremony.
- **owner gets:** The owner gets a pendant whose approvals and emergency revokes remain trustworthy after firmware updates, a stolen Mac session, or a replacement relay. A replacement pendant can be enrolled without silently inheriting an attacker’s identity, while the old pendant can be revoked globally.
- effort: High hardware and firmware change: add I2C wiring and secure-element provisioning, define recovery ceremony and manufacturing flow, update relay pairing and key rotation, and test reset/rollback/lost-device cases.  ·  risk: Provisioning mistakes or lost recovery material can strand the owner; provide a clearly documented offline recovery ceremony and a second recovery copy under the owner's control. Added silicon and I2C failure can make the pendant unavailable, so firmware should fail closed for approvals but retain ordinary local recording. Never print private keys or recovery secrets.
- cost: Roughly $1–$3 BOM increase plus PCB/enclosure revision; a few mA only during signing, negligible idle draw. Engineering/provisioning cost is substantial.  ·  latency: Signing adds tens to hundreds of milliseconds to approval/revoke gestures, acceptable because these are deliberate security actions.
- security: Strongly improves the physical approval boundary and prevents relay/database compromise from minting pendant approvals. Requires secure provisioning, anti-rollback handling, and public-key pinning across relay, Mac, and browser bridge.
- depends on: A canonical signed envelope shared by pendant, relay, Mac, and browser; The global revoke epoch and physical transaction approval latch; Owner-approved recovery policy and key rotation UX; Production PCB access to the pendant I2C bus
