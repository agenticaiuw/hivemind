## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Keep working on this even if my Mac or browser disconnects, tell me exactly what is blocked, and resume automatically when it is safe.”"
- **useful because:** Today the relay can hand off work, but the Mac is currently not ready (Accessibility and Screen Recording are missing) and the browser extension is offline with queued commands. A truthful cross-device pause/resume contract would prevent false success, preserve progress, and notify the owner through the pendant only when intervention is actually needed.
- **path:** relay-realtime → mac-planner → mac-vision → browser-extension → unified → faculty-perception → faculty-judgement → faculty-action
- **model tier:** Use a cheap background state machine for heartbeats, capability checks, checkpointing, and resume; use realtime only to explain a newly blocked or completed job in one short spoken sentence.
- **latency:** Readiness heartbeat within 10 seconds; checkpoint and durable state update under 1 second; spoken alert within 5 seconds of a newly blocked transition; resume within 10 seconds of all required surfaces returning.
- **cost:** Low API cost: heartbeat/checkpoint logic is local/relay code; occasional small text generation for a concise explanation, with realtime reserved for the alert. Storage is a few KB per job plus event history.
- **security:** Do not transmit page contents or file data in heartbeats—only capability state, job identifiers, and redacted error classes. Resume must require the same bound session/tab and revalidate the step's preconditions; never replay a non-idempotent browser or file mutation without an explicit durable intent. Owner's existing policy permits maximum access, but destructive operations still need the owner's chosen confirmation semantics.
- **missing:** A shared readiness lease schema covering Mac permissions, browser extension presence, relay reachability, and pendant delivery; Checkpoint/resume semantics with idempotency keys for every desktop/browser step; A pendant-visible blocked-job notification and a quiet-hours/escalation policy; A reconciliation worker that detects stale leases and safely reattaches jobs when a surface returns

### "“If the Mac gets stuck while I’m away, tell me what you need on the pendant, let me answer there, and continue the same job without making me reopen the Mac.”"
- **useful because:** A disconnected or permission-blocked Mac currently turns a multi-step task into a silent failure or forces the owner to return to the computer. The owner should be able to resolve ordinary blockers—choose a tab, provide a missing value, defer a step, or approve a retry—from the pendant, while the Mac remains the execution surface.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → unified → faculty-perception → faculty-judgement → faculty-action → mac-planner → mac-vision → browser-extension
- **model tier:** Use a background workflow engine for checkpointing, blocker classification, and state transitions. Use the realtime model only for the short spoken blocker question and the owner's reply; use a cheaper text model for composing the next execution plan.
- **latency:** Detect a blocker within 10 seconds, speak a concise question within 5 seconds, persist the owner's answer in under 1 second, and resume within 10 seconds after the relevant surface returns.
- **cost:** Low: workflow state and blocker forms are deterministic; one short realtime turn per blocker and occasional inexpensive replanning dominate cost. Store only compact blocker state and redacted evidence references.
- **security:** The pendant must never expose secrets or page contents unnecessarily. Every question needs a typed answer schema and provenance. A response can alter the plan but cannot silently authorize a new destructive action; preserve the owner's existing confirmation policy and require revalidation if the target tab, file, or app changed.
- **missing:** A durable blocker protocol with typed question/answer schemas and expiry; A pendant notification and reply channel correlated to a specific job checkpoint; A relay-side workflow state machine that can wait without consuming a realtime session; Safe plan regeneration that preserves completed steps and refuses stale targets; A readiness lease and checkpoint protocol for Mac/browser recovery


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Add a cross-surface readiness lease and resumable execution protocol. Each Mac/browser/relay surface publishes a signed heartbeat with capability bits (accessibility, screen recording, extension online, tab/session binding), lease expiry, and a monotonic epoch. The job runner checkpoints before and after every action, records idempotency keys and precondition hashes, transitions jobs to blocked instead of completed when a lease expires, and emits a compact pendant alert. On recovery, it revalidates the epoch, tab/app identity, and precondition hash before resuming; otherwise it asks the judgement faculty for a new plan. Expose a single timeline/receipt that distinguishes completed, blocked, skipped, and safely resumed steps.
- **owner gets:** The owner will stop hearing “done” when nothing happened—as is possible right now with Accessibility and Screen Recording both denied—and can leave the Mac or browser unattended without losing work or causing duplicate submissions. They get one clear spoken explanation and automatic continuation when the machine becomes usable again.
- effort: Medium-high: shared schema and relay D1 tables, Mac agent heartbeat and checkpoint hooks, browser bridge lease handling, pendant alert routing, and fault-injection tests for disconnects at every action boundary.  ·  risk: A stale or incorrect precondition hash could skip needed work or replay a step. Recover with conservative blocking on mismatch, append-only receipts, explicit epoch changes on reconnect, and a repair command that replans from the last verified checkpoint. Never claim GUI success when accessibility is untrusted.
- cost: Small relay storage and heartbeat traffic; roughly a few hundred bytes per lease/event. Background state machine uses negligible model cost; only mismatch explanations or replanning invoke a text model.  ·  latency: Heartbeats add sub-second local overhead; blocked alerts arrive within seconds; resume adds one precondition-validation round before continuing.
- security: Improves security by binding actions to a device/session epoch and preventing stale browser-tab replay. Heartbeats contain no page contents, credentials, or file payloads; receipts should redact paths and URLs according to sensitivity.
- depends on: Durable browser job runner and request-id/tab-affinity work (chg-16bc5dee, chg-14accc01); Existing action receipts/undo records (chg-5fc73ce3); Relay durable jobs and pendant notification delivery; A real read-only Mac readiness inspector implementation; the granted mac_readonly_inspect schema currently returns 'no implementation yet'

### `interaction` — Add a pendant-mediated blocker protocol for long-running Mac/browser jobs. At each verified checkpoint, the executor may emit a typed blocker (permission_missing, surface_offline, ambiguous_target, missing_input, retryable_failure, or owner_decision) with a one-sentence explanation, safe answer choices, expiry, and evidence references. The relay stores this as a suspended workflow rather than an active realtime turn, routes the question to the pendant, validates the reply against the schema, and resumes or replans from the checkpoint when the required surface is available. If the blocker expires, the workflow remains suspended and the owner receives no misleading completion claim.
- **owner gets:** They can keep moving while away from the Mac: answer a small question from the pendant, defer a step, or choose a safe alternative, instead of returning to the computer or discovering later that the task silently failed.
- effort: Medium-high: define typed blocker and answer schemas, persist workflow state in relay storage, add pendant notification/reply correlation, integrate Mac/browser checkpoint hooks, and test stale replies and reconnects.  ·  risk: A stale reply could affect the wrong job or target. Prevent this with job/checkpoint IDs, device/session epochs, expiry, precondition rechecks, append-only state transitions, and replanning on mismatch. Never treat a free-form spoken answer as authorization for an unreviewed destructive action.
- cost: Low storage and transport cost; deterministic state handling dominates. One short realtime turn per blocker, with cheaper background planning for recovery.  ·  latency: Adds only the time needed to deliver and answer a blocker; no realtime session needs to remain open while the job is suspended.
- security: Reduces data exposure by sending typed choices and redacted evidence rather than full page/file contents. Keep secrets local to the executing surface and bind replies to the originating job and checkpoint.
- depends on: Cross-surface readiness leases and resumable checkpoints; Durable pendant-to-relay reply correlation; Typed, provenance-aware browser and Mac execution results; Existing owner policy for destructive confirmations
