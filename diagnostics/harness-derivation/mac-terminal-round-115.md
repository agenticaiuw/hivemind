## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Just do it on whichever device can actually reach it, and if something is offline or unavailable, tell me immediately and continue with the parts that still work.”"
- **useful because:** Today the system can observe readiness but still sends browser work into an offline bridge and waits roughly 45 seconds per failed step; GUI receipts can also claim success when Accessibility is false. A cross-surface execution coordinator would preflight the pendant/relay/Mac/browser capabilities, route each subtask to a reachable surface, fail fast with truthful status, and salvage independent work. The owner gets a useful partial result instead of silence, retries, or misleading success.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → mac-vision → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use the cheap background/planning tier for capability preflight, decomposition, and retry/backoff; reserve realtime only for the spoken status update and interactive decisions. Use deterministic health/receipt logic rather than an LLM for availability checks and truthfulness.
- **latency:** Preflight under 300 ms from cached heartbeats; at most 1–2 s for a fresh health probe. Start reachable subtasks immediately, announce a partial result within 2 s, and never spend 45 s retrying a known-offline browser. Background continuation can run asynchronously with durable job status.
- **cost:** Negligible model cost for deterministic routing; roughly one compact planner call for ambiguous decomposition (about $0.001–$0.01 depending on task), dominated by any browser/page extraction rather than health checks.
- **security:** Health metadata may reveal app names, URLs, and permission state; keep it local or relay only typed capability summaries. Private page contents remain on the browser/Mac path. Never infer completion from an executor receipt alone: require an observed result, and explicitly label skipped, blocked, partial, and completed work. Owner policy keeps maximum execution access and no confirmation gates; this adds observability and recovery, not blocking.
- **missing:** A shared typed capability lease/heartbeat model across pendant, relay, Mac, and browser with freshness TTLs; Planner decomposition into dependency-aware subtasks and a salvageable execution DAG; Fail-fast circuit breakers and adaptive retry/backoff keyed to observed failure reasons; Receipt schema that binds every action to an attributed model tier, preflight snapshot, and evidence of effect; Pendant/relay event for immediate partial-progress and truthful failure announcements

### "“If my Mac, browser, or internet drops halfway through, remember exactly where you were and finish safely when the right device comes back—without repeating anything or making me start over.”"
- **useful because:** Today a multi-surface task can leave partial effects with no crash-consistent resume point: the relay, Mac executor, and authenticated browser each have job records, but they do not share a durable workflow checkpoint or an exactly-once handoff protocol. The owner needs continuity across sleep, network loss, browser restarts, and relay reconnects, with a concise spoken explanation of what already happened and what remains.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use deterministic workflow-state storage, idempotency, and reconciliation for all checkpointing and recovery. Use a cheap background model only to summarize the recovered state; use realtime solely to answer the owner during an active conversation.
- **latency:** Checkpoint each completed step within 250 ms locally and replicate to the relay opportunistically; on reconnection, reconcile within 2 s and resume independent safe steps. Never replay a committed effect merely because the acknowledgment was lost.
- **cost:** Negligible model cost during execution; small D1/local-storage writes per step. A brief recovery summary costs about $0.001–$0.01 when the owner asks what happened.
- **security:** Checkpoints may contain private URLs, form fields, and action results. Encrypt sensitive payloads at rest and keep the relay copy to opaque workflow IDs, hashes, and typed state unless recovery requires a sealed field. Bind resume to the paired pendant/Mac/browser identities, expire abandoned workflows, and distinguish committed, observed, attempted, and unknown outcomes. Keep the owner's maximum-access/no-gate policy; this is correctness and recovery, not approval blocking.
- **missing:** A crash-consistent workflow journal with per-step prepare/commit/observe states and durable causal sequence numbers; A cross-surface handoff protocol between relay, Mac agent, and browser bridge with lease expiry and fencing tokens; An effect-reconciliation adapter for actions whose acknowledgment was lost, so resume can query before retrying; A pendant-visible recovery event and dashboard timeline that clearly separates completed, skipped, and unknown steps


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `mac-harness` — Make every executor job take a read-only preflight snapshot and write it into the job and each action receipt: browser online/last heartbeat, Accessibility and Screen Recording trust, inputReachability, secure-input state, and relevant app foreground state. Add deterministic fail-fast outcomes when a required surface is known unavailable (for example browser offline or uiActionsWillReachTheScreen=false), with a typed reason and next probe time; do not invoke the action or wait for its normal 45–120 s timeout. For multi-action jobs, mark only dependent steps skipped and continue independent steps. Attribute every receipt to the selected model tier and planner request id.
- **owner gets:** The pendant would immediately say “browser is offline; I completed the Mac part” instead of waiting nearly a minute and repeating the same failure. GUI actions would no longer report apparent success when they cannot reach the screen, while the owner still keeps unrestricted execution and no confirmation gates.
- effort: Medium: central executor wrapper plus receipt schema migration, dependency-aware action scheduling, and dashboard rendering; add regression tests for offline browser and untrusted Accessibility.  ·  risk: A stale health snapshot could skip an action that would have recovered moments later; use short TTLs, one fresh probe for borderline states, and preserve an explicit force/override path. Old receipts need nullable preflight fields and remain readable.
- cost: No meaningful API cost; saves model retries and failed browser waits. Small local CPU/JSON storage increase per job (roughly a few KB).  ·  latency: Adds under 300 ms cached preflight, but removes 45 s browser timeout loops and unnecessary retries; reachable actions start in parallel where independent.
- security: Stores permission and app-state metadata in local job records; redact URLs/page contents and retain only typed states. Does not reduce command authority or add gates.
- depends on: A stable typed receipt/evidence schema (extend chg-5fc73ce3 rather than replacing it); A planner action dependency/idempotency representation; Fresh /observe and /browser/status probes exposed inside the executor

### `relay` — Introduce a crash-consistent cross-surface workflow ledger using prepare/commit/observe records, monotonic sequence numbers, per-step effect fingerprints, and fencing leases. The relay persists only encrypted/opaque checkpoint envelopes; the Mac and browser agents retain detailed local evidence. On reconnect, the coordinator first reconciles unknown steps against device receipts and current state, then resumes only uncommitted steps, preventing duplicate browser submissions or repeated Mac mutations. Expose a compact recovery timeline to the pendant and dashboard.
- **owner gets:** A dropped Wi‑Fi connection, sleeping Mac, or restarted browser would no longer force the owner to guess whether something happened or risk doing it twice. They could leave a task running and return to a truthful “completed through step 4; step 5 was never committed; resumed and finished” result.
- effort: High: new relay D1 schema and protocol, local journal adapters, fencing/lease handling, effect-fingerprint reconciliation for each action family, and fault-injection tests across disconnect points.  ·  risk: Some external effects cannot be queried reliably, leaving an unknown state; surface that explicitly and require a recovery policy per action type (query-before-retry, compensate, or stop). Lease loss must never cancel a committed effect; use monotonic fencing and bounded replay. Migration must preserve existing job and receipt reads.
- cost: Low storage and network overhead per step (typically hundreds of bytes plus hashes); no additional model calls during normal operation. One cheap summarization call only when the owner requests a spoken recovery explanation.  ·  latency: A local checkpoint adds tens of milliseconds; reconnect reconciliation should complete in 1–2 seconds for ordinary jobs, substantially faster than manual retries or existing action timeouts.
- security: Opaque relay envelopes minimize private-data exposure; detailed checkpoints stay on the paired Mac/browser. Encrypt sensitive fields, rotate workflow keys, expire abandoned leases, and audit every resume/reconcile decision. No confirmation gates are introduced.
- depends on: A stable action receipt/evidence schema with committed-vs-observed distinction; Cross-surface idempotency keys and effect fingerprints for Mac and browser actions; Relay-to-Mac and relay-to-browser authenticated leases with reconnect callbacks; A durable pendant event for resumed, completed, and indeterminate workflow states
