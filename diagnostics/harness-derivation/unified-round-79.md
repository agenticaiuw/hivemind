## Capabilities it proposed

### "“If I start talking on the pendant and the task needs my Mac or a logged-in browser, carry the conversation over there without making me repeat myself; show me what will happen, let me approve it from the pendant, and bring the result back as a short spoken receipt.”"
- **useful because:** Today the pendant, relay, Mac, and authenticated browser each lose conversational continuity at their boundary. This would make the hive feel like one assistant: the pendant captures intent and approval, the relay preserves state while the Mac/browser work, the Mac opens the right visible context, and the browser supplies private evidence. It is specifically staged and reversible, so the owner never has to approve an opaque remote action or repeat a long explanation.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Realtime only for the short spoken handoff/approval and final receipt; use the cheaper background planner for browser extraction and multi-step Mac work, with deterministic typed action execution wherever possible.
- **latency:** Acknowledge and announce the handoff in under 1 second; show the Mac/browser preview within 3 seconds when online; approval-to-action under 2 seconds; long research or queued work may continue asynchronously and return a receipt later.
- **cost:** One short realtime turn for handoff plus one for receipt (usually <$0.02 total at current voice-tier assumptions); background planning and browser extraction dominate variable cost. No paid model call is needed for typed previews, receipts, or state transitions.
- **security:** Private page content remains on the Mac/browser path and is summarized rather than copied into the pendant unless needed. Destructive actions (send, delete, purchase) remain confirmation-gated; approval tokens are single-use, bound to job/session and expire. The preview must include target account, tab, fields, and evidence timestamps so a stale logged-in tab cannot be mistaken for current state.
- **missing:** A durable cross-surface handoff object: conversation/job ID, intent, evidence, pending approval, and return channel, persisted through relay and Mac restarts.; A pendant-visible preview and approve/cancel protocol (button plus spoken confirmation) that binds approval to a specific action hash.; Mac UI integration that opens/focuses the relevant browser tab or workspace and renders the preview without relying on denied Accessibility/Screen Recording permissions; AppleScript/browser bridge is the fallback.; A result-to-pendant event path with typed receipts and deduplication, including offline/reconnect behavior.

### "“Run a quick audio check on the whole system and tell me whether the pendant, LTE link, relay, and Mac can all support a clear conversation right now; if not, say exactly which leg failed and leave a repair record.”"
- **useful because:** The current path mixes a 15,625 Hz I2S capture, 16 kHz/16 kbps Opus uplink, LTE-M bursts, a practically half-duplex link, relay storage, and Mac pipeline endpoints. A local-looking 'it sounds bad' complaint currently cannot distinguish microphone, clock/sample-rate conversion, radio loss, relay backpressure, or playback. A coordinated probe would make the 24 kHz path shippable and let the owner know whether to charge, move location, reconnect the bridge, or wait for repair instead of repeatedly retrying voice.
- **path:** pendant → bridge → relay-realtime → mac-planner → dashboard
- **model tier:** No realtime model for measurement; use deterministic firmware/relay/Mac probes. Use a cheap background model only to turn measurements and logs into a short diagnosis and repair checklist.
- **latency:** A 5–10 second test with a spoken result under 15 seconds; a failed leg should be identified within one round trip. Persist raw samples/metrics asynchronously, not in the voice prompt.
- **cost:** Negligible model cost; one short LTE test burst and temporary audio objects. Storage cost is minimized by retaining metrics and a short waveform excerpt only when a threshold fails, with automatic deletion after the configured retention.
- **security:** The test must use synthetic tones or locally generated phrases, never upload ambient room audio without an explicit start. Mark diagnostic recordings separately from conversation recordings, encrypt/authenticate the report, and provide a one-command purge. Do not expose network identifiers or raw UART logs in the spoken result.
- **missing:** A versioned audio probe protocol spanning pendant/ESP32 bridge, relay, and Mac with sequence numbers, timestamps, sample-rate/codec declarations, packet-loss/jitter/underrun metrics, and pass thresholds.; A server/Mac endpoint that runs the probe and correlates all four legs into one incident ID and repair receipt.; A firmware test mode that can generate/recognize a synthetic loopback tone while the one-button conversation state is idle.; A 24 kHz acceptance profile and downgrade report (24 kHz, then 16 kHz fallback) rather than a single binary pass/fail.

### "“Give me a private, searchable timeline of what the pendant heard, what the Mac or browser did, and what actually changed—with the source evidence for each step—and let me export or erase any day.”"
- **useful because:** The owner can currently obtain isolated job receipts, logs, browser evidence, and audio objects, but cannot reconstruct one trustworthy story across a spoken request, delegated work, authenticated tabs, and the resulting world change. A unified, owner-controlled timeline would make the system auditable, debuggable, and portable: they could answer “what did you do yesterday?”, verify a claim, or erase sensitive history without trusting an opaque database.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use deterministic indexing, hashes, and typed joins for the timeline; use a cheap background model only for optional natural-language labels and clustering. Realtime is unnecessary except for a short spoken answer when the owner asks from the pendant.
- **latency:** Recent-day queries should render in under 2 seconds and a spoken answer in under 4 seconds. Large exports can run as background jobs and return a completion receipt; erasure should report completion only after every surface confirms deletion.
- **cost:** Low ongoing model cost because indexing and joins are deterministic. Storage and export cost dominate: retain compact metadata and evidence hashes by default, with raw audio/page captures separately opt-in and expiring.
- **security:** This is more sensitive than ordinary logs because it joins private speech, browser sessions, and actions. Encrypt exports, keep them local by default, redact secrets and tokens, require explicit confirmation for exporting raw audio or page text, and make deletion cryptographically auditable without retaining the deleted content. Never expose the owner's captured secret fact in search results unless explicitly requested.
- **missing:** A canonical event schema and correlation ID shared by pendant utterances, relay turns, Mac jobs, browser commands, receipts, and external mutations.; A durable provenance index that can join those events without copying private page contents into every surface.; Owner-facing search/export/erase APIs and a dashboard view with per-event source, timestamp, confidence, retention, and deletion status.; A deletion fan-out protocol covering D1/R2, Mac workspace/logs, browser evidence, and any pendant/SD diagnostic cache, with verification receipts.


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Add an end-to-end audio-contract and diagnostic correlator between pendant firmware, ESP32 bridge, relay, and Mac. On an explicit test request it assigns one incidentId and monotonically numbered synthetic frames; each hop reports declared sample rate/codec, capture/playback timestamps, packet loss, jitter, queue depth, underruns, and LTE reconnects. The relay joins the reports and emits a typed pass/degrade/fail receipt with a 24 kHz result and a 16 kHz fallback result. It stores metrics by default and only a short synthetic waveform excerpt on failure; all records carry an expiry.
- **owner gets:** When audio is unclear, the owner gets a precise answer—microphone, clock conversion, radio, relay, or Mac—instead of guesswork and repeated failed conversations. It directly provides the acceptance evidence needed to finish the 24 kHz path without uploading ambient speech.
- effort: Medium: define a small protocol and thresholds, implement firmware/bridge counters and synthetic loopback, add relay correlation and a Mac diagnostic route, then exercise loss/reconnect cases. No Accessibility or Screen Recording permission is needed.  ·  risk: Incorrect thresholds could label a usable link degraded, or a probe could contend with a live conversation. Require explicit start, reserve a short test window, tag every frame with a probe ID, and abort on button press. If a component drops out, the correlator returns partial evidence rather than claiming pass; expire raw excerpts automatically.
- cost: Near-zero model/API cost. Small LTE test payload and a few D1/R2 writes per diagnostic; bounded retention keeps storage predictable. Firmware RAM impact is limited to counters and a small synthetic-frame ring buffer, not full speech buffering.  ·  latency: Adds an explicit 5–10 second diagnostic only when requested; normal conversations are unchanged. Correlation is asynchronous and the spoken summary can arrive as soon as the first complete leg reports.
- security: Synthetic audio only by default; authenticated incident IDs prevent mixing reports from different tests. Raw excerpts are opt-in/failure-only and short-lived. The report should redact device identifiers from the spoken result while retaining them for local repair logs.
- depends on: A versioned 24 kHz acceptance profile and thresholds (currently requested context is still outstanding).; A firmware/bridge test-mode hook that can generate and recognize synthetic audio without Accessibility.; A durable diagnostic receipt/index so the owner can ask what failed later (incident diagnostics and catch-up status requests remain outstanding).

### `memory` — Create a privacy-scoped, append-only event ledger that assigns one correlation ID at the pendant utterance and links relay turns, audio object hashes, Mac job/action receipts, browser tab evidence, and final external-state observations. Store typed metadata and content hashes centrally, keep raw speech/page text at its originating surface with independent TTLs, and expose a verified export/erase fan-out that reports which surfaces acknowledged deletion.
- **owner gets:** The owner gets one dependable answer to “what happened?” instead of manually reconciling unrelated logs, and can take the record with them or remove a sensitive day without deleting blind spots or leaving hidden copies.
- effort: High: define schema and retention policy, add correlation propagation to firmware/relay/Mac/browser, implement a local-first index and export format, then build deletion acknowledgements and failure recovery across online/offline surfaces.  ·  risk: A join bug could associate one person's private browser evidence with another task, or erasure could falsely claim success. Use owner-scoped authenticated IDs, immutable event hashes, explicit partial/deletion-pending states, and never treat missing acknowledgements as deleted. Keep raw content out of the central ledger by default.
- cost: Low model cost; metadata/index writes are modest. Export and temporary encrypted bundles consume storage, while raw audio/page evidence remains bounded by existing retention controls.  ·  latency: No meaningful impact on live voice if events are buffered asynchronously; recent timeline queries should be a fast indexed read. Export/erase of large histories is background work.
- security: Substantially improves auditability but creates a high-value metadata target. Encrypt at rest and in transit, minimize content, isolate per-surface keys, require confirmation for raw-content export, and make the owner's erase action stronger than ordinary undo.
- depends on: A canonical cross-surface event schema and propagation hooks, which do not exist as one contract today.; A durable deletion fan-out/acknowledgement mechanism across relay D1/R2, Mac workspace, browser evidence, and pendant SD.; An owner-facing timeline/search/export/erase surface.
