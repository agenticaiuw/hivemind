## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Finish this across my browser and Mac, even if I leave: use the logged-in page, save the result in my workspace, and tell me only when each step is proven complete.”"
- **useful because:** Today a browser action, Mac action, and relay job can each exist, but there is no single transaction tying them together. The owner can be told that a click or type returned success even when the Mac is unreachable, and a restart can cause a duplicate. This would make delegated work trustworthy: one stable task ID, explicit handoffs between private Safari and Mac storage, postcondition evidence for every step, resume after disconnects, and a spoken completion receipt that distinguishes completed, waiting for approval, and unverified.
- **path:** pendant → relay → browser → mac-planner → mac-vision → mac-terminal → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use the realtime tier only to capture the request and speak the final short receipt; use a cheaper background planner for decomposition, retries, evidence comparison, and reconciliation.
- **latency:** Acknowledge in under 2 seconds; private-page reads and Mac work may run asynchronously. Speak only after postconditions are observed or clearly report blocked/unverified, with retry backoff rather than busy polling.
- **cost:** Roughly $0.01–$0.05 per multi-step task depending on evidence extraction and retries; dominant costs are model calls for reconciliation and any public Browser Run minutes, not the relay bookkeeping.
- **security:** Private URLs, extracted text, and workspace paths leave the Mac only as encrypted task evidence; redact secrets and cap evidence retention. Never cross an irreversible checkpoint (send, purchase, delete, submit) without the owner's existing confirmation policy. A proof packet must include source URL/path, timestamp, and before/after hash so a false success is visible.
- **missing:** A durable cross-surface transaction coordinator joining browser session steps and Mac job steps under one task ID; The still-missing durable browser job runner and voice-path wiring noted in chg-16bc5dee; Read-and-enforce preconditions and postconditions (current receipt code records observations but does not gate execution, as noted in chg-5fc73ce3); Lease/heartbeat and exactly-once action claims across relay, Safari bridge, and Mac reconnects; A typed verification/proof packet and dashboard/pendant status stream

### "“Before you send, buy, delete, or submit anything, give me a compact receipt on the pendant and let me approve it there only if my Mac shows the same receipt; then carry out exactly that approved change and tell me what the other device proves afterward.”"
- **useful because:** The owner currently has confirmation as a policy, but not a tamper-evident, two-device approval ceremony. A spoken confirmation can be based on stale browser content, and a Mac prompt alone is not useful when the owner is away from the keyboard. This gives the owner a clear, remote approval boundary: the pendant presents the intended irreversible effect, the Mac displays the matching transaction digest, approval is bound to that exact digest and expires, and execution cannot silently substitute a changed recipient, amount, file, or form value. The final response includes independent post-action evidence rather than a generic success message.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-planner → mac-terminal → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use the realtime model only to explain the pending receipt and collect the short approval phrase; use a cheaper deterministic service for digest creation, expiry, signature checks, and post-action comparison.
- **latency:** Receipt should reach the pendant in under 2 seconds after a plan pauses. Approval should be accepted for a short window (for example 2 minutes); execution and proof may complete asynchronously, with one concise spoken result.
- **cost:** Usually under $0.005 per approval ceremony; cryptographic signing and digest comparison are local/relay bookkeeping. Model cost is limited to summarizing unusually complex effects.
- **security:** The digest must cover the normalized action, recipient/URL/path, amount or payload, and precondition evidence; approvals must be one-time, short-lived, replay-resistant, and scoped to the owner’s paired pendant. Do not speak secrets aloud. If Mac and pendant summaries differ, expire the approval and stop. Require a second explicit confirmation for especially high-risk categories if the owner configures one.
- **missing:** A signed action-digest format shared by relay, Mac executor, and browser bridge; A pendant-side approval and cancellation protocol that works without keyboard focus; A Mac display/receipt channel that can be compared to the pendant’s digest; Atomic binding of approval to exactly one executor step, with expiry and replay protection; Independent postcondition proof after execution, including browser DOM/source evidence or Mac file/application evidence


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Implement a cross-surface action transaction coordinator. On planning, assign a taskId and deterministic step IDs; each browser or Mac executor claims a short lease, records precondition observations, performs at most one side effect, then must attach a postcondition proof (URL/title/selected-text hash for browser; path/app/value/hash for Mac). The relay persists the state machine and evidence, reclaims expired leases without replaying completed steps, pauses at irreversible checkpoints for owner confirmation, and emits one typed receipt to dashboard and pendant. If a surface reports success but its reachability probe or postcondition fails, classify it as unverified and never advance the dependent step.
- **owner gets:** The owner stops having to repeat work or trust a misleading “done” after the Mac/browser silently failed. A task can move from a private logged-in tab to a saved Mac artifact and survive leaving the house, sleep, or a relay restart, while dangerous final actions still wait for confirmation.
- effort: Medium-high: durable D1/R2 task state and lease expiry, adapters around browser and Mac job APIs, proof schema/redaction, reconnect/resume tests, and dashboard/pendant status rendering.  ·  risk: A stale proof or overly broad idempotency key could skip needed work; mitigate with task-scoped step IDs, short leases, precondition hashes, and explicit unverified/blocked states. Recovery is retry only the unclaimed step or use existing per-job undo receipts; never auto-retry irreversible steps.
- cost: Negligible storage/relay overhead; approximately $0.001–$0.01 in background verification per task, with model cost only for ambiguous evidence reconciliation.  ·  latency: Adds one local reachability check and proof read after each side effect (typically sub-second); long browser/Mac legs remain asynchronous.
- security: Evidence is sensitive and must be encrypted, redacted, TTL-deleted, and scoped to the task owner. Keep private Safari evidence on the Mac where possible; relay stores hashes and minimal excerpts. Confirmation remains mandatory for send/buy/delete/submit.
- depends on: Durable browser job runner and voice-path wiring from chg-16bc5dee; Actual precondition gating rather than receipt-only behavior from chg-5fc73ce3; A verification/proof API and lease indicator (already requested upstream, not yet available); Truthful Mac reachability classification while Accessibility/Screen Recording remain owner-blocked

### `integration` — Add a causal action ledger that links each irreversible action to the exact observation, transformation, approval digest, executor, and postcondition proof that caused it. Store immutable content hashes and typed references rather than copied sensitive content; expose a human-readable timeline and machine-checkable export. Reject execution when an action has no current observation or when its cited evidence changed after planning, instead of allowing a receipt to exist without provenance.
- **owner gets:** The owner can answer “what did you use, what did I approve, and what changed afterward?” for a sent message, purchase, deletion, or submission, even when several agents and devices participated. It turns a vague action history into an auditable explanation and prevents stale browser data from quietly driving a later Mac action.
- effort: Medium: define the ledger schema and hash chain, add references at planner/browser/Mac boundaries, implement redaction and retention, then add timeline and export views with failure-injection tests.  ·  risk: Hashing the wrong normalization could produce false mismatches; version the canonicalization rules and retain typed field diffs. Sensitive evidence could leak through exports; default to local-only details, redact secrets, and require confirmation before sharing an export. Ledger writes must be append-only but tolerate relay retries through idempotent event IDs.
- cost: Low storage and CPU cost; no routine model call. A small background model may summarize a ledger only when the owner asks for an explanation.  ·  latency: A few milliseconds for hashes and event writes; no meaningful impact on ordinary reversible actions.
- security: Improves accountability but creates a sensitive audit trail. Encrypt at rest, scope by owner/task, apply short retention to page text and message payloads, and retain only hashes after expiry.
- depends on: A stable cross-surface task and step identity protocol; Postcondition verification hooks in browser and Mac executors; The owner’s existing confirmation policy for irreversible effects; A dashboard/pendant view capable of showing concise provenance without speaking private payloads
