## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Do the whole browser-and-Mac task, but keep me in control: show me exactly what will change, let me approve it on the pendant, execute it, and tell me whether the final state is verified.”"
- **useful because:** This is the single most useful missing behavior: one spoken request can cross the browser’s logged-in session and Mac apps without pretending that an executor receipt proves success. The relay coordinates planning, the Mac/browser hands perform the steps, the pendant is the out-of-band approval boundary, and faculty-perception independently verifies the postconditions. It turns 'I asked you to do it' into a truthful, recoverable transaction rather than a pile of disconnected actions.
- **path:** relay-realtime → faculty-judgement → faculty-action → mac-planner → browser-extension → faculty-perception → pendant
- **model tier:** Realtime only for the short conversational clarification and final spoken status; use the local planner/vision loop for execution and a cheaper background model for plan normalization and receipt summarization.
- **latency:** Preview in 2–4 seconds; physical approval immediately; execution normally under 15 seconds, with an explicit pending state for longer work. Never claim completion until verification returns.
- **cost:** Usually one realtime turn plus local Mac/browser execution; roughly $0.02–$0.10 depending on clarification and vision steps. Verification is primarily local and should not add a model call when structured state is available.
- **security:** The preview must contain canonical target, fields, risk, reversibility, deadline, and affected surfaces but never page secrets or form contents on the pendant. Approval binds to a digest and expiry. Executor receipts are not completion; verification evidence is sensitivity-labeled and defaults to hashes/minimal snippets. High-risk irreversible steps remain staged unless the owner changes policy.
- **missing:** A single cross-surface transaction envelope shared by relay, Mac, browser, pendant, and verifier (transaction ID, plan digest, step IDs, deadlines, reversibility).; An action-runner that can hand one transaction between mac_run_actions and browser_run_actions while preserving step IDs.; An amendment to verify_operation_step carrying actionId/attemptId and the canonical transaction digest.; A pendant rendering for a compact multi-step preview, not only the existing outcome beacon.

### "“Privacy shield on.” / “Privacy shield off.”"
- **useful because:** A wearable assistant is always near conversations, screens, and logged-in sessions. The owner needs one unmistakable physical way to stop the mind, not merely mute audio: stop new microphone/audio ingestion, cancel or freeze pending browser/Mac work, prevent new screenshots and page-body transfer, and show when the shield is actually active. The pendant can trigger this offline over the existing button path; the relay, Mac agent, and browser extension enforce it at their own boundaries.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → faculty-perception → faculty-action
- **model tier:** No model for enforcement. Use deterministic relay/device state transitions; use the cheap background tier only to summarize what was suppressed after the owner explicitly asks.
- **latency:** Local pendant acknowledgement under 100 ms; relay and Mac/browser enforcement under 1 second when connected. If disconnected, the pendant must remain shielded locally and queue only the state transition, never captured content.
- **cost:** Near-zero API cost during enforcement; a small background summary costs cents only when requested.
- **security:** Fail closed: an unacknowledged OFF command never disables the shield. Shield state must be monotonic per device counter, authenticated, and visible as a distinctive haptic/audio pattern. It must revoke pending transaction leases, stop browser polling/results, block screen capture and microphone forwarding, and scrub transient uncommitted context. It cannot claim that already-sent data was recalled; the owner gets a truthful boundary timestamp.
- **missing:** A firmware-local privacy-shield state and button/wheel gesture that works without LTE.; A relay-wide deny gate checked by every audio, capture, browser, and action route.; Mac/browser hooks that refuse new capture and discard already queued commands when the signed shield epoch advances.; A durable, owner-visible audit record of shield-on timestamp, enforcement acknowledgements, and the exact boundary of data that had already left the device.

### "“Fill this form with my saved details, but do not send the values to the assistant; show me the field names and final total, then ask for pendant approval before submitting.”"
- **useful because:** This makes the browser extension a real private hand rather than a remote screen. Logged-in forms, addresses, payment details, and health fields stay inside the browser/OS vault; the relay and Mac planner see only field labels, validation state, and a redacted digest. The owner can complete tedious forms by voice while retaining both data minimization and a deliberate physical submit boundary.
- **path:** browser-extension → mac-planner → faculty-judgement → faculty-action → faculty-perception → pendant → relay-realtime
- **model tier:** Use a small local deterministic field classifier for labels/types and validation. Use realtime only for the owner’s conversational disambiguation; never send field values to a model. Use the background tier to summarize the redacted result if requested.
- **latency:** Inspect and produce a redacted preview in 2 seconds; local fill in under 3 seconds; submission waits for pendant approval. If a field cannot be classified confidently, leave it untouched and ask.
- **cost:** Usually no model cost beyond one short realtime clarification; browser-side DOM and vault operations dominate latency.
- **security:** The extension must enforce an allowlist of field types and origins, never expose values through snapshots, logs, receipts, URLs, or error text, and bind approval to origin + form structure digest + redacted field summary + expiry. Payment, identity, health, and irreversible submissions require explicit approval; a changed DOM invalidates the digest. Verification returns only submitted/failed/unknown and safe provenance.
- **missing:** A browser-local vault/autofill adapter that can fill approved fields without returning values to the relay or Mac planner.; A browser snapshot/redaction contract that guarantees secrets cannot enter browser_inspect, browser_result, logs, or model context.; A form transaction digest and post-submit verifier that checks the target submission without recording sensitive values.; Pendant preview vocabulary for redacted fields and a distinct 'ready to submit' confirmation pattern.

### "“Pause this until tomorrow, then bring me back exactly where we stopped, explain what changed, and let me continue only after I approve the new plan.”"
- **useful because:** Today a multi-step task that hits a login, a changed webpage, a sleeping Mac, or an owner interruption becomes an opaque half-finished job. The owner should be able to suspend it for hours or days and resume safely: preserve the human-readable intent and completed-step evidence, re-observe the live Mac/browser state on resume, show only the differences, invalidate stale approvals, and continue from the first unverified step rather than repeating or guessing.
- **path:** relay-realtime → relay-job-queue → mac-planner → browser-extension → faculty-perception → faculty-judgement → faculty-action → pendant
- **model tier:** Use a cheap background model to normalize the paused intent and summarize state differences; use the realtime tier only when the owner asks a conversational clarification. Deterministic state comparison and approval expiry must not depend on a model.
- **latency:** A pause acknowledgement should be immediate. Resume should produce a changed-state summary within 5 seconds and wait indefinitely for approval; execution proceeds in normal step latency.
- **cost:** Low: one background summary on resume and local state probes. The dominant cost is only incurred when the live state materially changed and clarification is needed.
- **security:** Never reuse an old approval after expiry, browser-origin change, form digest change, or Mac account/session change. Preserve only redacted intent and evidence, not secrets or page bodies. A paused action must have an owner-visible deadline and cancellation path; if state cannot be reconciled, report unknown instead of retrying blindly.
- **missing:** A durable paused-work record with completed step IDs, evidence hashes, intent digest, expiry, and the exact resume predicate.; A re-observation/diff protocol spanning Mac app state and browser session state, with a formal 'changed/unchanged/unknown' result.; A resume planner that starts at the first unverified step and requires a fresh physical approval whenever material state changed.; Pendant controls for compact pause/resume/cancel status and a way to select among multiple paused tasks once the rotary input exists.

### "“Before I commit to this, check my calendar, open commitments, and the relevant browser page; tell me exactly what conflicts, and draft the least-disruptive option without sending or accepting anything.”"
- **useful because:** The assistant today can act on one surface at a time, but cannot reliably protect the owner from commitments that collide across Calendar, Mail, reminders, and logged-in web services. This capability makes the mind a pre-commitment guardian: it gathers current facts from each surface, identifies time/resource conflicts, proposes alternatives, and leaves every external commitment untouched until the owner explicitly chooses one.
- **path:** faculty-judgement → faculty-perception → mac-planner → browser-extension → relay-realtime → pendant
- **model tier:** Use deterministic calendar/time overlap and deadline rules first; use a cheaper background model to summarize conflicts and rank alternatives. Realtime is reserved for the owner’s final conversational choice.
- **latency:** Initial conflict report in under 5 seconds for local apps and one browser session; longer research is staged with a truthful pending status. No external write occurs during analysis.
- **cost:** Usually one short background summarization call; local AppleScript/browser reads dominate. No cost for deterministic overlap checks.
- **security:** Read only by default. Browser pages and mail may contain private data, so pass field-level redacted facts rather than full bodies to models. Every proposed change must include source, timestamp, timezone, confidence, and whether it is reversible. Never infer an acceptance from a draft or preview.
- **missing:** A unified commitment schema joining Calendar events, reminders, mail deadlines, and browser transaction dates while retaining provenance.; A conflict engine that distinguishes hard overlaps from soft constraints such as travel, preparation, focus blocks, and response deadlines.; A redacted cross-surface evidence view the owner can inspect on the pendant or Mac before choosing an option.; A separate write phase with fresh verification so drafts, replies, and calendar changes cannot occur during the read-only check.

### "“Forget everything from that conversation and prove where it was deleted; keep only an anonymous receipt that the deletion happened.”"
- **useful because:** A wearable mind accumulates audio, transcripts, browser results, Mac logs, job receipts, and offline SD artifacts in different places. Today the owner cannot issue one deletion request and know whether every copy—including queued or disconnected copies—was removed. This capability gives them a bounded, verifiable right to erase a selected conversation or artifact across pendant, relay, Mac, and browser while retaining only a non-content deletion receipt.
- **path:** relay-realtime → faculty-judgement → faculty-action → mac-planner → browser-extension → pendant → faculty-perception
- **model tier:** No model is needed for deletion or proof. Use deterministic object manifests, cryptographic hashes, and deletion acknowledgements; use the cheap background tier only to explain the receipt in plain language after deletion.
- **latency:** Acknowledge scope immediately; delete online copies within 10 seconds and queue signed deletion tombstones for disconnected devices. Do not claim completion until every reachable store acknowledges and unreachable stores have an explicit pending state.
- **cost:** Negligible model/API cost. Storage scans and authenticated deletion acknowledgements dominate.
- **security:** Deletion must be scoped (conversation, artifact, date range, or all), authenticated by the owner, idempotent, and resistant to replay. The receipt must not reveal deleted content or reconstruct it from hashes. Browser caches, Mac logs, relay D1 records, pipeline audio, and pendant failure-path SD objects need separate acknowledgements. If a third-party site retains a submitted value, report that boundary honestly instead of promising erasure.
- **missing:** A cross-surface data lineage manifest mapping each audio/transcript/action/browser artifact to its storage copies and retention class.; Signed, idempotent deletion tombstones that survive relay and pendant link loss and are applied before queued replay.; Deletion hooks for Mac logs/jobs/context graph, relay D1/pipeline objects, browser command/result caches, and pendant SD outbox/inbox.; A verifier that proves absence or states the exact remaining/unreachable boundary without exposing content.
