## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Can I take this on?” When I mention a new request or opportunity, compare it with my real commitments and deadlines, tell me the tradeoff in one short spoken answer, and—if it does not fit—draft a polite renegotiation or decline for my approval."
- **useful because:** People accept work conversationally before seeing the collision. This would turn the pendant into a timely decision aid: it checks calendar, existing promises, open tasks, and the relevant logged-in thread, then makes the cost visible without silently committing or sending anything.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → relay → mac-planner → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use the realtime tier only to capture the request and ask one clarification; use a cheaper background model to reconcile commitments and draft alternatives. Mac/browser agents retrieve evidence; the relay persists a decision packet and schedules a follow-up if the owner says “later.”
- **latency:** A spoken feasibility answer within 5 seconds when local context is warm; up to 30 seconds for authenticated browser evidence. If evidence is slow, say what is known, continue in background, and return a cited packet rather than blocking the conversation.
- **cost:** About $0.01–$0.05 per evaluation depending on browser pages and draft length; browser extraction and re-sent context dominate, not the brief spoken response.
- **security:** Read-only access to calendar, mail, task systems, and logged-in pages is needed; private snippets should stay in the relay's short-lived encrypted packet and be minimized in audio. Never send, accept, decline, or create a commitment without explicit confirmation. Treat deadlines and availability as uncertain when stale, and show source/time for consequential claims.
- **missing:** A first-class commitment/obligation index joining Calendar, Mail, Notes, reminders, and authenticated browser evidence; A decision packet with evidence, alternatives, expiry, and explicit approve/edit/reject state; A cross-surface impact scan that detects schedule/deadline collisions and distinguishes hard commitments from tentative ideas; Owner-configurable workload assumptions (working hours, buffers, protected time, and what counts as an automatic no)

### "“Who was that person I just spoke with, and what should I remember for next time?” After I explicitly mark a conversation as worth remembering, identify the meeting or contact from my calendar/Mac, assemble a private relationship card from what was actually said plus clearly separated public context, and make it available from the pendant before my next interaction."
- **useful because:** People lose valuable social context because names, roles, promises, and personal details arrive while their hands are occupied. This gives the owner durable, privacy-respecting continuity without requiring note-taking during a conversation. It must distinguish observed facts from inference and let the owner correct or delete the card.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → mac-vision → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Realtime handles only the explicit “remember this” marker and a short confirmation. A cheaper background model extracts facts, links the conversation to a calendar/contact candidate, and summarizes public context. The relay stores the result and serves a short pre-meeting audio reminder later.
- **latency:** A local acknowledgement under 1 second; a preliminary identity candidate within 10 seconds; enrichment and relationship-card construction can take minutes asynchronously. Before the next meeting, deliver a 15–30 second spoken card.
- **cost:** Roughly $0.02–$0.10 per saved conversation, dominated by transcription, entity resolution, and optional web enrichment. Most retrievals should be cached and text-only.
- **security:** Conversation audio and relationship data are highly sensitive. Recording must require an explicit local gesture or spoken confirmation, with a visible/haptic recording indicator and an immediate cancel/delete control. Never record continuously by default, never infer sensitive traits, and never send a message or expose the card to another person without confirmation. Public enrichment must be source-labeled and limited to the identified person.
- **missing:** An explicit, local conversation-bookmark event with start/stop boundaries and a deletion window; On-device or relay-side short audio capture/transcription that can retain only the bookmarked segment; Calendar/contact identity resolution with ranked candidates and an owner correction flow; A relationship-card data model separating direct observations, owner-entered facts, and public sources with independent retention controls; A pre-meeting trigger that can deliver a selected card to the pendant without replaying private conversation audio


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Add a commitment-collision compiler between capture/pipeline and the existing Mac/browser execution surfaces. On a spoken “can I take this on?” event, normalize the request into a candidate commitment, retrieve dated evidence from Calendar/Mail/Notes/reminders and authenticated browser tabs, classify each item as hard/soft/unknown, compute time and deadline collisions with explicit freshness, and persist a signed decision packet containing sources, assumptions, three options (accept/defer/decline), and an expiry. The relay speaks only the conclusion; the Mac creates a draft or reminder only after the owner chooses. Emit status and receipt events so a delayed browser result cannot overwrite a newer decision.
- **owner gets:** The owner gets an answer at the moment of commitment instead of discovering the conflict later, with a defensible explanation and a ready draft—but no accidental acceptance or message send.
- effort: Medium-high: typed schema plus evidence adapters, temporal/conflict scoring, packet persistence, stale-result handling, and a small review UI/audio renderer. Start with Calendar + reminders + one browser session, then add Mail and task boards.  ·  risk: False collision or missed promise could cause the owner to decline something valuable; stale private-page data could mislead. Mitigate by labeling uncertainty, quoting evidence timestamps, defaulting to “needs review,” and keeping all outward actions drafts until explicit approval. Recover by packet versioning and undo for reminders/drafts.
- cost: Low recurring model cost if extraction is cached; roughly $0.01–$0.05 per decision, with browser reads and long context dominating. Storage is a small JSON packet plus redacted evidence hashes.  ·  latency: Warm local answer 2–5 seconds; authenticated browser evidence may take 10–30 seconds and should complete asynchronously with a spoken provisional answer.
- security: Expands correlation of sensitive commitments across surfaces. Encrypt packets, minimize quoted content, enforce per-source scopes, expire raw snippets quickly, and require explicit confirmation for any draft creation or external change.
- depends on: A typed commitment/obligation schema and provenance-aware context projection; A durable decision-packet inbox/review endpoint; Authenticated browser bridge availability for private pages (currently owner must reconnect it); A clear owner policy for working hours, buffers, and protected commitments

### `hardware` — Add a coin vibration motor (or low-power LRA with driver), a second tactile button, and a physical microphone-mute/privacy slider to the pendant revision. Expose a tiny local event protocol: short buzz = pending decision, double buzz = evidence ready, long press = mute/cancel and queue acknowledgement offline. Keep audio unchanged; the nRF9160's GPIO/SPI budget and 211,608 B app RAM are sufficient, while the motor driver isolates current spikes from the modem/audio rail.
- **owner gets:** The owner can notice and control an important decision in a meeting, street, or quiet room without broadcasting speech or looking at a screen; the mute slider gives an immediate, trustworthy privacy boundary even when software is confused.
- effort: Low-to-medium hardware spin: motor, driver transistor/IC, button, slider, enclosure and firmware event mapping; requires board revision and power characterization on the prototype DK before productizing.  ·  risk: Vibration can annoy or drain the battery, and accidental presses could cancel a task. Use intensity/duty limits, quiet hours, distinct patterns, and require a deliberate long press for cancellation; if the motor fails, LED/audio remain available. Validate modem transmit current and audio noise during vibration.
- cost: Approximately $2–$6 BOM increase in volume (more on prototype), with brief vibration bursts adding tens of mA for under a second; negligible idle draw if switched off.  ·  latency: Immediate local feedback (<100 ms) for mute, cancel, and acknowledgement; no network round trip required.
- security: The physical mute slider should gate microphone power locally, not merely set a software flag. Record only coarse mute/unmute events, never raw audio; keep cancellation local and idempotent when replayed.
- depends on: Define the cross-surface attention/decision event schema; Pendant firmware event queue and offline replay; Power/audio coexistence test on the nRF9160 development hardware

### `memory` — Create a consent-scoped relationship memory layer distinct from generic capture/context-graph storage. A local bookmark event opens a bounded audio segment; the relay derives a structured card with three physically separate provenance classes—heard directly, owner-confirmed, and externally sourced—plus retention/deletion deadlines and an identity-confidence score. Before any card is spoken, a resolver must match it to a calendar/contact candidate and require correction when confidence is low. Pre-meeting delivery retrieves only the fields selected by the owner, never raw audio, and every card supports one-button purge from the pendant.
- **owner gets:** The owner can remember people and context naturally after a meaningful interaction, then receive a useful reminder before seeing them again, without trusting an opaque permanent recording or being surprised by an invented fact.
- effort: High: pendant bookmark firmware and local indicators, bounded audio handling, entity resolution, provenance-aware schema, encryption/retention jobs, correction UI, and pre-meeting delivery integration.  ·  risk: Misidentification or a hallucinated personal detail could damage a relationship; accidental recording could violate privacy. Mitigate with explicit start/stop consent, short automatic deletion of unconfirmed clips, confidence thresholds, source citations, no sensitive-attribute inference, and default silence when identity is unresolved.
- cost: Moderate per saved interaction: transcription and optional enrichment are the main costs, approximately $0.02–$0.10; storage stays small after deleting raw audio. Hardware cost is unchanged if existing button/LED can signal bookmarking, otherwise a dedicated haptic/privacy control is needed.  ·  latency: Bookmark acknowledgement is local and immediate; card generation is asynchronous. Pre-meeting retrieval should be under 3 seconds, with a compact spoken summary rendered once and cached.
- security: This creates a sensitive social-memory dataset. Encrypt it separately from ordinary notes, enforce per-card retention, audit every read, keep raw audio ephemeral, and make physical mute/delete override all server state as soon as connectivity returns.
- depends on: A firmware-level explicit bookmark/delete event and recording indicator; A provenance and privacy-aware relationship-card schema; Calendar/contact identity resolver with owner correction; Durable encrypted retention and purge processing; A pre-meeting scheduler that can target the pendant
