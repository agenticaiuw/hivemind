## Capabilities it proposed

### ""Make sure I actually get the spoken result, even when it is a long briefing." The system should hand off large audio without putting PCM into the relay's job/result record, verify each transfer, and tell me plainly if only the Mac rendered it."
- **useful because:** A live pipeline just failed after rendering 480,000 bytes of 24 kHz PCM: the bridge tried to persist a 62,301,439-byte serialized result and Cloudflare rejected it at 32 MiB. Today this can lose a perfectly good spoken briefing while the trace misleadingly contains a completed TTS stage. This capability makes long briefings dependable across Mac → relay → bridge → (when present) pendant, while preserving the honest distinction between rendered, transferred, and heard.
- **path:** mac-planner → relay-realtime → relay → mac-terminal → pendant
- **model tier:** Use deterministic streaming/chunk verification and existing job machinery; reserve realtime model only for the brief's language, not transport. No LLM is needed to move bytes or diagnose limits.
- **latency:** Start playback as soon as the first verified chunks are available; add under 1 second manifest setup and bounded chunk retries. A failed transfer should be reported immediately, not after the entire response is serialized.
- **cost:** Negligible model cost. Storage/network cost is proportional to audio (roughly 0.96 MB/minute for 24 kHz mono PCM before compression); chunk hashes and a small manifest dominate little.
- **security:** Use per-job opaque IDs, authenticated chunk upload/download, short-lived manifests, SHA-256 and byte-range checks, and never log PCM or spoken secrets. Require confirmation only for external actions, not playback. Expire abandoned manifests and keep the owner's existing audio-retention policy.
- **missing:** A streaming/chunked audio handoff contract that keeps PCM out of D1 job rows and bridge result RPC arguments; A relay/bridge receipt that records manifest, byte count, hash, and transfer completion separately from the text result; A Mac fallback that can play locally or retain a compact answer when the pendant is absent, rather than marking the run failed

### ""If a scheduled briefing fails, recover it and tell me what happened without making me ask again." The system should detect a failed routine across the relay and Mac, preserve a compact text receipt, retry only the failed delivery leg, and give me a short spoken recovery notice when a device is available."
- **useful because:** The live morning-news routine rendered audio but ended with a failed pipeline because a 62 MB bridge result exceeded Cloudflare's 32 MiB RPC limit. The owner currently gets neither the briefing nor a trustworthy explanation; routine status can say completed at other stages while the final run is failed. This turns silent loss into one bounded recovery and an honest notice.
- **path:** relay → mac-planner → browser-extension → relay-realtime → pendant
- **model tier:** Use deterministic failure classification and retry policy. Use a cheaper background model only if the compact text receipt must be re-summarized; do not spend realtime tokens on retries or transport diagnosis.
- **latency:** Classify within seconds of a failed pipeline event. Retry the delivery leg once after a short backoff; if it fails again, leave the compact receipt and speak one sentence on the next available connection.
- **cost:** Usually zero extra model calls; one retry may duplicate transfer bandwidth. If summarization is needed, under one cheap text call per failed run.
- **security:** Do not retry destructive actions—only generated briefing delivery. Bind retries to an idempotency key and preserve source URLs/capsule IDs where available. Expire retained text/audio according to policy; do not put raw PCM in job records.
- **missing:** A failure classifier that distinguishes content-generation failure from audio-transfer/storage failure; An idempotent delivery-only retry endpoint and a durable compact text receipt separate from audio bytes; A next-connection notification policy that does not claim the owner heard the original failed delivery

### ""Watch this page and tell me only what materially changed since yesterday, with the exact before-and-after evidence." The system should maintain a user-approved watch over a browser page, detect semantic changes rather than cosmetic ones, and deliver a concise spoken alert with citations and a way to inspect the old and new regions."
- **useful because:** Today the browser can read the current tab, and the Mac can store individual evidence capsules, but the owner cannot ask the system to establish a durable, cross-session change claim. A page can change between visits and the system has no owner-facing before/after witness, so an alert would be an unsupported summary rather than perception grounded in two observations.
- **path:** browser-extension → mac-planner → relay → relay-realtime → mac-vision → pendant
- **model tier:** Use deterministic content-addressed snapshots, DOM/region normalization, and hashes for change detection. Use a cheap background model only to classify whether a diff is materially relevant; use realtime only to speak an already-verified alert.
- **latency:** Capture and compare on the watch schedule, typically under 10 seconds after the page is available. Alert generation should be under 2 seconds after a confirmed material diff; no continuous realtime inference.
- **cost:** Browser capture and hashing are negligible. Background classification is one small text-model call per changed region, not per poll. Storage is bounded by retaining redacted region summaries and hashes rather than whole pages.
- **security:** The owner must explicitly approve each watched URL/region. Redact secrets and authenticated-page fields before persistence; never send page bodies to the relay unless the watch policy permits it. Use revocable watch IDs, encrypted local storage, retention limits, and require confirmation before following links or acting on a change.
- **missing:** A durable watch object linking URL, region selector, cadence, owner policy, and last accepted capsule; A semantic diff engine that compares redacted content-addressed capsules while distinguishing cosmetic layout changes from material text/data changes; A scheduler-to-browser capture path that can reacquire the watched tab or request a fresh authenticated browser read; An alert receipt containing old/new capsule IDs, changed claims, confidence, and source timestamps

### ""Keep working on that question after I walk away, and bring me back the answer with the sources you actually checked." The pendant should create a bounded research handoff, the relay should preserve the task and its evidence requirements, the Mac/browser should continue it asynchronously, and the next available device should receive a compact result with unresolved parts clearly separated."
- **useful because:** Today a spoken request, a relay job, a Mac planner run, and a browser read are separate records. Scope credentials to the browser session, redact secrets from task summaries, bind every claim to a capsule or source URL, and require confirmation before any external action discovered during research.

### ""Answer only with what you can verify right now; label anything inferred or stale."
