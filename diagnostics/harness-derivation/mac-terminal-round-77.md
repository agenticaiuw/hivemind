## Capabilities it proposed

### "“The thing I asked you to do on my Mac failed—figure out why, try the safest repair, and tell me exactly what you changed.”"
- **useful because:** Today a failed shell or browser job leaves the owner to diagnose it manually, and the model often repeats the same discovery. This gives the pendant a useful failure-recovery loop: identify the exact failed step, use evidence from the Mac receipt, retry with a cheaper known alternative, and report whether anything changed. It is explicitly more than Mac-only because the pendant is the low-friction trigger, relay preserves the job after the owner walks away, browser sessions can be the failing dependency, and the Mac is the only node able to inspect or repair local state.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-planner → mac-terminal → browser-extension → faculty-perception → faculty-action
- **model tier:** Background/cheap model for receipt parsing, error categorization, and selecting a previously successful repair recipe; realtime only for the short spoken acknowledgement and final concise explanation. Escalate to the expensive tier only when evidence is contradictory or the repair plan is novel.
- **latency:** A receipt-based diagnosis in under 2 seconds after the owner asks; one unattended retry/repair within 30 seconds; long repairs become a durable relay job with progress and a final pendant notification. Never loop indefinitely: max 2 attempts per step and stop on unchanged error.
- **cost:** About $0.003–$0.02 per ordinary incident, dominated by model calls only when the local error classifier cannot match a cached recipe; shell/browser execution is local. A successful cached diagnosis should require no expensive model call.
- **security:** The Mac remains maximum-access and unattended per owner policy; this is not a new gate. Repair recipes must record exact commands/actions, exit code, cwd, timestamps, and before/after observations, while redacting tokens, environment secrets, and page contents. Do not silently retry irreversible commands; mark them non-retriable and explain why. Browser repairs must preserve authenticated-session boundaries and never send a form as part of diagnosis.
- **missing:** A durable failure record linking each failed step to its parent job and surface; A local error taxonomy plus a small, signed recipe cache of known reversible repairs and pre/post checks; An executor mode that can run a bounded repair plan, capture structured stdout/stderr/exit code/duration, and emit a final receipt; Relay notification/event handling for completion after the pendant disconnects; Cross-surface correlation between browser command failures and the Mac job that depended on them

### "“While you’re working on my Mac, let me know from the pendant—without opening a conversation—when it finishes, gets stuck, or needs me.”"
- **useful because:** A durable Mac job is invisible once the owner walks away. The owner should be able to keep cooking, commuting, or working and recognize completion or a blocked step from a private LED/haptic pattern, then press once to hear a concise result. This is not another spoken briefing or job-status query: it is a low-attention, physical interrupt that only exists because the Mac executor, always-awake relay, and worn pendant are coordinated.
- **path:** mac-terminal → mac-planner → browser-extension → relay-realtime → pendant → faculty-perception → faculty-action
- **model tier:** No expensive realtime inference for routine state changes. Mac emits typed progress events; relay applies a deterministic state-to-signal map. Use a cheap background model only to turn the final receipt or blocker into a one-sentence spoken summary when the owner presses the button.
- **latency:** Signal completion or blocker within 1 second of the Mac event reaching the relay. Button press should produce the spoken summary within 2 seconds; stale/offline events are queued and coalesced rather than replaying every intermediate step.
- **cost:** Essentially $0 model cost for progress and LED/haptic signals; approximately $0.001–$0.005 for an optional final summary, dominated by text generation. Negligible relay storage for the latest state and one compact receipt pointer per job.
- **security:** Do not encode sensitive task names, page titles, command text, or account data in LED/haptic patterns; signals should reveal only state class (working, done, blocked, failed). Relay notifications must be authenticated and bound to the owner’s pendant. Quiet hours and a local mute button are needed, and a blocked browser task must not expose private page content until the owner requests audio.
- **missing:** A pendant firmware notification primitive supporting a small set of distinct LED/haptic patterns, queue/coalescing, mute, and acknowledgement; A relay event subscription from Mac/browser jobs with authenticated owner-device binding and latest-state persistence; A normalized job lifecycle event schema with working, waiting, completed, failed, and needs-owner states; A button-triggered lookup that retrieves the associated redacted receipt and speaks a short explanation; Dashboard controls for signal preferences, quiet hours, and per-job notification policy


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `mac-harness` — Add a failure-aware command journal and recovery runner between the existing Mac executor and job/receipt APIs. Every run_shell, run_applescript, typed action, and browser action emits one normalized step record: parent job/request ID, surface, argv or action type (with secrets redacted), cwd, start/end, timeout, exit code, bounded stdout/stderr hashes plus short excerpts, and reversible/irreversible classification for observability only. A classifier maps failures to stable categories (not-found, permission, timeout, dependency-offline, stale-browser-tab, bad-input, unknown). For known reversible categories it selects a versioned repair recipe with explicit precondition and postcondition probes, runs at most two attempts, and writes a linked before/after receipt; unknown failures become a durable diagnosis task rather than an unbounded retry. Expose GET /jobs/:id/failure and POST /jobs/:id/recover plus a compact event to relay/pipeline so the pendant can say what failed and whether recovery completed.
- **owner gets:** When the Mac fails while the owner is away from the keyboard, they get a concrete explanation and an automatic, bounded attempt at recovery instead of “something went wrong” or repeated blind retries. The owner can later audit exactly what changed and undo where supported, without giving up the deliberate maximum-access policy.
- effort: Medium: shared step schema and redaction, executor instrumentation, 6–10 built-in recipes with pre/post probes, two routes, and relay event wiring; add fault-injection tests for timeout, missing app, stale browser tab, and partial completion.  ·  risk: Capturing command text/output can leak sensitive data; redact known secret patterns, cap excerpts, hash full output, and retain only short-lived diagnostics. A repair recipe can make an incorrect but reversible change; require preconditions, postcondition verification, max-attempt limits, and automatic rollback through existing undo where available. If the runner crashes, the linked receipt remains marked unknown rather than falsely successful.
- cost: Near-zero API cost for local classification and cached recipes; roughly $0.003–$0.02 only when a background model must interpret an unknown error. Small local disk/database growth from bounded receipts; no new hardware.  ·  latency: Adds under 100 ms instrumentation overhead to normal actions. Known diagnosis/recovery starts immediately; unknown cases add one background model round trip and become durable rather than blocking the voice turn.
- security: Improves auditability without reducing owner-authorized access or adding confirmation gates. Store redacted command metadata, not secrets; isolate browser evidence to its existing authenticated session and never include page tokens in relay events.
- depends on: A single correlation/request ID shared by relay, Mac, and browser jobs; A documented receipt schema and retention/redaction policy; A small recipe registry with preconditions/postconditions and explicit retryability; Relay subscription to job failure/completion events

### `integration` — Implement the granted mac_read_diagnostics adapter as a fixed-query local-agent endpoint, not a shell passthrough. Support the eight declared checks with typed JSON (OS/model/user/uptime, battery, network interfaces, Wi‑Fi, Bluetooth, audio, agent health, processes, disk), per-check timeout, unavailable/error state, timestamp, and a single snapshot ID. Have get_mac_status and planning consume the same snapshot so a spoken answer and a subsequent action cannot mix observations from different moments.
- **owner gets:** The pendant can answer whether the Mac is actually reachable, online, low on space, or unhealthy without opening a microphone or running opaque commands. More importantly, it stops the agent from making decisions from stale or contradictory status facts and makes failures explainable.
- effort: Small-to-medium: typed collector on the Mac, route authentication, schema validation, per-check timeouts, and adapter wiring; add tests for offline and permission-denied checks.  ·  risk: Some checks expose personal device metadata. Return only requested fields, omit process arguments and network identifiers unless explicitly needed, and apply short retention. A stale snapshot could mislead; every result carries timestamp/age and unknown is preferred to guessed.
- cost: No model/API cost; negligible local CPU, disk, and network overhead. One compact JSON snapshot per status request.  ·  latency: Typical response under 300 ms with parallel bounded collectors; slow checks degrade individually instead of blocking the whole response.
- security: Narrow fixed queries reduce accidental shell exposure while preserving the owner's maximum-access action policy. Use existing local-agent authentication and never return secrets, command text, or raw process arguments.
- depends on: A real implementation behind the already-granted mac_read_diagnostics schema; Shared typed status schema consumed by get_mac_status and mac-planner; Local-agent authentication and request correlation IDs

### `firmware` — Add an offline-capable job-notification channel to the pendant: accept authenticated compact events (working, completed, blocked, failed), map them to distinct LED/haptic patterns, retain only the latest event plus a small acknowledged flag in flash, coalesce repeated progress events, honor a hardware mute/quiet state, and emit the associated job ID on a button press so the relay can speak details. The firmware must fail closed on malformed/unauthenticated events and continue normal button/audio behavior when the relay is unavailable.
- **owner gets:** The owner can know that a Mac or authenticated-browser task completed or needs attention without staring at a screen or starting a voice exchange. A single press turns an abstract vibration into a useful spoken result, while offline retention prevents a completion notification from disappearing during a brief link loss.
- effort: Medium: event authentication and compact queue in firmware, three or four signal patterns, acknowledgement/mute behavior, relay delivery and replay tests, and a small preference surface for quiet hours.  ·  risk: Too many signals could become annoying or reveal private activity. Coalesce to one latest state per job, suppress intermediate progress, support mute/quiet hours, and encode no sensitive text in the physical pattern. If flash wear or link loss occurs, drop old events and preserve normal pendant operation; never block audio or button handling.
- cost: No model/API cost. Firmware storage is a few hundred bytes plus wear-leveled event metadata; average power impact is negligible because signaling uses existing LED/haptic hardware, with short vibration bursts adding only transient draw.  ·  latency: Sub-second notification after relay delivery; no impact on normal voice latency. Acknowledgement lookup adds one relay round trip only when the owner presses the button.
- security: Requires device-bound authenticated event delivery and opaque job IDs. The pendant stores no command, page, account, or transcript content; relay authorization remains the source of truth.
- depends on: Authenticated Mac/browser-to-relay lifecycle events; Pendant firmware event parser and LED/haptic pattern API; Button event carrying acknowledged job ID to relay; Owner-configurable mute and quiet-hour preferences
