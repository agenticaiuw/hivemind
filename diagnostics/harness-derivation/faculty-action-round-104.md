## Capabilities it proposed

### "Handle this private webpage for me: gather the details, prepare the reversible changes, and finish only after I approve the exact result."
- **useful because:** The pendant can capture the owner's intent while away from the Mac; the relay can keep the job alive; the browser can read the logged-in page; and the Mac can use granted AppleScript APIs to carry out non-GUI work. The owner gets one understandable preview, a physical approval point, and a durable spoken receipt instead of an untraceable click or a lost job.
- **path:** pendant → relay → browser-extension → mac-planner → mac-terminal → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use the realtime tier only for the pendant conversation and final short confirmation; use a cheaper background planner for extraction, reconciliation, and queued execution. Use deterministic AppleScript/typed browser actions wherever possible rather than vision.
- **latency:** Preview within 5–10 seconds when Mac and browser are online; commit within 3 seconds after the owner's button/voice approval. If either surface drops, persist the lease and report queued/stalled status rather than guessing success.
- **cost:** About $0.01–$0.08 per transaction depending on page extraction and retries; realtime speech dominates interactive cost, while deterministic actions and background planning are cheap.
- **security:** Private page contents and proposed mutations leave the browser/Mac only to the authenticated relay path. Never submit mail, purchases, deletion, or other irreversible changes without an explicit final approval. Bind every step to sessionId/tabId, URL, freshness timestamp, and before-value evidence; expire the approval lease on navigation, timeout, or changed content. Accessibility-free GUI actions must be rejected, not reported as success.
- **missing:** browser heartbeat and pending-command reconciliation so the current 10 queued commands cannot be replayed blindly; a real precondition/commit gate in the Mac executor (the existing receipt/undo implementation records effects but does not refuse stale actions); fresh browser inspection/content provenance (current /browser/inspections is empty); pendant approval and cancellation event delivery plus a durable cross-surface transaction state machine

### "When I say ‘send this to Alex’ or ‘book it with Sam,’ resolve the right person across my Mac contacts, calendar, mail, and logged-in sites, tell me the candidates and why, and let me choose by pressing the pendant button before you prepare the action."
- **useful because:** Names are ambiguous across personal and work accounts. Today the system can act through separate Mac and browser surfaces, but it cannot safely reconcile identities across them or explain the match before an action is prepared. This would prevent sending private material to the wrong Alex, booking the wrong Sam, or silently mixing accounts while keeping the owner's approval small and physical.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-planner → mac-terminal → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use a cheap background resolver for deterministic contact/account matching and evidence collection; reserve the realtime tier for the short spoken candidate list and the owner's selection. Use exact identifiers and scripts for lookup, not free-form model guesses.
- **latency:** Return candidate identities and confidence in 3–8 seconds while the relevant Mac/browser surfaces are online. If one source is unavailable, say which source was omitted and do not collapse to a single guess.
- **cost:** Approximately $0.005–$0.03 per resolution; most cost is a small model call to normalize names and summarize evidence, with lookups handled deterministically.
- **security:** Contact names, email addresses, phone numbers, and account context are sensitive. Keep raw records on the Mac where possible; send only masked candidates and provenance to the relay. Never infer authorization from a name match. Require explicit pendant selection before preparing a send, booking, purchase, or other external action, and require the existing separate confirmation for irreversible submission.
- **missing:** a typed cross-surface identity record linking contact/account identifiers with source, freshness, and confidence; Mac read adapters for Contacts, Mail, Calendar, and account profiles that return masked candidates and stable IDs; browser extraction adapters that expose logged-in account identity without leaking page contents; a pendant candidate-selection event carrying the chosen stable ID, not merely spoken text; dashboard explanation of why each candidate matched and which sources were unavailable


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Create a durable cross-surface Action Transaction coordinator between browser bridge, Mac executor, relay jobs, and pendant events. It should issue a lease-bound transaction ID; collect typed observations (session/tab identity, URL, content hash/freshness, Mac app/path state); run only reversible prepare steps; pause at a commit gate; require a pendant button/voice approval bound to the exact preview hash; re-observe all preconditions immediately before commit; reject stale or untrusted GUI/browser results; then publish one receipt with per-step evidence, undoability, and final status. Reconcile queued browser commands by idempotency key after heartbeat and mark abandoned leases expired rather than replaying them.
- **owner gets:** A request begun in conversation remains safe and understandable even when the browser disconnects or the owner walks away. The owner sees exactly what will change, approves once on the pendant, and can later hear whether it prepared, committed, stalled, or was undone—without false 'success' from the currently untrusted computer-use loop.
- effort: Medium-high: shared transaction schema and D1 state machine, Mac executor hooks, browser queue reconciliation, pendant event plumbing, dashboard timeline, and integration tests for disconnects/stale tabs.  ·  risk: A coordinator bug could duplicate a reversible action or strand a job. Mitigate with idempotency keys, lease expiry, atomic state transitions, pre-commit snapshots, explicit unknown status, and never treating GUI success as proof while Accessibility/Screen Recording are absent. Recover via existing receipts and undo where supported.
- cost: Small relay/D1/R2 storage and a few cheap planner calls per transaction; no new model call required for deterministic gates. Interactive realtime cost remains limited to preview/approval speech.  ·  latency: Adds roughly 100–500 ms for state and precondition checks online; approval naturally waits for the owner. Offline/disconnected paths become queued rather than timing out ambiguously.
- security: Improves security by binding approval to a content hash and expiring it on navigation or mutation. Private page evidence remains within the authenticated relay/Mac path; irreversible effects remain confirmation-gated.
- depends on: Browser heartbeat with request-id/idempotency reconciliation; Mac executor precondition and commit-gate hooks (not just receipt recording); Pendant approval/cancel event route and durable event delivery; Browser inspection provenance: content hash, DOM/source evidence, freshness token

### `memory` — Add a local-first Identity Firewall and resolver: maintain per-account contact/person records as encrypted Mac-local entities with stable opaque IDs, source/account labels, validity intervals, and match explanations. Expose only masked candidate projections to relay and pendant. Require every downstream action to reference the opaque person/account ID plus a short-lived resolution token; reject actions whose source account, contact fields, or freshness changed between resolution and execution. Keep the raw address book and cross-account links out of generic fleet context and model prompts.
- **owner gets:** The owner can speak naturally while remaining protected from the most dangerous automation error: a plausible but wrong person or account. They get an intelligible choice instead of needing to remember exact email addresses, and private contacts are not copied into every agent context.
- effort: Medium: local encrypted store and Contacts/Mail/Calendar adapters, account-scoped browser identity extraction, resolver API, token validation in Mac/browser executors, and dashboard/pendant candidate UI.  ·  risk: A stale or incomplete directory could hide the intended person, and a resolver bug could present misleading matches. Mitigate by showing all materially plausible candidates, source/account labels, freshness, and an explicit 'no confident match' state; never auto-select on low confidence. Recovery is to ask for the exact address or let the owner cancel.
- cost: Low ongoing API cost; deterministic local lookups dominate, with a small model call only for normalization and spoken explanation. Encrypted local storage is negligible.  ·  latency: Adds roughly 1–4 seconds for local multi-source resolution and up to several seconds for a logged-in browser account lookup. It prevents much costlier mistaken actions.
- security: Reduces data exposure by keeping raw contact data on the Mac and using opaque IDs over the relay. Adds a sensitive local key store and therefore needs keychain protection, account separation, audit logging, and token expiry.
- depends on: A local encrypted identity store and stable opaque IDs; Read-only Contacts/Mail/Calendar and browser-account adapters; Executor validation of resolution tokens before any external action; Pendant selection events that identify a candidate by opaque ID
