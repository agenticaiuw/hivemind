## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Before you submit or send this, tell me if anything private is about to reach the wrong person or a public site, and fix it if I want.”"
- **useful because:** The most damaging assistant mistake is not a failed click but a successful disclosure: pasting a private note, API key, medical detail, or someone else’s contact information into the wrong form. The browser can see the destination and fields, the Mac can inspect the local source material, the relay can apply a consistent policy, and the pendant can give a brief warning while the owner is away from the keyboard. It should show the exact field, source, reason, and proposed redaction—not merely say “this may be sensitive.”
- **path:** browser-extension → mac-vision → mac-terminal → relay-realtime → pendant → faculty-perception → faculty-judgement → faculty-action → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use a cheap local/relay classifier for secret and PII detection plus deterministic destination rules; reserve realtime faculty-judgement for ambiguous social context (recipient, audience, or whether disclosure is intentional). No model call is needed for known secret patterns or same-origin moves.
- **latency:** Under 300 ms for deterministic scans and under 1.5 s for a model-assisted ambiguity check before a submit/send click. Never delay ordinary browsing; inspect only at a transfer or irreversible action boundary.
- **cost:** Usually near-zero per action with local pattern/NER checks; roughly $0.001–$0.01 only for ambiguous snippets, dominated by model classification. Do not upload whole pages or files—send field labels, destination, and minimal redacted evidence.
- **security:** The guard itself handles sensitive data, so classification must run locally when possible and redact values in logs. A warning must fail closed for high-confidence secrets, but allow an explicit owner override with a short-lived, destination-bound approval. Never silently rewrite a message: present before/after text, affected source, and recipient/audience. Keep an audit receipt without retaining the secret value.
- **missing:** A destination-and-data-flow policy engine that understands public/private audience, origin, sensitivity, and allowed transformations; Browser submit/send interception with a pre-submit snapshot and field-level provenance; Mac local secret/PII scanner that can return redacted spans from clipboard, editor, files, and selected text; A unified approval token bound to exact tab, URL, recipient, field values, and expiry; Pendant haptic/spoken warning and a dashboard showing disclosure receipts and overrides

### "“Forget everything about this topic everywhere, and prove what was deleted and what could not be reached.”"
- **useful because:** Today a personal AI can spread a sensitive fact across voice transcripts, relay jobs, browser receipts, Mac notes, queued audio, and pendant storage, but the owner has no single way to revoke it. A cross-surface erasure command would make the system safe to use for vulnerable conversations and accidental captures. It should search by an owner-confirmed topic/entity, show the deletion scope first, revoke queued work, remove local and relay artifacts, request browser-session cleanup, and return a signed receipt listing deleted, retained-by-law/system, and unreachable items.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → mac-terminal → browser-extension → faculty-perception → faculty-judgement → faculty-action → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use a cheap deterministic index and cryptographic labels to locate artifacts; use the expensive judgement tier only to resolve ambiguous references in the owner’s erasure request. Deletion execution and verification should be deterministic, not model-authored.
- **latency:** Show an initial scope in under 2 seconds. Complete reachable deletion in under 30 seconds, with asynchronous retries for offline Mac, browser, or pendant and a spoken completion/failure summary.
- **cost:** Usually below $0.01 per request; dominated by no model calls and small durable deletion receipts. The expensive part is engineering the indexes and reliable retry protocol, not inference.
- **security:** The erasure request itself must not reveal the secret in logs. Require confirmation of the scope when it includes broad or irreversible deletion, authenticate the owner, use tombstones and idempotent deletion, and retain only a minimal non-content receipt. A device that is offline must be marked unreachable rather than falsely reported deleted. Prevent later memory re-ingestion from surviving replicas.
- **missing:** A cross-surface content lineage index linking transcript spans, derived facts, jobs, audio objects, browser receipts, Mac files, and pendant records without storing plaintext in the index; An authenticated erasure protocol with tombstones, leases, retries, and per-surface deletion attestations; Deletion adapters for relay storage, Mac workspace/caches, browser extension session artifacts, and pendant flash; A post-deletion ingest filter that rejects tombstoned entities and topics; Dashboard and spoken receipt UX for scope preview, confirmation, partial failure, and recheck


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Add a cross-surface disclosure firewall between browser and Mac action execution. At every browser form submission, outbound message, clipboard paste into a browser field, or public-share action, create a short-lived transfer record: source surface and provenance, destination origin/recipient/audience, field-level sensitivity spans, normalized before/after values, and policy decision. Run deterministic secret/PII checks locally first; ask relay/faculty-judgement only for ambiguous intent. Block high-confidence secrets by default, offer redaction or an exact-value approval token, and bind any override to tab URL, recipient, field hash, and a 2-minute expiry. Emit a receipt and delete raw values after the retention window.
- **owner gets:** It prevents the high-cost class of mistake where the assistant does exactly what was asked but leaks something private. The owner gets a concrete warning and a safe repair while still being able to approve intentional sharing.
- effort: Medium-high: browser bridge interception and provenance schema, Mac clipboard/editor/file span extraction, policy service, and pendant/dashboard approval UX; build deterministic tests for forms, email, public issue trackers, and intentional overrides.  ·  risk: False positives could frustrate the owner or block urgent work; recover with clear reason codes, redaction preview, and explicit bound override. False negatives remain possible, so classify conservatively and never claim perfect protection. If relay is unavailable, high-confidence local rules still block; unknown policy state should be surfaced rather than silently allowing.
- cost: Small relay/storage cost per transfer receipt; model cost only for ambiguous cases, approximately $0.001–$0.01. Local scanning keeps private values off the network and reduces token/context spend.  ·  latency: No perceptible delay for ordinary browsing; 100–300 ms deterministic scan at transfer boundaries, up to 1.5 s only when ambiguity requires judgement.
- security: Improves confidentiality by making data flow explicit and fail-closed for secrets. Raw values must stay on-device, logs store hashes/redacted spans, approval tokens are destination- and value-bound, and receipts have short retention.
- depends on: Reliable browser command queue with request IDs and tab/session affinity (chg-14accc01); Browser provenance/workbench or equivalent field locator and before/after evidence (chg-e14fff33); Cross-surface durable saga/precondition executor described by faculty-action; A local Mac text/clipboard/file scanner and browser submit interception, which do not exist yet

### `memory` — Introduce a content-lineage and revocation layer spanning relay, Mac, browser, and pendant. Every persisted artifact receives a non-content lineage label (conversation/job/session/source entity, sensitivity class, retention, and parent hashes). Add an authenticated DELETE /memory/erase transaction that first returns a scope preview, then writes a tombstone, cancels queued jobs, invokes idempotent per-surface deletion adapters, retries offline nodes, and records signed per-surface attestations. Retrieval and scheduled jobs must consult tombstones before reusing or re-ingesting derived facts. The owner-facing receipt must distinguish deleted, retained, and unreachable artifacts without echoing the erased content.
- **owner gets:** They can safely tell the pendant something sensitive and later make it genuinely disappear across the whole hive, instead of guessing which transcript, job, note, audio file, or browser receipt still contains it.
- effort: High: lineage propagation through existing persistence paths, adapters on four surfaces, authenticated scope/confirmation UX, tombstone-aware retrieval, and failure-injection tests for offline devices and partial deletion.  ·  risk: A missed persistence path could create false confidence; mitigate with a deny-by-default artifact registry, periodic orphan scans, explicit unreachable status, and a recheck command. Broad erasure is irreversible, so require a scope preview and confirmation, while allowing immediate cancellation of queued work.
- cost: Low per request for hashes, tombstones, and receipts; modest storage overhead for lineage metadata. No routine model cost. Engineering cost is substantial because every persistence path must register its artifact type.  ·  latency: Scope preview seconds; deletion normally tens of seconds with asynchronous retries. Normal reads incur one tombstone/lineage lookup, bounded by a local cache.
- security: Strongly improves privacy and user control. Keep content out of lineage indexes and receipts, authenticate erase requests, encrypt attestations, and prevent tombstoned entities from being reconstructed by memory projection or scheduled jobs.
- depends on: A complete artifact registry for relay jobs, sessions, audio, browser receipts, Mac workspace files, and pendant flash; Cross-surface durable job leases and cancellation; Per-surface authenticated deletion adapters and offline retry queues; Tombstone-aware memory projection and scheduled routine execution; Owner privacy policy defining legal/operational retention exceptions

## Its own summary

This round produced a new cross-surface capability and integration change: a disclosure firewall that catches private data crossing into the wrong audience at browser/Mac transfer boundaries, gives field-level evidence and redaction, and requires a short-lived exact-value approval for intentional exceptions. It is deliberately beyond existing generic irreversible-action gates because it understands data provenance and destination audience. I also asked for the owner’s disclosure-boundary policy (public destinations, trusted recipients, sensitive data classes), which is the remaining product decision needed to avoid both leaks and nuisance blocking.

**Biggest unknown:** The owner’s actual privacy boundary rules are not yet known; without them the firewall can only use conservative generic secret/PII heuristics.
