## Capabilities it proposed

### "“If something I asked you to do fails while I’m away, keep going where it’s safe, tell me exactly what stopped, and give me one command to resume it.”"
- **useful because:** Today a long Mac/browser job can leave partial side effects with a receipt but no coherent explanation or continuation. This would turn failures into recoverable work: the owner wakes to a concise spoken status, a sourced report on the Mac, and a resumable plan rather than repeating commands or guessing what already happened.
- **path:** relay-realtime → relay → mac-planner → mac-terminal → browser-extension → browser → pendant → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use a cheaper background model (GPT-5.6-luna or equivalent) to classify failures, summarize receipts, and construct a resume plan; use realtime only to answer the owner's live follow-up through the pendant. Use the Mac vision model only for a failed GUI step that needs visual diagnosis.
- **latency:** Start/resume acknowledgement under 2 seconds from the relay; background recovery and report within 30 seconds for shell/API work and up to 2 minutes for browser/GUI work. Spoken report should be under 30 seconds.
- **cost:** Roughly $0.01–$0.05 per delegated job depending on output volume; most cost is the background recovery model and any mac-vision retry, not receipt storage or relay delivery.
- **security:** The Mac shell remains unrestricted by owner policy. Preserve exact command, cwd, exit code, stderr, touched paths/apps/URLs, and redaction metadata in the private ledger; do not send raw stdout, page contents, cookies, or secrets to the relay unless needed for the owner's request. Never silently repeat a non-idempotent browser submission or destructive shell command; mark it stopped/uncertain and report the last confirmed side effect. No new approval gate is introduced; existing action semantics remain unchanged.
- **missing:** A durable cross-surface job runner (the current backend still lacks one) with dependency-aware checkpoints and idempotency keys spanning Mac shell and authenticated browser actions.; A shared failure taxonomy and typed step state: succeeded, retryable, blocked-on-device/session, failed-before-effect, effect-uncertain, and irreversible.; A resume endpoint/tool that accepts a job ID, skips confirmed action IDs, revalidates sessions/tabs/files, and emits a new receipt chain linked to the original.; Relay-to-pendant delivery of compact failure/resume notifications and a dashboard view showing the step graph, evidence, and exact stopping point.

### "“Pack up what I’m doing so I can leave this Mac, and let me pick it up later from the pendant or another device exactly where I stopped.”"
- **useful because:** The owner currently loses the relationship between open Mac work, authenticated browser tabs, terminal state, unsent drafts, and the reason a task was paused. This would create a private, resumable handoff rather than a pile of tabs and vague notes: the pendant can explain the active threads while the Mac or browser later restores only the relevant state.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → relay → mac-planner → mac-terminal → browser-extension → browser → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use a cheaper background model to summarize and index the work session; use realtime only when the owner asks the pendant for a spoken handoff. Use mac-vision only to capture/restore GUI state that accessibility APIs cannot represent.
- **latency:** A spoken 'pack this up' acknowledgement in under 2 seconds; produce the handoff within 15 seconds for ordinary tabs/files and under 60 seconds when screenshots or GUI reconstruction are required. Resume should show a concise state within 5 seconds, then restore asynchronously.
- **cost:** About $0.01–$0.04 per pack/resume, dominated by summarization and occasional screenshots; storage and local state fingerprints are negligible. Repeated spoken queries should use cached summaries rather than re-running the model.
- **security:** Keep cookies, tokens, private page bodies, clipboard contents, and raw terminal output on the Mac/browser boundary; relay and pendant receive minimized summaries and opaque references. Encrypt the handoff bundle, bind browser restoration to the existing authenticated session, expire sensitive captures, and clearly label drafts as unsent. The feature should not submit forms, send messages, or mutate files merely by restoring a session.
- **missing:** A session-bundle schema that links Mac windows/apps, terminal jobs, browser tab/session IDs, selected files, drafts, and task intent while storing provenance and sensitivity per item.; A local Mac snapshot/restore adapter for accessibility-visible windows, terminal cwd/job IDs, editor locations, and browser tab reattachment; browser bridge support for redacted page anchors rather than copying private page contents.; Relay storage and retrieval for encrypted, expiring handoff manifests plus a pendant-friendly spoken-summary endpoint.; A cross-device resume protocol that can rehydrate on the Mac, or provide an actionable spoken queue when the Mac is unavailable, without pretending that GUI state was restored.


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `mac-harness` — Add a cross-surface execution ledger and recovery coordinator. Every delegated plan gets a durable job/step DAG with stable step IDs, parent/child dependencies, attempt records, start/end timestamps, command or browser action fingerprints, exit/HTTP/UI outcomes, touched-resource summaries, and a monotonic receipt chain. The Mac shell adapter records argv-equivalent parsing when possible but preserves the original command string, cwd, environment-difference hash, stdout/stderr size plus redacted excerpts, signal/timeout, and resource probes. A classifier labels failures as retryable, session/device unavailable, failed-before-effect, effect-uncertain, or irreversible. Recovery policies are declarative: retry only steps marked idempotent or proven to have failed before effect; otherwise stop downstream dependents, preserve the evidence, and create a resume token. Browser steps use tab/session affinity and page fingerprints; Mac steps use receipt snapshots and existing undo metadata. A relay worker polls/pushes state, and the pendant receives only a compact status plus job ID. Dashboard exposes 'resume from step', 'retry failed step', and 'show evidence', while retaining the owner's no-gate maximum-access policy.
- **owner gets:** A failed overnight task will no longer force the owner to rerun a whole workflow or wonder whether an email/form/file change already happened. They get an honest stopping point, automatic safe continuation, and a single resume action that avoids duplicate side effects.
- effort: Medium-high: new durable schema/worker, adapters in executor.js and browser bridge, failure classifier, relay notification, dashboard state view, and integration tests for timeout, disconnect, duplicate delivery, and uncertain side effects.  ·  risk: A classifier could wrongly call a step retryable and duplicate an external effect, or a stale browser session could produce misleading evidence. Default uncertain states to stop (not block owner actions), require explicit idempotency declarations for automatic retries, and preserve raw receipts for forensic review. If the coordinator dies, the existing executor and receipts continue operating; recovery can be manual.
- cost: Small D1/R2/storage and polling cost; approximately $0.001–$0.01 per job for ledger writes and background classification, plus occasional vision/model retries. No new hardware required.  ·  latency: Negligible overhead per action (one local ledger write, generally <50 ms); recovery reports arrive asynchronously. Durable writes may add modest latency before acknowledging a multi-step job.
- security: Improves accountability without reducing capability: raw shell commands and private browser data stay on the Mac/local-agent boundary, while relay/dashboard receive typed summaries and redacted evidence. Encrypt ledger at rest, limit job visibility to the owner's identity, and expire raw stdout/page excerpts separately from durable step metadata.
- depends on: The existing actionReceipts.js result/undo integration and GET /jobs/:jobId/receipts.; The browser request-ID/session-affinity work already present in browserBridge.js/browserSessions.js.; A durable backend job runner and authenticated relay-to-Mac job status channel, which are still absent.

### `integration` — Add a first-class encrypted Work Handoff Bundle protocol. At pack time, Mac-planner asks local Mac and browser adapters for typed state records (window/app identity, file path plus content hash, terminal cwd and job ID, browser tab/session ID plus URL/title and redacted anchor hashes, draft identifiers, and the current goal). A local model compiles these into an ordered task graph with 'safe to restore', 'needs revalidation', and 'reference only' classifications; private bodies remain local and the relay stores only encrypted manifests and short-lived opaque pointers. The pendant receives a compact spoken index. On resume, the target Mac requests each record, revalidates hashes/session ownership, restores tabs/windows/focus where possible, and reports omissions instead of fabricating continuity; from the pendant, the owner can ask for any thread and get its next concrete step. Add expiry, per-record deletion, and a provenance trail for every restored item.
- **owner gets:** They can leave a complicated task without losing their place or leaving a vulnerable Mac session open. Returning later—or asking from the pendant—restores the actual working context and tells them honestly what changed while they were away.
- effort: Medium-high: local state collectors and restore adapters, encrypted manifest storage, redaction/sensitivity rules, browser and terminal identity hooks, and end-to-end interruption/resume tests.  ·  risk: Some applications cannot restore state safely, browser pages can change, and stale file hashes may make a restoration misleading. Treat each item independently, require revalidation before opening or editing changed resources, preserve the original snapshot for comparison, and degrade to a spoken summary rather than claiming success. If bundle storage fails, leave the live Mac session untouched.
- cost: Low recurring storage and encryption overhead; roughly $0.002–$0.02 per bundle plus occasional model summarization. No pendant hardware change required.  ·  latency: Packing adds local collection time (typically seconds); restoration is asynchronous after the initial acknowledgement and should not block the pendant conversation.
- security: Improves privacy over sending whole desktop/browser snapshots to the relay by keeping sensitive payloads local and expiring encrypted manifests. Requires strict owner identity binding, no cookie/token export, and audit/delete controls for every bundle.
- depends on: A durable relay job/storage facility with authenticated owner binding.; Browser tab/session affinity and typed results from the existing browser bridge.; Mac action receipts and local-agent observability for terminal jobs and app state.; A pendant/relay protocol for retrieving compact handoff summaries.

## Its own summary

Round 27 produced a new cross-surface capability and implementation proposal: failure-aware durable execution with resumable jobs. Existing receipts/undo make individual Mac actions explainable, but they do not yet form a dependency-aware job graph spanning shell, authenticated browser tabs, relay, and pendant. The proposed system classifies failures, retries only safe/idempotent steps, stops on uncertain effects, preserves evidence, and delivers a concise spoken status plus a resume token. I do not need another permission or a narrower shell policy; the owner deliberately wants unrestricted execution. What is still needed is the durable backend job runner, shared failure/step-state schema, authenticated relay-to-Mac status channel, resume endpoint, and dashboard/pendant delivery. I also notified mac-planner and relay-realtime to avoid duplicate design and coordinate the protocol.

**Biggest unknown:** Whether another agent has already implemented a partial durable job runner or notification channel since the last snapshot; the established backend notes still say the durable runner is absent.
