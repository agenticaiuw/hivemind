## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Prepare this across my logged-in browser and Mac, then ask me on the pendant to approve it. After I press once, do it only during a short lease, verify the result from the source, and tell me exactly what changed.”"
- **useful because:** This is the missing safe handoff between judgement and action: browser-extension gathers private facts and stages a form, Mac performs local side effects, and the pendant provides an unmistakable physical approval that survives the owner leaving the screen. A durable relay lease prevents stale approval; postcondition reads catch silent failures (for example, a form that looked submitted but was rejected). The owner receives one sourced completion receipt or a clear failure/expiry, rather than trusting that an agent said it clicked.
- **path:** faculty-judgement emits a typed transaction plan and risk summary → browser-extension reads the authenticated tabs, stages reversible fields, and returns before-state evidence → mac-planner/mac-terminal performs the local reversible steps and records action receipts → relay holds the pending transaction, lease expiry, approval nonce, and completion event while the Mac/browser may reconnect → pendant displays approval-needed via its single LED, accepts one deliberate button press, and gives success/error indication → faculty-action dispatches only after the nonce is approved, then browser-extension or Mac rereads the authoritative source and publishes a receipt to relay-realtime
- **model tier:** Use the realtime tier only for the spoken explanation and approval prompt; use a cheaper background model for extracting fields, checking postconditions, and composing the completion receipt.
- **latency:** Stage in 5–15 seconds; approval prompt within 1 second of staging; after button press execute within 2 seconds; verify within 10 seconds. Expire the lease after 2 minutes or on any material plan/state change.
- **cost:** Typically 1 realtime turn plus 2–4 cheap extraction/verification calls; roughly $0.02–$0.10 excluding provider pricing, dominated by authenticated page extraction and any vision fallback.
- **security:** Never transmit secrets or full page contents to the relay when hashes/snippets suffice. Bind approval to a plan hash, account/session, target, and before-state; reject replay, changed tabs, duplicate presses, and expired leases. Require a second explicit spoken confirmation for irreversible financial, legal, deletion, or message-send actions. Store only redacted receipts and allow owner deletion.
- **missing:** Pendant firmware skill for a nonce-bound approval gesture and local LED states (one button/one LED); A durable relay transaction/lease store and push channel; Executor-enforced preconditions and approval gates (currently receipts exist but observeBeforeAction verdicts are ignored); Typed postcondition verifiers for browser and Mac actions; Owner-facing pending-approval and completion UI/audio queue

### "“Cancel everything you’re waiting to do right now.”"
- **useful because:** Today an owner can ask the system to act, but cannot reliably revoke all queued or in-flight authority from the thing they are wearing. A pendant emergency cancel would stop staged browser submissions, queued Mac jobs, and reconnecting workers without finding the laptop or opening a dashboard. It is a distinct safety primitive: cancellation is owner-controlled revocation, not approval, undo, or a report after the fact.
- **path:** pendant detects a deliberate long-press/chord and gives immediate local cancellation feedback → relay atomically records a per-owner revocation epoch and pushes it to connected Mac/browser workers → mac-planner/mac-terminal and browser-extension check the epoch before every side effect and acknowledge cancellation of queued work → faculty-action reports which jobs were cancelled, which were already irreversible, and which require manual follow-up → dashboard and relay-realtime expose the cancellation receipt without exposing page secrets
- **model tier:** No expensive model is needed for the stop path. Use firmware and relay logic first; use a cheap background model only to summarize any already-completed effects.
- **latency:** Local LED acknowledgement under 200 ms; relay broadcast under 1 second; workers must stop before their next side effect, with a 2-second escalation timeout for stale workers.
- **cost:** Negligible inference cost; small durable relay write and one broadcast per cancellation. Hardware uses existing button/LED and adds no meaningful power draw.
- **security:** Require a deliberate gesture to prevent accidental cancellation, but never require speech or network availability. The revocation epoch must be monotonic, authenticated, persisted, and checked by every executor; cancellation cannot erase receipts or pretend already-completed irreversible effects did not happen.
- **missing:** Pendant firmware emergency-revocation gesture and offline LED state; Relay-wide revocation epoch with worker acknowledgements; Pre-side-effect cancellation checks in Mac and browser executors; A typed cancellation receipt and stale-worker escalation path


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `mac-harness` — Complete the missing safety half of transactional execution: make every write action carry a canonical plan hash, expected target state/precondition, approval requirement, and expiry; have executor.js evaluate observeBeforeAction and refuse on mismatch before dispatch. For browser actions, bind the same transaction hash to tab/session and require the bridge result to echo it. Add an atomic approval-consumption endpoint/relay record so one pendant press can authorize exactly one plan, with duplicate/replay rejection. After dispatch, run declared postcondition reads and mark the job succeeded, failed, or uncertain; expose all decisions and evidence in the existing action receipt and /jobs/:jobId/receipts. Keep old jobs in compatibility mode but never silently treat a missing gate as approved for new jobs.
- **owner gets:** The owner can safely approve a prepared action from the pendant without worrying that a stale page, changed file, retry, or reconnect will apply the decision somewhere else. If execution cannot be proven, they hear “uncertain” with an undo option instead of a false success.
- effort: Medium: define a versioned transaction schema, add pure precondition evaluators for common file/app/browser targets, durable nonce consumption in relay, executor refusal paths, and integration tests for stale state, retry, reconnect, and postcondition mismatch.  ·  risk: A stricter executor may refuse currently working plans or leave jobs pending when an app changes. Recover by presenting the exact failed precondition, allowing the planner to re-observe and issue a new plan; never auto-reuse the old approval. Roll out behind a version flag and retain raw receipts.
- cost: Negligible API cost; one cheap verification/read per write. Small durable storage increase for hashes and redacted evidence.  ·  latency: Adds roughly 100–500 ms for local preconditions and up to several seconds for browser postcondition reads.
- security: Strongly improves replay and confused-deputy resistance. Approval tokens must be single-use, short-lived, device-bound where possible, and must not include secrets in logs.
- depends on: Durable relay transaction/lease record; Pendant nonce-bound approval gesture; Typed action schema shared by planner, browser bridge, executor, and receipt code

### `relay` — Add an owner-scoped emergency revocation epoch shared by relay, Mac executor, and browser bridge. Persist epoch and cancellation reason in D1; expose an authenticated stop endpoint that is callable from the pendant path without an LLM. Every worker must compare its leased job epoch immediately before each external side effect, atomically mark the step cancelled when stale, stop polling, and acknowledge the epoch. On reconnect, workers quarantine stale commands rather than replaying them. Emit a cancellation receipt listing completed, prevented, and uncertain steps; retain ordinary undo separately.
- **owner gets:** A single deliberate gesture on the pendant would let the owner stop queued automation even when the Mac screen is inaccessible or the browser reconnects later. They get a trustworthy boundary—what definitely did not happen versus what needs checking—instead of hoping cancellation reached every surface.
- effort: Medium: relay schema/endpoint, worker middleware in Mac and browser paths, pendant event forwarding, acknowledgement aggregation, timeout handling, and race-focused tests where cancellation arrives during dispatch.  ·  risk: A side effect can race the final check and remain uncertain; the receipt must say so and never claim reversal. Overly broad cancellation could interrupt useful work, so scope it to the owner and require a deliberate long press. Recover by issuing a new plan after the owner reviews the receipt.
- cost: Tiny D1 write plus worker polling/broadcast overhead; no model calls. Existing hardware is reused.  ·  latency: Adds one local epoch comparison before each side effect; stop propagation target under 2 seconds.
- security: Improves containment and limits replay. Endpoint must authenticate the paired pendant, reject stale epochs, rate-limit gestures, and prevent cancellation records from deleting audit evidence.
- depends on: Pendant event path that works offline until link returns; Shared job lease/epoch schema across Mac and browser workers; Typed cancellation receipts
