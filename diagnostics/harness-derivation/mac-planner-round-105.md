## Capabilities it proposed

### "Keep my private browser work reliable: if the browser bridge drops or has stuck commands, recover what can be recovered, tell me what was not run, and never silently replay a risky step."
- **useful because:** Today seven commands are pending while the bridge is offline, and failed browser jobs can sit indistinguishable from work that merely needs retry. A cross-surface watchdog would make the pendant a trustworthy status channel: it can drain safe reads after reconnection, quarantine stale or mutating steps, and give the owner an exact interruption report rather than losing work or duplicating a submission.
- **path:** relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → unified → faculty-perception → faculty-judgement → faculty-action
- **model tier:** Background/scheduled triage should use a cheaper model; realtime is used only for the short pendant alert and follow-up. Deterministic queue-state checks and idempotency comparisons should run without an LLM.
- **latency:** Heartbeat detection under 30 seconds; reconnect reconciliation under 2 minutes after the extension returns; pendant alert under 3 seconds once the decision is made. No polling or browser action while the extension is offline.
- **cost:** Near-zero for heartbeats, queue reconciliation, hashes, and receipts; roughly one inexpensive text-model call per incident (typically <$0.01), with realtime cost only if the owner asks a spoken follow-up.
- **security:** Private URLs, snippets, and session identifiers stay on the Mac/relay and are not sent to the public browser backend. Read-only steps may auto-retry only with an idempotency key and unchanged tab/session binding. Click/type/submit/delete steps are quarantined and require an explicit owner command after showing the last known URL, action, and evidence. On reconnect, cancel commands whose TTL expired rather than replay them.
- **missing:** A durable browser job runner with persisted step states, TTLs, idempotency keys, and retry policy (the router exists but the runner does not).; A bridge watchdog that can distinguish offline, stale polling, and Safari-dialog failure, then reconcile or cancel pending commands.; A typed browser receipt/evidence record exposed to the pendant and planner, plus a safe-read versus mutation classifier.; An extension wake/reconnect hook or a clear owner-facing instruction when Safari must be opened manually.

### "When I walk away from my Mac, pause browser work safely, and when I come back, give me a one-line spoken handoff before anything continues."
- **useful because:** A browser session can outlive the person who started it. Presence-bound leases prevent a queued private-page task from continuing unattended, while the pendant gives a useful handoff instead of forcing the owner to inspect logs or guess whether a form was submitted.
- **path:** relay-realtime → pendant → mac-planner → browser-extension → faculty-perception → faculty-judgement → faculty-action
- **model tier:** Presence detection, lease expiry, and safe pause are deterministic. Use a cheap background model to summarize the receipt; use realtime only to speak the short handoff when the pendant reconnects.
- **latency:** Pause within one heartbeat (target 10–30 seconds) after the pendant/Mac presence signal disappears. On return, produce the handoff within 5 seconds, and do not resume mutating steps until the owner explicitly says resume.
- **cost:** Negligible for lease and heartbeat state; <$0.01 per return summary when there is substantive work. No browser-model call is needed for simple receipts.
- **security:** Presence is sensitive metadata, so retain only coarse active/away state and short history. Private URLs remain local. A lost presence signal must fail closed for mutating browser actions, while read-only actions may be cancelled or retained—not silently run. The pendant’s explicit resume phrase should be bound to the same job/session and a displayed action hash.
- **missing:** A relay-to-Mac presence/lease channel with signed session and pendant identity.; Browser commands carrying a lease ID and action class, enforced by the executor rather than only by the planner.; A compact receipt-to-speech handoff formatter and a resume endpoint that requires the matching job/action hash.

### "Use my private accounts to answer a question, but never send the private page or raw personal data off my Mac—give me only the minimum derived result and show me exactly what was disclosed."
- **useful because:** The owner could get genuinely useful cross-site answers today only by exposing whole private pages to a general model or by doing the comparison manually. A local privacy boundary would let the pendant ask questions over logged-in data while keeping credentials, page contents, and unnecessary personal fields on the Mac.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → faculty-perception → faculty-judgement → faculty-action
- **model tier:** Use a small local Mac model or deterministic extraction for filtering and aggregation; use the cheaper background model for synthesis over the redacted derived dataset. Realtime is reserved for the final short spoken answer.
- **latency:** Local extraction and redaction under 10 seconds for a handful of pages; synthesis under 15 seconds. If the query requires broad browsing or an uncertain field mapping, pause and ask rather than widening disclosure.
- **cost:** Usually <$0.02 per request, dominated by one background synthesis call; local filtering and hashing are effectively free. No cloud token cost for raw page content because it never leaves the Mac.
- **security:** The Mac must treat authenticated browser DOM and credentials as private. A non-bypassable data diode should allow only typed aggregates, boolean predicates, ranges, and owner-approved snippets to cross into the relay. Every outbound field needs a reason, sensitivity label, provenance, and hash; the pendant should announce the disclosure summary. No automatic submission, message send, or account mutation. Encrypt the local intermediate and expire it after the answer.
- **missing:** A Mac-local privacy broker that can query authenticated tabs, transform raw DOM into a typed derived dataset, and enforce an allowlisted outbound schema.; A browser extraction protocol with field-level provenance and sensitivity labels rather than whole-page text results.; Relay/model routing that rejects payloads containing raw private fields and accepts only the signed derived-result envelope.; A pendant disclosure receipt and a way for the owner to approve expanding one field when the derived answer is insufficient.


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `browser-harness` — Implement a Browser Bridge circuit-breaker and reconciliation worker between the existing command queue and job receipts. On each heartbeat, classify the bridge as online, stale, or offline; assign every command a creation time, TTL, idempotency key, session/tab binding, and mutation class; persist a state machine queued→leased→acknowledged→completed/failed/quarantined. When the bridge returns, automatically retry only expired-safe read steps whose binding and request hash are unchanged, quarantine all mutating steps, and emit one compact interruption receipt listing what ran, what did not, and why. Reconcile the current seven pending commands on startup rather than replaying them blindly.
- **owner gets:** The owner gets honest recovery after closing a laptop or losing Safari: no duplicate form submissions, no silent dropped research, and a single pendant-ready answer to “did that happen?” even after a restart.
- effort: Medium: a persistent worker, schema migration, queue leases, and tests for crash/reconnect races; no new model is needed. Requires wiring the existing local browser queue, /browser/status heartbeat, /browser/poll, result callbacks, and job receipts.  ·  risk: A false stale classification could quarantine a harmless read or leave work unfinished; recovery is explicit retry from the receipt. The critical safety rule is never auto-replay a mutation after an uncertain disconnect. Existing commands need a one-time migration with unknown status marked quarantined, not completed.
- cost: Negligible compute/storage; one small receipt per command and occasional inexpensive background triage call only for ambiguous failures.  ·  latency: Heartbeat remains lightweight; reconnect reconciliation completes within seconds for reads and intentionally pauses mutations until the owner asks.
- security: Improves safety by preventing replay. Keep private page content and session tokens on the Mac/relay; receipts should store hashes and minimal snippets, with configurable redaction.
- depends on: Durable browser job runner persistence (chg-16bc5dee is still open; router/backends exist but runner does not); Typed browser action/result schema with idempotency and tab affinity (parts of chg-14accc01 exist, but need queue integration); Browser extension heartbeat/poll endpoint remaining available

### `context` — Create a Mac-resident private-data diode for authenticated browser work. It accepts a natural-language question, executes only read operations in bound private tabs, maps results into a typed schema (e.g. price, date, status, count), strips free text and identifiers by default, and emits a signed disclosure envelope containing only approved fields, provenance hashes, freshness, and a human-readable disclosure manifest. The relay rejects any payload outside that schema; the owner can approve a one-field expansion from the pendant, producing a new envelope rather than exposing the page.
- **owner gets:** They can ask for comparisons, eligibility checks, or summaries across private accounts without handing an AI service their whole logged-in pages. The owner gets the benefit of the hive's reach while retaining a clear, inspectable privacy boundary.
- effort: High: a local policy/extraction service, browser field provenance, envelope signing and schema validation at the relay, redaction tests, and a pendant disclosure UI/audio format. This is a new privacy architecture, not a planner prompt.  ·  risk: Incorrect extraction could produce a wrong derived answer; every value must retain source URL, timestamp, selector/hash, and confidence so the owner can inspect it. A schema bug could leak data, so default-deny validation, fuzz tests, payload size limits, and immediate intermediate deletion are required. If uncertain, return 'could not safely derive' rather than raw text.
- cost: Small local CPU/storage cost; typically one cheaper synthesis call over a tiny derived payload, with no cloud cost for raw page data. Engineering cost is substantial because the boundary must be security-reviewed.  ·  latency: Adds a local transformation step, roughly seconds; avoids uploading large pages and can be faster for multi-page comparisons once typed extractors exist.
- security: Strongly improves privacy by making raw authenticated DOM and credentials non-exportable. It introduces signing keys and a policy engine that become high-value targets; store keys in the Mac keychain, rotate them, and audit every envelope and field expansion.
- depends on: A browser result format with field-level provenance and sensitivity labels; A Mac-local policy broker able to inspect authenticated tabs without returning whole-page content; Relay-side schema enforcement and signature verification; A pendant disclosure receipt and explicit field-expansion interaction
