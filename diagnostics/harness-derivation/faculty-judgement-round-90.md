## Capabilities it proposed

### "“After my meetings, help me close the loop.” When a calendar event ends, ask me (through the pendant) for a quick voice debrief, extract decisions/owners/deadlines, update my private notes and reminders, and prepare—not send—any follow-up messages or browser forms. Show me the evidence and let me correct it before anything external changes."
- **useful because:** People lose the value of meetings in the transition afterward. This turns a two-minute, low-friction spoken recollection into durable next actions across the calendar, Mac workspace, and authenticated web accounts, without pretending an inferred commitment is fact or sending on the owner's behalf.
- **path:** relay-realtime → pendant → mac-planner → browser-extension → unified → faculty-perception → faculty-judgement → faculty-action
- **model tier:** Use a cheap background model to detect the calendar boundary, transcribe and structure the debrief, and reconcile it with the meeting invite/notes. Use realtime only for the short pendant conversation and clarification questions. Use the local Mac agent for private note/reminder writes; browser automation only for prepared drafts and evidence gathering.
- **latency:** A gentle prompt 1–5 minutes after the event, never during a detected call. Ask no more than three questions; first structured draft within 30 seconds of the debrief. External drafts can finish asynchronously, with a notification when ready.
- **cost:** Typically <$0.02 per meeting for transcription plus small-model extraction/reconciliation; realtime voice and long meeting transcripts dominate cost. No model call is needed when the event is cancelled or has no attendees unless the owner asks.
- **security:** Meeting audio and inferred commitments are sensitive: retain the debrief and derived facts locally or with short TTL, attach source/timestamp/confidence to every item, and never treat an inference as an instruction. Creating private reminders/notes may be allowed by the owner's policy, but sending messages, submitting forms, changing shared calendars, or contacting third parties requires explicit confirmation. Provide a spoken stop/skip latch and a review screen with quoted evidence.
- **missing:** A calendar-event-ended trigger with attendee/call-state awareness (to avoid interrupting active calls); A durable commitment/decision object with provenance, confidence, owner, due date, and review status; A cross-surface reconciliation pass that can update private notes/reminders and prepare browser/email drafts while preserving a single approval gate; A pendant delivery acknowledgement and quiet-hours/deferral policy for the prompt

### "“Keep my promises feasible.” Continuously detect when my calendar, travel, reminders, private notes, and authenticated work commitments make two obligations impossible or dangerously tight. Ask me on the pendant only when a real conflict is found, explain the evidence and tradeoff, then prepare the smallest set of reschedules, decline messages, or browser changes that would restore feasibility for my approval."
- **useful because:** Today the system can read or act on separate surfaces, but the owner still has to notice that commitments conflict and decide which promise to renegotiate. This would protect the owner from silent deadline collisions, impossible travel, double-booking, and accidental overcommitment while preserving their agency.
- **path:** faculty-perception → faculty-judgement → relay-realtime → pendant → mac-planner → browser-extension → unified → faculty-action
- **model tier:** Use a cheap background model for periodic constraint reconciliation and travel/time arithmetic. Use realtime only to ask the owner a short prioritization question when a conflict crosses a meaningful threshold. Use the local Mac and browser surfaces to gather private evidence and prepare reversible changes.
- **latency:** Reconcile after calendar/account changes and at most once per relevant commitment; alert within a few minutes of discovering a genuine conflict. Produce a proposed resolution asynchronously within one minute when sources are available. Never interrupt during an active call or quiet hours unless the conflict is imminent.
- **cost:** Usually under $0.03 per reconciliation; the dominant cost is authenticated page extraction and long task histories, which should be cached and summarized. Realtime cost occurs only for conflicts requiring a decision.
- **security:** Commitment inference can expose sensitive work, health, or relationship information. Keep a source-level privacy policy, minimize cross-account joins, show quoted evidence and confidence, and never send, decline, reschedule, purchase, or submit anything without explicit approval. A false conflict must be dismissible and must not retrigger until source evidence changes.
- **missing:** A constraint graph that represents commitments, travel buffers, dependencies, deadlines, locations, and confidence rather than isolated reminders; A conflict-severity and interruption policy that distinguishes impossible, risky, and merely busy schedules; A resolution compiler that generates a minimal set of private and external changes with before/after impact analysis; Cross-surface transaction grouping so all approved changes succeed together or leave a clear recovery plan


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Add a Meeting Closeout coordinator that consumes calendar event boundaries and pipeline audio/events, creates a provenance-bearing closeout record, and runs a single reconciliation job across Mac notes/reminders and authenticated browser drafts. It must pause at one explicit approval gate for any shared/external mutation, support owner correction, and emit a durable receipt linking each extracted decision to its spoken quote or calendar/source evidence.
- **owner gets:** After a meeting, the owner gets an accurate, reviewable list of decisions and next actions instead of relying on memory or manually copying notes between apps. Nothing gets sent or shared accidentally.
- effort: Medium-high: calendar/call-state adapter, schema and persistence, small-model extraction, Mac and browser adapters, approval UI/audio interaction, and failure/retry tests.  ·  risk: False attribution or deadlines could create wrong reminders or embarrassing drafts. Keep all writes private until approval, label confidence, require correction for low-confidence owner/date fields, make jobs idempotent, and expose undo receipts. If a surface is offline, retain a pending closeout rather than silently dropping it.
- cost: Low ongoing API cost (small model plus short transcription); engineering cost is concentrated in the coordinator and provenance schema. Storage is modest if raw audio is short-lived and only transcript spans/hashed citations are retained.  ·  latency: Prompt and extraction can occur within seconds after the event; browser preparation may take tens of seconds asynchronously. No impact on active call audio path.
- security: Sensitive meeting content crosses relay/Mac/browser only when needed. Enforce per-source sensitivity and TTL, redact attendees/secrets from prompts where possible, and require confirmation before external side effects.
- depends on: Calendar event boundary and active-call detection; Durable commitment/decision records with provenance and correction state; A shared approval gate spanning Mac/browser actions and pendant voice; Existing pipeline audio/event persistence and job receipts

### `memory` — Introduce a private constraint graph and reconciliation engine for obligations: each node stores commitment text, source citation, owner, deadline, location, travel buffer, dependency, confidence, sensitivity, and expiry; each edge expresses overlap, prerequisite, or incompatibility. On every relevant calendar, note, reminder, or authenticated-page event, compute feasibility windows and emit only newly material conflicts. Persist a proposed resolution set with before/after impact and an idempotent cross-surface transaction receipt.
- **owner gets:** The owner would learn about an impossible promise before it becomes a missed deadline, and would receive a concrete, minimal way to fix it rather than a vague warning or another noisy briefing.
- effort: High: new schema and privacy-aware joins, temporal/travel constraint solver, source adapters, deduplication, approval transaction orchestration, and recovery tests across offline Mac/browser states.  ·  risk: Bad extraction or stale pages could create unnecessary anxiety or suggest harmful prioritization. Require evidence and confidence thresholds, let the owner mark a source authoritative or dismiss a conflict, expire stale edges, and default to proposing rather than changing anything. If one approved surface fails, do not partially renegotiate silently; retain a receipt and recovery plan.
- cost: Small persistent graph and incremental background inference; API cost is low with event-driven deltas, but authenticated-page reads and occasional conflict explanations dominate. Raw source content should not be duplicated into the graph.  ·  latency: Incremental updates should complete in seconds to a minute; complex multi-account reconciliation can run as a background job without blocking voice interaction.
- security: This joins highly sensitive personal and work data, so store derived constraints separately from raw content, enforce per-source access labels and TTLs, and require explicit confirmation before any external or shared mutation.
- depends on: Authoritative timezone and travel-distance source; Typed provenance-bearing commitment/decision schema; Cross-surface approval and atomic-or-recoverable action grouping; Event subscriptions for calendar, reminders, notes, and authenticated browser changes
