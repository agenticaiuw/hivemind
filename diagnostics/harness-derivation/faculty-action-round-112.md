## Capabilities it proposed

### "“If this condition happens, carry out the bounded plan I gave you and tell me exactly what changed.” For example: if my authenticated travel page reports a cancellation, find an option under my stated limits, prepare it, and only commit automatically if every constraint and freshness check still passes; otherwise wake me on the pendant with the exact decision needed."
- **useful because:** Today the owner can ask for a check or a one-off action, but cannot safely leave a conditional, bounded plan running across a private browser session, their Mac, and an always-awake relay. This turns interruptions and changing external events into useful action without granting the system an open-ended standing mandate.
- **path:** browser-extension → mac-planner → mac-vision → relay-realtime → faculty-perception → faculty-judgement → faculty-action
- **model tier:** background for polling/event normalization and constraint evaluation; planner only when the event requires multi-step option generation; realtime only to wake the owner and handle the live approval conversation.
- **latency:** Event ingestion under 30 seconds when a watch changes; option preparation under 2 minutes; owner wake-up is immediate when a constraint is ambiguous or a commit boundary cannot be met.
- **cost:** Usually deterministic/background: roughly 1–3 cheap model calls per event (about $0.01–$0.08 depending on page extraction and option count). Planner escalation dominates cost only for ambiguous multi-site workflows.
- **security:** The contract must be scoped to named accounts/tabs, allowed vendors, maximum amount/time, and an expiry. Private page contents leave the browser only as minimized fields and cited evidence. No irreversible commit may occur without a fresh exact-constraint proof; if evidence is stale, a session changes, or the Mac/browser disappears, the system pauses and asks. Every attempted mutation needs an immutable receipt and replay protection.
- **missing:** conditional-action contract and durable trigger state (distinct from a passive page watch); constraint evaluator that produces a machine-verifiable commit predicate; event-to-action idempotency and lease ownership across relay, Mac, and browser; fresh authenticated-page evidence capture immediately before commit; pendant-resume approval bound to the exact contract revision and evidence hashes; owner-configurable policy UI for limits, expiry, and which consequences may be automatic


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `relay` — Add a cross-surface two-phase action-commit coordinator. Before any multi-step Mac/browser operation, mint an action_id containing the normalized plan hash, evidence/precondition hashes, allowed side effects, expiry, and required approval level. Each executor (mac_run_actions, browser_run_actions, or mac_delegate) must report prepare/commit/abort per step; the relay atomically records the checkpoint and receipt, revalidates evidence immediately before commit, and pauses on drift or lost connectivity. The pendant resume message approves the exact action_id+plan hash (never a vague task); abort leaves already-completed reversible steps with undo references. Expose a compact spoken status and a durable event stream for recovery.
- **owner gets:** If the owner says “do it” and walks away, a browser tab changing, a laptop sleeping, or a network drop will no longer turn a half-finished task into an unknown state. They can safely resume from the pendant, know exactly what did happen, and approve only the unchanged final step.
- effort: Medium-high: coordinator state machine and D1 schema, adapters in Mac/browser job runners, pre-commit evidence checks, resume/abort endpoints, and integration tests for crash/retry/drift.  ·  risk: A coordinator bug could incorrectly commit or duplicate an action; default all non-idempotent steps to prepare-only and require fresh approval. Recovery is action receipts plus per-step idempotency keys and undo-last where available. Irreversible operations remain blocked if preconditions or evidence are stale.
- cost: Negligible storage/CPU (one small checkpoint row and receipt per step); modest extra relay requests. No additional model calls for deterministic checks; a slower background model can summarize status.  ·  latency: Adds roughly 100–300 ms per step for receipt and precondition checks; no added model latency on the happy path.
- security: Stores hashes and minimal snippets rather than page contents; approval tokens are scoped to action_id, plan hash, expiry, and device identity. Prevents replay and accidental broad approvals, but requires careful token secrecy and audit retention policy.
- depends on: durable job runner (chg-16bc5dee); request-id/tab-affinity browser queue (chg-14accc01); action receipts and undo support (chg-5fc73ce3); fresh evidence/provenance workbench (chg-e14fff33); pending physical action confirmation and action-lease indicator skills

### `firmware` — Extend the pendant's single LED into a versioned cross-surface action-state protocol, driven by signed relay events: recording/audio-buffer states retain priority; otherwise encode staged-awaiting-approval, executing, paused-for-drift/offline, committed, and failed/undo-available as distinct bounded blink patterns. Events carry action_id, expiry, and sequence; firmware ignores stale/out-of-order states and returns to idle after a timeout. The relay exposes the same state to Mac/browser receipts so LED, spoken status, and dashboard cannot disagree.
- **owner gets:** While their hands and eyes are elsewhere, the owner can glance at the pendant and know whether a request is waiting for approval, still running, safely finished, or needs attention—without opening the Mac. A disconnected or stale task visibly stops claiming it is active.
- effort: Low-medium firmware state machine plus relay event schema and tests; no new sensor or radio required. Must arbitrate LED ownership with recording and error indications.  ·  risk: Blink patterns can be missed or misread; pair each non-idle pattern with a short spoken status when the voice channel is available, and fail closed to a distinctive error/unknown pattern on malformed or expired events. Never imply commit from LED alone; receipt remains authoritative.
- cost: Near-zero API cost and power impact (brief LED duty cycle); small firmware flash/RAM increase, likely under 8 KB flash and 1 KB RAM.  ·  latency: Relay-to-LED update within one event round trip, typically sub-second; no impact on audio path if LED updates are queued outside I2S processing.
- security: Only authenticated relay events scoped to the paired pendant may change action state; avoid putting task names or private data in LED payloads. Sequence numbers prevent replay.
- depends on: two-phase action-commit coordinator proposed this round; durable action receipts/undo (chg-5fc73ce3); pendant firmware event channel and existing recording LED arbitration; action-lease indicator request already pending

### `mac-harness` — Add an end-to-end typed-action preflight and argument-preservation test gate. Validate each action against its schema before enqueue (open_app requires appName, run_shell requires command, run_applescript requires script, browser actions require URL/session fields), then persist the normalized action payload unchanged through plan → relay job → Mac executor → receipt. Reject malformed actions before creating a job, with a structured field-level error; add a canary test for each high-level action and verify the receipt echoes the normalized inputs without secrets.
- **owner gets:** The owner gets a clear, immediate explanation instead of a job that appears to run and fails because required arguments vanished in transit. Valid requests reach the Mac on the first try, and the resulting receipt can be trusted when deciding whether to continue or undo.
- effort: Medium: shared schema/normalizer, enqueue validation, executor contract tests, and migration for existing receipts. No Accessibility grant required.  ·  risk: Overly strict schemas could reject a newly supported action; version schemas and allow explicit capability negotiation. Existing malformed jobs remain failed but harmless; provide a retry path after correction.
- cost: Small deterministic CPU/storage cost; no model call and no meaningful token cost.  ·  latency: Adds a few milliseconds pre-enqueue, saving seconds of failed Mac round trips.
- security: Reduces accidental execution and makes audit records truthful. Redact command/script secrets from receipt echoes while retaining a keyed hash for correlation.
- depends on: typed mac_run_actions contract; durable job receipts (chg-5fc73ce3); two-phase commit coordinator proposed this round

### `integration` — Create a durable conditional-action contract layer between page watches/events and executors. A contract stores a normalized trigger predicate, allowed action graph, hard limits (amount, destination, time window, account/session), expiry, and an owner-selected consequence policy. On each watch/event update, the relay evaluates the predicate deterministically, asks a background model only to extract candidate fields from cited page evidence, and emits either a no-op, a prepared action, or a pendant escalation. Immediately before any mutation it re-reads the authenticated source, recomputes the predicate, and requires the executor receipt to reference the same contract revision and evidence hashes. This is not a passive watch or a generic job runner: it is the missing connective control plane that converts an external event into a constrained, auditable action.
- **owner gets:** The owner can leave a narrowly defined contingency in place—rather than repeatedly noticing a change and manually restarting the whole workflow—while retaining hard limits and a clear wake-up when reality falls outside them.
- effort: High: contract schema/versioning, event deduplication, predicate evaluator, private-page extraction adapters, scheduler/expiry handling, and policy/receipt integration across relay, browser, and Mac.  ·  risk: A mistaken predicate could authorize an unwanted consequence. Default new contracts to prepare-only, require explicit opt-in per consequence class, enforce maximum spend and destination allowlists in deterministic code, and halt on missing/stale evidence. Contract revisions are immutable; revocation must be immediate and tested during network partitions.
- cost: Background polling/extraction adds modest relay and browser costs; deterministic predicate checks are negligible. Planner escalation is reserved for candidate generation and should be uncommon.  ·  latency: Adds one authenticated re-read before commit (typically 1–5 seconds); event detection remains watch cadence-limited. No realtime model is needed unless waking the owner.
- security: Contracts contain sensitive account/action policy and must be encrypted/access-controlled. Store field hashes and citations rather than raw private pages where possible; bind execution to session identity, contract revision, and one-time action nonce to prevent replay.
- depends on: authenticated page-watch/event normalization; typed action schemas and receipts; fresh evidence/provenance capture; owner policy and expiry model; pendant notification/resume channel
