## Capabilities it proposed

### "Do this, and don't tell me it's done until you can prove the result is actually true."
- **useful because:** Today the system can dispatch Mac/browser actions and produce receipts, but a receipt only proves that a command was accepted. This gives the owner a trustworthy closed loop: the pendant can trigger it, the relay can keep retrying after disconnects, the Mac or authenticated browser can act, and faculty-perception can independently verify the resulting state before speaking success. If verification fails, the owner hears 'not confirmed' rather than a false completion.
- **path:** pendant button/voice trigger sends a signed intent and displays an in-progress LED state → relay persists the intent, deduplicates retries, leases it to the reachable Mac/browser surface, and records an idempotency key → mac-planner selects mac_run_actions or browser_run_actions; browser-extension supplies tab/session-local evidence when private state is involved → faculty-perception runs a typed postcondition probe after a settle window (UI state, DOM field, file/reminder existence, or pipeline/device ACK) and returns pass/fail/unknown with timestamped evidence → relay delivers a compact spoken completion/failure result back to the pendant and Mac journal; unified retains the evidence link
- **model tier:** Use the cheaper background model for planning and postcondition interpretation; reserve realtime only for the owner's live utterance and the final short spoken result.
- **latency:** Reversible Mac actions: 2–5 seconds including verification. Browser actions: up to 15 seconds. If the target surface is offline, keep the job durable and report queued immediately; retry for a bounded TTL, then report unconfirmed. No destructive action is sent without the existing owner confirmation policy.
- **cost:** Roughly $0.01–$0.05 per completed job depending on browser screenshots/vision; most cost is the independent verification pass and any browser computer-use step, not relay persistence.
- **security:** Private browser evidence must stay on the Mac/bridge and be reduced to typed predicates or redacted snippets; never upload full DOM or screenshots by default. Bind proof to the same session/tab and idempotency key to prevent proving an unrelated state. Sending mail, deleting files, purchases, or other irreversible effects still require confirmation before dispatch; unknown proof must never be presented as success.
- **missing:** A shared typed action contract with expected postcondition, settle timeout, and verification recipe; A verifier endpoint/worker that can call Mac accessibility/file probes and browser DOM probes independently of the executor; Receipts need before/after evidence references and a three-state outcome: confirmed, failed, unconfirmed; Pendant/relay protocol needs durable job and result correlation so disconnect/retry cannot create duplicate actions; Fault-injection tests for executor success + stale observation, duplicate dispatch, tab replacement, Mac sleep, and relay reconnect

### "Use my private browser accounts and Mac files to answer this, but guarantee that raw private content never leaves my Mac."
- **useful because:** The owner could safely ask the hive to combine logged-in browser data, local files, and the pendant without turning the relay into a copy of their private life. Today authenticated browsing and relay orchestration exist as separate pieces, but there is no enforceable boundary that proves what data crossed it.
- **path:** pendant states the privacy mode in the spoken request and shows a distinct LED state → browser-extension reads the logged-in page locally and reduces it to an approved typed claim, aggregate, or redacted excerpt → mac-planner/mac-terminal combines those local claims with files on the Mac; a local policy engine classifies and blocks raw secrets, credentials, full page text, and screenshots → relay carries only the task, schema-constrained claims, hashes, and completion status; faculty-judgement answers from that projection → faculty-perception can inspect the local audit record and tell the owner exactly which sources contributed without exposing their contents
- **model tier:** Use a cheaper background model locally on the Mac for extraction and redaction; use realtime only if the owner is speaking live. The relay model should receive only the minimized projection.
- **latency:** Local extraction and policy checks in 1–5 seconds; complex multi-page work up to 20 seconds. If a source cannot be safely minimized, stop and report that it was withheld rather than silently sending it.
- **cost:** Approximately $0.005–$0.03 per request; the dominant cost is local or browser-side extraction, with relay inference reduced by smaller context.
- **security:** This must be enforced by code, not a prompt: deny-by-default egress schema, secret/token detection, page-origin and file-path allowlists, cryptographic audit hashes, and a visible privacy-mode indicator. The owner must explicitly opt into any raw excerpt or screenshot leaving the Mac. Logs and cached claims need short retention and encryption.
- **missing:** A local privacy firewall/policy engine intercepting every browser-to-relay and Mac-to-relay payload; Typed claim schemas and deterministic redaction/secret scanning for browser pages and local files; A relay endpoint that rejects unapproved fields rather than merely trusting clients; An owner-visible audit view showing sources, fields released, hashes, and retention expiry; Cross-surface tests proving credentials, cookies, screenshots, and raw DOM cannot escape under retries or failures


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Add a Proof-Carrying Action protocol shared by relay, Mac/browser harnesses, and faculty-perception. Every executable action is serialized as {actionId, idempotencyKey, targetSurface, precondition, effect, postcondition predicate, verification recipe, settleMs, expiry}. Executors return dispatch evidence only. A separate verifier performs the recipe after settle and appends a signed ProofRecord {actionId, observedAt, predicate, result: confirmed|failed|unknown, source, locator/tab/session or file/pipeline reference, evidenceHash}. Relay state transitions are queued→leased→dispatched→verifying→confirmed/failed/unknown, with duplicate dispatch coalesced and stale proofs rejected. Expose GET /jobs/:id/proof and a compact pendant result packet.
- **owner gets:** The owner gets an honest answer about whether something changed in the world, not merely whether our software ran. This is especially useful for reminders, browser form fills, volume/app changes, and queued work after the Mac wakes; it prevents silent false success.
- effort: Medium: typed schema and D1/job state migration, verifier adapters for Mac and browser, relay result packet, and a fault-injection test matrix.  ·  risk: A verifier may observe stale UI or the wrong tab and falsely pass; bind evidence to session/tab identity, freshness, and content hash, and return unknown on ambiguity. Verification can time out after a real-world success, so retain evidence and offer a reconcile action rather than retrying blindly.
- cost: Low relay storage/compute; approximately one extra model call only for ambiguous semantic predicates. Browser screenshots or vision dominate optional cost.  ·  latency: Adds a settle-and-probe delay: typically 1–3 seconds for local state and 3–10 seconds for browser state.
- security: Reduces data exposure by transmitting typed predicates/evidence hashes instead of private page contents. Proof records may still reveal sensitive facts, so encrypt at rest and apply short retention.
- depends on: A typed action/postcondition contract; Independent Mac accessibility/file probes and authenticated browser DOM/session probes; Durable idempotent job runner and result correlation; Owner confirmation gate for irreversible actions

### `relay` — Create an egress-closed Private Data Projection channel. The Mac/browser bridge sends relay requests only through a local policy broker that validates a versioned schema, strips raw DOM/file bodies and credentials, replaces permitted values with typed claims plus keyed hashes, and signs the exact field list. The relay rejects unsigned or out-of-schema payloads. Add an append-only local release ledger with source origin, field names, sensitivity class, recipient, and expiry; retries reuse the same release ID and cannot widen the payload.
- **owner gets:** They can ask the whole hive to work across private accounts and local documents without having to trust that a model prompt remembered not to leak their data. They get useful answers while secrets remain on the Mac, and can inspect what was released afterward.
- effort: High: local broker integrated into both browser and Mac harnesses, schema/redaction library, relay rejection path, signed release ledger, and adversarial retry/failure tests.  ·  risk: Over-redaction may make tasks less useful; return a clear blocked-field explanation and let the owner explicitly authorize a narrowly scoped excerpt. A compromised local agent could still misuse its own access, so keep the broker separate from the planner and require OS keychain-backed signing.
- cost: Low relay cost; modest local CPU and disk for classification, hashing, and ledger writes. No extra model call for deterministic fields; semantic extraction may add one local/background call.  ·  latency: Adds roughly 100–500 ms for policy and signing, plus local extraction time for complex pages/files.
- security: Strongly reduces private-data egress and makes release auditable. It does not protect data already displayed on the Mac or defend against a compromised OS; those limits must be explicit.
- depends on: Browser and Mac bridges exposing source-origin metadata; A versioned sensitivity taxonomy and allowed claim schemas; Relay request authentication and durable request IDs; Owner-facing release-ledger viewer
