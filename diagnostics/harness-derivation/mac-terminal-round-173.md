## Capabilities it proposed

### ""Do that, and don't tell me it worked until you have checked the result." Then carry out a voice-requested task across my Mac and browser, verify the post-condition independently, and tell me exactly what changed (or what did not)."
- **useful because:** Today the system can dispatch actions and report execution, but a successful process is not the same as the owner's goal. This is the single most useful missing behavior: a spoken request becomes a closed loop of intent, execution, independent observation, and honest completion. It can use the authenticated browser session when the Mac alone cannot, and the pendant gets a truthful result rather than a confident guess.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → mac-vision → browser-extension → faculty-perception → faculty-judgement → faculty-action
- **model tier:** Realtime only for the short voice turn and final answer; a cheaper background model performs plan decomposition and evidence comparison. mac-vision/faculty-perception use deterministic checks or a small model before escalating.
- **latency:** Acknowledge within 500 ms; execute within the task's natural duration; post-condition verification within 2 s after the last action, with a spoken progress update if longer than 8 s.
- **cost:** Roughly $0.01-$0.08 per ordinary task, dominated by screenshots/browser evidence and any escalation to the expensive model; trivial deterministic checks cost essentially zero.
- **security:** Evidence may include private browser pages and screen pixels, so keep raw captures on the Mac and send only a redacted claim/diff to relay. Never claim success when verification is unavailable; say 'executed but not verified.' Destructive actions still follow the owner's existing maximum-access policy, but the system must report their actual post-state and preserve a recovery hint.
- **missing:** A post-condition DSL that can express checks such as file exists with hash, app state, browser text/URL, or visible UI state; A correlation envelope connecting voice turn, /execute job, browser command, and evidence snapshot; A Mac-side verifier that runs after actions and returns evidence without asking the expensive model to reread the whole screen; A failure response that distinguishes action failure, verification failure, and ambiguous evidence

### ""Use the pendant over USB right now, even though it has no LTE, and read me the result.""
- **useful because:** The nRF9160 and ESP32 are physically attached to this Mac today, while LTE registration is absent. A USB-first local mode would make the worn device genuinely useful now: button/audio requests can reach the Mac with low latency, and Mac/browser work can return to the pendant without pretending the relay is reachable. It also becomes the field diagnostic path before trusting cellular transport.
- **path:** pendant → mac-planner → mac-terminal → relay-realtime → browser-extension → faculty-perception
- **model tier:** Use a deterministic local serial broker and a cheap parser for framing; reserve realtime model inference for the actual spoken request. No cloud model is needed for link negotiation, buffering, or acknowledgements.
- **latency:** USB discovery and handshake under 1 s; button-to-Mac dispatch under 300 ms; audio control/status acknowledgement under 500 ms. On disconnect, switch to offline behavior within 1 s and resume queued work after a verified reconnect.
- **cost:** Negligible API cost after setup; approximately 1-2 engineering days for serial framing/bridge integration. Existing hardware and USB cables incur no additional cost.
- **security:** Only accept serial devices matching the known VID/PID/serial patterns and bind the bridge to the local authenticated agent. Keep audio and command payloads local unless the owner explicitly invokes cloud inference. Use per-connection nonce and monotonically increasing sequence numbers to reject stale or replayed frames.
- **missing:** A real serial transport implementation for /dev/cu.usbmodem00096003658* and /dev/cu.usbserial-0287A9CA; A local Mac bridge session that maps pendant frames to /pipeline/audio and /pipeline/events and returns acknowledgements; A framed protocol with CRC, sequence, turn/job IDs, reconnect replay, and capability negotiation; A Mac-side device health/status route exposing USB link, firmware identity, and queue depth

### ""Pause this; when I come back, continue from exactly where you stopped and remind me what is still unfinished.""
- **useful because:** Long Mac/browser tasks currently have no owner-visible, durable notion of a safe pause point. A resumable handoff would let the owner stop speaking, close the laptop, or lose USB without restarting or duplicating side effects. The pendant can capture the interruption, the relay can retain the task envelope, and the Mac/browser can resume only from a verified checkpoint.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → mac-vision → faculty-judgement → faculty-action
- **model tier:** A cheap background model summarizes checkpoint state and unfinished intent; deterministic code owns checkpointing, idempotency, and resume. Realtime is used only to acknowledge pause/resume and answer the owner's short follow-up.
- **latency:** Pause acknowledgement under 500 ms when connected; checkpoint durable within 2 s. Resume should begin within 3 s of reconnect, after a quick state comparison; if state diverged, ask one concise clarification instead of replaying blindly.
- **cost:** About $0.005-$0.03 per checkpointed task, mostly summary/evidence tokens; local state comparison and persistence are free.
- **security:** Checkpoint records may contain private URLs, filenames, and snippets. Store raw state locally, encrypt or redact relay summaries, and expire abandoned checkpoints. Resume must reject stale job/ledger IDs and require explicit confirmation only where the owner's existing policy says the action itself warrants it—not as a new global gate.
- **missing:** A durable task envelope linking pendant turn ID, Mac job ID, browser command IDs, and ledger step keys; Checkpoint snapshots containing completed side effects, observed post-state, and the next action, written before interruption; A resume endpoint that compares current Mac/browser state to the checkpoint and emits only uncompleted, idempotent steps; Pendant UI/audio wording for paused, resumable, diverged, and expired states

### ""What changed on my computer and in my browser while I was away, and which of those changes were caused by you?""
- **useful because:** Today activity is fragmented across Mac jobs, browser commands, relay turns, and physical pendant events. The owner cannot obtain a trustworthy, causal after-the-fact account: a log may show that something ran, but not what the screen/account state was before and after, whether the owner or an agent caused it, or which events belong to the same request. A cross-surface change ledger would turn unexplained drift into a concise, evidence-backed answer.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → mac-terminal → mac-vision → browser-extension → faculty-perception → faculty-judgement
- **model tier:** Collect and correlate events deterministically; use a cheaper background model to summarize the causal timeline. Escalate to realtime only for the owner's spoken question and final concise explanation.
- **latency:** Recording is asynchronous and should add under 20 ms to an action. A normal 'while I was away' answer should arrive in 2-5 seconds; large histories can be summarized in the background with a progress indication.
- **cost:** Approximately $0.002-$0.03 per query, dominated by summarizing selected diffs rather than replaying raw screenshots. Local event capture and hashing have no API cost.
- **security:** The ledger could expose sensitive URLs, document names, and screen content. Keep raw snapshots on the Mac, encrypt retention, redact secrets and page bodies by default, and return only the minimum evidence needed. The owner must be able to erase a time range. Clearly distinguish owner-originated, agent-originated, browser-extension-originated, and unknown changes.
- **missing:** A durable cross-surface event schema with actor, causal parent, monotonic sequence, wall time, surface, before-state digest, after-state digest, and evidence pointers; Mac filesystem/app/window observers and browser DOM/URL/storage change observers that produce bounded diffs rather than unrestricted screenshots; A relay/pendant turn correlation mechanism that stamps physical button events and spoken requests onto the same causal chain; A query service that reconstructs a causal timeline, detects unexplained changes, and cites local evidence without uploading raw private state

### ""Look in the sites I am already signed into, compare the relevant information, and tell me the answer without sending my page or credentials to the cloud.""
- **useful because:** The browser is the only node that physically holds authenticated sessions, but the voice agent cannot safely turn those sessions into a private, cited answer. This would let the owner ask about current account-specific information while keeping page contents and credentials on the Mac. It is more useful than a generic browser action because it returns a comparison with source locations and explicitly reports when the local session cannot answer.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → faculty-perception → faculty-judgement
- **model tier:** Run DOM extraction, redaction, comparison, and citation generation locally with a small model; use realtime only to interpret the short question and speak the result. Cloud receives intent and a minimal redacted answer, never raw authenticated page content.
- **latency:** Answer within 5 seconds for already-open pages; up to 15 seconds when several tabs need inspection. Tell the owner immediately which sites are being checked without exposing their contents.
- **cost:** Near-zero cloud cost for local extraction; roughly $0.001-$0.02 for intent parsing and speech-turn handling. Engineering cost is the dominant expense.
- **security:** Authenticated content remains local and is never included in relay prompts. Apply field-level redaction for tokens, payment data, and personal identifiers; cite a local tab title/URL and timestamp rather than uploading excerpts. The owner can require a spoken confirmation before any navigation that changes state, while read-only queries remain hands-free.
- **missing:** A browser-extension command for local structured extraction and field-level redaction, distinct from screenshot inspection; A local comparison worker that can join results from multiple authenticated tabs and attach citations; A privacy contract in the relay protocol carrying only intent, redacted answer, and citation metadata; A policy that marks navigation, downloads, and form submission as mutations so a question cannot silently change the session


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `mac-harness` — Add a provenance-preserving shell execution record: before dispatch, store the planner-authored action and the exact rewritten action side by side; execute via a child-process API that records pid, start/finish monotonic timestamps, exit code/signal, cwd, timeout actually used, and a redacted environment fingerprint. On failure, retain bounded raw stderr/stdout plus a machine-readable failure class, and attach the receipt to the parent job and ledger step. Do not gate or restrict commands; this is evidence and recovery only.
- **owner gets:** When the Mac says a task failed, the owner can know whether the command never started, timed out, was killed, or ran and returned an error—and whether a planner rewrite changed what was executed. That makes spoken follow-up ('retry the same thing' or 'undo what changed') reliable instead of guesswork.
- effort: Medium: replace exec with an argv-capable spawn path where possible while retaining arbitrary shell compatibility, add receipt fields, redaction, and tests for rewrite/timeout/exit-signal cases; then join receipt IDs to job and ledger records.  ·  risk: Changing process invocation can alter shell quoting and working-directory behavior. Preserve a compatibility mode and compare command output in tests. Redaction could hide useful diagnostics, so retain hashes/variable names and local-only raw data with bounded size.
- cost: No model/API cost; modest local disk growth, capped per receipt. Engineering effort several days.  ·  latency: Negligible (<10 ms bookkeeping); spawn startup may differ slightly from exec.
- security: Improves auditability without adding approval gates. Never persist secret environment values; store only names plus stable hashes or a sensitivity class.
- depends on: Close the orchestrator ledger on every terminal path and populate planMeta.jobId so receipts can be joined to jobs.; Make the configured SHELL_TIMEOUT_MS effective or record the hard-coded effective timeout explicitly.; Pass abort signals to the child process so the recorded cancellation state matches reality.

### `context` — Introduce signed, content-addressed evidence capsules at every surface boundary. A Mac or browser observer emits a small local capsule containing the claim, relevant diff, source locator, timestamp, confidence, and hash of retained raw evidence; relay and models exchange capsule IDs and summaries instead of resending full screenshots, logs, or page text. On request, the Mac materializes the raw evidence locally for inspection. Capsules expire and can be revoked independently.
- **owner gets:** The owner gets quicker, cheaper, more consistent answers about what the system saw and did, without repeatedly shipping private screen contents or making the model reread entire histories. When an answer is disputed, it can point to concrete local evidence rather than an opaque assertion.
- effort: Medium-to-large protocol and harness change across Mac, browser extension, relay, and faculty perception; requires canonical schemas, signing, local retention, and adapters for existing jobs and inspections.  ·  risk: A bad summarizer could omit the one detail that matters, and hashes do not prove semantic correctness. Preserve raw evidence locally, include confidence and coverage, and fall back to a fresh inspection when a capsule is stale, incomplete, or challenged.
- cost: Reduces repeated context-token spend substantially; small local storage overhead for hashes and bounded diffs. No new hardware cost.  ·  latency: Usually lowers response latency by avoiding large uploads; first capture may add tens of milliseconds.
- security: Strongly improves data minimization, but signing keys and local evidence stores become sensitive. Rotate keys, scope access per session, and make expiration/erasure enforceable.
- depends on: A cross-surface actor and causal-parent identity so capsules cannot be confused across jobs or browser sessions.; Local browser structured extraction and Mac post-state observation.; A relay protocol that accepts capsule metadata and fetches raw evidence only through an authenticated local request.

This round produced three owner-facing capabilities and one concrete Mac-harness change: (1) closed-loop action verification that never claims success without an independently observed post-condition, (2) USB-first pendant operation over the physically connected nRF9160/ESP32 despite absent LTE, and (3) durable pause/resume across pendant, relay, Mac, and authenticated browser; plus provenance-preserving shell receipts with exit code, signal, pid, timing, effective timeout, rewrite history, and job/ledger joins. The live Mac agent health endpoint is healthy (v0.5.0).

**Biggest unknown:** The granted Mac diagnostic schemas still do not resolve in the live inventory, and the already-queued mac_usb_serial_diagnostics request remains unavailable. I still need a real, read-only serial inventory/health path (device identity, baud/ framing, firmware, link state, queue depth) before USB-first mode can be validated end to end. The proposed verifier/checkpoint/serial broker pieces also do not yet exist; current routes can be wired together, but they do not supply those missing post-condition and durable-envelope semantics.
