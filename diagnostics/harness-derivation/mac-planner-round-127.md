## Capabilities it proposed

### "When I ask “what am I on the hook for today?”, gather my Calendar and Mail plus the logged-in pages I have open, identify commitments, deadlines, and conflicts, and leave me a sourced review board with a short spoken summary and suggested next actions—never send, submit, or reschedule anything."
- **useful because:** This turns scattered obligations into one actionable view: a meeting promise in Mail, a due date in a private portal, and a calendar collision are linked as one item rather than three separate alerts. The Mac leaves an auditable board the owner can review later, while the pendant gives only the highest-priority exceptions.
- **path:** relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → unified → faculty-perception → faculty-judgement → faculty-action
- **model tier:** Use a cheaper background model for extraction, deduplication, deadline/conflict reasoning, and board generation; reserve realtime only for the spoken request and a brief answer. Use no realtime context replay for the overnight/background work.
- **latency:** Spoken acknowledgement under 1 second; initial answer in 10–20 seconds when browser is healthy. If private tabs are unavailable, say so immediately and complete Calendar/Mail-only results rather than hanging. Background board can finish asynchronously.
- **cost:** One background text invocation per request plus bounded authenticated-page extraction; dominant cost is browser page reads and context, not the short realtime acknowledgement. TTS is a few seconds of generated PCM and should be cached with the board.
- **security:** Calendar/Mail and logged-in page contents leave the Mac only as scoped extracts; redact message bodies by default and retain source URLs, timestamps, and snippets rather than whole pages. Never submit forms or send mail. Browser session IDs and tab URLs must be kept out of the pendant transcript; require explicit owner action later for any mutation.
- **missing:** A durable cross-source commitment schema (source, evidence snippet, due timestamp, owner, confidence, status, conflict links) and deduplication pass; The durable browser job runner and resumable result stream for private-page reads; current router/backends exist but jobs do not survive restart; A review-board writer that atomically creates/updates one dated folder with a brief, evidence manifest, and draft suggestions; A healthy authenticated browser tab/heartbeat; current browser status is online but has no active tab and nine pending commands

### "When I miss or am about to miss a commitment, tell me what is at risk, find the smallest set of recovery options across my Calendar, Mail, and logged-in work pages, and prepare a choice card on my Mac with draft messages and proposed times—then let me choose one from the pendant. Never send, submit, or reschedule without my explicit choice."
- **useful because:** Today the system can summarize sources, but it cannot connect a slipping commitment to concrete, evidence-backed recovery choices. This would turn “I’m late” into a calm decision: what can be moved, who needs a draft notice, what deadline is hard, and which option causes the least downstream damage.
- **path:** relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → unified → faculty-perception → faculty-judgement → faculty-action
- **model tier:** Use a background model for monitoring, commitment extraction, dependency analysis, and generating alternatives. Use the realtime tier only for the pendant’s brief alert and the owner’s selection. Use a cheap deterministic rules layer for time arithmetic, conflict checks, and redaction.
- **latency:** A risk alert should reach the pendant within 30 seconds of a detected change or missed start. A choice card should appear on the Mac within 1–2 minutes; selecting an option should acknowledge in under 1 second and queue the selected plan for explicit execution.
- **cost:** Periodic checks are mostly deterministic and low-cost; one background model call only when a commitment changes or crosses a risk threshold. Browser extraction and authenticated session availability dominate cost and latency; spoken alerts are short cached audio.
- **security:** Private calendar, mail, and portal evidence must remain scoped to the relevant commitment, with source snippets and timestamps rather than whole-page retention. Drafts must be visibly marked unsent. No browser form submission, email send, or calendar mutation may happen from the alert alone; the chosen option must produce a human-readable before/after plan and a second explicit execution request.
- **missing:** A commitment lifecycle model with planned, at-risk, missed, recovered, and dismissed states, including evidence and dependency edges; A cross-surface change detector that compares Calendar/Mail/browser observations over time instead of only answering on demand; A choice-card renderer and pendant selection protocol that carries an opaque option ID back to the Mac without replaying private content; A transactional action bundle that can execute a selected multi-app recovery plan and return per-step receipts or restore the prior draft state


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Add a cross-surface browser-queue reconciler in the Mac planner: correlate each pending browser command with its local jobId, journal/receipt, tab/session lease, and last heartbeat; classify it as runnable, duplicate, orphaned, or stalled. A command with no live tab, an expired lease, or a high attempt count must be quarantined (not replayed), emit one durable diagnostic event, and expose a resume/ discard operation. Reconcile after every browser heartbeat and before any new browser action, then surface a compact Mac review item and, when appropriate, a pendant alert. Keep the existing owner policy of maximum access: this is observability and replay prevention, not an approval gate.
- **owner gets:** The owner stops getting silent duplicate clicks or 45-second hangs after Safari/Chrome closes. They receive one clear explanation—what was attempted, whether it changed anything, and whether it can safely resume—instead of the system repeatedly acting on a stale private tab.
- effort: Medium: a small persistent ledger and state machine in local-agent, adapters for /browser/status, /browser/poll, /browser/result/:commandId, /jobs/:jobId/receipts, and journal events, plus dashboard/briefing presentation and tests for restart/heartbeat races.  ·  risk: Incorrect classification could quarantine a valid long-running read or discard a command whose result is merely delayed. Preserve the raw command and receipt, use lease expiry plus attempt/time thresholds rather than attempts alone, and make resume/discard explicit operations. Recovery is replay from the preserved command only after a fresh tab/session binding.
- cost: Negligible API cost; one lightweight ledger write per heartbeat/command and bounded diagnostic context. No extra model call unless generating the human summary.  ·  latency: Adds tens of milliseconds before a browser action; removes the current worst-case 45-second blocking wait for commands that can never complete.
- security: Improves privacy by preventing stale authenticated commands from replaying into a different tab. Store opaque session/tab IDs and hashes, not page contents; redact URLs in pendant alerts.
- depends on: chg-14accc01's request IDs/idempotency and resumable polling (currently missing progress/resume implementation); chg-16bc5dee's durable browser job runner (router exists, persistence/retry/result stream still missing); A browser heartbeat that reports active tab/session identity rather than online=true with tabId=null

### `integration` — Add a commitment-risk and recovery transaction layer that consumes timestamped Calendar/Mail/browser observations, maintains a state machine (planned → at-risk → missed → recovery-pending → chosen → executed), and emits an option envelope rather than an action. Each envelope contains 2–3 ranked recovery plans with evidence IDs, affected apps, exact proposed changes, and an opaque selection token. The pendant can select an option; the Mac then materializes drafts/previews, executes only the selected reversible bundle, and records per-step receipts plus a compensating plan.
- **owner gets:** The owner gets an immediate, concrete way out of a missed obligation instead of another passive summary: choose the least damaging recovery plan from the pendant, with the Mac preparing the exact drafts and schedule changes for review.
- effort: High: new durable state and evidence schema, cross-source diffing, option ranking, pendant option-selection messages, transactional multi-app planner, and receipt/compensation handling.  ·  risk: A false positive could cause stressful alerts or propose an inappropriate message. Require confidence and quiet hours, suppress duplicate alerts, preserve drafts only, and make every mutation a separate explicit execution step. If execution partially fails, leave a discrepancy card with receipts rather than guessing.
- cost: Low-to-moderate background model cost only on state transitions; storage and deterministic conflict analysis dominate routine operation. No additional realtime model call beyond the short alert/selection exchange.  ·  latency: Detection depends on source polling, but once an at-risk transition is observed, option generation should complete in 1–2 minutes; pendant acknowledgement remains sub-second.
- security: The option envelope sent to the pendant contains opaque IDs and short redacted labels, never private message bodies or portal content. Evidence and drafts remain on the Mac/relay under existing authenticated scope; execution logs record app and field changes.
- depends on: A durable commitment lifecycle and cross-source observation diff store; A pendant protocol for presenting/selecting opaque option IDs; A transactional multi-app action bundle with per-step receipts and compensating recovery; Reliable authenticated browser observation with tab/session continuity
