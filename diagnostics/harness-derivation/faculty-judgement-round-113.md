## Capabilities it proposed

### "“I’m in the middle of something—hold this thought, work out what’s needed, and bring me back to the exact next decision when I’m free.”"
- **useful because:** Today an interruption destroys the thread: the pendant hears the intent, but the Mac/browser later lack the owner’s original reason, uncertainty, and stopping point. This would turn a spoken half-thought into a durable, cross-surface handoff. The pendant captures the intent and constraints locally; the relay preserves it while disconnected; the Mac and authenticated browser gather evidence or prepare reversible work; and the pendant later presents one short, sourced decision slate rather than replaying a long job result. It is specifically useful during commutes, meetings, and context switches, where no single surface can both hear the thought and finish the research.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use the realtime tier only to capture/clarify the owner’s intent and to deliver the eventual one-sentence re-entry prompt. Use a cheaper background model for evidence gathering, summarization, and uncertainty labeling on the relay/Mac; use deterministic code for state transitions, expiry, and approval gates.
- **latency:** Capture and acknowledgement under 1 second when online, with an offline pendant marker immediately. Background handoff work may take minutes. On re-entry, deliver a 5–10 second spoken slate: what changed, what is blocked, and the single decision or next reversible action. If evidence is stale or identity/session changed, pause and say so rather than guessing.
- **cost:** Roughly $0.01–$0.08 per handoff depending on browser/Mac research length; most cost is background model context and authenticated page extraction, not the short realtime utterances. Persist compact intent/evidence references rather than replaying full transcripts each turn.
- **security:** Private browser content and local files leave their respective surfaces only as bounded excerpts and hashes; do not copy secrets into the pendant or relay. Require explicit confirmation for sending, deleting, purchasing, or other irreversible actions. Display source, timestamp, and what changed before approval; expire abandoned handoffs and provide delete-all. Treat a resumed voice phrase as intent clarification, never as approval by itself.
- **missing:** A first-class cross-surface handoff object containing original utterance, intended outcome, constraints, uncertainty, stopping point, evidence references, owner-visible expiry, and current approval state.; A re-entry arbiter that chooses a quiet delivery window and suppresses duplicate prompts across pendant, Mac, and dashboard.; A typed decision-slate payload with cited evidence and a strict distinction between prepared/reversible and approved/irreversible actions.; An interruption-aware event path from the pendant that survives dropped links and reconnects to the same handoff ID.

### "“Before you use my Mac or logged-in browser for this, tell me exactly what private information would leave my devices, remove anything unnecessary, and let me approve the smallest safe version.”"
- **useful because:** The owner can currently approve an action, but not inspect or constrain the information flow that makes it possible. This gives them a practical privacy boundary across the hive: the pendant states the proposed data footprint in one short sentence, the Mac/browser perform local minimization, and the relay records only a redacted proof of what was shared. It is different from an action receipt or an irreversible-action confirmation: the decision is about disclosure before research or execution begins, including disclosures to the model itself.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use deterministic classifiers and field-level redaction for known secrets, contacts, tokens, and private-page regions. Use a cheaper background model to explain the remaining data footprint. Use realtime only for the short spoken disclosure summary and the owner’s approve/edit/deny response.
- **latency:** For ordinary requests, compute a disclosure preview in under 2 seconds and speak it in one sentence. If classification is incomplete, pause and label the unknown rather than silently transmitting. Redaction can add seconds to a browser or Mac job, but must happen before any model or remote service receives the content.
- **cost:** Usually under $0.01 per request for local deterministic scanning; $0.01–$0.05 when an explanation model is needed. The dominant cost is not the scan but any subsequent browser/Mac extraction, which should be reduced by sending minimized excerpts instead of full pages.
- **security:** This is itself security-sensitive: the preview must be generated locally where possible and must not echo secrets into relay logs or pendant audio. Maintain an append-only disclosure manifest with hashes, categories, destination, and approval—not raw data. Require fresh approval if the destination, data category, target account, or action hash changes. Deny by default when a token, password, financial identifier, or unrelated person’s private content cannot be reliably removed.
- **missing:** A preflight data-flow manifest spanning relay, model, Mac, and browser boundaries, with field-level redaction and destination labels.; Local secret/PII classification on the Mac and browser extension, plus a way to return redacted excerpts and proof hashes rather than raw captures.; A disclosure approval token separate from action approval, bound to the exact minimized payload and destination.; Dashboard and pendant rendering for a compact privacy preview, including an explicit unknown/blocked state.


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Add a durable Handoff Envelope and Re-entry Arbiter between the existing event/capture, job, browser, and execution paths. On a pendant interruption, create one handoffId with the raw intent reference, normalized goal, constraints, uncertainty, stopping point, owner timezone/quiet hours, and expiry. Every downstream job, browser extraction, and prepared action must attach that handoffId and append typed evidence (source, timestamp, freshness, confidence) rather than returning an unlinked result. On reconnect or a chosen quiet window, the arbiter compiles a 3-field decision slate—what changed, what is blocked, next reversible step—and routes exactly one delivery to the pendant or Mac. Approval is a separate state transition; execution receipts and undo references close the same handoff. Expire or cancel must fan out idempotently to jobs and queued audio.
- **owner gets:** The owner can say “hold this thought” once and later resume at the exact decision, even if they leave the Mac, lose the browser bridge, or cross an offline interval. They stop getting orphaned job completions and repeated reminders, and never mistake prepared work for approved action.
- effort: Medium-high: schema and event migration, idempotent fan-out/cancellation, one arbiter worker, and pendant reconnect handling; can ship read-only decision slates before enabling action execution.  ·  risk: A stale or wrongly merged handoff could prompt the owner about the wrong task. Mitigate with explicit handoff IDs, source/timestamp display, confidence thresholds, owner confirmation on ambiguous re-entry, expiry, and a global cancel. If a downstream surface disappears, preserve the handoff as blocked rather than silently completing it.
- cost: Small durable storage and queue overhead; approximately one compact background-model call per handoff plus existing browser/Mac costs. No need to resend full transcripts.  ·  latency: Sub-second acknowledgement; background processing is asynchronous. Re-entry compilation should be under 2 seconds from fresh evidence, with stale evidence clearly marked.
- security: Keep raw private page text on the owning surface where possible; relay stores references, hashes, and minimal excerpts. Never place secrets in pendant audio. Approval tokens are single-use, scoped to handoffId and action hash.
- depends on: A typed cross-surface event schema with correlation IDs; A durable job cancellation/expiry primitive; A decision-slate and approval-state representation; A pendant reconnect/offline marker path

### `integration` — Insert a Disclosure Preflight stage before /plan, browser extraction, model invocation, or /execute. It should build a typed manifest of every proposed input and destination (pendant, relay, model tier, Mac app, browser origin, external recipient), run local redaction on the Mac/extension, replace raw values with minimized excerpts or salted proof hashes, and issue a single-use disclosure token bound to the exact payload hash, destination set, and expiry. Downstream routes must reject requests without a matching token; any changed tab, target, field, or model destination invalidates it. Store only the manifest and approval decision in receipts, never the unredacted payload.
- **owner gets:** The owner gets a real, enforceable boundary instead of a reassuring prompt: private account pages, unrelated messages, credentials, and personal files cannot silently become model context just because a task crossed surfaces. They can approve a useful minimized request without choosing between total automation and total refusal.
- effort: High: define the manifest schema, implement local scanners/redactors in the Mac and browser bridge, enforce token checks in relay routes, add safe failure behavior, and add compact pendant/dashboard rendering. Start with known credential/PII classes and expand classifiers after telemetry proves low false-negative rates.  ·  risk: Over-redaction can make tasks fail or produce misleading results; under-redaction is a privacy breach. Fail closed for secrets and unknown destinations, show what was omitted, and allow the owner to explicitly widen scope by category—not by blindly forwarding the original page. Recovery is to re-run preflight after edits; never fall back to raw capture.
- cost: Low per-request compute for local scanning; modest storage for manifests and hashes. It may reduce model token cost by shrinking context, while adding engineering and maintenance cost for classifiers.  ·  latency: Adds roughly 0.5–2 seconds for local preflight; remote/model work starts only after approval. Cached classification for unchanged pages can make repeat requests near-instant.
- security: Strongly improves least-privilege and auditability, but the classifier and token verifier become security-critical. Keep manifests redacted, isolate keys, rotate token signing keys, and ensure logs cannot reconstruct sensitive values.
- depends on: A shared typed cross-surface payload manifest and destination taxonomy; Mac/browser local redaction and proof-hash services; Relay enforcement of scoped disclosure tokens; Owner-visible privacy preview and approval UI/audio
