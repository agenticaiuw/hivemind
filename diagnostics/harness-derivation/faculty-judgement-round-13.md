## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Only interrupt me when it is genuinely worth breaking my concentration; otherwise queue it and catch me at the next natural pause.”"
- **useful because:** The owner currently has separate voice, Mac, browser, and scheduled workflows, but no shared decision about when an event deserves an interruption. This would make the pendant feel considerate rather than noisy: it combines calendar state, active Mac/browser work, urgency and deadlines, then either speaks now, queues a compact card/audio item, or suppresses it with an explainable reason. At the next pause it can summarize what accumulated, without losing the original source or actionability.
- **path:** relay-realtime → pendant → mac-planner → browser-extension → unified → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use a cheap background classifier/rules engine for event scoring and queue maintenance; use gpt-5.6-luna only to summarize a grouped queue or resolve ambiguous priority; reserve gpt-realtime-2.1 for the owner's live request to interrupt, defer, or review. The pendant should enforce a local emergency/voice override when disconnected.
- **latency:** Urgent events should score in under 2 seconds; ordinary events can wait 30–60 seconds for batching. A spoken review should begin within 500 ms after the owner asks. Context should be compact (event, source, deadline, current activity, confidence), not a full transcript.
- **cost:** Usually <$0.01 per day if scoring is rules plus a small model only for ambiguous items; a review costs roughly $0.01–$0.05 depending on queue size. The dominant cost is summarization, not event ingestion.
- **security:** Calendar titles, browser page metadata, notifications and active-window state leave the Mac only as normalized event records; raw page text and notification bodies should stay local unless explicitly needed. Never auto-dismiss, send, submit, or alter a reminder. Require confirmation before suppressing an item permanently; show source, score factors, expiry, and an undo path. Treat secrets and message content as sensitive.
- **missing:** A cross-surface interruption policy and durable event queue with urgency/deadline/expiry fields; Mac hooks for notification and active-window/context signals, plus browser tab activity summaries; Pendant-side queue/override protocol that works offline and survives a dropped relay link; A dashboard showing queued, deferred, suppressed, and expired items with one-tap restore; Owner interruption preferences (already requested upstream) must be applied once available

### "“When I double-press the pendant, make the whole system private until I say otherwise.”"
- **useful because:** The owner cannot currently establish a trustworthy, immediate privacy boundary across a live voice session, Mac automation, authenticated browser tabs, and queued background work. A physical gesture would give them a simple social-space control: stop listening, stop screen/page observation, pause outgoing jobs and notifications, and prevent sensitive queued material from being spoken or shown until explicitly resumed. The owner should receive one discreet confirmation (LED/haptic), not an explanation that could itself disclose private content.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → mac-vision → browser-extension → unified → dashboard
- **model tier:** No expensive model is needed for entering or leaving private mode. Firmware and deterministic relay/Mac/browser policy enforcement do the work; gpt-realtime is used only if the owner later asks what was paused, and a cheaper background model can summarize the paused queue after explicit resume.
- **latency:** The pendant must enter local mute/privacy state in under 100 ms, with relay audio cut and Mac/browser observation paused within 1 second. Resume should reconcile state in the background without replaying anything automatically.
- **cost:** Negligible API cost during privacy transitions; a later explicit queue summary is roughly $0.01–$0.05. Engineering cost dominates: synchronized state, interruption of in-flight jobs, and safe recovery after power or network loss.
- **security:** Privacy mode must be fail-closed: mute microphone locally, revoke/disable capture streams, stop new browser DOM/screenshot reads, pause Mac actions and relay pushes, and mark queued envelopes as withheld. Do not rely on a model to honor it. Persist only a signed mode transition and timestamp; encrypt withheld metadata, expire it, and give the owner a visible dashboard count without revealing titles. Require an explicit physical gesture or spoken confirmation to resume; never resume merely because the network reconnects.
- **missing:** A pendant firmware privacy latch with physical confirmation and persistent fail-closed state; A signed, monotonic privacy-mode signal understood by relay, Mac agent, vision loop, and browser bridge; Cancellation/pause semantics for in-flight jobs, with safe checkpoints and idempotent resume rather than replay; Capture-stream revocation hooks and an audit trail showing which surfaces actually acknowledged privacy mode; A dashboard control to inspect and discard withheld work after resume


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `interaction` — Introduce a signed Interruption Envelope protocol shared by relay, Mac, browser, and pendant. Every candidate interruption carries source, event time, deadline, confidence, sensitivity, urgency rationale, expiry, recommended channel, and an idempotency key. The relay groups envelopes; the Mac contributes focus/activity signals; the browser contributes authenticated-page deltas; the pendant locally applies the last-known policy and stores a bounded queue. Each spoken alert includes a short reason (“urgent because…”), and every defer/suppress/restore action is reversible and auditable.
- **owner gets:** The owner gets one predictable behavior across all surfaces instead of duplicate alerts, stale reminders, or a browser change spoken at the wrong moment. If connectivity drops, the pendant still behaves safely; when it reconnects, queued events reconcile rather than replaying duplicates.
- effort: Medium-high: define the envelope schema and policy evaluator, add Mac/browser adapters, implement relay persistence and reconciliation, then add a small pendant queue and dashboard controls. Roll out in observe-only mode before enabling suppression.  ·  risk: A bad score could hide something important or interrupt at a bad time. Start with no suppression, only recommendations; require explicit opt-in to suppress, maintain an emergency allowlist, expire stale envelopes, and provide a physical pendant override plus dashboard restore.
- cost: Low recurring API cost: mostly local rules and durable metadata; occasional summarization is <$0.05 per review. Storage is small (envelopes and hashes, not raw content).  ·  latency: Adds under 2 seconds to event scoring and avoids repeated model calls. Spoken review remains realtime; background grouping is asynchronous.
- security: Use device-bound signing and least-privilege source adapters. Keep raw notification/page content on the Mac by default; relay receives redacted envelopes. Encrypt queued sensitive envelopes, enforce TTLs, and log policy decisions without logging secrets.
- depends on: Cross-surface durable event/job persistence primitives; Mac notification and focus/activity observation; Authenticated browser delta extraction; Pendant offline queue and reconnect reconciliation; Owner interruption preferences

### `firmware` — Add a hardware-backed privacy latch to the pendant: a deliberate double-press enters a retained PRIVATE state, disables microphone/audio capture and radio transmission at the device boundary, emits only a nonverbal LED/haptic acknowledgement, and signs a monotonic state transition. The relay, Mac agent, vision loop, and browser bridge must treat that transition as a revocation, canceling or checkpointing work and refusing new capture until an explicit physical resume gesture.
- **owner gets:** In a meeting, public place, or unexpected visitor situation, the owner can know—without looking at a screen or trusting an AI response—that every surface has stopped observing and speaking. Privacy becomes a physical fact with a clear recovery path, not a preference buried in prompts.
- effort: High: firmware state machine and secure persistence, relay revocation propagation, Mac/browser capture hooks, cancellation checkpoints, acknowledgement monitoring, reconnect reconciliation, and dashboard discard/review controls. Test power loss, packet duplication, clock skew, and an already-running browser action.  ·  risk: If the latch is too easy to trigger it may interrupt useful work; if resume is automatic it can violate privacy. Use a deliberate gesture, fail closed on ambiguous state, make resume physical and explicit, and show a local indicator. Any in-flight irreversible action must be stopped before submission or require fresh confirmation.
- cost: Near-zero per-use API cost. Firmware uses a few kilobytes of flash and under 8 kB RAM for state, signing buffers, and acknowledgement tracking; engineering and integration are the main cost.  ·  latency: Local mute is sub-100 ms; cross-surface revocation target is under 1 second. Paused work may take longer after resume because it must revalidate state rather than replay stale actions.
- security: Strongly positive if microphone/radio gating is below the application layer and transitions are signed. Avoid storing raw content; retain only state, timestamp/counter, acknowledgements, and discard decisions. Treat missing acknowledgements as private, not successful.
- depends on: A physical pendant button/gesture path and nonverbal indicator; Relay-to-Mac/browser revocation channel; Cooperative cancellation/checkpoint support in long-running jobs; Capture permission hooks on Mac and authenticated browser bridge; Durable privacy-state audit and discard semantics


## What it asked for

### `s2-1y2n` (skill) — local_interruption_gate
- does: Maintains a small encrypted queue of signed interruption envelopes and applies the last-known owner policy locally. It can immediately allow emergency/allowlisted alerts, defer ordinary alerts, suppress expired duplicates, blink the LED for queued attention, and let a button press request the queue summary or override the current gate. On reconnect it uploads dispositions and reconciles envelopes idempotently.
- must be on-device because: The pendant must remain considerate when LTE or the relay is unavailable, and emergency/override behavior must not depend on a round trip. Local storage also avoids sending raw notification content to the relay; the server only needs signed, redacted envelope metadata and dispositions.
- trigger: Relay push or local audio/event arrival; button press for review/override; reconnect event for reconciliation; optional scheduled quiet-hours tick.
- storage: Encrypted flash ring buffer of 32–64 envelopes plus disposition records and one policy blob; roughly 12–24 kB depending on signatures and metadata. Drop expired low-priority items first and never overwrite unacknowledged emergency items.
- RAM budget: Target 10–16 kB peak: fixed-size envelope pool (8–12 kB), policy evaluator (2 kB), crypto buffers (2 kB), and queue/reconnect bookkeeping (2 kB). Stay below 24 kB, leaving ample headroom within the 211,608 B application RAM already shared with audio, modem buffers, and Zephyr.

### `c4-2wst` (context) — notification and focus signal inventory
- why: The proposed interruption gate needs to know which Mac notification sources, focus modes, calendar state, and browser activity signals actually exist before its policy can be safe.
- would change: I would constrain the proposal to available signals, identify the minimum Mac/browser adapters, and avoid claiming suppression or emergency classification is implementable until the source coverage and permissions are known.
