## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Is the thing I asked for actually done, or is it only queued/blocked/offline?”"
- **useful because:** Today the system can report a job receipt or a pipeline event, but those can disagree: a Mac plan may be waiting for approval while TTS was already uploaded, browser commands can be pending while the extension is offline, and UI actions can claim success while Accessibility makes them no-ops. This gives the owner one spoken, evidence-backed reality answer that explicitly separates observed, stale, inferred, and unverified state instead of confidently declaring completion.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Cheap background/text model for reconciliation; realtime only when the owner asks from the pendant and needs a short spoken answer. Deterministic freshness/state rules should do most of the work, with the model verbalizing the resulting evidence table.
- **latency:** On-demand response in 1–3 seconds from cached observations; up to 8 seconds if it must refresh Mac ops/pipeline and browser heartbeat state. Never blocks or mutates the underlying job.
- **cost:** Roughly $0.001–$0.01 per check depending on whether a text-model reconciliation call is needed; most checks should be deterministic and cost-free. Dominant cost is re-sending evidence context, not tool calls.
- **security:** Read-only metadata may include URLs, app names, job text, and private-page titles; redact secrets and page contents by default and show source scope. No action or approval is performed. A stale or inaccessible surface must be labeled unknown, never treated as success.
- **missing:** A first-class read-only perception snapshot API that atomically gathers /observe, /ops/snapshot, /pipeline, /browser/status, relay job status, and pendant upload/playback acknowledgements with timestamps.; A typed state vocabulary (observed, delivered, playing, acknowledged, queued, blocked, failed, stale, unknown) and contradiction resolver shared by relay, Mac, and dashboard.; A compact spoken/evidence response schema with per-claim source, age, and confidence; current routes expose pieces but not one correlated snapshot.

### "“Make sure private answers only play when I’m actually wearing the pendant; if I take it off, pause and tell me later.”"
- **useful because:** The current pendant can know that it is decoding audio, but not whether it is still on the owner. A response can therefore play aloud on a desk, in a bag, or after the lanyard is removed. Wear-state perception would protect privacy and make missed answers recoverable without requiring the owner to remember what happened.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → dashboard
- **model tier:** No realtime or background model is needed for wear-state detection: firmware should classify sensor signals locally. A cheap background model may summarize deferred responses; realtime is used only for the original conversation.
- **latency:** Pause within 250 ms of a confirmed removal and resume/announce availability within 1 second of a confirmed re-wear. Avoid false transitions with a 1–2 second hysteresis window.
- **cost:** Hardware prototype roughly $3–$8 incremental BOM (6-axis IMU plus capacitive/skin-contact or proximity sensor), with negligible per-event API cost. LTE metadata and deferred-response summarization dominate ongoing cost, if enabled.
- **security:** Wear-state and proximity timestamps are sensitive behavioral data. Keep raw sensor readings on-device, transmit only state transitions, use short retention, and never infer location or another person’s identity. A paused private response must not be resumed automatically in a public context without the owner’s configured preference.
- **missing:** A product pendant revision exposing a low-power IMU and a reliable skin-contact, capacitive, or proximity signal on an I2C/GPIO path; the prototype currently has free I2C/SPI but no attached wear sensor.; A firmware wear-state classifier with calibration, hysteresis, battery-aware sampling, and a local privacy-safe pause/mute path that survives LTE loss.; Relay semantics for deferred audio: response status must become paused_due_to_unworn, expire by policy, and be replayable as text or regenerated speech after re-wear without duplicating a command.; A cross-surface policy and dashboard control for private playback behavior, plus an explicit test mode so a desk mock cannot be mistaken for a worn device.


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `context` — Add a read-only cross-surface evidence ledger. Every observation from /observe, /ops/snapshot, /pipeline, browser status, relay job status, and pendant telemetry becomes a typed claim {subject, predicate, value, observedAt, source, age, expiry, confidence}; correlate claims by request/job/pipeline ID, detect contradictions (for example action receipt success while inputReachability=false), and expose a single snapshot without allowing the ledger to gate or execute actions.
- **owner gets:** When the owner asks whether something happened, they get the truth of what was verified—not a misleading success receipt from a surface that was offline, stale, or unable to control the screen. It also makes delayed pendant audio and browser work explainable instead of mysteriously absent.
- effort: Medium: schema plus adapters for existing read-only routes, TTL/contradiction rules, and a compact dashboard/spoken projection. No new device hardware required; pendant acknowledgements would improve it later.  ·  risk: Incorrect correlation could merge unrelated jobs or mark a real completion as contradictory. Keep raw source claims immutable, show both claims when resolution is uncertain, and expire observations aggressively. Recovery is replaying the raw ledger after fixing an adapter.
- cost: Negligible storage and compute; one small D1/local JSON record per observation. No model call required for ingestion; optional cheap model only for natural-language explanation.  ·  latency: Sub-100 ms for cached claims; refresh fan-out adds roughly 1–3 seconds. It is strictly read-only and must never delay action execution.
- security: Ledger metadata can reveal private URLs, app names, and request text. Encrypt at rest where possible, redact secrets/page contents, scope reads to the owner, and enforce per-claim sensitivity/TTL.
- depends on: A stable correlation ID propagated between relay jobs, Mac jobs, pipeline IDs, browser commands, and pendant sessions; A pendant playback acknowledgement event (currently relay_result 'waiting for pendant' is delivery, not proof of playback); Existing read-only routes GET /observe, GET /ops/snapshot, GET /pipeline, GET /browser/status, GET /jobs/:jobId/receipts

### `firmware` — Emit a compact playback-verification event for every response: response ID, first-buffered timestamp, first-audible timestamp, last-audible timestamp, underrun count, decode error count, and a short rolling PCM/Opus checksum. Send it after playback (or persist it on microSD if LTE is unavailable), then upload the event idempotently when connectivity returns. Distinguish received/staged from actually rendered and heard-by-device; never claim human-heard.
- **owner gets:** A spoken answer that was uploaded can still be silent, late, truncated, or stuck in the offline queue. The owner can finally learn whether the pendant received it, began rendering it, and completed it, rather than hearing “done” when nothing came out.
- effort: Medium firmware/relay integration: event ring buffer, playback hooks in audio_opus/I2S path, SD fallback, and relay ingestion. Must fit the existing single full-duplex I2S peripheral and tight CPU budget.  ·  risk: Extra event traffic and flash/SD writes; timestamps can be wrong across sleep/reconnect, and checksum proves bytes rendered—not that the owner heard them. Keep events small, batch them, use monotonic uptime plus server receipt time, and label human audibility unverified. If upload fails, retain only bounded recent events under existing failure-buffer policy.
- cost: Negligible API cost and a few hundred bytes per response; tiny LTE overhead. CPU impact should be under 1% if checksum is incremental; no new hardware cost.  ·  latency: No added conversational latency; upload is post-playback and asynchronous. SD fallback may add a few milliseconds only on failure paths.
- security: Response IDs and timing can reveal interaction patterns; avoid storing audio or transcript, encrypt/authenticate telemetry in the existing TLS channel, and enforce short retention.
- depends on: Existing relay_result delivery events and GET /pipeline/:pipelineId/audio/:direction; A durable relay endpoint for pendant playback receipts with deduplication; The pending playback-lifecycle-telemetry work, extended from upload/decode to audible-render lifecycle

### `hardware` — Replace the prototype’s single-button/single-LED pendant audio module with a production enclosure that adds a low-power 6-axis IMU and capacitive skin-contact electrode (or short-range proximity sensor) around the lanyard-facing shell, connected to the currently unused I2C/GPIO. Add a hardware mute gate in the audio path controlled by the local wear-state classifier, so removal can silence playback without waiting for LTE or the Mac.
- **owner gets:** Private speech stops following the owner onto a table or into a bag, even during a dropped link; when the pendant is worn again, the system can safely explain what was paused instead of silently losing it.
- effort: High hardware and firmware effort: enclosure/electrode design, low-power sensor driver, calibration across clothing/skin conditions, state-machine testing, and an audio-gate integration that cannot corrupt the single full-duplex I2S stream.  ·  risk: False removal could interrupt a sentence; false wear could fail to protect privacy. Use hysteresis, a physical long-press override, conservative default-to-muted when uncertain, and local event logging for calibration. Recovery remains possible by replaying deferred text/audio after re-wear.
- cost: Approximately $3–$8 component/BOM increase in volume prototypes; IMU current roughly 10–100 µA depending on part and duty cycle, proximity/ capacitive sensing a few additional µA to mA during sampling. No recurring model/API cost for classification.  ·  latency: Local mute response under 250 ms after debounce; no LTE or Mac round trip. Sensor sampling adds negligible CPU and battery load compared with the modem and Opus path.
- security: Raw proximity/skin signals stay on-device. Only coarse transitions (worn/unworn/uncertain) leave the device, authenticated over existing TLS, with short retention. Default mute while uncertain protects private playback.
- depends on: A pendant firmware wear-state classifier and local audio mute gate; Relay support for paused_due_to_unworn responses and replay after re-wear; A product hardware revision; the current nRF9160 DK prototype has no wear sensor despite free I2C/SPI
