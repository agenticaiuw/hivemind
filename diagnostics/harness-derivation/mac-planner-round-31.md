## Capabilities it proposed

### "“If something I asked you to do can’t run right now, keep it safely queued, tell me what’s blocked, and finish it automatically when the Mac or browser comes back.”"
- **useful because:** Today a pendant request can disappear into a 45-second browser timeout or fail because Mac permissions are missing. This gives the owner one durable, truthful handoff: the pendant captures intent while away from the desk, the relay keeps the task alive, and the Mac/browser resume exactly the unfinished step after recovery instead of making the owner repeat themselves.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-bridge → browser → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use gpt-realtime-2.1 only for the brief spoken acknowledgement and recovery notification; use a cheaper background model for step normalization, retry classification, and reconciliation. Mac/browser execute the concrete steps and relay remains the durable coordinator.
- **latency:** Acknowledge on pendant in under 2 seconds and expose blocked reason immediately. Retry with exponential backoff; on heartbeat/readiness recovery, resume within 5 seconds. No 45-second synchronous browser wait: lease expiry emits a progress receipt and parks the task.
- **cost:** About $0.002–$0.01 per task lifecycle, dominated by background reconciliation and any eventual speech; idle queued tasks cost mostly D1/R2 storage and negligible model usage.
- **security:** Task intents, URLs, and evidence may contain private data; keep them encrypted/retained briefly in relay D1/R2, with redacted pendant messages and local-only page contents. Never retry a submit/send/delete step automatically; park at that cursor and tell the owner. Use idempotency keys, tab/session binding, and a visible dashboard cancel/delete control.
- **missing:** A durable cross-surface task-escrow schema with step cursor, lease, retry policy, and expiry; Relay-to-Mac resume webhook or polling protocol carrying idempotency keys and readiness requirements; Browser bridge progress/lease events and fast offline classification (rather than blocking 45 seconds); Mac readiness preflight state (Accessibility, Screen Recording, foreground/session) attached to receipts; Pendant protocol for concise blocked/resumed/cancelled notifications and offline acknowledgement; Dashboard view for queued, blocked, resumed, expired, and cancelled tasks

### "“Rehearse this whole task first, exactly as it would happen across my Mac and logged-in browser, then tell me what would change before doing anything.”"
- **useful because:** The owner can safely test a multi-application workflow—such as organizing files, filling a private form, or preparing a meeting—without relying on a vague summary. They get a step-by-step replay with concrete before/after effects, including places where the browser session, current app, or local files make the plan impossible. This is different from merely previewing the final submission: it validates the entire cross-surface procedure before execution.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-bridge → browser → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use a cheaper background reasoning model to compile and check the plan. Use the realtime model only to explain the rehearsal conversationally. Mac and browser harnesses produce structured observations and simulated effects; no live mutation is allowed during rehearsal.
- **latency:** A short workflow should produce an initial spoken feasibility result in 3 seconds, then a detailed replay in under 15 seconds. Large workflows can continue in the background and notify the pendant when the rehearsal is ready.
- **cost:** Approximately $0.01–$0.08 per rehearsal, dominated by model reasoning and browser snapshot processing; local Mac simulation and structured diffing are inexpensive.
- **security:** Authenticated page contents and local filenames must remain on their originating surfaces unless explicitly summarized. Rehearsal artifacts should be encrypted, expire automatically, and clearly label simulated versus observed data. The simulator must not trigger network writes, form submissions, clipboard leaks, downloads, notifications, or shell commands. Any transition from rehearsal to execution must create a new, explicit action plan rather than reusing an untrusted simulation.
- **missing:** A side-effect-free Mac action simulator that can model filesystem, app, shortcut, and UI effects and emit typed diffs; A browser replay/snapshot harness that can execute reads and DOM transitions against a frozen authenticated snapshot without network mutations; A common effect algebra for files, tabs, fields, messages, reminders, and focus changes, with confidence and unknown-effect markers; A cross-surface rehearsal trace format linking pendant intent, relay plan, Mac observations, browser observations, and predicted effects; A dashboard replay viewer with per-step before/after state, uncertainty, and impossible-step explanations; An execution handoff protocol that invalidates the rehearsal when relevant app, file, tab, or page state changes


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Add a cross-surface task escrow coordinator. On every pendant-originated multi-step plan, persist a compact intent envelope (taskId, owner-visible goal, ordered step IDs, idempotency keys, required surfaces, cursor, lease, expiry, sensitivity, and last receipt). Mac/browser report readiness and lease transitions instead of blocking; relay parks, retries, or resumes from the cursor. On recovery, reconcile the last receipt before replaying a step, and emit a pendant notification plus dashboard timeline for blocked/resumed/expired states.
- **owner gets:** The owner can ask once while walking away and trust that a temporary offline bridge or Mac permission problem will not silently lose the request or duplicate an action. They hear exactly what is blocked and later what actually completed.
- effort: Medium-high: shared schema and D1 migration, relay scheduler, Mac/browser adapters, receipt reconciliation, pendant notification events, dashboard UI, and failure-injection tests across dropped heartbeats.  ·  risk: A stale lease or incorrect reconciliation could duplicate a mutation. Default automatic retries to read-only and reversible steps; require an explicit parked boundary for send/submit/delete. Expire envelopes, support cancel, and make every transition auditable. Recovery from coordinator failure is D1 transaction replay.
- cost: Low persistent storage and queue cost; roughly <$0.01 per task in background model/API work, with no realtime model calls except notifications.  ·  latency: Immediate acknowledgement; removes the current ~45-second browser timeout from the user path. Recovery adds a few seconds after a fresh heartbeat.
- security: Private URLs and task metadata stay encrypted/short-lived in relay storage; page contents remain on Mac/browser unless the owner asks for a summary. Bind envelopes to paired device/account and never expose session cookies.
- depends on: chg-14accc01 fast-fail/progress browser lease behavior; Mac readiness status and per-action receipts; relay durable state and pendant event delivery; cross-surface trace/continuation IDs

### `integration` — Create a cross-surface speculative execution layer (“rehearsal graph”) that consumes a spoken goal and runs it against frozen observations: Mac snapshots model app/filesystem/UI effects, the browser harness models authenticated DOM navigation and field changes, and the relay merges both into a typed predicted-effect graph. Every predicted mutation carries evidence, confidence, and an unknown-effects marker. A state fingerprint on apps, files, tabs, and relevant page regions invalidates the rehearsal before any real execution handoff.
- **owner gets:** Before letting the system touch anything, the owner can see the actual sequence it intends to perform, the specific files/tabs/fields it would affect, and where its prediction is uncertain. The system will not quietly act on a stale page or changed desktop state.
- effort: High: new simulators or snapshot replayers on Mac and browser, shared effect schema, state fingerprinting, trace merger, replay UI, and adversarial tests ensuring no simulator path can invoke a real mutation.  ·  risk: A simulation could falsely claim an effect is safe or complete. Mark unsupported effects explicitly as unknown, refuse to present a complete rehearsal when required state is missing, and require a fresh plan after invalidation. Keep simulation APIs physically separate from execution APIs.
- cost: Moderate storage for short-lived snapshots and diffs; roughly $0.01–$0.08 per workflow for background reasoning, with no realtime call except the spoken explanation.  ·  latency: Adds seconds before execution, but replaces uncertainty and accidental side effects with a bounded preview; small plans can return in roughly 3–15 seconds.
- security: Snapshots may contain private browser and local metadata. Encrypt them, keep them local where possible, apply short TTLs, redact sensitive values in dashboard/pendant output, and never send cookies or credentials to the relay.
- depends on: Structured read-only Mac and browser observation APIs; A typed effect/diff schema shared by relay, Mac, and browser; State fingerprints and invalidation events; A separate execution handoff that cannot reuse simulator privileges
