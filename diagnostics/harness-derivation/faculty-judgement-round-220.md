# Harness derivation — faculty-judgement — round 220

Model: `gpt-5.6-luna`  ·  probes against `http://localhost:8000`

## What it established

_Nothing recorded._

## Capabilities it proposed

### "“When I’m in a meeting, keep the pendant quiet and terse, but tell me immediately if something truly time-critical arrives; when the meeting ends, give me one compact catch-up.”"
- **useful because:** It turns a noisy collection of mail, calendar, browser-watch, and audio events into an interruption behavior the owner can feel: silence during the meeting, urgent exceptions only, and no lost context afterward. No single node knows both the meeting schedule and the wearable’s playback/attention state.
- **path:** mac-planner reads calendar/reminders, foreground app and idle presence, and writes a meeting-window state → browser-extension contributes authenticated page-watch changes and browser availability → relay aggregates mail/watch/routine events and applies the owner-editable attention policy → pendant receives only the resulting urgent alert or post-meeting digest, with audio delivery ACKs proving it was heard
- **model tier:** Cheap deterministic policy and attention_arbitrate first; use the expensive realtime model only to compress the deferred digest or classify an ambiguous deadline. Do not call the expensive model for every event.
- **latency:** Urgent classification under 1 second; meeting-end digest within 2 minutes of the end event. Calendar polling can be 30–60 seconds.
- **cost:** Usually near-zero model cost for policy/rules; roughly $0.01–$0.05 per meeting-end digest depending on deferred item count. Dominant costs are TTS/audio bytes, not judgement.
- **security:** Calendar titles and mail subjects are private and must not be spoken by default; the policy must name whether content may leave the Mac. Empty EventKit reads must be treated as unreadable, never as an empty calendar. Every suppression and alert needs evidence refs and an explanation receipt.
- **missing:** A real meeting-state adapter that reads EventKit with an explicit authorized/unreadable result and exposes the current interval; A scheduler/trigger that runs briefing triage at meeting end; today briefingTriage is pull-only; A durable deferred-item store that links event IDs to the meeting window and deduplicates across Mac, browser, and relay; A policy field for meeting-mode speak classes, to be set by the owner rather than guessed

### "“If I ask whether you told me something, answer whether it was generated, downloaded, started playing, and finished—and if it was interrupted, offer exactly the missing part.”"
- **useful because:** Today a server receipt can mean accepted or generated while the owner never heard it. This makes the wearable honest about the difference between queued, delivered, heard, and interrupted, then repairs only the missing audio instead of replaying an entire brief.
- **path:** relay joins job/pipeline receipts with authenticated pendant delivery ACKs and keeps an opaque artifact timeline → mac-planner exposes the corresponding job and briefing item, and can prepare a short repair clip → pendant emits downloaded/playback-started/finished/interrupted events and supports the existing spoken-status barge-in → browser-extension is only consulted when the original item came from a browser source, never as proof of audio delivery
- **model tier:** No model for status. Use deterministic event reconciliation and idempotency keys; use a cheap model only to produce a shorter repair summary when the owner asks for one. Realtime is reserved for the spoken answer.
- **latency:** Status answer under 500 ms from stored events; after a repair request, start the missing segment within 2 seconds if cached, otherwise under 10 seconds.
- **cost:** Near-zero judgement cost; storage and a small amount of regenerated TTS dominate. A repair summary costs roughly $0.005–$0.02, and should be avoided when the original audio can resume.
- **security:** Artifact IDs must remain opaque and ACKs authenticated to a device session; do not expose raw audio URLs or transcript content in the spoken status. Duplicate and offline-replayed ACKs must be harmless. A repair must preserve the original sensitivity and delivery policy, not downgrade it because the owner asks loudly.
- **missing:** A durable cross-surface join between relay job ID, Mac pipeline ID, briefing item ID, and artifact ID; current IDs are unrelated; A server-side ACK reconciliation store with sequence checks, offline replay handling, and a query route for owner-facing status; A resume/clip operation that can regenerate from an audio cursor without replaying or re-speaking already finished content; A clear owner-facing vocabulary for generated/queued/downloaded/started/finished, rather than treating receipt completion as hearing

### "“Keep work and personal contexts separate automatically: when I switch projects or browser identities, stop carrying facts across, warn me before a note crosses the boundary, and let me inspect or undo any cross-boundary use.”"
- **useful because:** A personal assistant that silently carries a client name, document detail, or browser session into the wrong context is more dangerous than one that forgets. The Mac knows the active project, the browser knows the origin/session, and the pendant knows the owner’s physical intent; the relay can enforce one shared boundary instead of trusting each caller.
- **path:** mac-planner publishes active project/session context and records the proposed memory/action → browser-extension supplies origin, authenticated-session identity, and provenance for page-derived facts → relay evaluates a signed context boundary before memory projection, delegation, or audio delivery → pendant shows a short non-sensitive warning and requires the existing physical transaction approval for a cross-boundary mutation
- **model tier:** Deterministic boundary matching and sensitivity checks should decide almost everything. Use a cheap model only to classify an ambiguous project label or explain a blocked transfer; never use a model as the authorization decision.
- **latency:** Under 100 ms for a same-context read; under 1 second for a warning/approval decision. A cross-boundary review can wait for explicit owner confirmation.
- **cost:** Negligible model cost in the normal path; occasional explanation costs <$0.01. The main cost is local provenance/index storage and a small relay lookup.
- **security:** Default deny for unknown or conflicting contexts. Do not speak the names or snippets that caused the block. Context labels themselves can be sensitive. The browser’s authenticated session identity must be represented by a non-secret handle, never cookies or tokens. Approval must bind to the exact source, target context, and action hash so it cannot be replayed.
- **missing:** A durable cross-surface capability/context registry; the current registry is Mac-local and in-memory; A shared context-bound memory record with source and target context IDs; current facts and fleet events lack an enforced boundary and some derived facts lack capsule links; A relay-enforced policy check before projection and external action, rather than advisory redaction after the fact; A dashboard review of cross-boundary attempts and a reversible propagation/forget operation across facts, graph, capsules, and relay memory

### "“When the connection is bad, do not make me wait for a full brief: send the most important sentence first, then continue in the background and tell me exactly what was skipped.”"
- **useful because:** A wearable conversation should degrade by meaning, not merely by dropping packets. The relay can rank and split a response, the Mac can prepare a compact fallback, and the pendant can play the first useful sentence while buffering the rest; the owner gets timely value instead of silence or a misleading ‘done.’
- **path:** relay monitors link/queue/latency evidence and partitions a response into signed priority segments → mac-planner prepares a short fallback and retains the full artifact for later continuation → pendant stores and plays the priority segment first, reports delivery/playback ACKs, and exposes the remaining segments through the existing alert/brief controls → browser-extension contributes source priority and page-read completion when the response depends on browser work
- **model tier:** Use deterministic byte/latency thresholds and policy for segmentation; a cheap background model makes the compact fallback only when the response has no safe sentence boundary. Realtime is used only for the active conversational answer.
- **latency:** First useful segment within 2 seconds on a degraded link; continuation retries in the background with a 30-second budget. Status must distinguish ‘not attempted’ from ‘skipped by policy.’
- **cost:** Usually no extra model cost; compact fallback generation is roughly $0.005–$0.02. Network retries and duplicate audio are the dominant resource costs.
- **security:** Every segment inherits the original sensitivity and source policy; never create a supposedly safe fallback by dropping context that made the content private. Signed segment ordering and artifact IDs prevent replay or mixing responses. The owner must be able to cancel all remaining segments with the universal stop latch.
- **missing:** A measured link-quality/queue estimator shared by relay and pendant; today delivery events exist but no policy consumes them; A response segmentation format with sequence, priority, expiry, and parent artifact hash; A relay scheduler that sends priority segments ahead of bulk audio without causing duplicate playback; An owner-visible skipped/deferred ledger linked to delivery receipts

### "“Never speak private material when another person is nearby; if you cannot tell, give me a neutral chime and put the answer in my private queue instead.”"
- **useful because:** The system currently knows what content is sensitive but has no idea whether speech is safe in the physical moment. A wearable is uniquely positioned to detect competing voices and immediately gate its own speaker, while the Mac/relay can preserve the answer for later. This prevents a calendar title, client name, or secret from being broadcast merely because a server policy allowed it.
- **path:** pendant runs a conservative local acoustic bystander detector and hard-gates sensitive playback before PCM reaches the bridge → ESP32 audio bridge remains a dumb playback sink; it receives only already-approved audio or a neutral chime → relay tags each artifact with an owner-configured disclosure class and queues blocked items without exposing their text to the detector → Mac dashboard lets the owner review false positives, choose which classes are private, and replay the queued item when alone
- **model tier:** A small on-device classifier or deterministic voice-activity/multi-speaker heuristic handles the immediate gate; no realtime model call is allowed in the safety-critical path. A cheap background model may summarize the blocked item after the owner explicitly requests it.
- **latency:** Playback gate decision under 150 ms; if uncertain, silence before the first sensitive frame. Queueing is immediate, and replay begins only after a local-clear signal or explicit owner action.
- **cost:** No per-event API cost in the normal path. Firmware/model work and a few hundred KB of flash/RAM are the main costs; optional background summarization is <$0.02 per item.
- **security:** Fail closed for private classes and fail safe for the microphone: the classifier must not upload raw ambient audio or retain it. Acoustic features should be transient and local. False positives cost convenience; false negatives are disclosure events, so every blocked/allowed decision needs a local reason and durable receipt without storing bystander speech.
- **missing:** An on-device, privacy-preserving multi-speaker/bystander classifier validated against the pendant microphone and 24 kHz playback timing; A firmware playback firewall that can reject sensitive PCM before the ESP32 bridge, while still allowing neutral status tones; A signed disclosure-class field carried from source/evidence through artifact generation to the pendant; An owner-configurable policy and review route for private classes and false-positive corrections

## Its own summary

Round 220 produced three recorded capabilities: meeting-aware interruption and post-meeting catch-up; truthful end-to-end “did you hear it?” status with repair; and automatic work/personal context quarantine with reviewable cross-boundary prevention. A news-correction idea was rejected as a duplicate of the existing epistemic news layer, and a degraded-link priority audio idea was accepted but flagged as connective work over existing delivery/attention primitives.

**Biggest unknown:** I still need the owner’s explicit policy values where the system must not guess: which meeting alerts may speak content aloud, which project/browser contexts are trusted to share memory, and whether a repaired or priority audio segment may interrupt. Technically, the largest missing pieces are durable cross-surface IDs, a real meeting-end trigger/deferred store, relay-side delivery-ACK reconciliation, and an enforced context-bound memory/forget cascade.
