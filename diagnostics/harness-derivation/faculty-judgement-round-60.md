## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Did that actually happen?” After you act through my Mac or a logged-in site, prove the external result—not just that you clicked—and tell me if it is confirmed, probably done, still pending, or unknown."
- **useful because:** Today a successful local command or browser dispatch can leave the real-world effect uncertain, especially with offline tabs, stale pages, redirects, or a dropped link. This gives the owner closure and prevents duplicate payments, messages, bookings, or edits. It is specifically cross-surface: one surface acts, another independently re-observes the target, and the relay/pendant reports the result even after the owner leaves.
- **path:** unified: classify the intended external effect and its idempotency/retry policy → mac-planner: perform Mac action and capture before/after state → browser-extension: dispatch authenticated browser action and retain tab/session identity → faculty-perception: independently re-read the target and compare an effect predicate against fresh evidence → faculty-action: reconcile receipts, retry only safe idempotent steps, and escalate conflicts → relay-realtime: deliver a one-sentence closure or ask the owner when evidence remains ambiguous → pendant: provide a tactile/audio 'confirmed vs unknown' status and queue the result across a dropped link
- **model tier:** Use the cheaper background model for effect-predicate extraction, receipt correlation, and re-check scheduling; reserve realtime for the owner's “did it happen?” question and for ambiguous or high-consequence conflicts.
- **latency:** For reversible edits, initial confirmation within 10 seconds and one re-check after 30–90 seconds. For external systems with eventual consistency, continue in the durable job runner and report within the service's observed SLA; never claim success solely on dispatch.
- **cost:** Usually 1–2 cheap verification passes plus existing tool calls; roughly 2–5x a normal action's tool/API overhead, with model cost dominated by extracting a predicate from the page/receipt. Realtime cost occurs only for the final short spoken result or an ambiguity.
- **security:** Re-checking may expose private authenticated pages to the relay/model, so send only the target fields and hashes, not whole pages. Never infer a financial or destructive effect from a local success receipt. Require confirmation for retries that could duplicate an irreversible action; preserve evidence URLs, timestamps, and tab identity for audit.
- **missing:** A first-class effect predicate (expected external state) and idempotency classification attached to every plan step; An independent re-observation/reconciliation job primitive with expiry and explicit CONFIRMED/LIKELY/PENDING/UNKNOWN/CONFLICT states; Per-surface adapters for post-action refresh/readback and external receipt correlation; A pendant/relay result packet that survives a dropped link without replaying the action

### "“Before you change anything, make sure it is the right account and the right person.” Bind every browser or Mac action to the owner’s intended identity and workspace, detect account/profile changes before and during the task, and refuse to proceed when identity evidence conflicts."
- **useful because:** A logged-in browser can contain several accounts, workspaces, or profiles, and a successful action in the wrong one is worse than a failure. The owner should not have to remember which tab, profile, or tenant is active before asking the pendant to help. This provides a hard safety boundary that ordinary tab affinity and action receipts do not provide.
- **path:** pendant: capture the owner’s local approval gesture and a compact intended-account hint when needed → relay-realtime: bind the spoken request to a short-lived identity-and-workspace claim without storing credentials → mac-planner: identify the active macOS user, app workspace, and browser profile before planning → browser-extension: attest the active profile, origin, account label, tenant/workspace, and tab identity before dispatch and after navigation → faculty-perception: independently compare identity evidence from page chrome, URL/origin, session metadata, and Mac state → faculty-action: enforce the identity match as a non-bypassable precondition and abort on drift → unified: explain the mismatch in one short sentence and offer a read-only recovery path
- **model tier:** Use deterministic adapters and a cheap background model to normalize account/workspace labels. Use realtime only when the owner must resolve an ambiguity or explicitly choose among identities.
- **latency:** Preflight should complete in under 2 seconds for a known profile. Re-check identity immediately before any mutation and after redirects; ambiguity should pause rather than add latency through guessing.
- **cost:** Small additional browser/Mac metadata reads per action; typically negligible model cost when labels are structured. The dominant cost is engineering and test coverage for multi-account sites, SSO redirects, and profile changes.
- **security:** Never use passwords, cookies, or page contents as identity evidence. Store only origin, profile/session identifiers, normalized account/workspace labels, timestamps, and hashes. Treat a missing or stale label as a block, not as permission to infer. Owner confirmation is required when multiple identities legitimately match.
- **missing:** A signed identity-attestation schema shared by pendant, Mac, relay, and browser bridge; A browser adapter that exposes profile and authenticated account/workspace metadata without exposing secrets; A hard identity-drift gate evaluated immediately before mutation; Fixtures and tests for multiple browser profiles, delegated tenants, SSO redirects, and account switching


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Add an External Effect Verification (EEV) stage to every multi-surface job. At plan time, compile each step into an effect predicate (target identity, expected state delta, freshness window, idempotency class, and forbidden duplicate outcome). After dispatch, require a separate read-only observer—browser re-open/readback for authenticated web effects, Mac inspection for local effects, and relay receipt correlation for asynchronous jobs—to emit a signed observation. Reconcile observations into CONFIRMED, LIKELY, PENDING, UNKNOWN, or CONFLICT; make retries impossible for non-idempotent steps unless the owner explicitly approves. Persist the evidence bundle and send a compact closure packet to the pendant.
- **owner gets:** The owner gets an honest answer about whether something changed in the outside world, rather than a misleading “completed” message when only a click or local command succeeded. It prevents duplicate sends and gives them a recoverable explanation when a site, network, or permission fails.
- effort: Medium-high: typed effect predicates and reconciliation state machine in the relay/job runner, plus browser and Mac readback adapters; substantial test matrix for redirects, eventual consistency, dropped links, stale tabs, and duplicate prevention.  ·  risk: A false UNKNOWN could annoy the owner or delay a safe action; a false CONFIRMED is dangerous. Fail closed, require independent fresh evidence, bound re-checks, and expose the exact reason/evidence. If an observer is unavailable, leave the action unresolved rather than retrying. Recover by replaying only read-only verification jobs.
- cost: Low-to-moderate extra API calls (normally one readback and optionally one delayed readback per action); use a cheap background model for predicate/reconciliation and hashes for large evidence. No hardware cost.  ·  latency: Adds roughly 1–10 seconds for immediate readback; eventual-consistency checks run asynchronously and do not block the owner unless the effect is high consequence.
- security: Private page/state snippets must be minimized and encrypted; retain hashes and scoped fields by default. Effect evidence needs tab/session identity and timestamps, but must not leak page contents to the pendant or relay unnecessarily.
- depends on: A durable job/event persistence primitive with step-level receipts; The signed state witness proposed by faculty-perception; The action lease proposed by faculty-action; Browser bridge reconnection and Mac Accessibility/Screen Recording permissions for observation adapters

### `integration` — Introduce an Identity Boundary Gate shared by plan, browser dispatch, Mac execution, and job completion. Before planning, mint a short-lived identity claim containing intended origin, browser profile, authenticated account/workspace label, macOS user/workspace, and request nonce. Require independent attestations from the browser bridge and Mac observer immediately before mutation; compare normalized labels and origin, invalidate the claim on redirect/profile/session change, and emit IDENTITY_MISMATCH rather than executing. Store only signed metadata and hashes, never credentials or page bodies.
- **owner gets:** The owner can delegate work without risking a perfectly successful action in the wrong Gmail account, GitHub organization, cloud tenant, or browser profile. It turns a subtle, expensive mistake into a visible pause with a clear choice.
- effort: Medium: protocol and middleware work plus browser/Mac metadata adapters, with extensive multi-account and SSO test fixtures.  ·  risk: Some sites expose ambiguous or changing account labels, causing safe false blocks. Recover by offering a read-only account chooser and requiring explicit owner selection; never silently weaken the gate.
- cost: Negligible runtime/API cost beyond a few metadata reads and signature checks; moderate engineering and QA cost.  ·  latency: About 0.5–2 seconds of preflight metadata reads; no extra model turn for unambiguous identities.
- security: Improves security by minimizing identity data and preventing cross-account actions. The claim must be short-lived, scoped to one plan hash, and unusable as authentication.
- depends on: Browser bridge profile/account metadata adapter; Mac read-only workspace/user observer; Shared signed-attestation and nonce format; Plan-step mutation middleware
