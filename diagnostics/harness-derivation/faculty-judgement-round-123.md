## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Make spoken replies and my queued briefings sound clear, but never let them stutter: use 24 kHz when the link and pendant can sustain it, fall back smoothly when they cannot, and resume at the exact sentence if I walk out of coverage.”"
- **useful because:** The current pendant path can decode 24 kHz playback but capture/uplink is 16 kHz, and simultaneous Opus encode/decode consumes roughly 87% of one Cortex-M33 core. A fixed mode will either waste quality or cause glitches. A hive-level adaptive session would let the owner hear consistently intelligible speech, preserve queued work through connectivity loss, and avoid replaying a briefing from the beginning.
- **path:** relay-realtime → relay → pendant → mac-planner → faculty-perception → faculty-action
- **model tier:** Use the realtime tier only for the live conversational turn and codec-mode negotiation; use a cheaper background worker for transcoding, queue preparation, and sentence-boundary indexing.
- **latency:** Mode negotiation under 300 ms at session start; fallback within 1–2 lost frames (120 ms) with a short crossfade; queued-briefing recovery can take seconds and must not block live speech.
- **cost:** Small incremental API cost: mostly background Opus transcode/storage already paid for; roughly one extra cheap worker invocation per queued item, with bandwidth/storage dominating. Realtime token cost is unchanged if negotiation is structured metadata rather than prose.
- **security:** Audio remains private and should stay in the existing authenticated relay storage. Telemetry should contain only codec mode, packet loss, battery/thermal buckets, and sentence IDs—not transcript text. Switching mode is reversible and needs no confirmation; deleting or pinning recordings still follows the owner's retention policy.
- **missing:** A firmware capability/telemetry handshake exposing supported playback modes, decoder CPU headroom, queue cursor, and a local sentence-boundary resume marker.; Relay-side adaptive jitter buffer and Opus profile switch with sequence continuity and crossfade, rather than starting a new unrelated stream.; A background audio compiler that emits sentence/word boundary indexes and low/high-fidelity variants, plus garbage-collectable retention metadata.; Mac-side preflight and fallback notification so a briefing can be prepared or surfaced locally when the pendant is offline.; An end-to-end fault-injection test covering loss, reconnect, duplicate packets, SD failure buffer, and resume without duplicate speech.

### "“Erase everything this hive knows about [topic/person/project] everywhere, prove what was erased, and stop any queued or offline action that depends on it.”"
- **useful because:** Today a private fact or sensitive project can be distributed across relay job records, generated audio, Mac workspace files, browser-session state, pending commands, receipts, and the pendant’s failure buffer. The owner has no single, verifiable way to revoke that knowledge across all bodies. A topic-scoped cryptographic erasure and revocation operation would provide a real safety boundary, not merely a promise that future prompts will omit it.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → relay → mac-planner → mac-vision → browser-extension → faculty-perception → faculty-action → unified
- **model tier:** Use a deterministic background control-plane worker for indexing, revocation, deletion, and verification; use the realtime tier only to clarify the topic scope and speak the final receipt.
- **latency:** Acknowledge the request immediately; revoke queued work within 2 seconds; complete online erasure and produce a receipt within 30 seconds. Offline pendant and Mac components must apply the tombstone on reconnect before accepting work.
- **cost:** Low model cost and modest control-plane/API cost. The dominant cost is enumerating and verifying distributed stores; no transcript generation is needed.
- **security:** The erase request itself must not echo the secret in logs or spoken output. Use a keyed topic fingerprint, per-surface deletion capability, authenticated tombstones, and append-only proof metadata that records locations and outcomes without retaining content. Destructive erasure requires explicit confirmation, except for a separately configured physical panic gesture. The system must distinguish successful deletion, inaccessible storage, and cryptographic invalidation, never claim complete erasure when a surface is offline.
- **missing:** A hive-wide data inventory that maps each artifact, pending action, browser session value, audio object, file, and device journal to privacy-scope fingerprints without copying sensitive content.; A signed, monotonic revocation/tombstone protocol consumed by relay, Mac, browser bridge, and pendant offline queue before execution or replay.; Per-surface secure deletion adapters: R2/D1 and logs on relay; workspace/receipt/job artifacts on Mac; browser extension/session caches; pendant SD failure-buffer records and local journals.; A verification sweep that returns a surface-by-surface receipt and retries or escalates offline failures, with an owner-visible distinction between erased and merely revoked.; A policy preventing model context, scheduled routines, and queued jobs from rehydrating a revoked scope.

### "“Keep the pendant useful all day: spend battery and bandwidth on urgent things, defer or compress everything else, and tell me what was deferred before it becomes stale.”"
- **useful because:** Today the hive has no owner-facing energy contract. A long briefing, live conversation, queued browser job, and urgent interruption compete blindly for the same pendant radio, CPU, and failure-buffer space. The owner should get graceful degradation rather than a dead device or silently lost work: urgent contact remains available, routine work is deferred with an expiry, and the Mac/relay can carry on when the pendant cannot.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → relay → mac-planner → faculty-perception → faculty-action → unified
- **model tier:** Use deterministic policy and a cheap background worker for energy prediction, queue reprioritization, and transcoding. Invoke the realtime tier only for an active conversation or a truly urgent spoken alert.
- **latency:** Urgency classification and budget update under 1 second; routine deferral is immediate. The Mac or relay may continue background work without pendant availability, while urgent delivery retries within 2 seconds.
- **cost:** Low model/API cost because the decision is policy over device telemetry; occasional background summarization/transcoding dominates. It can reduce cost by avoiding unnecessary high-fidelity streaming and repeated retries.
- **security:** Battery, link, and queue telemetry should be coarse and authenticated. Never silently discard owner work: every deferral needs an expiry and receipt. Urgency rules must be owner-configurable, with conservative defaults and an emergency override that may require confirmation for noisy alerts.
- **missing:** A signed low-rate device telemetry stream for battery, radio quality, thermal/CPU headroom, SD capacity, and charging state.; A shared resource ledger that assigns budgets to live audio, queued briefings, browser/Mac jobs, and retry buffers with explicit expiry semantics.; Relay/Mac ability to continue and later reconcile work while the pendant is unavailable, without duplicating spoken output.; A policy evaluator that can distinguish urgent owner-defined events from routine work and explain each defer/compress decision.; A compact pendant status interaction so the owner can inspect or override the current budget without opening a screen.


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `firmware` — Add an audio-session capability handshake and resumable playback cursor to the pendant protocol: advertise supported Opus rates/frame sizes, report coarse CPU/battery/link buckets, accept a mode switch only at a packet boundary, persist the current sentence ID plus sequence number in a tiny journal, and expose an explicit reconnect/resume event. Pair this with relay-side mode arbitration and a Mac preflight that selects 24 kHz, 16 kHz, or a pre-rendered fallback before delivery.
- **owner gets:** Briefings and replies would remain understandable instead of glitching or restarting when the owner moves between coverage or the pendant is busy. The owner gets one continuous conversation and can resume a long briefing where they stopped.
- effort: Medium-high: protocol versioning, nRF9160 Opus scheduling, relay jitter/transcode state, Mac preflight, and network fault-injection tests across the four surfaces.  ·  risk: A bad mode switch could produce silence, duplicated speech, or an unrecoverable cursor. Mitigate with capability-version negotiation, boundary-only switches, a watchdog fallback to the known 16 kHz path, journal checksum, and server-side duplicate suppression. Roll back by disabling negotiation and using the current fixed profile.
- cost: Negligible model cost; modest relay CPU for dual-variant transcode and temporary boundary metadata. Firmware flash/RAM impact should stay small (roughly 8–16 kB flash and under 4 kB RAM if the journal is compact).  ·  latency: Adds under 300 ms at session setup; recovery adds at most one short buffered segment. Normal speech should not gain a model round trip.
- security: Only coarse device telemetry and opaque sentence/sequence IDs leave the pendant; no transcript is added. Resume journals on SD must obey the existing rule that SD is a failure buffer, with bounded expiry and encryption if it contains audio references.
- depends on: A versioned audio control message path between pendant, ESP32 bridge, and relay; Relay adaptive jitter/transcode state and sentence-boundary indexing; A Mac offline/preflight hook; End-to-end audio loss/reconnect tests
