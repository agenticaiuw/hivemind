## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Cancel everything you’re doing right now.”"
- **useful because:** When the owner notices an unwanted or unsafe action, one spoken phrase or a physical pendant cancel should halt queued and in-flight work across the relay, Mac, and private browser instead of requiring them to find the right window or job ID.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-bridge → browser → dashboard
- **model tier:** No expensive model required for cancellation: relay-side deterministic emergency stop, with faculty-judgement only summarizing what was successfully stopped afterward.
- **latency:** Pendant-to-relay acknowledgement under 500 ms when connected; Mac/browser cancellation fan-out target under 2 s. If disconnected, pendant records a cancel tombstone and transmits it on reconnect before any queued work resumes.
- **cost:** Negligible API cost; mostly authenticated control messages and local state writes. Optional summary uses a small/cheap text model.
- **security:** The command must be authenticated to the paired pendant/voice session and scoped to the owner's jobs, not arbitrary relay tenants. Cancellation should be fail-closed: uncertain in-flight side effects become needs-review, never reported as undone. Do not promise rollback for external actions already committed; show a receipt and available undo.
- **missing:** A relay-wide revocation/tombstone primitive that every worker checks before dispatch and between steps; Mac endpoint to cancel a job set, not just one job ID; browser queue flush bound to session/owner; Pendant offline cancel persistence and local feedback; Dashboard view of stopped, committed, and uncertain actions


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Build a durable cross-surface action saga runner. Compile a judged plan into typed steps spanning Mac, authenticated browser, and pendant; persist a per-step idempotency key, precondition fingerprint, attempt count, evidence receipt, and compensating action. Before retry/resume, faculty-perception must re-check the precondition (Accessibility/Screen Recording/input reachability, browser heartbeat/tab identity, and focused-app/UI fingerprint); a receipt is not success unless it includes typed proof. If the Mac disappears, relay keeps the saga and wakes the Mac bridge; if a step is irreversible, the pendant's one-button action lease must be explicitly armed and expires on timeout. Expose resume-from-checkpoint and safe-abort, never replay completed side effects.
- **owner gets:** A long request like “move this reservation into my calendar and prepare the confirmation” will not duplicate events or silently claim success after the laptop/browser goes offline. The owner can leave, return, and receive a concise pendant prompt only for the one consequential step, with evidence of what already happened.
- effort: Medium-high: durable job schema/state machine and resume endpoint in local agent/relay; idempotency adapters for Mac and browser commands; perception proof contract; pendant lease event and timeout; dashboard timeline and tests for crash/offline/reconnect races.  ·  risk: A stale fingerprint could incorrectly permit a step, or a lost receipt could cause a conservative duplicate-prevention halt. Recover by requiring proof for every side effect, treating uncertain steps as needs-review, and retaining cancel/undo where available. Never auto-resume irreversible actions without a fresh pendant lease.
- cost: Low incremental API cost: state transitions and verification are local/relay; occasional perception model call only on resume or mismatch, not every step. Storage is small JSON/D1 metadata plus bounded evidence snippets.  ·  latency: Adds roughly 0.2–1.5 s for local precondition/proof checks; resume after reconnect is slower but safe. No extra latency for already verified reversible steps.
- security: Private browser evidence remains on the Mac/paired relay; redact field values in relay state, encrypt job metadata, bind leases to pendant/session, and expire them. Cross-surface correlation IDs must not expose account secrets.
- depends on: Durable browser job runner and planner result stream (chg-16bc5dee); Typed action receipts with real UI proof; current receipts alone are insufficient while permissions.ready=false; Pendant action-lease indicator/confirmation firmware skill; Permission remediation for Accessibility and Screen Recording before claiming Mac UI success

### `hardware` — Replace the prototype's single-button/single-LED interaction with a production pendant interaction module: retain the talk button, add a distinct guarded confirm/cancel button (or capacitive confirm strip), a low-power haptic motor, and a multicolor status LED driven by a tiny I2C GPIO/LED controller. Define a physical two-step affordance: short talk press is never approval; a distinct confirm press/chord arms one expiring action lease, while cancel/haptic double-pulse aborts it. Include a hardware watchdog path that can cancel an active lease even when the link is down.
- **owner gets:** The owner can safely approve a prepared payment, message, booking, or browser submission from the pendant without touching the Mac, and can feel an unmistakable cancel/timeout signal in a pocket or noisy room. Accidental talk-button presses stop being ambiguous approval.
- effort: High for the prototype spin: enclosure/PCB redesign, button debounce and haptic driver, battery characterization, firmware event protocol, accessibility testing, and relay/Mac lease integration. Keep current DK firmware as a compatibility mode during bring-up.  ·  risk: More controls can confuse users or cause false confirms; use guarded geometry, long-press/chord confirmation, haptic+LED acknowledgement, and an always-available cancel. Added motor noise and power draw need testing; if the new module fails, fall back to talk-only with all consequential actions blocked.
- cost: Rough prototype BOM increment: about $3–8 (momentary switch/capacitive pad, ERM/LRA motor, driver, RGB LED controller, enclosure changes), with short haptic events drawing tens of mA and negligible standby. Production cost depends on enclosure/assembly.  ·  latency: Local confirmation feedback under 100 ms; no network round trip required to cancel. Approval still waits for relay acknowledgement before execution.
- security: Physical approval becomes a possession factor, not sole authorization: bind each lease to job hash, display/announce a short spoken summary, expire quickly, and require server-side policy checks. Never transmit raw secrets through the pendant.
- depends on: Action-lease protocol shared by faculty-judgement, faculty-action, relay, and Mac/browser runners; Durable checkpointed saga runner with typed proof receipts; A production pendant PCB/enclosure revision; current nRF9160 DK has only one button and one LED

### `model-routing` — Add a locality-aware private-data execution plane. Before a job crosses the Mac/relay boundary, classify each field and task segment locally; keep authenticated-page DOM, credentials, message bodies, and screenshots on the Mac/browser, run extraction/redaction there, and send the relay only a typed minimal projection (or an encrypted model request when local inference is insufficient). Return a provenance map so faculty-action can still act on the original private tab without copying its contents into shared prompts or logs. Make the policy explicit per task and fail closed when classification is uncertain.
- **owner gets:** The owner could ask the system to work across logged-in accounts without having private emails, financial details, or page screenshots silently become relay context. They would get the same useful recommendation or prepared action, but sensitive source material would remain on the machine that already holds the session.
- effort: High: field-level sensitivity classifier and redaction engine on the Mac/browser, typed projection schema, relay payload enforcement, encrypted ephemeral exceptions, provenance references, audit/redaction tests, and model-routing fallback for cases local processing cannot handle.  ·  risk: Over-redaction can make a task fail or lose necessary context; under-redaction is a privacy incident. Recover by showing which fields were withheld, requiring explicit approval for an exceptional encrypted relay request, and retaining only short-lived hashes/references. Never allow raw browser DOM or screenshots into generic logs.
- cost: Slightly higher local CPU and storage; lower relay token cost because prompts contain projections rather than full pages. A small classifier can run locally; expensive inference is reserved for ambiguous fields.  ·  latency: Adds roughly 100–500 ms for local classification/redaction. Large private-page tasks may become faster because less context crosses the network.
- security: Meaningfully reduces exposure and cross-surface context leakage, but introduces a high-value local policy boundary. Use per-job keys, memory-only sensitive buffers where possible, strict log scrubbing, and explicit provenance labels.
- depends on: A typed cross-surface task/projection schema; Local browser and Mac execution hooks that can return references instead of raw page contents; Relay enforcement that rejects unclassified sensitive payloads; Dashboard/audit display of what data stayed local versus what was transmitted

### `hardware` — Add a hardware-backed identity and key store to the production pendant (for example a secure element on the currently free I2C bus), with per-device keys, monotonic anti-replay counter, and tamper-aware pairing state. The pendant signs action-lease approvals, cancellation tombstones, and offline voice/event spools; the relay and Mac bridge verify signatures before accepting them. Keep private task content out of the secure element—only hashes, counters, and keys live there—and provide a physical re-pair/revoke procedure.
- **owner gets:** The pendant becomes a trustworthy possession rather than merely an authenticated network client: an approval or emergency cancel made from the device remains attributable to that device even after a reconnect, queued offline events cannot be replayed, and replacing a lost pendant does not require trusting an old session forever.
- effort: Medium-high hardware/firmware/security effort: secure-element selection and PCB integration over free I2C, manufacturing provisioning, TF-M/Zephyr driver, relay and Mac verification, key rotation/revocation UX, and recovery testing.  ·  risk: Lost-device recovery or provisioning mistakes could lock the owner out; a compromised relay could still misuse a currently valid lease if policy is weak. Mitigate with short-lived signed leases, server-side job hashes and expiry, a second recovery factor, revocation records, and a factory-reset procedure that invalidates old keys.
- cost: Approximately $0.80–$2.50 component cost in volume plus provisioning/assembly work; negligible standby power and a few milliseconds per signature.  ·  latency: Tens of milliseconds for local signing; no meaningful impact on ordinary conversation. Offline approval remains available and verifies on reconnect.
- security: Improves authenticity and replay resistance substantially, while creating manufacturing-key custody obligations. Provisioning keys must never be shipped in firmware or logs; audit all key lifecycle operations.
- depends on: A production pendant PCB revision with secure-element footprint and protected provisioning flow; Signed action-lease/cancellation protocol shared by pendant, relay, Mac, and browser; Owner recovery and revocation UX; Privacy-preserving payload/projection policy so signatures cover hashes rather than sensitive content
