## Capabilities it proposed

### "After I approve an action, carry it out across my devices and tell me whether it actually happened—not merely that a command was sent. If a device goes offline, keep the action safe, resume when it returns, and show me the evidence or the exact point where it stopped."
- **useful because:** Today receipts can confirm that the Mac agent accepted work, while the browser is offline and the pendant is absent; neither proves the external world changed. This gives the owner a trustworthy boundary between requested, attempted, and externally verified, with safe recovery rather than duplicate clicks or silent failure.
- **path:** relay-realtime → faculty-judgement → faculty-action → mac-planner → mac-terminal → mac-vision → browser-extension → unified
- **model tier:** Use the realtime tier only to capture the owner's approval and report urgent state; use a cheaper background worker for retries, polling, reconciliation, and evidence summarization.
- **latency:** Acknowledge approval in under 1 second; reversible Mac actions can complete in seconds. Offline recovery may wait for the bridge/browser heartbeat, with bounded retries and a spoken/status update rather than holding the conversation open.
- **cost:** Typically <$0.01 per action lifecycle when verification is mostly typed receipts/API reads; occasional model-based reconciliation costs more. Dominant costs are browser/Mac round trips and any screenshot or long page extraction, not the durable state machine.
- **security:** Private browser pages and Mac state leave their devices only as minimum evidence (URL/title, target identifiers, before/after hashes, timestamps), never raw page contents by default. Irreversible sends/purchases/deletions require a fresh explicit confirmation immediately before execution. Leases, idempotency keys, expiry, cancellation, and duplicate suppression are mandatory; if proof is unavailable, report unknown rather than claiming success.
- **missing:** A durable cross-surface action transaction record with states requested/approved/leased/attempted/verified/unknown/failed; Step-level preconditions and postconditions plus typed proof adapters for AppleScript/Mac APIs and authenticated browser tabs; An offline-safe retry/resume worker keyed by idempotency and tab/session affinity; A compact owner-facing evidence receipt and pendant notification path; no pendant is currently registered; Owner-configurable expiry and confirmation policy for actions that become stale

### "Give you a bounded standing permission for a kind of task—such as scheduling meetings within my working hours or reordering a specific item below a price cap—and have you carry out matching actions across my Mac and logged-in browser without asking me every time, while stopping and asking when any limit, ambiguity, or sensitive field is encountered."
- **useful because:** Today the owner must either approve every individual mutation or grant broad, opaque control. A constrained authority lets routine work happen while preserving their intent: the mind can decide and act within explicit limits, but cannot silently widen scope or improvise past a boundary.
- **path:** faculty-judgement → faculty-action → relay-realtime → mac-planner → mac-terminal → browser-extension → unified
- **model tier:** Use the realtime tier only when obtaining or changing the owner's authority policy. Compile policies and evaluate routine actions with a cheaper background model plus deterministic checks; escalate ambiguous matches to realtime or the owner.
- **latency:** Policy evaluation should be sub-100 ms locally at the relay/Mac boundary. Routine approved actions can complete asynchronously within seconds; boundary crossings should interrupt promptly and request confirmation.
- **cost:** Usually <$0.01 per routine action because matching, caps, time windows, and field classifications are deterministic. Model cost is reserved for ambiguous task-to-policy matching and concise exception explanations.
- **security:** Permissions must be least-privilege, explicit, revocable, expiring, and scoped by app/site/account, action type, amount, time window, and sensitive-field class. Never infer permission from conversational tone. Show an action preview when a policy is first used; require confirmation for sends, purchases, deletions, identity/security changes, or any over-cap action. Keep credentials on the Mac/browser and record policy version plus evidence, not secrets or raw private-page contents.
- **missing:** A typed authority-policy schema and compiler shared by judgement and action; A deterministic preflight evaluator that can inspect planned Mac/browser steps and reject out-of-scope mutations before dispatch; Per-policy leases, quotas, expiry, revocation, and an owner-visible audit/review surface; Cross-surface identity/account binding so a rule cannot accidentally apply to the wrong browser session or Mac account; A safe exception path that pauses at the exact violating step and asks for one-time approval without broadening the standing rule


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `relay` — Add an Action Transaction Coordinator between /plan, /execute, the job queue, Mac/browser executors, and the reporting surfaces. Persist one transaction per owner-approved intent with an idempotency key, action lease expiry, ordered steps, preconditions, postconditions, and evidence references. Dispatch only leased steps; accept typed executor receipts; run read-only postcondition checks through AppleScript/typed Mac routes or the bound browser session; classify each step as verified, failed, blocked, or unknown. On bridge/browser recovery, resume only unexpired, uncommitted leases and never replay a verified step. Expose a compact status/evidence endpoint for relay-realtime and a durable event stream for the pendant when one exists.
- **owner gets:** The owner will stop hearing “done” when the Mac merely queued a command, and will not accidentally perform an action twice after a disconnect. They get a clear, evidence-backed answer—what changed, what did not, and what needs their decision—even when the browser or Mac disappears mid-task.
- effort: Medium-high: shared transaction schema and migrations, coordinator worker, executor adapters, postcondition library for common Calendar/Mail/Finder/browser operations, recovery tests, and a small status projection.  ·  risk: A wrong postcondition could falsely report success or trigger an unsafe retry. Default to unknown and stop on ambiguity; require idempotency and explicit confirmation for irreversible steps. Recover by canceling expired leases and retaining immutable receipts.
- cost: Small persistent D1/R2 metadata cost and background polling; model cost near zero for typed checks, with occasional cheap summarization. No hardware cost.  ·  latency: Adds one typed verification round trip (usually <2 s); asynchronous tasks are reported through status rather than blocking voice.
- security: Store hashes, identifiers, timestamps, and minimal snippets instead of private page contents; enforce bearer auth and per-session affinity. Never export credentials or raw authenticated-page data to the relay by default.
- depends on: Durable job runner (chg-16bc5dee) or equivalent queue worker; Step verification/proof tools already requested but not yet delivered; Owner confirmation and action-lease policy; At least one online executor; current Mac bridge is online but browser and pendant are offline

### `browser-harness` — Implement a reconnect quarantine for pending browser commands. When the extension heartbeat returns, stop automatic execution, inventory all queued commands with request/idempotency key, target tab/session, age, and intended mutation, compare them with relay/Mac transaction records, and mark each as duplicate, stale, safe-to-resume, or requiring fresh approval. Resume only read-only or explicitly re-approved mutations; publish a reconciliation brief and preserve a per-command audit trail.
- **owner gets:** The browser is currently offline with nine pending commands. When it comes back, the owner should not face a burst of stale clicks, duplicate submissions, or actions against the wrong tab. They get one concise review of what survived the outage and safe continuation only where intent is still valid.
- effort: Medium: queue metadata and transaction lookup, heartbeat transition hook, quarantine state, reconciliation worker, and a review/status endpoint.  ·  risk: A classifier may misjudge whether a command is stale or duplicate. Fail closed for mutations, retain commands for explicit review, and allow cancellation without execution. Recovery is deterministic replay only after a new lease and approval.
- cost: Negligible storage and one background reconciliation pass; no extra model call for key/age/session checks, optional low-cost summary for the review brief.  ·  latency: Adds a short inventory delay after reconnect; read-only commands can resume within seconds, while mutations wait for review.
- security: Keep private URL/page details local to the browser/Mac; relay receives only command type, session fingerprint, timestamps, and hashes. Require bearer authentication and do not log form contents.
- depends on: Browser command queue with request IDs, idempotency keys, and tab/session affinity (chg-14accc01); A durable transaction/lease record connecting browser commands to /jobs receipts; Browser heartbeat/poll/result routes; Owner confirmation policy for stale or irreversible commands

### `integration` — Introduce a capability-token authority layer shared by faculty-judgement, relay, Mac, and browser executors. Judgement issues a signed, narrow token containing policy ID/version, permitted operation grammar, resource/account scope, quantitative limits, sensitive-field exclusions, expiry, max uses, and required confirmation levels. Before every concrete Mac or browser step, the executor verifies the token against the normalized step and consumes a nonce; a mismatch hard-stops without dispatch. The relay exposes revoke-all and token-status, while the Mac/browser surfaces return the exact failed predicate. Persist only token hashes and audit metadata, and support an offline revocation cache on the Mac so a stale grant cannot survive relay loss.
- **owner gets:** The owner can delegate routine authority without handing the system a vague promise or permanent access. A mistaken interpretation becomes a blocked step with a precise explanation, not an accidental purchase, message, deletion, or account change. Revoking authority remains possible even during a network outage.
- effort: High: policy grammar, signed-token issuance/verification, normalization of Mac and browser actions, nonce/replay protection, revocation distribution/cache, UI for review/revoke, and adversarial boundary tests.  ·  risk: Normalization bugs could either over-block useful work or authorize a step that only appears equivalent. Fail closed, use conservative operation grammars, require explicit confirmation for unknown verbs, and ship test vectors for account, amount, recipient, time, and sensitive-field boundaries. Recovery is token revocation and manual one-time approval.
- cost: Small D1 metadata and signature-verification overhead; no recurring model cost for in-scope checks. Engineering cost is substantial; no hardware purchase required, though an offline pendant revoke control would be a later enhancement.  ·  latency: Adds local signature/policy verification per step (milliseconds); remote revocation may add one check at transaction start, with cached deny lists for offline operation.
- security: Improves least privilege and replay resistance, but creates a high-value authority service. Protect signing keys in the relay, rotate them, bind tokens to device/session identity, redact audit payloads, and make revocation auditable. Never put credentials or unrestricted browser content in a token.
- depends on: A typed action representation spanning Mac and browser executors; Owner-facing policy creation/review/revocation UX; Durable per-step transaction records and idempotency; A secure relay-to-Mac/browser key-distribution and revocation channel
