# Harness derivation — faculty-action — round 69

Model: `gpt-5.6-luna`  ·  probes against `http://localhost:8000`

## What it established

- **action-execution integration gap** — The live route table has planning, execution, jobs, receipts, cancellation/undo, browser sessions/results, and pipeline events, but no route or state named escrow, lease, precondition, postcondition, confirmation, or verification. The missing value is the atomic connective state machine between existing primitives, not another executor.
  - evidence: Fresh discover(routes) returned /plan, /execute, /jobs..., /browser/result/:commandId, /pipeline/events and no escrow/lease/verify/confirmation route.

## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Do this, but only if the right account, page, and conditions are still true; ask me on the pendant at the last safe moment, then prove it actually happened.”"
- **useful because:** Today the mind can decide an action and individual surfaces can execute it, but there is no single durable handoff that binds the decision to the correct authenticated browser tab/Mac account, obtains owner-presence confirmation, expires safely, and verifies the resulting world state. This would prevent a stale tab, wrong account, navigation race, or dropped connection from turning an intended action into a silent mistake. It is genuinely cross-surface: the pendant establishes presence, relay holds the lease, Mac/browser performs the act, and perception proves the postcondition.
- **path:** faculty-judgement emits a typed intent, allowed scope, deadline, and machine-checkable postcondition → relay creates a durable action escrow/lease, binds browser session/tab or Mac target, and waits through reconnects → pendant presents a terse local summary and nonce; owner confirms with the physical button or spoken confirmation, with a short expiry → mac-planner/mac-terminal or browser-extension executes only the listed reversible/irreversible steps and emits before/after evidence → faculty-perception re-reads the target (page state, Calendar/Mail record, filesystem, or API result) and checks the postcondition independently → faculty-action closes the escrow with a signed receipt, or marks it expired/failed and tells the owner exactly which condition was not met
- **model tier:** Use a cheaper background model to compile and validate intents and postconditions; reserve realtime only for the pendant's confirmation exchange. Use deterministic route/action validation and perception checks wherever possible, not an expensive model call.
- **latency:** Confirmation prompt should reach the pendant in under 1 second after the target is ready; execution generally under 10 seconds; postcondition verification may take up to 30 seconds and can continue as a durable job after disconnect. Never keep an approval lease alive indefinitely: default 2 minutes, configurable per action.
- **cost:** Roughly $0.01–$0.08 per invocation depending on whether semantic page comparison is needed; most cost is one planner/verification inference and browser/Mac round trips. Deterministic receipts and lease handling are negligible.
- **security:** The escrow must contain least-privilege target/account/session bindings, a nonce to prevent replay, an expiry, and an explicit irreversible-action class. Do not send credentials or page contents to the pendant; show only a redacted summary and destination. Require fresh confirmation for send/purchase/delete/permission changes, reject if account, URL origin, tab identity, or relevant fields drift, and retain evidence only for the owner's configured short retention. A failed verification must never be reported as success. Browser bridge and bearer-token scope remain trust boundaries.
- **missing:** durable action-escrow/lease state with atomic claim and expiry; pendant local confirmation/nonce UI and offline-safe confirmation semantics; typed precondition and postcondition schemas plus deterministic drift checks; a faculty-perception verification call that can inspect the same bound target after execution; cross-surface signed evidence envelope and idempotency key

### "“Don’t just do it—tell me when it is independently witnessed as done, and tell me immediately if the witnesses disagree.”"
- **useful because:** The owner cannot currently distinguish an executor's acknowledgement from a real-world completion. A browser can report a click while the server rejects it, a Mac can report a file write while sync fails, or a relay can lose the result. This gives the owner a trustworthy completion answer based on independent witnesses rather than another optimistic receipt. It is new beyond ordinary job status, receipts, or undo because completion requires correlated evidence from the action surface and a separate observer, with disagreement surfaced as uncertainty.
- **path:** faculty-judgement declares the expected outcome and which independent witnesses are required → relay correlates a single action id across the pendant, Mac, browser, and any public endpoint, and applies a quorum policy → Mac/browser executor emits signed before/after evidence tied to the target session and idempotency key → faculty-perception independently re-reads the resulting state or observes a separate confirmation channel, producing a freshness-bounded witness → pendant announces witnessed, disputed, or unknown in plain language and keeps a compact unresolved alert until the owner dismisses it → faculty-action reconciles witnesses, retries only when safe, and records the disagreement and final disposition
- **model tier:** Use deterministic witness correlation and state comparison first; use a cheap background model only to normalize heterogeneous evidence. Realtime is needed only to announce a dispute or answer the owner immediately.
- **latency:** Normal completion in 2–10 seconds; a dispute should reach the pendant within 2 seconds. Witnesses may continue asynchronously for up to 60 seconds, after which the result is explicitly unknown rather than success.
- **cost:** About $0.005–$0.04 per action, dominated by one independent readback and occasional normalization; correlation, hashes, and timeouts are negligible.
- **security:** Witnesses must be independent in both credentials and failure mode where possible; never count two reports derived from the same executor as separate proof. Bind evidence to origin/session/tab, nonce, timestamp, and action hash. Redact private page contents, retain only hashes/snippets for short configurable retention, and require owner confirmation before any retry that could duplicate an external side effect.
- **missing:** witness-quorum protocol and typed evidence envelope; independent readback adapters for browser, Mac apps, and public APIs; dispute/unknown state in spoken and dashboard UX; idempotent safe-retry classification


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Add an Action Escrow state machine between /plan, /execute, the browser command queue, and job receipts rather than treating them as independent calls. Persist one escrow record with intent hash, idempotency key, bound surface/session/tab/account, preconditions, postcondition, owner-confirmation nonce, lease expiry, and evidence pointers. Transition atomically through proposed → target-verified → awaiting-pendant-confirmation → executing → independently-verified → committed, with expired/drifted/failed terminal states. Make /execute refuse an unconfirmed or drifted escrow; make browser/Mac results append evidence to the same record; make faculty-perception close only after a fresh read; expose the complete state through existing /jobs/:jobId and receipts and make undo reference the committed escrow, not merely the last action.
- **owner gets:** A spoken decision becomes a trustworthy real-world outcome instead of a best-effort chain of disconnected calls. If the owner walks away, a tab changes, Wi-Fi drops, or the wrong account is active, the action expires or stops visibly; if it proceeds, the owner gets proof tied to the exact target and before/after state.
- effort: Medium-high: shared schema and atomic transitions in relay/backend, adapters in Mac and browser agents, pendant confirmation transport, and a postcondition verifier. Start with Calendar/Mail/browser form submissions and filesystem changes before expanding to purchases or deletes.  ·  risk: A state-machine bug could block legitimate actions or duplicate an action. Use idempotency keys, compare-and-set transitions, dry-run simulation, replay tests, and retain the existing receipts/undo path as fallback. If verification times out, report unknown—not success—and leave the escrow open only until its explicit deadline.
- cost: Small persistent metadata/evidence overhead per action; one extra verification request and occasional cheap model call. No continuous polling when the owner is absent.  ·  latency: Adds roughly 0.5–2 seconds for target/precondition verification and confirmation; postcondition verification can be asynchronous, with durable job status available immediately.
- security: Improves safety by binding actions to origin/session/account and requiring a fresh nonce. Evidence must be redacted and short-lived; bearer tokens never go to the pendant. Irreversible actions remain separately classified and confirmation-gated.
- depends on: Typed precondition/postcondition schema; Pendant confirmation transport (currently unavailable); Faculty-perception readback endpoint; Atomic durable job/lease storage

### `integration` — Introduce a Witness Quorum layer between execution and completion reporting. Every side effect gets an action hash and evidence envelope. The executor's receipt is only one witness; a separate observer must independently re-read the target or an independent confirmation channel. Correlate witnesses by action hash, target binding, and freshness; classify the outcome as witnessed, disputed, or unknown. Expose that classification through job status/receipts and prevent the relay from saying “done” when only the executor acknowledged. For safe operations, allow an explicitly classified retry; for non-idempotent operations, freeze and request owner review.
- **owner gets:** The owner gets an honest answer about whether something actually happened, rather than a confident “done” that may mean only that a click or API call was attempted. Failures become actionable disputes with evidence, and silent partial completion stops being mistaken for success.
- effort: High integration effort: evidence envelope and correlation service, independent readback adapters, state/timeout handling, and pendant/dashboard language. Pilot with one browser form and one filesystem operation.  ·  risk: Independent observers can disagree or observe stale state; false disputes could annoy the owner, while weak independence could create false confidence. Enforce freshness windows, source independence metadata, explicit unknown outcomes, and never auto-retry irreversible actions.
- cost: One additional readback per action and small durable evidence records; cheap compared with the risk of an unverified external side effect.  ·  latency: Adds 1–10 seconds before declaring completion; the owner can hear “executor finished, verification pending” immediately.
- security: Evidence must be scoped to the target and redacted. Independent observers need narrowly scoped read access, never broad credential export. Action hashes and nonces prevent replay or cross-job evidence substitution.
- depends on: Typed evidence envelope; Independent observer/readback adapters; A status vocabulary that distinguishes attempted, witnessed, disputed, and unknown; Idempotency classification for retry policy
