## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Do this for me, but only if the world still matches what we agreed; tell me exactly what changed, and stop safely if anything is different.”"
- **useful because:** Turns the mind’s decision into a safe, auditable real-world action across the pendant, relay, Mac, and private browser. The owner gets a single spoken result—completed with evidence, paused for approval, or safely stopped—rather than trusting that a queued command probably ran.
- **path:** faculty-judgement creates a typed action contract with target, preconditions, allowed mutations, expiry, and postconditions → relay validates the contract and issues a short-lived action lease; the pendant button/LED and voice indicate leased/running/paused state → mac-planner dispatches reversible steps through mac_run_actions or mac_delegate; browser-extension executes tab-affine private-page steps through browser_run_actions → relay records before/after evidence, receipts, and failures; faculty-perception verifies postconditions and pendant playback acknowledgement → relay-realtime speaks only the compact status/result and offers retry or approval when required
- **model tier:** Use the cheaper background model for contract compilation, evidence comparison, and retries; reserve realtime only for the owner’s live utterance and the short final status.
- **latency:** Lease issuance under 300 ms; first progress cue under 1 s; ordinary Mac/browser actions complete in their native time. A dropped link pauses rather than silently continuing.
- **cost:** About $0.01–$0.05 per ordinary action depending on evidence reconciliation; realtime speech dominates interactive cost, while receipts and hashes are negligible.
- **security:** Private page text and screenshots must stay on the Mac/relay under existing bearer/session controls; persist hashes and minimal excerpts by default. Never submit an irreversible browser or external message action without explicit confirmation bound to the exact contract hash. Expired leases, changed preconditions, tab loss, or missing pendant playback ACK must halt and surface the reason.
- **missing:** A durable action-contract/lease schema shared by judgement, relay, Mac, and browser; A verifier that can check typed preconditions/postconditions and a real pendant playback ACK; An atomic pause/resume boundary and contract-hash-bound confirmation UI/voice flow

### "“When I walk away from my Mac, put my private work into a safe state; when I come back, restore exactly what I was doing.”"
- **useful because:** The owner gets automatic, presence-bound privacy and continuity: sensitive browser sessions can be locked or obscured when the pendant leaves, while non-sensitive work remains recoverable. Returning to the desk restores the prior workspace without requiring them to remember a security ritual.
- **path:** The pendant emits a rotating, privacy-preserving proximity nonce over BLE and gives a local tactile cue when the protection boundary changes → The Mac bridge measures authenticated proximity, captures an encrypted workspace manifest, locks or masks designated applications and private browser tabs, and later restores only the recorded state → The browser extension labels tabs by sensitivity and can replace private pages with a local lock screen while preserving tab/session identity → The relay stores only the encrypted manifest and receives presence transitions, surviving a Mac sleep or temporary link loss → faculty-judgement selects the policy and asks once which applications/tabs are protected; faculty-action executes the transition and reports a compact result to the pendant
- **model tier:** Use a small/background model only to classify workspace sensitivity and reconcile restoration conflicts; proximity transitions and lock/mask actions must be deterministic local code. Realtime is unnecessary except for an optional spoken warning.
- **latency:** Protection should begin within 2 seconds of confirmed departure and restoration within 3 seconds of return. If confidence is ambiguous, leave the workspace protected rather than oscillating.
- **cost:** Near-zero model cost during normal use; occasional background classification is roughly $0.001–$0.01 per workspace change. Main cost is engineering and modest encrypted manifest storage.
- **security:** The pendant must not broadcast a stable owner identifier or location. Use rotating authenticated nonces, local key agreement, replay protection, and a grace period for brief signal loss. Never transmit page contents or credentials to the relay. Restoration must require the same pendant identity and a fresh nonce; if the pendant is lost, the owner can revoke its key from the Mac. Sensitive browser pages should be masked or locked, not closed or reloaded, to avoid data loss.
- **missing:** A product pendant with authenticated BLE proximity and a secure key store; A Mac-local presence daemon integrated with lock/mask APIs and sleep/wake handling; Browser-extension sensitivity labels and a tab-preserving lock surface; An encrypted workspace-manifest store with key revocation and conflict handling; Owner-configurable protected application/tab policy and a clear local override

### "“If I hold the pendant button, revoke this device and stop anything acting as me everywhere.”"
- **useful because:** Provides a fast, physical kill switch when the owner suspects a lost pendant, exposed screen, or runaway automation. It can stop queued Mac/browser work and revoke the pendant’s authority even when the owner cannot reach the Mac or speak safely.
- **path:** The pendant detects a deliberately long press locally, shows a distinct LED/tactile pattern, and sends a signed emergency-revoke event when connectivity exists → The relay immediately marks the pendant key revoked, cancels queued jobs, invalidates action leases, and fans out the event to all reachable surfaces → The Mac bridge locks the session, terminates or freezes agent-owned processes, and removes temporary credentials and browser bridge authorization → The browser extension closes or locks designated authenticated tabs and invalidates its command channel without deleting user data → faculty-action returns only a terse revoked/partially-revoked status and retains a durable incident receipt for later owner review
- **model tier:** Deterministic firmware and relay control path; no model call is needed for activation or propagation. Use a cheap background model only later to summarize what was stopped and what could not be reached.
- **latency:** Local revoke cue immediately; relay fan-out under 1 second when connected; unreachable surfaces remain revoked at the relay and must fail closed when they reconnect.
- **cost:** Negligible per event; one signed event and small receipt. Engineering cost is concentrated in credential scoping, cancellation, and fail-closed reconnect behavior.
- **security:** Require a long press plus a clearly different local cue to reduce accidental activation, but do not require speech or network access. Protect the revoke key in secure device storage, prevent replay, and make revocation monotonic. Since a panic action can destroy unsaved work, lock/freeze before termination and preserve only minimal diagnostic metadata. Re-enrollment must require deliberate owner action on a trusted surface.
- **missing:** Secure per-pendant revoke key and monotonic revocation registry; Firmware long-press handling that works offline; Relay fan-out and fail-closed reconnect protocol; Mac process/credential quarantine and browser bridge invalidation hooks; Owner-controlled re-enrollment and incident review UI


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Implement a contract-bound action transaction that sits between /plan, /execute, the job queue, Mac/browser runners, and pipeline events. At plan time, canonicalize target, preconditions, permitted side effects, expiry, and confirmation requirement into contractHash. At execution, atomically acquire a short lease and emit lease/running/paused events; every Mac or browser step must include contractHash, requestId, tab/session affinity, and a before-state digest. Reject stale or mismatched steps. On completion, run postcondition checks against fresh reads, append a signed receipt containing evidence references and the exact contractHash, and only then emit completed for spoken playback. If the pendant playback predicate is absent, emit delivered-to-relay rather than falsely claiming the owner heard it. Reuse jobs/undo-last only for steps explicitly marked reversible.
- **owner gets:** The owner can trust “done” or “stopped” in a way they cannot today: a changed webpage, lost tab, stale plan, dropped pendant, or partial Mac failure cannot silently turn into an unverified action. They also get a precise explanation and an undo path for the parts that are genuinely reversible.
- effort: Medium-high: shared schema and state machine in relay, adapters in Mac/browser runners, postcondition verifier, receipt format, and pipeline event vocabulary; then failure-injection tests for expiry, duplicate delivery, tab replacement, and link loss.  ·  risk: A strict verifier may pause work that would have succeeded, and old clients may omit contractHash. Roll out in shadow mode, accept legacy jobs as unverified, and provide an explicit retry with a newly compiled contract. Never auto-replay an irreversible step.
- cost: Negligible storage/CPU for hashes and state; one extra lightweight verification/read per mutation. Background model cost remains low; no extra realtime call except the final concise status.  ·  latency: Adds roughly 100–500 ms for lease and local digest checks, plus native time for a postcondition read. Failures become earlier pauses instead of long silent retries.
- security: Improves replay and confused-deputy resistance by binding every mutation to a short-lived, user-approved contract and tab/session. Keep evidence redacted/minimal and avoid storing private page bodies by default.
- depends on: A durable shared action-contract schema; A verifier for preconditions/postconditions (the pending verify_operation_step/verify_action_proof capability); A real pendant playback acknowledgement predicate; until then, use the honest delivered-to-relay state; Existing receipt/undo implementation and browser request-id/tab affinity

### `integration` — Add a compensating-recovery planner for partially completed cross-surface actions. When a job stops after step n, derive a bounded recovery graph from each completed step's declared compensation (for example, restore a draft field or move a file back), distinguish safe compensation from irreversible external effects, and present the owner a spoken/inspectable choice: leave as-is, compensate selected reversible steps, or resume from the exact checkpoint after a fresh precondition check. Store the partial-state snapshot and compensation result in the same receipt, rather than treating jobs/undo-last as a single global undo.
- **owner gets:** A failed multi-step request will not leave them guessing whether half the work happened or risk an indiscriminate undo. They can recover a draft or file operation safely while being warned clearly about the message, purchase, or submission that cannot be rolled back.
- effort: Medium: extend job step metadata with compensation descriptors and state snapshots, add recovery planning and explicit user choice, then test crashes between every pair of steps across Mac and browser adapters.  ·  risk: A compensation can itself fail or have side effects, and snapshots may contain private data. Permit only adapter-declared reversible operations, require fresh verification before compensation, encrypt/minimize snapshots, and mark the transaction unresolved if recovery cannot be proven.
- cost: Small durable metadata overhead; one cheap background planning call only for failed multi-step jobs. No additional realtime model call beyond the status prompt.  ·  latency: No cost on successful jobs; failed jobs gain seconds for recovery analysis, with immediate paused status first.
- security: Prevents unsafe blanket replay/undo and limits recovery to the original contract scope, tab/session, and owner approval. Redact sensitive field values from receipts unless needed for recovery.
- depends on: The existing durable job/receipt and actionReceipt work; Per-step contractHash, idempotency key, and before-state evidence; Mac/browser adapters declaring which operations are compensatable; The pending verify_operation_step/verify_action_proof capability
