## Capabilities it proposed

### "“I got interrupted—catch me up from where I left off.”"
- **useful because:** The pendant is the only surface present at the moment of interruption, while the Mac and authenticated browser contain the work state. This would reconstruct a bounded delta (messages, calendar changes, open tabs, unfinished jobs, and the last agreed next action), distinguish facts from guesses, and speak one short prioritized handoff rather than making the owner remember which surface held what.
- **path:** pendant records a local interruption marker with time and optional spoken context, even if the link is down → relay persists and routes the marker, correlating it with the last conversation/job/session IDs → mac-planner reads recent pipeline/journal/job receipts and calendar/mail/notes through the granted AppleScript routes, then produces a cited delta → browser-extension, when it reconnects, reads only already-open authenticated tabs and reports changes since the marker → relay merges the evidence, asks faculty-judgement to rank only actionable deltas, and returns a short spoken handoff plus a resumable 'next action' to the pendant
- **model tier:** Use a cheap background model for extraction/diffing and citation normalization; reserve realtime only for the spoken request, ambiguity, or an unsafe suggested action.
- **latency:** Acknowledge locally in under 300 ms. Return an initial Mac-only handoff in 5–10 s; append browser evidence when the bridge reconnects, with a clear late-arrival cue. No outbound sends or destructive actions without the existing confirmation policy.
- **cost:** Usually <$0.01 per handoff when extraction is bounded; dominant costs are model tokens for cross-surface reconciliation and optional speech generation, not the local marker.
- **security:** The marker and evidence may expose private mail/calendar/browser data, so encrypt the pending marker, use a short TTL, and include source/scope/timestamp in the spoken answer. Never infer that an unread message is urgent without evidence; never read closed tabs or send replies. Require confirmation before any resume action that sends, deletes, buys, or changes a meeting.
- **missing:** A durable interruption marker schema shared by pendant, relay, Mac, and browser, with monotonic time and last-known session/job IDs; A late-evidence merge protocol that can revise a spoken handoff without duplicating or contradicting it; A small resumable-state record containing the last user-approved next action and its preconditions; The already-requested local interruption gate and cross-surface recovery primitives need to be implemented, not merely named

### "“Why did I decide this, and what would change my mind?”"
- **useful because:** People lose the reasoning behind choices even when the underlying messages and calendar events still exist. The owner should get a dated, source-linked reconstruction of the decision—not a confident invented explanation—with the unresolved assumptions and the concrete evidence that would invalidate it. This is different from a search, briefing, or task reminder: it preserves agency and makes old decisions revisable without erasing why they were made.
- **path:** pendant captures the question and can read the answer aloud privately → relay assigns a bounded decision inquiry and preserves provenance across the participating surfaces → mac-planner searches dated Mail, Calendar, Notes, reminders, and local project journals for the relevant decision window → browser-extension inspects only owner-selected already-open authenticated tabs for supporting or contradicting evidence → faculty-perception extracts claims with timestamps and source snippets; faculty-judgement separates explicit reasons from inferred hypotheses, identifies conflicts, and states what evidence would change the conclusion; faculty-action may create a review reminder only after confirmation
- **model tier:** Use a cheaper background model for retrieval, chronology, and claim clustering; use realtime only to clarify an ambiguous referent or deliver the short spoken answer.
- **latency:** Acknowledge in under 300 ms, return a first evidence-backed answer in 8–15 seconds, and allow a longer cited report to finish asynchronously. Never block the owner on exhaustive history.
- **cost:** Approximately $0.01–$0.05 per inquiry depending on the number of dated sources; retrieval and context transmission dominate, while speech is minor.
- **security:** This crosses highly private mail, notes, calendar, and authenticated pages. Require an explicit scope (decision/topic and time window), minimize excerpts, retain claim provenance rather than raw content, encrypt the inquiry, and expire it. Clearly label explicit owner statements versus model inference; never contact anyone or alter a calendar item without confirmation.
- **missing:** A first-class decision record with immutable source claims, competing hypotheses, confidence, and an owner-editable rationale; Cross-surface temporal retrieval that can align Mail/Calendar/Notes/browser evidence without leaking unrelated private text; A contradiction and revision protocol so the owner can mark a reason as wrong or superseded while preserving the historical record; A spoken answer format that says what is known, inferred, and still unknown in one short turn


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Add an interruption/resumption ledger spanning pendant, relay, Mac, and browser. On a pendant button/voice marker, persist markerId, monotonic and wall-clock time, conversation/job/session IDs, user words, and a bounded evidence scope. Each surface returns evidence packets keyed to markerId with source, observedAt, freshness, confidence, and content hash. Relay emits one initial handoff and later patch events for late browser evidence; deduplicates by packet hash and expires private payloads while retaining an auditable receipt. Store only the approved next action and its preconditions, never an inferred action.
- **owner gets:** After a meeting, commute, or dropped connection the owner can resume without repeating context or wondering whether the answer came from stale mail, a browser tab, or an unfinished Mac job. Late browser reconnection becomes a useful update instead of silent failure.
- effort: Medium: shared schema, relay persistence/TTL, Mac journal adapter, browser reconnect adapter, pendant marker event, and merge tests for duplicate/late/out-of-order packets.  ·  risk: Stale or contradictory evidence could produce a misleading handoff. Mitigate with explicit as-of times, source labels, confidence, conflict statements, bounded scope, and never auto-executing the resumed action. If relay storage fails, the pendant speaks that the marker was not saved and keeps a short local retry record.
- cost: Negligible storage and relay compute; roughly 1–3k model tokens for reconciliation, with no model call for a simple receipt. Optional TTS is the dominant incremental API cost.  ·  latency: Local marker acknowledgement under 300 ms; first handoff typically 5–10 s; browser evidence may arrive later and be announced as an update.
- security: Potentially sensitive cross-surface correlation. Encrypt pending markers, avoid storing raw mail/page text after TTL, scope browser reads to explicitly open tabs, redact secrets, and require owner confirmation for any external side effect.
- depends on: The pending local_interruption_gate and cross_surface_recovery implementations; A durable cross-surface event/job store with idempotency and receipts; 24 kHz pendant audio path so the initial acknowledgement and late update remain intelligible

### `memory` — Introduce a decision provenance ledger rather than ordinary memory: decisionId, topic/time window, explicit owner-stated reasons, source-anchored claims with quoted-span hashes, alternatives considered, assumptions, confidence, counterevidence, and an owner-editable 'changed my mind' revision chain. Add a temporal retrieval compiler that asks Mac and the authenticated browser for only the scoped evidence, then a contradiction pass that refuses to turn absence of evidence into a reason.
- **owner gets:** Months later, the owner can recover the real rationale behind a choice and see exactly what new fact would justify revisiting it, instead of receiving a plausible but false explanation or having to search every private account manually.
- effort: High: schema and encrypted storage, source adapters for Mail/Calendar/Notes/browser, temporal entity resolution, claim hashing, revision UX, and a short spoken rendering with a cited long form on Mac.  ·  risk: The system may misattribute a reason or expose sensitive context from an unrelated thread. Fail closed on ambiguous identity/time windows, show source and confidence, ask one clarifying question, and let the owner delete or correct any claim. No automatic policy or external action follows a reconstructed rationale.
- cost: Moderate per inquiry (retrieval plus 2–3k reconciliation tokens); durable storage is small if raw excerpts are not retained. Cost is reduced by caching source hashes and only re-reading changed windows.  ·  latency: 8–15 seconds for a first answer; a deeper timeline can complete asynchronously without making the owner wait.
- security: High sensitivity: encrypt at rest and in transit, use per-source scopes and short-lived raw excerpts, retain hashes/provenance by default, and keep secret fields out of model context unless explicitly requested.
- depends on: A typed cross-surface context compiler with temporal and sensitivity filtering; Authenticated Mail/Calendar/Notes/browser adapters that return source spans and timestamps; Owner-editable memory and deletion semantics; A provenance-aware spoken answer renderer
