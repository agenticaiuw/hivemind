## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Do this wherever it can actually be done, and tell me whether it really happened.” For example: send the prepared message, update the appointment, or submit the form—even if I leave the pendant conversation."
- **useful because:** Today the mind can decide an action but can mistake a queued/no-op UI action for success. This capability makes action faculty a reliable hand: choose the reachable surface, execute only where the preflight says it is viable, independently read back the postcondition, and leave a durable receipt or an honest blocked/queued result. It is specifically cross-device: the pendant carries the intent and status back to the owner, the relay persists work while the Mac sleeps, the Mac uses granted AppleScript where possible, and the browser bridge handles private sessions when online.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-planner → browser-extension → unified → faculty-judgement → faculty-action
- **model tier:** Use the realtime model only to capture the short intent and speak status. Use a cheaper background planner/worker for multi-step execution, retries, evidence comparison, and receipt generation.
- **latency:** Preflight and first spoken status under 2 seconds; reversible local actions usually 3–10 seconds; long work continues in the durable queue and reports completion or blockage later. Never say done until a postcondition read-back succeeds.
- **cost:** Low per invocation: mostly Mac/relay/browser I/O and a few background planner turns. Cost is dominated by retries and page extraction, not the realtime conversation; cache preflight and evidence fingerprints to avoid resending context.
- **security:** Private page contents and action evidence stay on the Mac/browser session unless explicitly routed to the relay. Never expose credentials. Destructive or externally visible steps need the existing owner policy/confirmation; a failed preflight must not be silently retried through a different account. Receipts should record target, before/after evidence, timestamp, and why a fallback was or was not used.
- **missing:** A durable cross-surface execution worker with jobId, retry/resume, and result stream (the browser router exists but its durable runner is still missing); A mandatory execution preflight/postcondition contract that distinguishes queued, blocked, no-op, and verified-done; A verification operation that can read back the target without repeating the write; Browser extension online/pending-command drain; Owner-granted Accessibility and Screen Recording if a task truly requires GUI/computer vision; AppleScript fallback must remain usable without them

### "“If part of this multi-step task fails, finish it safely or tell me exactly what is left—not just that the job failed.” For example, reschedule an appointment across my calendar and a logged-in portal, then notify the right person without duplicating or losing any step."
- **useful because:** The current system can receipt individual Mac actions, but the owner cannot safely ask for a consequential workflow spanning Calendar/Mail, a private browser account, and the relay: one side effect may succeed while the next times out, leaving an ambiguous real-world state. A cross-surface saga would checkpoint each effect, detect partial completion, apply safe compensations where possible, and produce a concise reconciliation listing confirmed changes, unperformed steps, and the single next action. This is not a generic durable job or undo button; it handles inconsistent external systems where rollback may be impossible.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-planner → browser-extension → unified → faculty-judgement → faculty-action
- **model tier:** Realtime only captures intent and reads the short checkpoint aloud. A cheaper background worker executes the saga, compares evidence, retries idempotently, and prepares reconciliation; use the expensive model only when evidence conflicts or the recovery plan is ambiguous.
- **latency:** Speak an initial checkpoint within 2 seconds. Each step can run asynchronously; after every side effect, persist evidence before advancing. On interruption, resume from the last confirmed checkpoint rather than replaying earlier writes.
- **cost:** Low-to-moderate background cost: mostly existing Mac/browser calls and receipts, with one cheap reconciliation pass. The dominant cost is conflict resolution when an external site has changed, not routine execution.
- **security:** Private account data remains on its owning browser session unless the owner explicitly permits relay processing. Never infer success from a click response; require read-back evidence. Compensating actions (cancellation, deletion, sending a correction) must be classified as new externally visible effects and follow the owner’s confirmation policy. Preserve an immutable audit trail without storing credentials or full sensitive page contents.
- **missing:** A cross-surface saga state machine with step dependencies, idempotency keys, durable checkpoints, and explicit partial-success states; A typed evidence/reconciliation record that can compare Mac receipts, Calendar/Mail reads, and browser page read-backs; Compensation adapters for safe local effects plus a recovery planner that stops when compensation would itself be risky; A durable browser job runner/result stream for private authenticated steps


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Create an Execution Truth Contract shared by faculty-judgement and faculty-action. Before dispatch, action faculty snapshots /ops/status and /browser/status and classifies each candidate path as viable, blocked, or queued. The dispatcher must attach an idempotency key and expected postcondition to every step, route private browser work only to an online authenticated bridge, prefer granted AppleScript for Mac operations when Accessibility/Screen Recording are false, and persist the classification. After dispatch, a separate read-only verifier checks the postcondition (receipt plus independent state read); only that result may produce “done”. If the bridge is offline or the Mac reports ready:false, emit a truthful blocked/queued state and retain the job rather than claiming success or burning retries.
- **owner gets:** When the owner says “handle it,” they get a dependable answer instead of a success-shaped lie: completed, still working, blocked by a named device permission/offline session, or safely queued. It also lets useful Calendar/Mail/Reminders actions continue through already-granted automation while GUI/browser work waits for the owner’s bridge.
- effort: Medium: shared typed schema, preflight middleware, verifier adapters for receipts/AppleScript/browser reads, durable state transitions, and tests for no-op UI results and offline browser.  ·  risk: A strict verifier may leave work queued that previously appeared successful; recovery is transparent retry or manual completion. Idempotency and expected-state mismatches prevent duplicate sends but can require a fresh plan when the target changed.
- cost: Negligible API cost; one small status read and one read-only verification per job. Background retries use the cheaper tier.  ·  latency: Adds roughly 0.5–2 seconds for preflight and verification on short jobs; long jobs are asynchronous.
- security: Improves safety by preventing private-page failover to public relay and by recording target/evidence without secrets. Externally visible writes remain subject to existing owner policy.
- depends on: Durable browser job runner and result stream (chg-16bc5dee remains incomplete); A verification operation/tool (currently requested but not granted); Action receipts already implemented in chg-5fc73ce3; Owner must manually grant Accessibility/Screen Recording only for GUI paths; this change must not treat those grants as globally available

### `integration` — Add a cross-surface saga coordinator above the existing action executors. A saga is a signed plan of named effects and read-only verification steps, with dependency edges and idempotency keys. Persist a checkpoint only after independent evidence confirms each effect. On timeout or contradictory evidence, freeze downstream effects, classify the workflow as partial_success/unknown/blocked, and generate a recovery plan; execute a compensating effect only when its reversibility and target identity are proven. Merge Mac action receipts, Calendar/Mail state, and browser evidence into one reconciliation object that faculty-action can speak and the owner can inspect.
- **owner gets:** A task that touches several services will no longer silently stop halfway or duplicate a message after a retry. The owner gets an honest, actionable answer such as “calendar moved, portal unchanged, notification not sent,” plus a safe way to continue.
- effort: High: new coordinator/state model, evidence adapters, conflict tests, and recovery UX across relay and Mac dashboard; browser durable execution must feed step results.  ·  risk: External systems may reject or change data between steps, and some effects cannot be undone. The coordinator must prefer stopping with a clear partial state over guessing; recovery is a new planned action, never an automatic blind retry.
- cost: Small storage and background-model cost per step; conflict cases add a reconciliation call. No realtime cost beyond short spoken updates.  ·  latency: Adds a verification read after each write, typically 1–5 seconds per step; long workflows continue asynchronously.
- security: Reduces duplicate and misdirected external actions. Keep account content session-local, minimize evidence sent to relay, bind every step to account/session identity, and require owner policy for irreversible compensation.
- depends on: Durable browser job runner/result stream; Read-only postcondition verification adapters; Existing action receipts and jobs APIs; A dashboard/pendant representation for partial_success and recovery choices
