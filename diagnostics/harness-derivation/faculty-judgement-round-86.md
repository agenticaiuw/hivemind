## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Wrap up that meeting.” Then give me a 60-second debrief: what was decided, who owns what, deadlines, and drafts for any follow-ups—without sending anything."
- **useful because:** Meetings currently evaporate into memory and scattered tabs. A deliberate pendant boundary lets the always-with-you device capture only the relevant conversation, while the Mac contributes the calendar event, existing notes, mail, and open browser context. The result is an actionable, sourced handoff rather than another transcript, with follow-ups ready for review.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → mac-terminal → unified
- **model tier:** Use the realtime tier only to recognize the owner's explicit “wrap up” boundary and confirm capture state. Send the bounded audio plus calendar/time context to a cheaper background model for diarization, decision/action extraction, conflict checking, and draft generation; use the planner only for bounded reads and optional draft-file creation.
- **latency:** Acknowledge the boundary on the pendant in under 1 second; produce the spoken headline in 30–90 seconds and a detailed review packet within 3 minutes. If transcription or Mac access fails, retain a local handoff marker and finish from audio alone rather than blocking.
- **cost:** Roughly $0.03–$0.20 per meeting depending on audio duration and background transcription; Mac/browser reads and a small local Markdown packet dominate neither API spend nor latency. Never pay the expensive realtime tier for the full recording.
- **security:** Capture must be explicitly armed per meeting and visibly acknowledged; default to a short rolling buffer and delete raw audio after extraction (or immediately on request). Redact unrelated speakers and secrets before cloud processing where possible. Treat inferred owners/deadlines as suggestions with citations and confidence. Creating drafts is reversible; sending mail, changing calendar, or assigning tasks requires explicit confirmation. Provide “discard this meeting” and a spoken stop latch.
- **missing:** A first-class meeting-boundary/capture session that binds pendant audio to the active Calendar event and supports immediate discard; Speaker/decision/action extraction with citations back to time ranges and source tabs; A cross-surface reconciliation pass that compares spoken commitments with Calendar, Mail, Notes, and authenticated browser context; A durable review packet and draft follow-up queue with explicit send/modify/ discard states; A privacy-preserving raw-audio retention and per-meeting consent policy

### "“Coach me through this conversation.” While I have the pendant armed, listen for ambiguity or a commitment I may regret, and discreetly suggest one short clarifying question or boundary—without speaking to the other person or saving the conversation unless I ask."
- **useful because:** The owner often needs judgment at the exact moment a promise, deadline, or boundary is being negotiated—not a summary afterward. The pendant can be the private channel in the room, while the Mac supplies only the project or appointment context the owner explicitly selected. This gives the owner a timely second mind without turning every conversation into surveillance or an automated participant.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → unified → mac-planner → browser-extension
- **model tier:** Realtime model for low-latency speech-turn and commitment-risk detection; a cheaper background model may prepare the selected project context before the conversation. No full recording or background summarization by default.
- **latency:** A discreet suggestion within 1–2 seconds after a likely commitment or ambiguity; otherwise remain silent. Arming and disarming must be acknowledged in under 1 second.
- **cost:** Approximately $0.01–$0.08 per actively coached 30-minute conversation, dominated by realtime audio tokens; preloaded context should be cached and bounded.
- **security:** Explicit per-conversation arming, a visible/physical indicator, and a hard local stop button are mandatory. Default to transient audio processing with no raw-audio retention or transcript. Never identify or profile the other person, send messages, or make commitments. Context must be selected by the owner and secrets excluded. The suggestion must be framed as uncertainty (“possible ambiguity”) and never as a claim about intent. Require confirmation before saving a note or creating a reminder.
- **missing:** A pendant-local armed/coaching state with an unmistakable indicator and immediate stop behavior; A low-latency event stream that can deliver private suggestions without leaking them to the room; A bounded context capsule API for one selected project, appointment, or thread with automatic expiry; A realtime detector/output contract limited to short questions and boundary prompts, with confidence and cooldowns; A privacy audit trail proving whether audio was retained, transcribed, or discarded


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Add a Meeting Wrap Coordinator spanning the existing pipeline and job primitives. On an explicit pendant “wrap up” event, create a meeting-scoped correlation ID, close the bounded audio segment, resolve the Calendar event on the Mac, and enqueue one idempotent background job. The job gathers only the event's time-windowed Mac notes/mail and explicitly bound browser tabs, runs extraction with time-range/source citations, writes a review packet plus unsent drafts, and emits a short audio summary. Store a state machine (armed→captured→reconciled→review-ready→discarded/approved), raw-audio expiry, per-item confidence, and retry receipts; never let retries send or mutate external systems.
- **owner gets:** The owner gets a dependable end-of-meeting ritual instead of a transcript that may or may not connect to their calendar and work. If the Mac or browser is offline, the pendant still records the handoff and the system resumes later with an honest partial result; nothing is silently lost or accidentally sent.
- effort: Medium-high: one coordinator/service, event schema and correlation IDs, bounded Mac/browser collectors, extraction prompt/output schema, audio queue item, and dashboard/review UI. Reuse existing pipeline, jobs, receipts, and browser session primitives rather than adding another agent.  ·  risk: Wrong event binding or speaker attribution could create misleading commitments. Require explicit boundary confirmation, show event/time and evidence before drafts, mark uncertain items, and make discard/undo idempotent. If any source is unavailable, label it missing rather than infer. Recover interrupted jobs from the state machine and receipts.
- cost: Background transcription/extraction only; approximately $0.03–$0.20 per meeting, with raw audio storage minimized by short TTL. No recurring polling cost.  ·  latency: Sub-second pendant acknowledgement; 30–90 seconds for headline; up to 3 minutes for review packet. Offline Mac/browser adds queue delay, not blocking capture.
- security: Meeting audio and private account content cross the relay only after explicit arming. Encrypt and expire raw audio, minimize source windows, redact unrelated content, and require confirmation for any outbound or calendar mutation.
- depends on: A shared meeting-boundary event envelope with owner consent and stop/discard semantics; A durable cross-surface job correlation/state primitive and audio queue item; Bounded Calendar/Notes/Mail reads and authenticated browser tab binding; A review packet schema carrying evidence, confidence, and draft status

### `interaction` — Create a private Conversation Coach runtime rather than routing live audio through the ordinary assistant transcript. It must establish a per-session consent token, keep a bounded ring buffer in pendant/relay memory, emit only typed events (possible ambiguity, explicit promise, missing owner/amount/date, escalation risk), apply cooldowns, and send a maximum-one-sentence suggestion to the pendant. Context is a separately signed, expiring capsule selected by the owner; the runtime must cryptographically attest discard of audio when the session ends and expose a local stop latch that cuts capture before any network call.
- **owner gets:** The owner can get a quiet, timely nudge in a difficult conversation while retaining control of what is remembered and what the system is allowed to say. Today the existing audio and planning pieces can move data, but cannot guarantee this private, transient, non-participating behavior.
- effort: High: pendant firmware/session UX, a relay realtime event contract, a constrained detector, context-capsule issuance, privacy attestations, and end-to-end tests for stop, timeout, reconnect, and accidental speech output.  ·  risk: False positives could distract or undermine a conversation; false negatives are inevitable. Use conservative thresholds, cooldowns, explicit uncertainty, and an easy “not useful” dismissal. Network loss must fail closed (no suggestion, no queued recording). A crash must expire the consent token and discard the buffer.
- cost: No background cost when disarmed. Active use costs realtime inference, roughly $0.01–$0.08 per 30-minute session; firmware memory/storage impact should be limited to a small ring buffer and state machine, with no persistent transcript.  ·  latency: Target 1–2 seconds from detected turn boundary to private nudge; local stop is immediate even when relay is unavailable.
- security: Meaningfully reduces privacy exposure versus ordinary transcription: no raw audio retention, no third-party identity inference, signed context expiry, explicit indicator, and auditable discard. Still requires careful consent UX for bystanders and secure transport.
- depends on: A firmware-local consent/stop state and indicator; A typed transient audio-event protocol distinct from transcript storage; A bounded expiring context-capsule service; Realtime output guardrails and cooldown policy; A verifiable discard/retention audit primitive
