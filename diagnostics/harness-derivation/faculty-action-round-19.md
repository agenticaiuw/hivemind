## Capabilities it proposed

### "If I give you an instruction from the pendant while my Mac or browser is temporarily unavailable, remember it safely, tell me exactly what is waiting, and carry it out automatically when the required device comes back—without repeating an action or acting on stale information."
- **useful because:** Today an owner can issue an intent while walking away, but a disconnected browser or unready Mac turns it into an opaque failure. This makes the pendant a reliable front door: capture once, show pending state, resume only against a fresh readiness snapshot, and provide a completion receipt. It is not merely background Mac work because the pendant is the only always-with-owner capture and confirmation surface, while the relay survives device absence and authenticated browser state supplies the action reach.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-planner → browser-extension → faculty-judgement → faculty-perception → faculty-action
- **model tier:** Realtime only for the short pendant capture/acknowledgment; use a cheaper background model for intent normalization, readiness checks, and resume planning. Use the action model only when execution begins or an ambiguity is found.
- **latency:** Immediate local acknowledgment under 300 ms; relay persistence under 2 s when connected. Resume on next qualifying heartbeat, with a visible/prompted delay if the action is consequential. Completion or blocked receipt within 10 s of the device becoming ready.
- **cost:** About $0.001–$0.02 per queued instruction depending on background extraction and any browser reasoning; storage/heartbeat traffic dominates at scale, not realtime inference.
- **security:** Commands and authenticated-page results must be encrypted in transit and at rest, with short retention and device-bound ownership. Never replay an irreversible step: require an idempotency key, a precondition hash/readiness snapshot, expiry, and renewed approval for changed or consequential targets. Pendant should store only a bounded encrypted audio/text envelope and purge after relay acknowledgment or expiry.
- **missing:** A pendant offline-intent buffer and sync protocol with local receipt/expiry; Relay durable intent escrow with idempotency, TTL, encrypted payloads, and wake-on-readiness scheduling; Typed readiness/precondition snapshots that distinguish Mac GUI readiness from browser-extension/session readiness; Resume cursor and exactly-once/reconciliation semantics across Mac and authenticated browser; Pendant status/confirmation UX for queued, blocked, resumed, and completed states; End-to-end conformance tests for disconnect, retry, stale page, duplicate delivery, and power loss

### "When a task you are carrying out breaks because a website, app layout, or login state changed, diagnose the break across the browser, Mac, and relay, repair the plan, and continue from the last verified step—telling me what changed instead of making me start over."
- **useful because:** Today a long-running action can fail after spending time navigating, leaving the owner with an opaque error and lost progress. Automatic recovery would turn brittle computer use into a service that can adapt to ordinary UI drift while preserving a precise boundary around what was and was not done. This is different from merely queuing, watching, or undoing work: it repairs an execution strategy after reality diverges.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-planner → mac-vision → browser-extension → faculty-perception → faculty-judgement → faculty-action
- **model tier:** Use a cheap background model to classify failures and compare prior/current page or UI structure. Use mac-vision/browser reasoning only for localized repair. Realtime is limited to the pendant explanation or a short approval request when the repaired target is consequential.
- **latency:** Detect and classify within 5 seconds of a failed step; attempt one bounded repair within 30 seconds. Pause and report rather than loop after two failed repairs or any changed consequential target.
- **cost:** Roughly $0.01–$0.10 per recovered task, dominated by screenshots/DOM extraction and localized computer-use inference; normal successful tasks incur no extra model call beyond lightweight failure classification.
- **security:** A visually similar control could be the wrong control, and a changed login or recipient could cause an unintended action. Keep the last verified state, compare target identity and semantic labels, require renewed approval for irreversible or materially changed actions, retain before/after evidence, and never silently broaden the task. Screenshots and authenticated DOM data need encrypted, short-lived storage with redacted logs.
- **missing:** A cross-surface failure envelope carrying step ID, intent, preconditions, DOM/UI evidence, and side-effect status; A repair planner that can propose an alternate locator or interaction while preserving the original goal and scope; Mac-vision and browser-extension support for bounded alternative inspection without committing changes; A verified-step ledger and reconciliation protocol for unknown side effects; Policy classification for when repaired execution may continue automatically versus requiring pendant approval; Fault-injection and UI-drift conformance tests across browser offline, permission loss, login renewal, and partial submission


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `relay` — Add a readiness-bound intent escrow protocol rather than treating queued work as a generic job. Each intent stores an encrypted payload reference, owner/device identity, monotonic idempotency key, expiry, required surface capabilities, precondition/readiness snapshot, and a resumable step cursor. Readiness events from the Mac bridge and browser extension wake only eligible intents; the action executor must atomically claim a cursor, return typed blocked reasons, and reconcile duplicate/unknown outcomes before advancing. Emit pendant-readable state transitions (queued, blocked with exact reason, resumed, completed, expired, canceled).
- **owner gets:** An instruction given while walking away will not silently disappear or run against a changed page. The owner gets a truthful pending status and a completion receipt, while recovery from browser-offline or missing Mac permissions happens automatically when safe.
- effort: Medium-high: relay schema/state machine, signed readiness events, executor claim/reconciliation, pendant sync, and fault-injection tests for disconnects and duplicate delivery.  ·  risk: A bad eligibility rule could delay work or resume it unexpectedly. Mitigate with conservative expiry, changed-precondition pause, explicit reapproval for consequential actions, append-only transition receipts, and an operator cancel endpoint. If relay storage is unavailable, pendant keeps only the bounded local spool and reports unsynced.
- cost: Low storage and queue overhead; background model calls only for normalization/reconciliation, roughly cents per complex resumed task. No new inference on every heartbeat.  ·  latency: Immediate local ack; resume typically within one heartbeat (seconds), with extra latency only when fresh browser state or reconciliation is required.
- security: Adds sensitive queued-command retention, so encrypt payloads, minimize metadata, bind claims to device/session keys, use short TTLs, and redact command content from ordinary logs.
- depends on: Typed Mac/browser readiness snapshots, including Accessibility/Screen Recording and browser extension/session state; Durable job records with idempotency and receipts; Pendant offline_intent_spool firmware skill; A signed bridge event or authenticated polling mechanism; A policy hook from faculty-judgement for actions requiring renewed approval

### `integration` — Introduce a signed cross-surface execution-causality record. Every action step emits a compact envelope containing goal version, step ID, selected surface, readiness snapshot, locator/target identity, evidence hash, side-effect classification, and outcome (including unknown). When a step fails, the relay freezes the cursor and fans the envelope to perception, judgment, and the appropriate Mac/browser executor; a repair proposal must reference the failed evidence and prove target equivalence before action can resume.
- **owner gets:** When an automation task breaks halfway through, the owner can recover without guessing whether anything was already sent, changed, or duplicated. The system can explain the exact break and safely continue instead of abandoning the whole task.
- effort: High: shared event schema and signing, adapters in Mac/browser/relay, cursor freeze/reconciliation, repair proposal API, and fault-injection tests.  ·  risk: More instrumentation can create false confidence if an adapter reports success incorrectly. Treat unknown outcomes as a hard stop, retain raw evidence hashes, and provide a cancel/review path. Schema rollout needs backward-compatible envelopes.
- cost: Small relay storage and serialization overhead; modest background inference only on failures. No cost added to ordinary successful steps beyond telemetry.  ·  latency: Negligible on successful steps; failed steps gain one evidence-upload round trip and bounded repair analysis.
- security: Authenticated-page and UI evidence becomes sensitive operational data. Encrypt payloads, store hashes rather than raw evidence by default, use short retention, and bind records to the owner/device session.
- depends on: A shared typed event schema across relay, Mac planner, Mac vision, and browser extension; Existing action receipts and idempotency support; A judgment policy for target equivalence and renewed approval; Executor support for frozen/resumable cursors
