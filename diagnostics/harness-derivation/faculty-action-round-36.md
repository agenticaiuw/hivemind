## Capabilities it proposed

### "If something I asked you to do is blocked because a device or permission is unavailable, keep it safely parked, tell me what is missing, and resume it automatically when that device becomes usable—without repeating completed steps."
- **useful because:** Today an action can fail after a long wait (browser extension offline, Mac permissions absent) and the owner must remember and retry. This makes the hive dependable: judgement's intent survives disconnects, the always-awake relay watches readiness, the pendant explains the block, and the appropriate Mac/browser hand executes exactly the remaining steps with a final receipt.
- **path:** faculty-judgement emits a typed action envelope with idempotency key, preconditions, expiry, and safe-to-resume policy → relay stores the envelope and subscribes to Mac/browser heartbeat/readiness changes → faculty-perception reports fresh Accessibility/Screen Recording/browser-online facts and distinguishes unavailable from failed → home-macbook-bridge executes resumable Mac steps; browser-extension executes browser steps when polling resumes → pendant presents a short blocked/resumed/completed status and accepts cancel or expiry commands → dashboard shows the exact unmet precondition, next retry time, completed step receipts, and remaining steps
- **model tier:** Use the planner/judgement tier once to compile and risk-classify the envelope; use deterministic relay/job code for readiness, retries, leases, and idempotency; use a cheap text model only to summarize failures. Reserve realtime for the pendant's live explanation or cancellation.
- **latency:** Immediate (<1 s) acknowledgement that the job is parked and why; readiness changes detected within one heartbeat (5–15 s); resumed execution starts within 2 s of a valid lease. No polling loop should hold a request open for 45 seconds.
- **cost:** About $0.001–$0.01 per parked/resumed job depending on whether a model is needed for re-planning; most retries and receipts are code-only. Realtime cost occurs only if the owner asks follow-up questions.
- **security:** Persist only the typed plan, preconditions, and redacted receipts—not page contents or secrets. Never auto-resume an irreversible or externally visible step; resume only idempotent/reversible steps, and invalidate the envelope on expiry, changed tab/session, changed target, or permission scope. Require explicit owner cancellation and show a before/after receipt for any write.
- **missing:** A durable resumable job state machine with per-step checkpoints, leases, retry/backoff, expiry, and idempotency across relay and Mac agent; A readiness-event contract carrying source, timestamp, freshness, and reason (not ready because Accessibility, Screen Recording, or browser bridge is offline); Non-blocking browser progress events/resumable polling instead of the current single 45-second wait; A typed action-envelope schema shared by faculty-judgement, faculty-action, relay, Mac bridge, and browser bridge; Pendant/dashboard status and cancel controls for parked jobs

### "Undo the whole thing I just asked you to do, across every device—not merely the last Mac step—and show me what will be restored before you do it."
- **useful because:** Today a compound request can touch a private browser session, Mac files/apps, and generated relay artifacts, but undo is surface-local and may leave a half-completed workflow behind. A cross-device compensation plan would let the owner recover from an unwanted result without manually reconstructing every change.
- **path:** faculty-judgement classifies the completed workflow into reversible effects and selects a compensation strategy → faculty-action gathers the signed receipts and before/after evidence from Mac, browser, relay, and pendant-produced artifacts → relay holds the durable cross-surface effect ledger and coordinates an ordered compensation transaction with leases and expiry → home-macbook-bridge performs supported inverse operations on files, reminders, app state, and typed Mac actions → browser-extension restores saved field values or removes drafts only when the original page/session fingerprint still matches → pendant summarizes the proposed restoration and lets the owner approve, cancel, or narrow it → dashboard presents a dependency graph of effects, uncertain steps, and the final restoration receipt
- **model tier:** Use deterministic compensation planning from typed receipts wherever possible; use the planner tier only to explain ambiguous effects or propose a human-reviewed alternative. Realtime is used only for the owner's spoken approval and status, not for ledger or rollback logic.
- **latency:** Build the compensation preview in under 3 seconds for a normal job; execute independent inverse steps in parallel and report each result within 10 seconds. Never hold a single synchronous request open while waiting for a device.
- **cost:** Usually $0.001–$0.02 per undo preview/execution, dominated by an occasional planner call for ambiguous inverse actions; storage and coordination are inexpensive.
- **security:** Never infer an inverse for an irreversible external action (sent email, submitted form, deletion without recovery). Require an explicit confirmation for any compensating write, bind restoration to the original device/session and target fingerprint, redact private page contents from receipts, and mark uncertain effects instead of pretending they were restored.
- **missing:** A cross-surface effect ledger with signed before/after receipts and dependency links; A compensation-plan API distinct from the existing single-job or last-action undo endpoints; Stable inverse-operation metadata for each Mac, browser, relay, and artifact action; Target/session fingerprint checks and an uncertainty state for effects that cannot safely be reversed; A pendant approval and dashboard preview surface for whole-workflow restoration


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `relay` — Introduce a cross-surface Action Envelope and blocked-state protocol. The relay accepts a plan only as versioned, step-scoped records containing idempotency key, target surface/session, side-effect class, preconditions with freshness TTL, checkpoint receipt, expiry, and resume policy. It transitions parked→leased→running→blocked→resumed→completed/expired, publishes readiness-triggered wakeups, and rejects stale or duplicate leases. Mac and browser bridges must acknowledge each checkpoint before the next step is dispatched.
- **owner gets:** The owner gets a reliable answer instead of a mysterious timeout: 'waiting for Browser Bridge; nothing changed' followed by automatic continuation when it is back. Completed work is never repeated, and a dropped Mac or browser connection does not lose the request.
- effort: Medium-high: shared schema and migration, D1 state machine, heartbeat-to-event adapter, bridge checkpoint APIs, and dashboard/pendant status. Add fault-injection tests for disconnects between every step.  ·  risk: A bad resume policy could repeat an external write or resume against a changed page. Default all writes to non-resumable, require stable target fingerprints and idempotency, expire envelopes, and provide cancel. Recover orphaned leases by timeout and mark uncertain effects for inspection rather than retrying.
- cost: Low relay storage/CPU; roughly a few D1 rows and events per step. One cheap model call only when a failed step needs re-planning; no model cost for normal wakeups.  ·  latency: Sub-second park acknowledgement; wakeup on the next heartbeat rather than a blocking 45-second request. Additional checkpoint round trips add roughly 100–500 ms per step.
- security: The envelope must exclude secrets and page bodies, bind to the paired device/session, enforce capability scopes, and cryptographically correlate receipts to the originating intent. Dashboard should redact sensitive target values.
- depends on: A readiness-event contract from faculty-perception; A durable resumable job runner (not just the current jobs JSON); Non-blocking browser progress/resumable polling; An owner policy defining which side-effect classes may auto-resume

### `integration` — Add a cross-surface compensation ledger rather than extending the existing last-job undo. Every action adapter must publish an effect record with stable target fingerprint, before/after capsule reference, inverse operation (or explicitly irreversible), dependency edges, and confidence. A coordinator computes a previewable compensation DAG, pauses on uncertain effects, and executes only owner-approved inverse nodes in dependency-safe order across Mac, browser, relay storage, and generated pendant artifacts.
- **owner gets:** The owner can recover an entire multi-device task coherently instead of being left with a browser draft, a changed Mac file, and an orphaned relay artifact after undoing only one step.
- effort: High: adapter contracts for each surface, encrypted receipt/capsule storage, dependency-aware compensation engine, fingerprint validation, and pendant/dashboard preview plus approval.  ·  risk: An incorrect inverse could destroy newer user changes or create duplicate external effects. Require exact target/version matching, preserve current state as a recovery snapshot, stop on any mismatch, and treat irreversible or uncertain effects as manual follow-up rather than auto-compensating.
- cost: Moderate storage for encrypted before/after capsules and receipts; low compute. Planner-model cost only for explaining or resolving ambiguous inverse candidates.  ·  latency: Normal actions gain a small receipt-write overhead (tens of milliseconds). Undo preview is seconds; execution is asynchronous so it does not block the voice session.
- security: Receipts may contain sensitive browser and file metadata; encrypt capsules, minimize retention, bind them to the paired owner identity, and redact values in spoken/dashboard summaries.
- depends on: Typed effect adapters for Mac, browser, relay artifacts, and pendant output; A durable cross-surface job identity and receipt correlation mechanism; Owner policy for irreversible effects and compensation approval
