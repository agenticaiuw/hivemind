## Capabilities it proposed

### "When I say “what’s that?”, use whatever is in front of me right now and answer the question out loud."
- **useful because:** The pendant becomes a genuinely eyes-free pointer to the owner’s current attention: no app name, tab URL, or screenshot description is needed. It can identify the focused UI object, read visible text, and answer in one turn instead of making the owner explain context.
- **path:** pendant captures the utterance and plays the answer → mac-vision captures the focused window and locates the likely object under the cursor or recent focus → browser-extension supplies the authenticated tab DOM/text when the target is in Safari → relay-realtime transcribes, asks the local perception loop for grounding, and speaks a short answer → faculty-perception attaches freshness, source, and confidence so faculty-judgement can refuse guesses
- **model tier:** Realtime handles the short utterance and final spoken response; a cheaper vision/text model handles screenshot OCR and element localization; no expensive model is used for raw extraction.
- **latency:** Target 1.5–3 seconds: 300 ms capture/route, up to 1.5 s local vision or DOM extraction, then one realtime turn. If the target is ambiguous, ask one clarification rather than hallucinating.
- **cost:** Usually near-zero incremental API cost when DOM/accessibility text suffices; roughly $0.005–$0.03 for a vision fallback, dominated by screenshot vision tokens.
- **security:** Screenshots and authenticated page text remain on the Mac unless explicitly sent to the relay; redact passwords, payment fields, and secret locators before any model call. Require a visible/voice confirmation before reading sensitive content aloud.
- **missing:** A focused-target perception endpoint that joins Screen Recording/accessibility coordinates to the browser extension’s active tab; A local redaction and confidence gate for screenshots; A relay request/response contract carrying a short-lived grounding id and source timestamp

### "Tell me when the information on my Mac and the information on the web disagree, and show me exactly what each source said."
- **useful because:** This catches expensive or embarrassing errors—stale dashboards, changed prices, conflicting appointment details, or a local document copied from an outdated page—while preserving the distinction between observed facts and inferred explanations.
- **path:** browser-extension reads the authenticated page and its current DOM → mac-vision or mac-terminal reads the local app/document view without leaving the Mac → relay performs an independent public-web read when a second source is needed → faculty-perception normalizes timestamps, URLs, content hashes, and confidence, then emits a contradiction record → faculty-judgement decides whether to interrupt the owner or wait for a request → pendant speaks only the concise conflict and offers the two source excerpts
- **model tier:** Use a cheap structured extraction model for each source and a small text model for pairwise comparison; reserve realtime for the owner-facing interruption or follow-up.
- **latency:** On demand under 5 seconds; background checks should be scheduled and quiet, with interruption only when a high-confidence material contradiction is found.
- **cost:** $0.01–$0.08 per comparison, dominated by browser/vision extraction and source text; local DOM/accessibility cases can be effectively free.
- **security:** Never send private local documents to a public relay read. Keep local evidence on the Mac, hash and redact excerpts, and require confirmation before quoting sensitive data aloud. Public pages must remain marked untrusted.
- **missing:** A cross-source observation schema with source URL/app, capturedAt, content hash, and region; A local-only comparator that can compare private Mac evidence with public relay evidence without exporting the private body; A user policy for which contradiction severities may interrupt

### "While I am working, quietly notice what I am trying to do from my screen and browser, and tell me the single next fact or action that would unblock me when I ask."
- **useful because:** It turns passive computer use into a useful second set of eyes: it can recognize a blocked login, missing attachment, error dialog, or form validation failure and answer “what’s blocking me?” without the owner narrating the screen.
- **path:** mac-vision watches only explicit snapshots or a user-triggered watch, not continuous raw recording → browser-extension reports the active tab, visible error state, and pending command state → mac-terminal and mac-planner inspect local job receipts or command output when the blocker is outside the browser → faculty-perception builds a compact state transition with evidence and freshness → faculty-judgement ranks one unblocker and respects quiet hours → relay-realtime and pendant deliver the answer on request
- **model tier:** A cheap local classifier handles stable UI states and diffs; use a stronger model only for ambiguous multi-app interpretation; realtime is only the final conversational turn.
- **latency:** Snapshot-to-state under 1 second locally; answer under 2 seconds on request. Watching is event-driven and rate-limited rather than a continuous vision stream.
- **cost:** Mostly local compute; $0–$0.02 per ambiguous interpretation. Storage is bounded to hashes and short observations, not screenshots.
- **security:** Explicit opt-in watch sessions with a physical/voice stop; exclude password managers, banking, private messages, and camera feeds by app policy. Do not retain screenshots by default; retain only redacted state transitions.
- **missing:** An event-driven screen-diff/watch session with app and region exclusion policies; A blocker taxonomy and confidence-calibrated next-step ranking; A bounded observation ledger joinable to browser commands and Mac action receipts

### "During a meeting, keep a private, time-aligned record of what was said, what appeared on my screen, and which commitments were actually made; when I ask afterward, tell me only what is supported by the recording and screen evidence."
- **useful because:** This gives the owner a reliable answer to “what did we agree to?” instead of a generic summary that blends spoken claims, slides, and model guesses. It can distinguish a commitment that was explicitly spoken from one merely inferred from a document or task list.
- **path:** pendant captures microphone audio locally, segments it, and emits quality/sequence metadata without continuously uploading raw audio → mac-vision captures explicit meeting-window frames and visible slide/document regions, keyed to the same monotonic timeline → browser-extension supplies authenticated meeting captions or collaborative-document changes when the owner opts into those tabs → relay stores encrypted session manifests and performs deferred transcription/alignment only after the meeting or on request → faculty-perception aligns transcript spans, screen evidence, speaker/source provenance, and clock uncertainty → faculty-judgement classifies explicit commitments versus suggestions and asks before creating reminders → mac-planner can create a reminder or task only after the owner confirms the evidence-backed draft
- **model tier:** Use a cheaper background transcription/alignment model during or after the meeting; use a small extraction model for commitments and contradictions. Reserve realtime only for an owner question asked live through the pendant.
- **latency:** Live captions need not be instantaneous: 2–5 seconds is acceptable. Post-meeting evidence indexing may take 1–3 minutes. An owner query should answer within 5 seconds, or explicitly say the relevant segment is still processing.
- **cost:** Approximately $0.05–$0.50 per hour of meeting audio plus vision/text extraction, dominated by transcription and the number of selected screen frames; local-only mode can reduce cloud cost substantially.
- **security:** Meeting audio, private screens, and authenticated documents are highly sensitive. Default to local encrypted storage, explicit per-session recording consent, visible recording state, participant-policy reminder, app/tab exclusions, configurable retention, and no reminder/task creation without confirmation. Relay receives only encrypted chunks or derived evidence when the owner enables cloud processing.
- **missing:** A consented meeting-session controller with visible start/stop state and participant-policy acknowledgement; A shared clock/sequence format joining pendant audio, Mac screenshots, browser captions, and action receipts despite clock skew; An encrypted, searchable evidence bundle with per-span hashes, source provenance, revocation, and configurable deletion; Speaker diarization and an uncertainty model that refuses to attribute a statement when evidence is weak

### "At any moment, let me ask “what has left my devices?” and hear a trustworthy, human-readable account of which microphone, screen, browser, and file data was transmitted, to whom, for what purpose, and what is still only local."
- **useful because:** The owner can wear and use the system without guessing whether a private conversation, screen, or authenticated tab reached the relay or a model. It turns invisible data movement into an auditable privacy boundary, not a vague settings page.
- **path:** pendant exposes a local monotonic outbound-transfer counter and physical capture state, including queued/offline data → Mac agent reports microphone, screenshot, browser-read, file-read, and network-send events with source and destination class → browser-extension labels authenticated-origin reads and tab identity without exposing page bodies to the audit service → relay records receipt of encrypted envelopes, model-processing purpose, retention deadline, and deletion status → faculty-perception joins device-originated telemetry with Mac and relay records, explicitly separating observed transfer from claimed transfer → faculty-judgement applies the owner’s privacy policy and can recommend or block a future capture → pendant speaks a compact answer and offers an audit id for the Mac dashboard
- **model tier:** No generative model is needed for collection or accounting. Use deterministic event joins and a small summarizer only to turn the audit trail into speech; realtime handles the question and response.
- **latency:** Current-state answers under 2 seconds; historical audit queries under 5 seconds. Transfer events should be durable before a session is reported as complete.
- **cost:** Low ongoing API cost; bounded event metadata is cheap. The main cost is encrypted local storage and relay retention, not inference.
- **security:** The audit itself can reveal sensitive activity, so encrypt it separately, minimize URLs/content, hash bodies rather than retaining them, enforce owner-only access, and make the pendant’s local indicator authoritative for active capture. A compromised relay must not be able to rewrite “never transmitted” claims; use device-signed sequence ranges.
- **missing:** A signed, append-only transfer ledger emitted at each capture and network boundary; A shared event taxonomy covering pendant, Mac, browser, relay, and model-processing destinations; A relay receipt protocol that proves acceptance without claiming playback or semantic use; A privacy dashboard and retention/deletion controls driven by the owner’s policy

### "Before you send anything private to solve a task, give me a one-sentence preview of exactly what will leave this device and let me approve the smallest useful redacted version by voice."
- **useful because:** The owner gets control at the boundary where privacy is actually lost. Instead of choosing between refusing all computer help and blindly uploading a screenshot or recording, they can approve a narrowly scoped excerpt, region, or field.
- **path:** pendant presents the preview and captures approve / edit / deny → mac-vision identifies the smallest screen region relevant to the task and applies deterministic redaction → browser-extension supplies only the required DOM fields rather than a whole authenticated page → relay receives a purpose-bound, expiring upload token only after approval → faculty-perception describes the proposed payload, uncertainty, destination, and retention before transmission → faculty-judgement enforces data-minimization rules and rejects payloads exceeding the approved scope → mac-planner or browser-extension executes only against the approved evidence envelope
- **model tier:** Deterministic classifiers and redaction first; a small model may summarize the proposed payload. Realtime is used only for the short approval dialogue.
- **latency:** Preview under 1 second for DOM/accessibility data and under 3 seconds for a screenshot region. Approval should expire after 60 seconds or when the source changes.
- **cost:** Low; local extraction and redaction dominate. Cloud inference is avoidable until after approval.
- **security:** The preview itself must not leak the secret it is protecting. Show field classes, lengths, and redacted excerpts by default; bind approval to a content hash, destination, purpose, and expiry; invalidate it on any source change; maintain a deny-by-default policy for passwords, payment data, private messages, and microphone audio.
- **missing:** A purpose-bound evidence envelope with content hash, allowed fields/regions, destination, expiry, and approval signature; A deterministic sensitive-data classifier and redaction preview on the Mac; A relay upload gate that rejects bytes outside the approved envelope; A pendant-visible confirmation state that survives a dropped link without silently retrying
