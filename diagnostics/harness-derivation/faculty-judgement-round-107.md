## Capabilities it proposed

### "“If I say I’ll do something, remember the promise, figure out the next concrete step, and bring it back to me at the right moment—without nagging me.”"
- **useful because:** People routinely make commitments in passing and then lose them. This would turn a spoken promise into a small, reviewable loop: detect it on the pendant, preserve the exact words and uncertainty, use the Mac/calendar/browser to identify a realistic next step or deadline, and resurface it only when context makes sense. It is deliberately not an auto-task creator: ambiguous commitments become a one-tap/one-sentence confirmation request.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → unified → mac-planner → browser-extension → mac-terminal → dashboard
- **model tier:** Realtime model only for the short utterance classification and clarification; a cheaper background model compiles the commitment, searches permitted calendar/mail/browser context, and schedules follow-up. The final reminder is a concise cached audio item, not a fresh expensive turn.
- **latency:** Acknowledge locally/server-side in under 500 ms (“I’ll hold onto that”) and ask clarification only when needed. Background enrichment may take 1–3 minutes. Resurfacing must be quiet-hours and attention-policy aware, with a snooze/stop response under 1 second.
- **cost:** Roughly $0.001–$0.01 per spoken commitment for classification plus background enrichment; most cost is avoided by storing a compact transcript span and only invoking the slower model when a deadline, person, or next step is missing.
- **security:** Commitment audio/transcript is sensitive and should retain only a bounded transcript span plus structured fields, with expiry and deletion controls. Mail, calendar, and logged-in pages must be read-only unless separately approved. Never infer a promise from private audio without showing the quoted evidence; never contact another person or create an external submission automatically. Secret-like content must be excluded from reminders.
- **missing:** A durable commitment record with quoted evidence, confidence, status (tentative/accepted/done/abandoned), and expiry; Cross-surface enrichment that can query calendar, mail, browser sessions, and local files while retaining provenance; A policy-aware resurfacing scheduler with quiet hours, deduplication, snooze, and a universal stop latch; A review UI/audio queue where the owner can accept, edit, defer, or dismiss one commitment; Owner-granted audio/event persistence and the already-requested intent-continuity and attention arbitration primitives

### "“Show me what I knew, what I had promised, and what changed when I made that decision.”"
- **useful because:** Today the owner can get current facts or an action receipt, but not reconstruct the state of their life at the moment a decision was made. A provenance-preserving time slice would let them revisit a missed commitment, disputed detail, or forgotten rationale without relying on memory: the pendant’s spoken context, relay events, Mac files/calendar, and authenticated browser evidence are aligned into a dated, uncertainty-labeled snapshot.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → unified → mac-planner → browser-extension → mac-terminal → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use a cheap background model to index and summarize already-captured records; use the realtime tier only when the owner asks a spoken follow-up. Retrieval should return citations and quoted evidence before any interpretation.
- **latency:** Return a first compact timeline in 2–5 seconds from indexed records, then progressively add Mac/browser evidence within 30 seconds. No live crawling is needed unless the owner explicitly asks for a current comparison.
- **cost:** Low ongoing indexing cost and roughly $0.005–$0.03 per reconstruction depending on the number of records; storage/indexing and provenance joins dominate, not realtime inference.
- **security:** This is unusually sensitive: snapshots may expose private audio, mail, calendar, browser pages, and secrets. Store structured facts and short evidence spans with strict TTLs, source sensitivity labels, per-surface inclusion controls, and deletion propagation. Never present inferred motives as facts; distinguish captured evidence, absent evidence, and model synthesis. Require confirmation before sharing or exporting a snapshot.
- **missing:** An append-only, timestamped cross-surface evidence ledger that preserves event IDs, source, freshness, and deletion links; A queryable time-slice compiler that can answer “as of” questions without silently substituting current values; Retention and redaction controls spanning R2 audio, D1 jobs/memory, Mac workspace artifacts, and browser inspection records; A review view/audio format that speaks uncertainty and cites the original source without replaying sensitive audio by default

### "“Before I act on an important detail, tell me if my sources disagree—and show me the smallest thing I can check to resolve it.”"
- **useful because:** Current assistants summarize sources, but can quietly blend a stale calendar entry, an email, and a logged-in webpage into a confident false answer. The owner should receive a contradiction alert: exactly which claims conflict, which source is newer or more authoritative, what remains unknown, and one reversible verification step. This is especially valuable for appointments, travel, payments, deadlines, and addresses.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → unified → mac-planner → browser-extension → mac-terminal → dashboard
- **model tier:** Cheap background extraction normalizes claims and compares them; realtime is reserved for speaking the concise conflict and asking whether to verify. No model should resolve a conflict by plausibility alone.
- **latency:** For an explicit question, speak a provisional conflict notice within 2 seconds and complete read-only verification in under 30 seconds. Passive checks run asynchronously and notify only high-impact contradictions.
- **cost:** Approximately $0.002–$0.02 per comparison; extraction and source reads dominate. Deduplicate unchanged claims and use hashes so routine checks are nearly free.
- **security:** Claim records may contain sensitive account, travel, or health information. Keep source snippets bounded, label sensitivity, redact secrets, and avoid reading a private source unless the owner’s request authorizes it. Verification must be read-only by default; any correction or submission requires the existing confirmation policy.
- **missing:** A typed claim/contradiction graph with source authority, timestamps, confidence, and materiality; A cross-surface read planner that can re-check only the conflicting fields in Mac, browser, and relay records; An impact policy that distinguishes harmless wording differences from conflicts capable of causing missed deadlines or financial harm; A concise pendant conflict-card/audio format with a one-step verification action and explicit uncertainty


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Add a commitment compiler and resurfacing loop between the existing audio/event pipeline and jobs: POST /pipeline/audio and /pipeline/events emit candidate utterance spans; a cheap classifier writes a typed commitment with evidence, confidence, expiry, and requested confirmation state; /plan enriches only accepted candidates via calendar/mail/browser read routes; /jobs runs delayed checks; /execute emits a pendant-ready audio item. Every state transition records provenance and supports idempotent snooze, dismiss, complete, and delete. Do not silently create calendar tasks or send anything.
- **owner gets:** The owner gets continuity across a real day: a promise made while walking is still available when they sit at the Mac, but ambiguous or private speech does not become nagging or an unwanted task. It uses the pendant for capture and timely spoken feedback, the relay for durable state, and the Mac/browser for context that the pendant cannot reach.
- effort: Medium-high: schema/state machine, event-to-job wiring, background enrichment adapters, quiet-hours/attention integration, and a small review surface/audio queue; can ship first with manual confirmation and no external writes.  ·  risk: False positives could create clutter or make private speech feel surveilled; mitigate with quoted evidence, confidence threshold, explicit accept/ignore, short retention, and per-category disable. Duplicate events and dropped links require event IDs, idempotency keys, and receipt-based recovery. If enrichment fails, preserve the original tentative item rather than guessing.
- cost: Low incremental API cost: one small classification call per candidate and background calls only after acceptance; storage is compact structured records plus bounded evidence spans. Existing job/receipt infrastructure avoids a new durable execution system.  ·  latency: Sub-second acknowledgement can be generated from the pendant/relay path; enrichment is asynchronous. Resurfacing is precomputed and cached, so it does not require a live expensive model turn.
- security: Read-only access by default; provenance labels every calendar/mail/browser fact. Enforce sensitivity filtering and TTL deletion for transcript evidence. External actions remain behind the owner's existing destructive-action confirmation policy.
- depends on: A durable commitment record and evidence-retention policy; Intent continuity and attention arbitration primitives currently requested; A scheduler that can wake /jobs and deliver a bounded audio queue; A review endpoint/UI for accept, edit, snooze, complete, or delete

### `hardware` — Revise the pendant from the prototype nRF9160 DK into a wearable revision with a low-power ERM/LRA haptic actuator, a proper coulomb-counting fuel gauge, and a protected local event ring buffer on the microSD path. Expose haptic patterns (acknowledge, urgent, snoozed) and battery/estimated-runtime events to the relay; keep audio playback unchanged until the 24 kHz path is validated.
- **owner gets:** The owner can receive a discreet “I remembered that” or “this needs attention” cue in a meeting or while walking without speaking aloud, and the system can avoid starting a conversation or scheduling a wake-up when the pendant is about to die. This makes the commitment loop useful in real life rather than only when the owner is looking at a screen.
- effort: Medium hardware spin and firmware driver work: actuator driver, fuel-gauge I2C integration, calibration, event schema, enclosure/acoustic validation, and low-battery fallback tests.  ·  risk: Haptics can annoy or drain the battery; use short patterns, owner-configurable quiet hours, and a physical long-press stop. Fuel-gauge estimates can drift; surface confidence and fall back to voltage thresholds. Added parts introduce assembly and waterproofing risk; retain the existing button/LED and audio path as fallback.
- cost: Approximately $2–$6 BOM increase in small volume; haptic bursts roughly 5–25 mA for under 300 ms, fuel gauge under 100 µA, with negligible average draw if rate-limited. No per-call API cost.  ·  latency: Local haptic acknowledgement is effectively immediate (<100 ms), independent of LTE-M half-duplex latency. Battery telemetry is periodic and does not affect voice turn latency.
- security: No new cloud data is required for haptic patterns. Battery and local event records should be non-sensitive; ring-buffer contents must obey the same deletion/retention policy as audio and never contain raw transcripts.
- depends on: A defined pendant event schema and relay ingestion route; Owner-configurable attention/quiet-hours policy; 24 kHz audio path validation so haptic fallback is not mistaken for audio delivery; A product enclosure/PCB revision (current board is explicitly a prototype)
