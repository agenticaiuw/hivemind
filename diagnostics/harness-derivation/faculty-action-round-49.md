## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Once you’ve decided to do it, carry the action through whichever device has the right reach, and don’t just tell me it ran—prove that the intended change actually happened.”"
- **useful because:** Today judgement can decide an outcome, but execution is fragmented: the relay may lose a job, the Mac may be offline, Safari may require its private session, and a browser click can report success without the server state changing. This gives the mind one action contract: a durable intent ID and exact payload travel from relay to Mac/browser, retries are idempotent, the pendant can report progress while the owner walks away, and a postcondition read provides evidence (or an honest unresolved state) rather than a false success. It complements existing draft/preview capabilities and respects the no-approval-gates policy: judgement authorizes; action executes and verifies.
- **path:** faculty-judgement emits a typed intent with target, allowed effects, idempotency key, and postcondition → relay persists the intent, leases it to the currently reachable Mac/browser, resumes after disconnect, and sends compact progress/failure events to the pendant → mac-planner routes each step to mac_run_actions or browser_run_actions; browser-extension binds the exact tab/session and returns typed before/after evidence → faculty-perception performs the postcondition read from the appropriate surface and reconciles it with the intended effect → pendant speaks only state transitions (accepted, working, blocked, verified/uncertain) and offers retry/cancel for reversible work; the relay stores the receipt and evidence manifest
- **model tier:** Use the realtime tier only for the live spoken status and immediate clarification. Use a cheaper background model for intent normalization, retry planning, and evidence reconciliation; deterministic code should own idempotency, leases, schemas, and postcondition checks.
- **latency:** Acknowledge on the pendant within 300 ms; first execution attempt within 2 s when Mac/browser are online. Long work is asynchronous and survives disconnects. Verification should follow each mutation within 5 s, with exponential retry and a clear uncertain result after the deadline.
- **cost:** Roughly $0.01–$0.08 per ordinary action depending on whether a background model is needed; deterministic routing and browser/Mac calls dominate latency, while any long-form reasoning should be background-tier. Spoken progress is a few short realtime turns, not a full context resend.
- **security:** The relay must not invent authority: it executes only a signed/typed intent from judgement, scoped to named surfaces and effects. Store payload hashes and redacted evidence, never raw secrets or page text by default. Private reads stay in Safari; public work may use the relay. A mismatch between postcondition and intent must be reported as uncertain, never auto-corrected. Cancellation must stop before the next irreversible step, but must not claim rollback where none exists.
- **missing:** A cross-surface ActionIntent/ActionReceipt schema with payload hash, scope, idempotency key, lease, retry policy, and explicit postcondition; Durable relay worker with lease renewal, resumable step polling, and delivery of compact pendant progress events; Typed browser and Mac executors that return before/after evidence and distinguish accepted, applied, and verified; A perception verifier that can query the authoritative result surface after mutation; Pendant/relay protocol for progress, cancellation, retry, and offline queued status

### "“If I get interrupted, save exactly where I was across my open apps and logged-in tabs, then let me say ‘resume that’ later—on the pendant, another Mac, or the browser—and continue from the same point without making me reconstruct the task.”"
- **useful because:** The owner loses work at the boundary between surfaces: spoken intent lives in the pendant, evidence and drafts live in Safari, and edits or files live on the Mac. Existing jobs can keep running, but they do not create a human-usable checkpoint of the unfinished task. This capability would preserve the task’s semantic state, not merely a process status, and resume only after rechecking what changed while it was paused.
- **path:** pendant captures an interruption command and identifies the active task without retaining raw audio → relay stores an encrypted, expiring task checkpoint containing intent, completed steps, open questions, artifact references, and the last known surface/session bindings → Mac-planner snapshots relevant app/document state and browser-extension snapshots named tabs, selected regions, and draft values without copying credentials → faculty-perception revalidates each referenced artifact and marks stale or missing state → faculty-judgement chooses whether to continue, revise, or abandon the checkpoint → mac-vision/browser-extension/mac-terminal restore the required view and execute only the next bounded step; pendant narrates what was restored and where continuation begins
- **model tier:** Use deterministic checkpoint serialization and cheap background reconciliation by default. Use realtime only to identify the owner’s ‘resume that’ request and speak the concise restoration status; reserve expensive reasoning for genuinely ambiguous stale state.
- **latency:** Capture a checkpoint in under 2 seconds after interruption. Restore a concise status on the pendant within 1 second, then revalidate and reopen surfaces asynchronously, usually within 5–15 seconds. Never block the owner’s live conversation on full restoration.
- **cost:** About $0.005–$0.04 per checkpoint/resume, dominated by encrypted metadata storage and occasional background reconciliation; no screenshot or transcript needs to be sent to the realtime model unless explicitly needed.
- **security:** Checkpoints must be encrypted, short-lived, revocable, and scoped to the owner and originating device. Never persist passwords, cookies, access tokens, or unrestricted page dumps. Browser references must reattach only to the same authenticated profile and show a stale-state warning if the account or page changed. Resumption must not silently send, purchase, delete, or submit; it may restore context and prepare the next reversible step, while irreversible continuation requires the existing policy path.
- **missing:** A cross-surface TaskCheckpoint schema with semantic step graph, artifact references, staleness rules, and TTL; Mac/browser snapshot adapters that can restore app and tab context without exporting secrets; Encrypted relay checkpoint storage and a resume lookup bound to the owner’s identity; A revalidation service that compares checkpoint references with current documents/pages before continuation; Pendant protocol for naming, listing, expiring, and resuming checkpoints


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Introduce a cross-surface ActionIntent/ActionReceipt protocol and executor coordinator. An intent contains an immutable canonical payload hash, target surface, permitted effect class, idempotency key, deadline, and machine-checkable postcondition. The relay owns a durable state machine (queued→leased→attempted→applied→verified or uncertain), renews leases, and emits compact progress events. Mac and browser adapters must return typed accepted/applied evidence plus a verification query; the coordinator retries only steps marked idempotent and never silently replays an unknown irreversible step. Keep a redacted receipt/evidence manifest for the owner and allow cancellation/retry of safe steps.
- **owner gets:** The owner gets one reliable answer to “did it happen?” even when the Mac sleeps, Safari is private, or the network drops. It prevents duplicate submissions and makes failures recoverable instead of requiring the owner to inspect several logs or guess whether a click took effect.
- effort: Medium-high: shared schema and migrations, relay worker/state machine, Mac/browser adapter changes, postcondition verifier hooks, pendant event mapping, and fault-injection tests for disconnects and duplicate delivery.  ·  risk: A verifier may read stale UI or misclassify a legitimate result; show verified versus uncertain explicitly and retain evidence. A crash between remote commit and receipt can still be ambiguous, so irreversible actions need endpoint-specific idempotency or a human-readable reconciliation path. Recover by querying the authoritative page/API before retrying.
- cost: Small durable storage and polling overhead; background model calls only for ambiguous reconciliation. No new per-action realtime generation beyond short status utterances.  ·  latency: Adds one authoritative verification read, typically 1–5 seconds, but makes long actions resumable and avoids unsafe blind retries.
- security: Payload hashes and scoped capability tokens reduce confused-deputy risk. Redact page content and secrets from receipts; bind browser steps to the intended session/tab and reject scope changes.
- depends on: A typed browser result contract with tab/session affinity; Durable relay jobs with resumable polling; A Mac executor result schema and authoritative postcondition reads; A pendant progress/cancel event protocol

## Its own summary

I found and proposed a genuinely new execution primitive: a durable cross-surface ActionIntent/ActionReceipt protocol. Judgement authorizes a typed, hashed intent; relay leases and resumes it; Mac/browser execute with idempotency; perception verifies the authoritative postcondition; the pendant reports progress and uncertainty. This is deliberately not another draft/preview or approval gate. I also proposed the concrete integration change and sent the gap to faculty-judgement.

**Biggest unknown:** Which authoritative postcondition APIs are available for each action family (browser mutation, Mail/Calendar, filesystem, and external services), and whether the current relay job runner can be extended with leases without changing its existing receipt/undo behavior. Still needed to build: shared intent/receipt schema, durable resumable coordinator, typed executor evidence, verifier hooks, and pendant progress/cancel protocol.
