## Capabilities it proposed

### ""When a browser-dependent task is waiting, keep it safely queued and tell me on the pendant exactly whether it is queued, stale, blocked, or completed; when Chrome reconnects, resume only still-valid steps and give me one completion receipt instead of replaying old commands.""
- **useful because:** The live system currently has Chrome offline with 10 pending commands, while the Mac journal shows browser_navigate failed 9/9 times and 112/120 jobs lack tier attribution. Today the owner cannot distinguish a command that never reached Chrome from one that ran against an old tab, and reconnecting risks duplicate or stale actions. This makes the wearable/Mac/browser/relay combination dependable rather than silently accumulating work.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-planner → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Deterministic state machine and background model only for summarizing receipts; never spend the realtime tier on queue bookkeeping. Use the planner only if a resumed multi-step goal needs replanning after a tab or auth state changed.
- **latency:** Immediate pendant acknowledgement under 300 ms from relay state; reconnect reconciliation under 2 s for command metadata; browser execution remains bounded by the extension's normal response time. If offline, speak one short queued/stale sentence and do not pretend completion.
- **cost:** Near-zero model cost for state transitions and receipts; roughly 1–2k background tokens only when generating a human summary after a batch. Main cost is small durable metadata (command hash, tab/session binding, expiry, result cursor), under 10 KB per queued task.
- **security:** Never persist page contents or cookies in the relay; persist only opaque session/tab ids, action hashes, timestamps, and typed outcome codes. Auth pages, CAPTCHA, changed origin, and missing tabs become blocked/stale and require a fresh owner request; do not auto-replay clicks or form submissions. Browser reads may proceed under the owner's existing preference, but sending/deleting/purchasing stays confirmation-required. The pendant should announce only status, not sensitive page text.
- **missing:** A durable browser command state machine with leases, TTL, idempotency hash, tab/session fingerprint, and explicit queued/running/stale/blocked/completed outcomes; A reconnect handshake in the browser extension that reports current tabs and accepts only unexpired commands whose fingerprint still matches; Relay-to-pendant compact status events and a single cited completion receipt correlating browser result, Mac job, and relay job; A read-only reconciliation endpoint that can mark pending commands stale without executing them

### ""When I'm in a meeting or presenting, keep the pendant and Mac quiet unless something is genuinely urgent; hold everything else, then give me one short catch-up when the meeting ends—without recording the meeting unless I explicitly press the button.""
- **useful because:** Today Calendar, the Mac foreground state, relay delivery, and pendant output do not share a single interruptibility decision. The owner can be interrupted by routine mail/browser/job results at exactly the wrong moment, or miss them because each surface queues independently. This creates a real capability no one node can provide: calendar-aware, device-wide attention arbitration with a privacy-preserving meeting boundary.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-planner → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Deterministic policy for meeting boundaries, urgency, quiet delivery, and queue coalescing; background model only to produce the post-meeting three-item digest. Realtime is reserved for an explicit spoken interruption or emergency alert.
- **latency:** Calendar/foreground changes reflected across surfaces within 1 second. Emergency alerts bypass within 300 ms; routine events are held. Post-meeting digest is ready within 5 seconds of the boundary, with no waiting on a large model call for the mode switch.
- **cost:** Almost no model cost during meetings; one short background summarization call per meeting digest, typically 500–1,500 input tokens. Durable queue metadata is under 1 KB per event; audio recording remains zero unless the owner explicitly activates capture.
- **security:** Calendar event titles and foreground-app names should be reduced to a local sensitivity class (meeting, presenting, private, free) before leaving the Mac. Never infer or transmit meeting audio; the relay receives only mode, urgency class, and opaque event ids. Emergency bypass must be configurable and auditable. Digest sources remain redacted by default and sending/deleting actions remain separate from notification.
- **missing:** A shared interruptibility lease issued by the Mac/relay with start, end, reason class, and expiry, consumed by both pendant delivery and Mac/browser notifications; A deterministic urgency taxonomy and coalescer that turns held events into one bounded digest without silently dropping them; Calendar boundary and presentation detection using the already-authorized Calendar automation plus explicit owner button override; no microphone-based detection; A durable audit trail showing why an item was held, bypassed, delivered, or expired, and a recovery path for missed boundaries

### ""For any answer or action, let me ask ‘what did you rely on?’ and hear a short source trail—what came from my Mac, browser, pendant, or the cloud, when it was observed, and what was inferred—without exposing page contents or secrets.""
- **useful because:** The current system can execute across surfaces and produce receipts, but the owner cannot distinguish a fresh Calendar/Mail fact from a stale browser observation, a planner inference, or a relay-generated claim. A compact provenance trail would let them trust, correct, or retract an answer without reading raw logs or sending private page text to the cloud. This is a new epistemic capability, not another action gate or job-status screen.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-planner → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Deterministic provenance graph and freshness/confidence calculations; use a cheap background model only to verbalize the already-structured trail into one sentence. Never use the realtime tier merely to build provenance.
- **latency:** Attach provenance identifiers during execution with under 20 ms local overhead; answer a spoken ‘what did you rely on?’ query in under 500 ms from the durable graph. Raw evidence stays on the originating device; only the compact trail crosses surfaces.
- **cost:** No per-turn model cost for capture or graph updates; roughly 200–600 tokens for an optional natural-language explanation. Storage is small: source id, timestamp, freshness, transform labels, and redacted hashes, under 2 KB per claim.
- **security:** Store source classes, timestamps, redacted field names, and content hashes—not mail bodies, cookies, URLs with query secrets, or page text. Use per-owner encryption and expiry for sensitive provenance. The owner can delete a trail without deleting the underlying source. Clearly label inference and stale evidence; never claim a source was consulted when it was not.
- **missing:** A claim-level provenance envelope propagated from Calendar/Mail/source reads and browser observations through planner, relay, TTS, and Mac action receipts; A privacy-preserving evidence registry with freshness/expiry, redacted hashes, and source-local retention; A compact pendant query/response protocol for ‘why do you think that?’ and ‘forget this evidence’; Dashboard visualization that joins claim ids to job/action/pipeline ids without revealing protected content


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Add an execution-truth reconciler between POST /execute, the job/action receipt writer, and POST /pipeline/events. On every action, derive the displayed state from the actual executor result and FULL_CONTROL_MODE, not from the planner's generic risk message. Emit a typed lifecycle (accepted, running, succeeded, failed, cancelled) with jobId/actionId, executor, and undoability; suppress or rewrite legacy 'Waiting for your approval' events when fullControlMode=true and no gate exists. Reconcile orphaned processing pipeline runs on bridge restart from the durable job/receipt store, and attribute each receipt to deterministic/background/planner/realtime tier.
- **owner gets:** The owner currently hears a false approval prompt even though their chosen policy is maximum access and the action path executes without approval; this makes them unsure whether a task ran. A single truthful spoken/dashboard status would prevent duplicate commands and make completion receipts trustworthy. It also turns the observed 112 unattributed jobs into an actionable history instead of an unexplained cost/latency bill.
- effort: Moderate: shared event schema, replay/reconciliation worker, migration for legacy pipeline events, and tests covering execute success/failure, bridge restart, and policy modes.  ·  risk: A bad reconciliation could mark an action complete twice or hide a real failure. Use monotonic event sequence numbers, durable jobId/actionId keys, and retain raw legacy events for diagnostics; never execute during reconciliation. Recovery is replay from pendant-jobs.json and actionReceipts.
- cost: No model calls; a few hundred bytes of metadata per action and one low-rate background reconciliation pass. Saves planner spend by preventing duplicate retries caused by phantom waiting states.  ·  latency: Less than 50 ms local bookkeeping on execution; reconnect reconciliation completes in the background. Spoken status can arrive immediately from deterministic events.
- security: No new data access. Receipts should contain action type and redacted outcome only; keep command text/page content out of relay events. Existing owner policy remains unchanged—this fixes reporting, not authorization.
- depends on: A canonical actionId/jobId correlation persisted by the executor and relay; A versioned pipeline event schema with monotonic sequence numbers; A small read-only reconciliation worker over GET /jobs, GET /jobs/:jobId/receipts, and GET /pipeline
