## Capabilities it proposed

### "“I’m back—catch me up on anything you did or anything that changed while I was away, and tell me the one thing I should do next.”"
- **useful because:** Today async work, browser watches, scheduled briefs, and pendant conversations can each produce updates, but the owner has to reconstruct them. This gives them a single interruption-safe return point: a short spoken delta, evidence links on the Mac, explicit uncertainty, and one prioritized next action. It is more useful than another dashboard because it answers the human question at the moment they regain attention.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-bridge → browser → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use the relay realtime model only to recognize the short return command and speak the final sentence. A background/cheap model on the relay should gather and rank completed/changed items; mac-planner should reconcile Mac jobs, browser watches, schedules, and pending confirmations; browser supplies private-page evidence; dashboard/Mac writes the detailed evidence packet.
- **latency:** A spoken acknowledgement under 300 ms, then a 1–3 second concise answer from cached event summaries. If reconciliation is still running, speak the top known item and continue in the background, delivering a second update only if it changes the recommendation.
- **cost:** Roughly $0.01–$0.05 per return check if event summaries are precomputed; dominant cost is model ranking and any browser re-read. Avoid sending raw page contents or full transcripts by using typed event deltas and cached evidence snippets.
- **security:** Private browser titles/snippets and Mac activity must remain local or encrypted to the relay; do not read unrelated tabs. Clearly label inferred versus verified changes. Never send, delete, purchase, or submit as part of catch-up; any suggested action remains a separate confirmation-gated job. The owner should be able to say 'only tell me urgent' and 'show evidence.'
- **missing:** A cross-surface event journal with durable completion/change records and deduplication IDs; A resume/return packet compiler that ranks items by urgency, owner commitments, and freshness; A single spoken catch-up intent routed from pendant to relay, with detailed evidence exposed on the Mac/dashboard; A privacy filter that redacts sensitive event payloads from voice while preserving local evidence links

### "“Before I commit to this, show me what it will make harder, what it displaces, and the safest alternative.”"
- **useful because:** The owner can currently ask for calendar facts or have a form prepared, but not see the downstream consequences of a decision across their real life. This capability turns a proposed meeting, purchase, deadline, or browser action into a compact consequence map: conflicts, displaced commitments, travel or time costs, unfinished Mac work, and reversible alternatives. It helps the owner decide before creating a problem rather than receive another reminder afterward.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-bridge → browser → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use a cheaper background reasoning model to construct the consequence graph from typed calendar, task, job, and browser facts. Use the realtime model only for the owner's spoken question and a concise explanation. mac-planner gathers local jobs and files; browser reads only explicitly relevant authenticated pages; the dashboard presents the evidence and alternatives.
- **latency:** A spoken acknowledgement under 300 ms, with a useful first answer in 2–5 seconds from cached facts. Deep comparisons can continue in the background, but the owner must never be blocked from making the original decision.
- **cost:** Approximately $0.02–$0.10 per analysis, dominated by graph construction and any authenticated-page reads. Cache stable commitments and use deterministic conflict checks before invoking a model.
- **security:** This crosses highly sensitive calendar, mail, task, and browser data, so raw content should stay on the Mac whenever possible and only redacted facts should reach the relay. Never make the commitment automatically. Clearly separate observed conflicts from inferred risks, show source/time for each claim, and require confirmation before opening, sending, buying, or rescheduling anything.
- **missing:** A typed personal commitment/dependency graph spanning calendar, reminders, Mac jobs, and browser tasks; A consequence-analysis service that models displacement, deadlines, travel, and reversibility rather than merely listing conflicts; A Mac-local privacy boundary that performs sensitive joins before sending a minimal projection to the relay; A dashboard view for alternative scenarios and an explicit 'do not change anything' mode


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `interaction` — Add an interruption-aware attention broker shared by pendant, relay, Mac, and browser. It maintains a small local state machine (owner speaking/listening, active meeting or focus mode, recent acknowledgment, urgency, quiet hours), scores each event, and chooses one of four outcomes: speak now, vibrate/queue on pendant, place a review card on Mac, or suppress as redundant. Every suppression has an expiry and escalation rule. A single 'what did I miss?' command drains the queue into the return packet rather than replaying notifications.
- **owner gets:** The owner gets fewer pointless interruptions without losing a genuinely time-sensitive account, calendar, or job failure. Returning from a meeting, commute, or dropped connection becomes calm and actionable instead of a pile of repeated alerts.
- effort: Medium-high: define an event schema and urgency policy; implement relay arbitration and durable queue; add Mac Focus/calendar signals and browser-watch severity; add pendant acknowledgment/queue controls; test duplicate, offline, and reconnect cases.  ·  risk: A wrongly suppressed event could matter. Start in shadow mode, show every decision in the dashboard, use conservative defaults, and escalate unacknowledged high-severity items. If relay is unavailable, Mac keeps the queue and pendant falls back to local critical-link indicators.
- cost: Low ongoing API cost because scoring uses typed metadata and a small background model; periodic event summarization is the main cost. No new hardware required.  ·  latency: Immediate local queue/vibration decision under 100 ms when metadata is available; spoken decisions add normal relay round-trip. Reconciliation after reconnect may take seconds.
- security: Event metadata should be minimized and encrypted in transit; sensitive snippets stay on Mac/browser. Calendar Focus state and browser tab identity are private and need explicit retention limits. Dashboard must expose and allow deletion of the queue.
- depends on: A durable cross-surface event journal and idempotent event IDs; The pending resume/return packet primitive; A relay-to-pendant queue/ack protocol; Mac Focus/calendar and browser-watch severity adapters

### `integration` — Create a cross-surface signed action receipt protocol. Every delegated job gets a stable intent ID; Mac, browser, relay, and pendant append compact signed milestones (accepted, read, drafted, submitted, verified, failed) with timestamps, actor, evidence locator, and sensitivity class. The relay can speak a one-sentence receipt, while the Mac stores the full chain and exposes replay/undo where possible. Reconnect reconciliation merges by intent ID instead of producing duplicate 'done' messages.
- **owner gets:** The owner can trust a short answer like 'drafted, not sent' or 'submitted and verified' instead of guessing from a vague success message. It prevents duplicate actions after dropped connections and makes failures recoverable without manually retracing tabs or voice history.
- effort: Medium: define receipt schema and signing keys, add adapters in mac-planner/browser runner/relay, implement local durable storage and reconciliation, then add spoken and dashboard renderers.  ·  risk: A false verification or leaked evidence locator could be worse than no receipt. Verification milestones must require an independent read-back, destructive actions remain confirmation-gated, and receipts should degrade to 'unknown' rather than infer success. Rotate keys and support local deletion.
- cost: Small storage and negligible signing cost; background reconciliation is cheap. No per-receipt realtime model call if renderers use templates; use a cheap model only for ambiguous human summaries.  ·  latency: Adds milliseconds locally and at most one extra read-back for verified actions; voice acknowledgement remains immediate and can say 'working.'
- security: Receipts may reveal private URLs, email subjects, or account identifiers; store detailed evidence locally, transmit only redacted summaries, encrypt the journal, and provide per-intent deletion.
- depends on: A durable cross-surface event/job journal; Idempotent browser and Mac command IDs; A local key store on the Mac and relay verification of device identity; A completion receipt renderer with explicit unknown/verified states

### `context` — Add a local-first commitment graph and counterfactual evaluator. Normalize calendar events, reminders, active Mac jobs, browser transactions, travel buffers, and explicit spoken promises into time-bounded nodes with dependency edges (blocks, requires, displaces, can-defer, irreversible). Given a candidate action, compute a before/after schedule and an uncertainty-labeled set of consequences; return only the minimum redacted facts needed for voice, while retaining inspectable source links on the Mac.
- **owner gets:** The owner gets an answer to 'what happens if I say yes?' instead of a flat list of disconnected reminders. They can choose a viable alternative while preserving existing commitments, with no accidental edits to their calendar or accounts.
- effort: High: define the graph and temporal semantics, build adapters for calendar/task/job/browser sources, implement local privacy-preserving joins, add scenario evaluation and evidence rendering, and test ambiguous or overlapping commitments with human review.  ·  risk: Bad dependency inference could cause the owner to decline something valuable or trust a false conflict. Every edge must carry provenance and confidence; inferred edges should be editable, low-confidence cases should ask one focused question, and the evaluator must be advisory-only until explicitly approved.
- cost: Moderate implementation and local storage; low per-query API cost because deterministic temporal checks handle most cases and a small background model resolves only semantic ambiguity.  ·  latency: Simple schedule checks should answer in under a second locally; semantic dependency analysis may take a few seconds and can stream a preliminary result first.
- security: The graph is exceptionally sensitive because it reveals routines and obligations. Keep the canonical graph encrypted on the Mac, use field-level redaction before relay transmission, enforce TTLs for browser-derived facts, and provide a one-command purge and audit trail.
- depends on: A durable typed representation of owner commitments and dependencies; Calendar/task/browser/Mac-job adapters with source timestamps; A local privacy boundary for cross-source joins; A scenario renderer on the Mac and concise spoken renderer on the pendant

## Its own summary

Round 10 produced three new stack-level directions: (1) an owner-facing 'I'm back—catch me up' return packet that reconciles background work and changes into one short spoken next action with local evidence; (2) an interruption-aware attention broker that decides speak, queue, show, or suppress across pendant/relay/Mac/browser; and (3) signed cross-surface action receipts so 'drafted, not sent' and 'submitted and verified' are trustworthy and reconnects cannot duplicate work. These extend rather than restate the backlog's generic async, watch, resume, and provenance ideas.

**Biggest unknown:** The exact durable event/job persistence and resume primitives, plus interruption preferences and the 24 kHz audio acceptance criteria, remain unavailable to me. To implement these proposals I still need the shared event schema/retention rules, severity and quiet-mode policy, receipt verification semantics, and confirmation that the end-to-end audio path can carry queued acknowledgements and return packets.
