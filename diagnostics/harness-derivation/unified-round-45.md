## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Remember this commitment: I said I’d send Alex the revised budget Friday.” Later, find the right thread, remind me before it is due, and prepare the follow-up without sending it."
- **useful because:** A spoken commitment is easy to lose while away from the desk. The pendant can capture it even with LTE loss, the relay can retain it, the Mac can turn it into a structured commitment and reminder, and the authenticated browser can locate the correct private thread. The owner gets a reviewable follow-through packet rather than a dead voice note or an unsafe automatic message.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-planner → browser-extension → dashboard → unified
- **model tier:** Use realtime only for the short capture/acknowledgement. Use a cheaper background model on the relay to normalize person, action, deadline, and confidence; use Mac-planner for context-graph/calendar matching; use the browser harness only for authenticated retrieval and draft filling.
- **latency:** Immediate pendant acknowledgement under 2 seconds when connected; offline acknowledgement immediately and sync on reconnect. Background extraction under 60 seconds. At deadline-minus-configured-lead-time, a concise pendant alert; dashboard review can wait for the Mac.
- **cost:** Usually one short realtime turn plus background text extraction; roughly $0.01–$0.05 per commitment depending on captured audio duration. Browser and Mac work dominate wall-clock time, not model cost.
- **security:** Audio and the extracted commitment leave the pendant for relay storage; encrypt in transit and at rest, retain raw audio briefly, and let the owner delete it. Authenticated page contents stay on the Mac/browser bridge and only cited snippets plus normalized fields go to the relay. Never send mail or submit a form automatically; require explicit approval and show recipient, thread, body, deadline, and source evidence.
- **missing:** A first-class commitment object with owner-editable fields, confidence, due time, provenance, and lifecycle state (captured, matched, drafted, approved, completed, dismissed).; An offline pendant capture/sync contract that distinguishes a deliberate commitment recording from a generic bookmark and deduplicates replay after reconnect.; A cross-surface matcher linking spoken names/actions to Calendar, Reminders, Mail, and authenticated browser tabs, with citation-preserving draft generation.; A durable deadline scheduler and pendant alert policy with quiet hours, snooze, escalation, and a spoken confirmation receipt.

### "“When you have a prepared high-impact action, read me the exact consequence and let me approve that exact version with a deliberate pendant gesture—even if I am away from my Mac.”"
- **useful because:** Today a browser or Mac draft can reach a review point, but there is no durable, device-bound way for the owner to approve the exact content from the thing they are wearing. This would turn the pendant into a clear human-consent surface: the owner can approve a specific bank transfer, email, purchase, deletion, or submission without returning to the desk, while preventing stale drafts, replayed approvals, or vague spoken confirmations from authorizing a different action.
- **path:** browser-extension → mac-planner → relay → pendant → dashboard → unified
- **model tier:** Use a cheaper background model to summarize the already-prepared action and identify irreversible consequences. Use realtime only to read the concise approval prompt and interpret the owner's immediate response. Deterministic code, not a model, binds approval to the action hash and enforces expiry and single use.
- **latency:** Generate the approval card in seconds. Pendant acknowledgement should be immediate when connected and queued safely when disconnected. Approval expires after a short owner-configured window, such as five minutes; execution receipt should return within seconds when the target surface is online.
- **cost:** Usually negligible model cost after the draft already exists; one short realtime turn for spoken confirmation, roughly fractions of a cent to a few cents. The dominant cost is implementation and secure key storage, not inference.
- **security:** The relay must never treat a free-form ‘yes’ as sufficient. Bind a one-time approval to an action hash, target account, recipient, amount or content summary, and expiry; require a deliberate button pattern or explicit spoken confirmation that includes a distinguishing detail. Reject stale or changed drafts, duplicate approvals, offline ambiguity, and missing receipts. Store only an approval token and redacted summary in relay logs; keep page contents and credentials on the Mac/browser. Support immediate local mute/revoke and owner-visible audit history.
- **missing:** A pendant-backed, replay-resistant approval primitive with a deliberate gesture and a device-held secret or attested key.; A canonical action hash and normalized consequence summary shared by Mac, browser, relay, and dashboard.; An approval escrow in the relay with expiry, nonce consumption, cancellation, and exactly-once delivery semantics.; A browser/Mac executor gate that refuses any action whose prepared state differs from the approved hash and returns a verifiable execution receipt.; A concise spoken approval UX that remains understandable over the current narrowband/half-duplex audio path.


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Add a durable Commitment Ledger spanning pendant sync, relay D1, Mac context graph, and browser workbench. Each record stores a normalized action, people, due/lead times, confidence, source audio hash or bookmark id, cited private-page snippets, current state, and idempotency key. On reconnect, the relay atomically ingests pendant records and emits one work item; Mac-planner enriches it from Calendar/Reminders/Mail; browser actions can only create a draft packet with before/after evidence. A scheduler emits one deduplicated alert, and every mutation yields a receipt with approve/snooze/dismiss/complete transitions. Raw audio and sensitive snippets have independent retention/deletion controls.
- **owner gets:** The owner can make a promise once, in the moment, and reliably find it later with the right private thread and a ready-to-review follow-up—even when the Mac or browser was unavailable at capture time.
- effort: Medium-high: new relay schema/API and sync protocol, pendant record type, Mac context-graph adapter, browser evidence adapter, scheduler, dashboard review UI, and end-to-end fault-injection tests for duplicate/reordered/offline delivery.  ·  risk: False person or deadline matches could create a misleading draft or nag. Keep confidence and citations visible, default to review-only, require approval for any external write, and make every state transition reversible. If a node is offline, retain the record and report pending rather than claiming completion.
- cost: Small D1/storage and scheduler cost; background extraction is cheap. Raw audio retention should be bounded and user-configurable. No realtime call after initial capture unless the owner asks.  ·  latency: Immediate acknowledgement is unchanged; matching may take seconds to a minute. Offline capture has zero network wait and syncs later.
- security: Sensitive commitment text and private-page snippets need per-field encryption/access controls and deletion propagation. Browser content should remain on the Mac except cited minimum evidence; never transmit session cookies or full page dumps.
- depends on: A durable offline pendant note/record format and replay-safe sync; The typed context projection service (still backlog); A browser command queue with tab affinity and provenance receipts; A relay scheduler and alert deduplication policy; Mac Accessibility/Screen Recording permission repair is not required for API-only Calendar/Mail/Reminders paths, but GUI fallback must fail closed

### `hardware` — Add a low-power secure element to the production pendant, with a dedicated approval input path: generate and retain a non-exportable device key, sign a canonical action hash plus nonce and expiry only after a deliberate button gesture or confirmed local interaction, and expose a tamper-evident approval result over the existing relay link. Keep the current single button available for conversation; use a distinct long-press/double-press sequence only after the pendant has locally announced the redacted consequence summary. Include a visible LED pattern and local cancellation gesture.
- **owner gets:** The owner can safely authorize an exact prepared action from the device they wear, without trusting an ambiguous ‘yes’, returning to the Mac, or risking that a stale or modified draft is executed.
- effort: High for production hardware and firmware: secure-element selection and PCB revision, key provisioning/recovery, gesture state machine, relay verification, Mac/browser executor gates, and failure-injection testing for replay, loss, reordered delivery, and changed action contents.  ·  risk: A confusing gesture or compromised pendant could block needed work or authorize the wrong thing. Default closed, require local confirmation, make tokens single-use and short-lived, provide a cancel gesture, and retain a dashboard audit trail. If the secure element or link fails, fall back to ordinary review—not automatic execution.
- cost: Roughly a few US dollars per production unit plus PCB and certification work; negligible runtime API cost. Secure-element operations add tiny local latency and a small idle power draw, while LTE transmission remains the dominant power cost.  ·  latency: Local signing should add milliseconds; end-to-end approval remains bounded by LTE/relay/browser availability. Offline approval should be held as an unexecuted intent, never treated as completed authorization.
- security: Strongly improves phishing/replay resistance and separates approval identity from the Mac. It introduces key provisioning, backup/replacement, revocation, and attestation obligations; no private page contents or credentials should enter the pendant.
- depends on: Canonical cross-surface action hashing and consequence summaries; Relay approval escrow with nonce/expiry/exactly-once semantics; Mac and browser execution gates that verify the signed hash; A production pendant design beyond the current nRF9160 development kit
