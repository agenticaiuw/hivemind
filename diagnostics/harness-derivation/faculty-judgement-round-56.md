## Capabilities it proposed

### "Tell me what I can safely ignore today, and keep anything risky from slipping through."
- **useful because:** The owner currently gets briefs and alerts, but still has to decide what not to read. This gives them a finite attention budget: it separates harmless noise from items whose delay could cost money, time, or a missed commitment, then quietly defers the harmless items while preserving an auditable way back. It is a judgement service, not another inbox summary.
- **path:** browser-extension: inspect already-authenticated mail, calendar, task, order, and work tabs without exposing credentials → mac-planner/mac-terminal: read local reminders/notes and current focus state, and write a reversible daily triage artifact → relay-realtime: deliver a single short spoken verdict and accept 'show me deferred' or 'why not ignore this?' → pendant: provide a one-button/voice 'defer for today' and an offline latch for the chosen quiet policy → relay: run the scheduled/background scan, retain evidence and wake the owner only when the risk score crosses a threshold
- **model tier:** Background scan and clustering use a cheaper slower model; realtime is used only for the owner's follow-up question or a newly urgent event. A deterministic policy layer computes deadlines, sender/account importance, and confidence before any model judgement.
- **latency:** Initial daily scan may take 1–3 minutes in the background; the spoken answer should be under 2 seconds from cached results. Any new page read can take up to 10 seconds and must return source links and freshness.
- **cost:** Roughly $0.02–$0.10 per daily scan depending on number of private pages and model context; most cost is authenticated-page extraction and the final ranked synthesis, not the short spoken response.
- **security:** Private mail/calendar/task contents leave the browser bridge only to the relay's authenticated job path. Never archive, delete, send, or change deadlines automatically. Deferral is reversible and records item URL, source timestamp, reason, expiry, and risk confidence. Require confirmation before muting an entire sender, changing a reminder, or suppressing an item beyond one day.
- **missing:** A durable attention-arbitration/risk policy with per-item expiry and a user-visible deferred queue; Cross-surface authenticated reads normalized into one evidence record (not just independent summaries); A pendant-local offline attention latch and a relay wake rule; A review/undo endpoint for deferrals and a feedback loop that learns 'ignore this' without silently broadening permissions

### "Before I say yes to something, tell me what it will really cost me and give me a reply I can send."
- **useful because:** Today the owner can ask for calendar or browser summaries, but cannot turn a spoken opportunity or request into a consequence-aware decision. This would notice hidden costs—travel, preparation, recovery time, overlapping obligations, and deadline risk—then offer a concise accept, decline, or negotiate response before the commitment becomes real.
- **path:** pendant: capture the spoken offer or request in the moment, including an offline provisional marker when disconnected → relay: maintain the provisional decision window, calculate expiry, and deliver the short spoken recommendation → mac-planner/mac-terminal: inspect calendar, reminders, notes, travel context, and current work state; create a provisional hold and response draft → browser-extension: read the relevant authenticated message/event/page and place a reply draft in the correct private session without sending → unified/faculty-judgement: compare opportunity value against existing obligations and explain the recommendation with evidence → faculty-action: after explicit approval, send the reply or commit the calendar change and return a receipt
- **model tier:** Use deterministic checks for time conflicts, travel buffers, preparation estimates, deadlines, and existing commitments. Use a cheaper background model to infer preparation/recovery cost and draft language; use the expensive realtime tier only for the owner's live follow-up or an ambiguous tradeoff.
- **latency:** A cached spoken recommendation should arrive within 2 seconds. Fresh private-page/calendar inspection may take 5–15 seconds and should be announced as a pending decision window rather than blocking the conversation. Provisional captures must survive a dropped link and reconcile later.
- **cost:** Approximately $0.02–$0.12 per decision, dominated by authenticated-page extraction and ambiguous-cost reasoning; routine conflict checks are local and nearly free. Sending or booking is not performed without explicit confirmation.
- **security:** The system handles private messages, calendar, location/travel, and possibly sensitive commitments. Keep evidence scoped to the job with short retention and provenance hashes. Never infer or disclose private constraints in a drafted reply unless the owner approves the wording. External sends, purchases, and irreversible bookings require confirmation; stale drafts must be revalidated against the source page and calendar before commit.
- **missing:** A durable provisional-commitment and decision-window store shared by pendant, relay, Mac, and browser; A typed obligation/cost graph that represents preparation, travel, recovery, deadlines, and uncertainty rather than only calendar events; A cross-surface preflight and stale-source revalidation step immediately before sending or booking; Pendant offline capture/replay with conflict resolution when the same decision is edited on the Mac; A review UI showing assumptions, alternatives, and the exact reply or calendar mutation before approval


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `routines` — Add a cross-surface 'attention budget' routine between existing browser/mac readers and relay delivery. It emits a typed triage record for each candidate (deadline, consequence-of-delay, confidence, evidence URL/timestamp, defer-until, and required confirmation), maintains a reversible deferred queue, and only wakes the pendant when an item's risk exceeds the owner's current budget. 'Why?' speaks the evidence; 'show deferred' reopens the queue. Run policy checks before model ranking so the model cannot silently suppress a deadline or destructive request.
- **owner gets:** Instead of another long morning summary, the owner hears what is safe to ignore and can spend attention on the few things where delay matters. Low-value noise stays quiet without being lost, and every suppression can be inspected or undone.
- effort: Medium: typed schema and D1/R2 persistence, adapters for existing browser/mac results, deterministic deadline/consequence rules, background scheduler integration, spoken query/pendant controls, and dashboard review. One integration test per source plus expiry and offline-reconnect tests.  ·  risk: False negatives could hide a genuinely important item; default to 'surface' when confidence is low, sender/account is unknown, or evidence is stale. Never auto-send/delete/pay/change appointments. If persistence or scoring fails, fall back to the unfiltered brief and report the failure. Revoke all deferrals on account/session loss until revalidated.
- cost: Low incremental API cost: rules handle most items, with a small background model call only for ambiguous clusters (roughly $0.01–$0.05/day). Storage is small JSON records plus evidence hashes; no new hardware required.  ·  latency: No foreground cost for scheduled scans; cached verdict under 2 seconds over relay. Fresh authenticated extraction remains network-bound (about 5–10 seconds) and is explicitly labeled.
- security: Preserve source URLs and hashes rather than copying full private content into long-lived memory; encrypt item evidence, apply short TTLs, and scope each record to its browser session. Deferral is an attention preference, not permission to act.
- depends on: A durable cross-surface job/event persistence primitive; The pending attention_arbitrate policy/tool or an equivalent deterministic policy evaluator; An offline pendant attention latch with reconnect replay; A user-visible review/undo route for deferred items

### `integration` — Create a first-class Commitment Decision object and compiler spanning pendant capture, relay persistence, Mac/browser evidence, judgement, and action. It must store the owner's exact spoken offer, candidate commitments, assumptions, cost intervals, conflicts, source references, response alternatives, expiry, and approval state. Compile it into typed reversible actions (calendar hold, reminder, browser draft), then run a fresh preflight against URL/field hashes and calendar version immediately before any external commit. If the owner is offline, queue only the provisional object and replay it with a conflict prompt—not an automatic send.
- **owner gets:** The owner can make a good decision in the moment instead of accepting first and discovering the time and emotional cost later. They get an answer, a usable reply, and a safe path to act without losing the context that motivated the decision.
- effort: High: new shared schema and persistence, pendant event protocol, Mac/browser evidence adapters, cost/obligation estimator, decision-window UX, stale-source preflight, and end-to-end receipt/undo tests across reconnect and duplicate events.  ·  risk: Bad estimates could make the owner decline worthwhile opportunities. Show ranges and assumptions, never present estimates as facts, and default to 'needs review' when evidence is incomplete. Never send, book, or disclose a private constraint automatically. On any replay conflict, stop and ask.
- cost: Moderate implementation cost and small durable storage footprint. Per decision is roughly $0.02–$0.12 for private extraction and ambiguous reasoning; deterministic checks avoid repeated model context.  ·  latency: Immediate capture is local; cached recommendation under 2 seconds. Evidence collection is asynchronous (5–15 seconds), with a pending-window notification rather than a blocked voice turn.
- security: Commitment objects contain sensitive personal plans and relationship context. Encrypt at rest, apply short evidence TTLs, separate assumptions from source facts, bind approvals to exact source hashes and expiry, and redact private reasons from outward drafts unless explicitly selected.
- depends on: A new durable cross-surface event/decision store; A typed obligation and consequence graph; A pendant offline handoff/replay protocol; Fresh browser/Mac preflight and stale-approval checks; A review surface for assumptions and exact outbound mutations
