## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Save where I am and remind me at 3—or when I get back to my Mac—then put me back exactly where I left off and give me the short version.”"
- **useful because:** Interruptions currently lose the relationship between the pendant request, the Mac's active work, and authenticated browser state. A resume capsule would let the owner safely switch contexts without re-finding tabs, files, or the last unfinished step. It is genuinely multi-surface: the pendant supplies intent and timing, the always-on relay persists a compact capsule, the Mac observes and restores desktop state, and the browser reattaches private tabs.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-bridge → browser → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use the realtime model only to parse the spoken save/resume intent and answer the immediate acknowledgment. Use a cheaper background model to compress the snapshot into a structured capsule, classify stale/unsafe restoration steps, and generate the short spoken brief. The relay scheduler delivers the reminder without an LLM call unless the capsule needs summarization.
- **latency:** Acknowledge in under 1 second; capture in 3 seconds. At the scheduled time, deliver the reminder immediately, then restore reversible app/tab state in under 10 seconds and speak a 20–30 second summary. If the Mac or browser is offline, retain the reminder and report the missing surface rather than silently pretending to restore it.
- **cost:** About $0.001–$0.01 per save/resume, dominated by one small background summarization call; scheduled reminders and deterministic state capture cost no model tokens. A long or noisy snapshot should be truncated before model use.
- **security:** Capsules can contain private URLs, window titles, document names, and snippets from logged-in tabs. Store only an owner-selected allowlist (active app, tab titles/URLs, file path, explicit note), redact page bodies and secrets by default, encrypt at rest, attach an expiry, and show provenance on the Mac. Never replay clicks, submits, sends, purchases, or destructive actions during restore; reopen/read only and ask the owner separately. The pendant should support a physical privacy cancel before saving a sensitive capsule.
- **missing:** A durable resume-capsule schema with redaction, sensitivity, expiry, and provenance fields; A trigger that combines a one-shot schedule with Mac-return/foreground-online presence; Read-only capture of active window, caret/selection, workspace, and authenticated browser tab metadata in one atomic snapshot; Browser session/tab reattachment by stable session and tab identifiers, with stale-tab detection; A deterministic restore plan that opens apps/files/URLs but never performs consequential UI mutations; A pendant delivery path for capsule reminder plus a concise audio summary and a dashboard listing/expiry control

### "“Protect my focus: interrupt me on the pendant only for something genuinely urgent; queue everything else and give me one concise catch-up when I’m free.”"
- **useful because:** The system can already produce briefs, watch pages, and finish Mac jobs, but it lacks a shared attention policy. Today each surface can alert independently, causing duplicate or badly timed interruptions. This capability turns the pendant into one attention gate: browser changes, mail/calendar signals, and Mac job receipts are deduplicated, ranked against the owner's current focus and calendar, then either delivered now or batched for a quiet window.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-bridge → browser → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use deterministic rules for urgency, duplicate suppression, quiet hours, and calendar conflict. Use the cheaper background model to rank ambiguous changes and write the batch digest. Reserve realtime for an explicit spoken override such as “what am I missing?” or “interrupt me now.”
- **latency:** Ingest events within 30 seconds and make the interrupt/batch decision in under 2 seconds without an LLM. Urgent delivery should reach the pendant within 5 seconds. A batched digest can be generated at the next quiet window in under 30 seconds.
- **cost:** Near-zero for rule-based event routing; roughly $0.002–$0.02 per digest depending on queued item count. Cost is dominated by one compact summarization call, not by every event.
- **security:** The policy engine sees sensitive mail subjects, private browser changes, calendar titles, and Mac job details. Keep raw content on the originating surface, pass only minimal typed event facts to the relay, encrypt queues, set per-source sensitivity and retention, and make high-sensitivity events opt-in. Never infer urgency from message content without retaining source/provenance. A physical pendant gesture must pause all delivery immediately; dashboard must expose why each item was interrupted or deferred.
- **missing:** A shared attention-policy object with urgency thresholds, source sensitivity, quiet windows, batching limits, and a physical pause state; An event envelope and idempotent deduplication key shared by browser watches, Mac receipts, calendar/mail readers, and relay jobs; A relay-side policy evaluator that can fan out one event to pendant audio/haptics, Mac notification, or a deferred queue; A cross-surface urgency classifier with provenance and an explainable 'why now' result; A durable queue with expiry and a one-tap spoken review/acknowledge flow; Dashboard controls for previewing, editing, and temporarily overriding the policy

### "“Use what I’m looking at on my Mac: read the selected text or page, answer my question about it on the pendant, and—when I say so—put the result back into the right document or browser field.”"
- **useful because:** Today the owner must manually copy context from the Mac into the pendant conversation and then manually carry the answer back. This would make the wearable a hands-free companion to the exact desktop focus: a selected paragraph, spreadsheet cell, document, or authenticated page. It is not a generic Mac command or a browser watcher; it is a secure, ephemeral semantic bridge from current desktop context to pendant conversation and back.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-bridge → browser → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use a small background/text model for extraction cleanup and question answering; use realtime only for the live spoken exchange. Deterministic routing should decide whether the source is selected text, current document, or active browser tab. Do not send screenshots or full page bodies unless explicitly requested.
- **latency:** Capture the focused context in under 2 seconds, answer a short question in under 4 seconds, and stage a requested insertion in under 3 seconds. If context capture fails, say exactly which surface is unavailable rather than guessing.
- **cost:** Approximately $0.002–$0.02 per interaction, dominated by text understanding and answer generation. Most short selections fit in one inexpensive request; no model call is needed for capture, hashing, or insertion staging.
- **security:** Selected text and authenticated pages may contain passwords, health data, or confidential work. Keep the payload ephemeral, encrypt transport and relay storage, redact secrets/credentials, attach source app/URL and timestamp, and delete it after the response unless the owner explicitly saves it. Never submit, send, or overwrite on a spoken 'answer' alone: insertion must be a separate explicit command and show the target app/field plus a before/after preview. Disable capture while the pendant privacy pause is active.
- **missing:** A Mac bridge operation that reads the current selection and focused target without requiring a screenshot, with app/URL/title provenance; A browser operation that returns the active tab's selected text and a stable editable-target descriptor; An ephemeral context envelope shared between Mac, relay, pendant, and browser, with redaction, TTL, and size limits; A staging API that proposes an insertion and returns a diff/target preview before any write; Pendant protocol messages for context-ready, answer-ready, and insertion-preview audio/haptic states; A dashboard view showing the source, payload expiry, and exact target of any staged insertion

### "“If one part of the system is unavailable, keep the useful part moving and tell me exactly what is waiting—then finish the private step automatically when that device comes back.”"
- **useful because:** A disconnected browser currently strands an entire request even when the relay, pendant, and Mac are healthy. The owner should get graceful partial completion: public research, local drafting, and a clear pending private step can proceed now; authenticated reads or writes wait without being retried blindly, then resume only after the original session and target are verified. This is a fault-tolerant cross-surface workflow, not another watcher or daily brief.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-bridge → browser → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use deterministic dependency analysis and health checks to split work. Use a cheap background model to summarize completed portions and explain the blocked portion. Use realtime only to answer the owner's immediate question or accept a change to the fallback plan.
- **latency:** Detect a missing surface within 5 seconds, produce a partial result within the normal task budget, and notify the owner once. On reconnection, validate state in under 10 seconds before proposing continuation; never silently execute a stale private mutation.
- **cost:** Usually $0.001–$0.01 extra for a partial-result explanation; health checks, dependency tracking, and continuation validation are deterministic. This reduces wasted model and browser retries.
- **security:** Private steps must retain only opaque session/tab references and a redacted intent until the browser returns. Public fallback must never receive authenticated page content. Continuation must compare origin, URL, title, session binding, and a page fingerprint; any mismatch becomes a review item. Mutations always require a newly rendered preview, even if the original request was approved.
- **missing:** A typed task dependency graph that can mark steps complete, blocked, substituted, or awaiting a surface; A policy-controlled fallback planner distinguishing public data, local files, authenticated reads, and irreversible writes; A durable continuation record with session/tab binding, page fingerprint, expiry, and stale-state detection; A single partial-result protocol for pendant speech, Mac receipts, and dashboard status; A reconnect validator that re-plans only the blocked steps and emits a clear diff before continuing


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Add a cross-surface causal event ledger. Every spoken request, relay event, browser command, Mac job, and pendant delivery receives a correlation ID, parent ID, origin surface, intended effect, sensitivity, and terminal outcome. The relay maintains a lease per correlation ID so reconnects cannot duplicate a reminder or action; Mac and browser receipts append typed evidence, and the pendant can ask 'what happened to that?' and get the same timeline. Expire abandoned leases and expose a compact redacted timeline in the dashboard.
- **owner gets:** The owner gets one trustworthy answer about whether a request was heard, queued, acted on, or failed, instead of duplicate Mac actions, repeated browser submissions, or a pendant saying 'done' when a surface was offline. It also makes recovery after Wi‑Fi loss understandable without exposing raw private content.
- effort: Medium-high: define the event/receipt schema, add correlation propagation to relay, browser and Mac adapters, persist an append-only or compacted ledger, and add reconciliation tests for retries and reconnects.  ·  risk: A schema or clock mismatch could make work appear stuck or incorrectly completed. Recover by treating terminal receipts as immutable, using monotonic sequence numbers per job, and providing a replay/reconcile worker. Never infer success from a timeout.
- cost: Small D1/R2 storage and negligible compute; compact old entries and retain only hashes/metadata by default. One additional receipt write per cross-surface step; no model cost.  ·  latency: Usually under 20 ms per event write; reconciliation is asynchronous. A first response can be acknowledged before the ledger is fully compacted.
- security: The ledger is a sensitive activity trail. Store redacted metadata by default, encrypt it, scope reads to the owner/session, and hash or omit URLs, snippets, file names, and message contents unless explicitly requested. Correlation IDs must be unguessable.
- depends on: Existing Mac job receipts and undo routes; Browser command queue request IDs and tab/session affinity; A relay durable job/event store; A typed pendant delivery acknowledgment path

### `browser-harness` — Add an offline-queue circuit breaker and stale-command reconciler. When browser/status reports offline or a command exceeds its deadline, stop issuing retries, atomically mark the command blocked_on_surface with the last heartbeat and reason, and expose a single resumable queue entry. On the next heartbeat, revalidate tab/session affinity and page identity before replaying only idempotent reads; discard or require a new explicit plan for writes. Also alert the relay/pendant once, not once per retry.
- **owner gets:** The current Mac has an offline browser bridge with 10 pending commands, and prior navigation jobs can wait 45 seconds before failing. This change prevents invisible backlogs, duplicate browser work, and repeated false 'working' status. The owner gets one honest message—browser unavailable, N items held—and automatic safe recovery when the extension returns.
- effort: Medium: implement heartbeat-aware queue state transitions, deadlines, deduplication, typed read/write replay rules, and a small reconciliation worker plus tests for disconnect/reconnect and stale tabs.  ·  risk: A false offline signal could delay a harmless read, or a tab may change between disconnect and reconnect. Recover by expiring leases, rechecking URL/title/session IDs, and requiring a fresh plan for any mutation. Keep held commands inspectable and cancellable.
- cost: Negligible model cost; a few D1/local queue writes and one heartbeat check per interval. Reduces wasted browser action retries and their associated latency.  ·  latency: Failures become fast (seconds rather than 45-second timeouts); safe reads resume within one heartbeat plus validation. Writes never replay automatically.
- security: Do not retain page bodies in the held queue. Store only command type, redacted session/tab identifiers, deadline, and reason. Encrypt queue records and ensure stale authenticated commands cannot replay against a different tab.
- depends on: Existing GET /browser/status and POST /browser/heartbeat; Browser command queue with request IDs, idempotency keys, and tab/session affinity; Existing Mac job receipts and cancellation/status routes; A relay notification path for one deduplicated surface-health event
