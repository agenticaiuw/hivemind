## Capabilities it proposed

### "When something I asked you to do fails, tell me on the pendant exactly what broke and offer to repair or retry it; if I ask later, 'what happened?', show the command, app/tab, error, and whether anything changed."
- **useful because:** Today a failed shell action loses exit code, cancellation cannot stop a running command, and jobs, receipts, and ledgers do not join cleanly. The owner should not have to inspect JSON or guess whether a browser action partially happened. This turns opaque failures into a trustworthy, actionable answer across the worn interface and Mac.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use the realtime model only to understand the short spoken question and speak the result; use a cheaper background model to normalize receipts, classify failure, and draft a repair plan. The Mac executes only after the normal owner instruction policy.
- **latency:** Pendant failure beacon immediately; structured incident capsule within 2 seconds after job completion; spoken explanation under 3 seconds. Repair can run asynchronously with progress pushed to the pendant.
- **cost:** ~$0.005–$0.03 per incident depending on whether diagnosis needs a model; most incidents are deterministic receipt/error formatting. Storage is a small bounded incident record.
- **security:** Command strings, stderr, URLs, and browser titles may contain secrets or personal data. Redact environment values and known token patterns before relay storage, retain locally by default, and require explicit confirmation before retrying a non-idempotent repair. Never claim success without a receipt.
- **missing:** Capture shell exit code and signal instead of flattening exec errors; Record effective timeout, pid, and a redacted environment fingerprint; Close the action ledger and persist a jobId↔ledgerId link; Make cancellation pass an AbortSignal to the child process; Add a bounded incident endpoint and pendant push event; Define retry/idempotency metadata for repair plans

### "Let me say 'pick up where I left off' and have the pendant reopen the exact work state from my last interrupted task—active Safari tab, app/window, project and branch, terminal directory, and the unfinished step—then ask one concise question if re-entry is ambiguous."
- **useful because:** A Mac restart, bridge disconnect, or abandoned multi-step task currently leaves the owner to reconstruct context manually. This is not a bookmark: it is an automatically generated, resumable work capsule tied to an interrupted execution, with a safe re-entry action and a spoken disambiguation path.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Generate and rank capsules in the background with a cheap model and deterministic host collectors; use realtime only for the owner's spoken resume request and one clarification. Mac-planner performs the re-entry actions; browser-extension restores the authenticated tab without exporting page contents.
- **latency:** Create capsule on interruption/restart in under 5 seconds; answer the spoken request in under 2 seconds; restore app/tab/window in under 5 seconds, then report the exact unresolved step.
- **cost:** ~$0.002–$0.015 per capsule; collection is local and deterministic, with model cost only for summarizing the unfinished step. Bounded local storage under a few MB.
- **security:** Capsules can reveal sensitive app names, URLs, project paths, and session identifiers. Keep page bodies and credentials out; encrypt or hash sensitive fields, retain on the Mac by default, and require confirmation before replaying a mutation. Reject stale capsules after project/branch changes.
- **missing:** Boot-time reconciliation that marks processing jobs interrupted rather than leaving them forever running; Automatic interrupted-job capsule creation from job and action-ledger state; Collectors for foreground app/window, terminal cwd/git state, and active browser tab; A durable capsule schema with freshness and stale-state checks; A Mac action to focus an existing window and browser command to select a specific tab; Relay storage/push route for capsule summaries

### "Tell me when my personal computer setup is unhealthy, and fix the harmless parts automatically: 'my pendant says the Mac is unreachable', 'Safari bridge is stale', or 'why is this slow?' should produce a spoken diagnosis with the exact stale component and one-click repair, not a generic offline message."
- **useful because:** The relay, Mac bridge, browser extension, and pendant can each be alive while the path between them is broken. The owner currently sees fragmented health signals and may not know whether to restart an app, reconnect USB, or wait for the relay. A cross-node health explanation saves the most frustrating daily failures.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use deterministic probes and a cheap background classifier for health snapshots; reserve realtime for the spoken question and terse explanation. Do not spend the expensive model on periodic polling.
- **latency:** Relay detects a missed heartbeat within 30 seconds; pendant receives a local status pattern immediately and a spoken diagnosis within 3 seconds of asking. Harmless repair (restart bridge, reopen extension host, reconnect polling) should complete within 15 seconds.
- **cost:** Usually <$0.001 per health cycle with deterministic probes; occasional model explanation <$0.01. Network and local polling dominate, not inference.
- **security:** Health data exposes hostnames, app inventory, tab URLs, and connectivity. Send only booleans, timestamps, component IDs, and redacted error classes to relay; keep detailed diagnostics on Mac. Automatic repair must be limited to reversible service/app restarts and should leave an audit receipt.
- **missing:** A signed end-to-end health envelope with monotonic timestamps and component identity; USB serial probes for the physically connected nRF9160 and ESP32 (currently attached but pendant is not LTE-registered); A relay route that correlates Mac, browser, pendant, and bridge heartbeats into one causal status; Typed reversible repair actions and receipts for restarting/reconnecting local agents; Pendant push mapping from component-specific health state to the existing truthful status beacon

### "Make sure this gets done by morning, and only wake me if you need a decision: keep the authenticated browser task and Mac work alive across disconnects, retry transient failures, verify the final result independently, and tell me on the pendant if the goal is blocked or complete."
- **useful because:** Today the system can dispatch actions, but a multi-step goal dies as soon as one command fails, the bridge disappears, or a browser command goes stale. The owner cannot delegate an outcome—only a sequence of steps. This would turn the pendant into a genuine asynchronous agent: it carries the goal while the owner is away, uses the Mac and authenticated browser session together, and interrupts only for an irreversible choice or missing information.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use a cheap background model for goal decomposition, transient-error classification, checkpoint verification, and scheduling. Use the realtime model only when the pendant must ask a concise decision question or report completion. Deterministic planners should handle known retryable actions without model calls.
- **latency:** Accept the goal in under 3 seconds and provide a durable queued confirmation immediately. Resume within 10 seconds of a Mac/browser reconnect. Retry with bounded exponential backoff; wake the owner within 30 seconds of a true blocker and within 5 seconds of verified completion.
- **cost:** ~$0.01–$0.10 per delegated goal depending on number of checkpoints; most polling, retries, and verification are deterministic. Mac/browser execution and relay storage dominate infrastructure cost, not speech.
- **security:** A long-lived goal can mutate authenticated web accounts while the owner is absent. Require an explicit scope and expiry, forbid silently broadening the goal, keep credentials in the browser session, and stop at payment, deletion, sending, publishing, or other irreversible boundaries. Store only redacted checkpoint evidence and give the owner a pendant-visible cancel/status path.
- **missing:** A durable goal state machine separate from one-shot jobs, with expiry, checkpoints, dependencies, and wake conditions; Real action retries and idempotency keys for Mac and browser operations, with a distinction between transient and semantic failure; A browser-session lease/heartbeat that can detect stale authenticated tabs and resume the same tab without exporting secrets; Independent postcondition verification actions and evidence capsules; A scheduler that can run a goal while the owner and realtime model are offline; A pendant command/status protocol for queued, running, blocked, expired, and verified-complete goals; A single cancellation path that terminates active Mac processes and withdraws pending browser commands

### "When a website needs me to sign in or approve something, tell me on the pendant which site, account, and exact action it is requesting; let me approve that one request by pressing the button, without sending my password or exposing the page to the cloud."
- **useful because:** Authenticated browser sessions are the strongest reach in the system, but the owner has no trustworthy bridge between a remote browser prompt and a physical, private decision. Today an agent either stalls on MFA/approval or asks for sensitive credentials in conversation. A signed, origin-bound approval lets unattended work proceed while keeping secrets and the final consent with the wearer.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → browser-extension → mac-planner → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use deterministic browser instrumentation to identify origin, account label, requested operation, and challenge type; use a cheap model only to summarize ambiguous page text locally. Realtime should speak only the short approval prompt. Never use the expensive model to handle credentials or OTP values.
- **latency:** Detect and push an approval card within 2 seconds of the browser challenge; button approval reaches the extension within 1 second; report success or rejection within 3 seconds.
- **cost:** <$0.005 per approval, mostly relay/event overhead; no credential transcription or large page upload. A small local approval ledger is the main storage cost.
- **security:** This is a high-risk authorization surface. Bind each approval to exact origin, tab, account, action hash, expiry, and nonce; reject navigation or DOM changes after preview; never transmit passwords, OTPs, cookies, or page bodies through relay. Require a distinct physical press and show an unmistakable local pending pattern; log both approval and browser result. A stale or mismatched challenge must fail closed.
- **missing:** Browser extension instrumentation for a signed, structured approval request and action hash; A relay route that transports only redacted challenge metadata and nonce-bound responses; Pendant firmware support for a one-shot approval decision distinct from ordinary conversation recording; Mac/browser executor enforcement that an approval cannot be replayed on another tab, origin, or action; A local audit receipt joining the physical press to the browser result; Origin/account verification that does not rely solely on model interpretation of page text

### "If I say 'privacy now' or press the pendant's privacy control, immediately stop listening, mute/stop playback, pause cloud work, hide sensitive browser tabs, and lock or cover the Mac; when I return, tell me exactly what was paused and restore only what I explicitly resume."
- **useful because:** The system can reach the owner's microphone path, authenticated browser, Mac screen, and cloud jobs, but there is no single physical privacy boundary. In a meeting, public place, or unexpected visitor situation, the owner should not have to find the right app, tab, audio route, and pending job individually. One local action should make the whole hive quiet and leave a truthful recovery trail.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** No model is needed for the emergency transition: firmware and deterministic relay/Mac handlers execute it. Use realtime only to confirm the owner's later spoken resume request; use a cheap background process to summarize what was paused.
- **latency:** Local microphone/playback mute and pendant indication under 100 ms; relay pause and browser/Mac privacy actions under 2 seconds. If disconnected, local mute and a durable pending privacy command happen immediately and reconcile on reconnect.
- **cost:** Near-zero inference cost; small local state record and one relay event per transition. Screen lock/tab hiding uses existing Mac/browser control paths.
- **security:** Privacy mode must be fail-safe and local-first: never depend on cloud acknowledgement to mute audio. Do not destroy evidence or silently cancel irreversible work; freeze it, mark its state, and expose a physical cancel/restore policy. Browser tab hiding must not export URLs or page contents. The pendant needs a distinct physical control or a firmware-safe edge, not a gesture that delays recording.
- **missing:** A dedicated physical privacy input or firmware action with priority over conversation state; A local audio gate that mutes capture and playback independently of relay availability; Relay-wide pause semantics for active pipelines, scheduled goals, and pending browser commands; Mac actions to lock/blank display and browser actions to hide or restore a specified tab set; A durable privacy epoch so late completions cannot speak or reveal data after the transition; A truthful resume manifest reporting paused, completed, failed, and unknown work
