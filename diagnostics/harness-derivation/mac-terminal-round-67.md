# Harness derivation — mac-terminal — round 67

Model: `gpt-5.6-luna`  ·  probes against `http://localhost:8000`

## What it established

- **Mac diagnostic and typed-action grants** — The granted mac_read_diagnostics, mac_readonly_diagnostics, and mac_execute_typed_actions interfaces are present in the tool registry but have no backend implementation; calls return an explicit no-implementation error. mac_run_actions remains the production Mac execution surface.
  - evidence: Parallel tool calls in round 67 returned 'This tool was granted a schema but has no implementation yet' for all three.

## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Do that on my Mac, and if anything fails or the Mac disconnects, keep the work safe, resume from the last confirmed step, and tell me exactly what ran and what remains.”"
- **useful because:** Today Mac shell is deliberately unrestricted and trusted, but its receipts are mostly per-job and undo covers only a few action types. A pendant request that crosses relay → Mac → authenticated browser needs one owner-readable proof packet: intended steps, exact commands/actions, stdout/stderr or typed results, timestamps, which steps are reversible, the last durable checkpoint, and a concrete recovery plan. This turns silent partial completion into a dependable handoff rather than making the owner repeat work or guess what happened.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-planner → mac-vision → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use the realtime tier only for the short spoken status and decisions; use a cheaper background model on the relay to normalize receipts, detect a failed/ambiguous step, and draft a retry plan. The Mac planner/vision loop performs execution; the browser extension contributes tab-scoped evidence when a step touches logged-in pages.
- **latency:** Immediate acknowledgement under 1 s from the relay. Each execution step remains bounded by its existing Mac timeout; reconnect reconciliation should complete within 5–10 s after the Mac heartbeat returns. No extra model call is needed for successful typed actions; background summarization can take 1–3 s.
- **cost:** Usually <$0.01 per job in incremental model cost: most work is structured receipt aggregation and hashing; ~$0.01–$0.05 only when a failure needs background diagnosis. Storage is small JSON proof packets plus optional bounded stdout/stderr, with redaction before relay persistence.
- **security:** Unrestricted shell remains the owner's deliberate policy; this does not add a gate or reduce capability. Proof packets must redact environment secrets, tokens, cookies, and likely personal data before leaving the Mac. Browser evidence must be tab/session scoped and never include raw credentials. Require explicit owner confirmation only where existing product policy already requires it; never claim a step succeeded without a typed result or captured exit status.
- **missing:** A shared step/checkpoint schema spanning mac_run_actions, browser_run_actions, mac_delegate, and relay jobs; Mac-side capture of exit code, stdout/stderr truncation, working directory, timeout, and action reversibility in every receipt; Idempotency and resume markers that distinguish completed, started-but-unknown, and not-started steps; Relay reconciliation on Mac reconnect plus a dashboard proof-packet view and concise pendant status messages; Secret/PII redaction and bounded retention for shell/browser evidence

### "“Whenever you tell me something important, let me ask ‘show me why’ and take me straight to the exact source on my Mac or in the browser—without making me repeat the question.”"
- **useful because:** Today evidence is fragmented: browser work can have tab context, Mac jobs have receipts, and spoken relay answers are not consistently linked back to either. The owner cannot reliably distinguish a remembered fact, a shell observation, a browser extraction, and a model inference—or jump from a pendant answer to the exact file, tab, command output, or timestamp that supports it. This would make the system trustworthy for consequential everyday decisions while preserving the fast spoken interface.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → mac-planner → mac-vision → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use the realtime model only to answer the short follow-up and select an existing evidence reference. Build evidence links and source classification asynchronously with a cheaper background model or deterministic adapters; no expensive call is needed when the source is already typed.
- **latency:** Normal answers remain unchanged. “Show me why” should acknowledge in under 1 second, return a source summary within 2 seconds, and open the selected Mac file/window or browser tab within 3–5 seconds when the relevant surface is online.
- **cost:** Low incremental model cost, generally under $0.01 per evidence lookup. Main costs are bounded local metadata indexing and small relay records, not inference. Store hashes/snippets and pointers rather than duplicating files or pages.
- **security:** Evidence may expose private files, authenticated pages, commands, or personal data. Keep raw content on the Mac/browser where possible; send only a redacted excerpt, source type, timestamp, and opaque reference to the relay. Opening a private source should require the same existing owner control as the underlying Mac/browser action. Never expose shell environment values, cookies, access tokens, or full page contents merely because an answer cites them.
- **missing:** A typed evidence-reference envelope emitted by every Mac, browser, memory, and relay answer; A provenance graph linking spoken claims to source spans, command/job IDs, tab IDs, timestamps, and confidence/inference status; Mac actions that can focus the exact source window/file and browser actions that can reattach to the cited tab; Pendant protocol support for a short source summary plus a follow-up reference token; Dashboard rendering that clearly separates observed evidence from model inference and stale memory


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `hardware` — Add a low-power I2C fuel-gauge IC (for example MAX17048-class, with an interrupt line) to the pendant revision, then expose battery percentage/voltage and a low-battery event in the device heartbeat. Relay routing should use that telemetry to avoid starting long audio capture or Mac/browser work when the pendant cannot reliably receive the result; it should switch to a short text/LED status and queue the job until charging. The Mac agent should include pendant power state in job receipts so a disconnected result is distinguishable from a dead pendant.
- **owner gets:** The current pendant has no battery gauge, so the system cannot tell whether silence means a lost Mac, LTE failure, or a dying wearable. The owner gets fewer cut-off conversations and a clear “queued until charged” outcome instead of repeating a task. It also lets the system prefer compact spoken summaries when power is low without disabling the owner's unrestricted Mac access.
- effort: Moderate hardware revision and firmware/relay integration: route I2C (currently free), add gauge driver/calibration, heartbeat schema, relay policy, and dashboard status. Prototype on the DK with an external breakout before a PCB change.  ·  risk: I2C wiring, gauge calibration, and LTE burst noise can produce inaccurate readings; fall back to voltage-only and mark confidence low. If telemetry goes stale, treat it as unknown rather than falsely declaring low battery. Preserve the existing one-button/one-LED behavior and SD failure buffer.
- cost: Approximately $2–$5 BOM increase at volume prototype quantities, plus <1 mA typical gauge draw (interrupt-driven). Firmware and relay storage cost is negligible; no new model call is needed for normal routing.  ·  latency: Heartbeat adds a few bytes and at most tens of milliseconds; low-battery routing decisions are local/relay-side and should not add perceptible conversational latency.
- security: Battery telemetry is low sensitivity. Do not include location or raw audio in the same heartbeat; keep device identifiers scoped and authenticated.
- depends on: A heartbeat payload/route that can carry device telemetry; Relay routing that can distinguish stale device state from live state; Pendant firmware build and a spare I2C address/interrupt GPIO in the next board revision

### `hardware` — Add a tiny coin vibration motor or linear resonant actuator with a dedicated driver in the next pendant revision, plus a low-power haptic event protocol. Relay job completion, browser-watch alerts, and Mac failure/reconnect events should produce distinct short patterns; the pendant firmware must queue/coalesce patterns while audio is active and fall back to the existing LED when haptics are disabled.
- **owner gets:** The owner currently has only one LED and audio output. If headphones are disconnected, audio is buffered, or the owner is in a noisy environment, completed work and urgent failures are easy to miss. A private, silent tap lets the owner know that something finished without opening a screen or starting the microphone, while the relay/Mac still provide the details when asked.
- effort: Hardware revision, one GPIO/PWM driver and haptic patterns in firmware, then relay event mapping and dashboard settings. Prototype with an external driver board first; reserve a GPIO and validate vibration does not couple into the microphone mechanically.  ·  risk: Motor current spikes can disturb LTE/audio rails and vibration can enter the mic. Use a transistor/current-limited driver, physical isolation, rate limits, and a firmware fallback to LED. A failed motor must not block conversations or job delivery.
- cost: Roughly $1–$4 BOM increase in prototypes and approximately 10–80 mA only during brief pulses; negligible model/API cost. Battery impact is small if patterns are capped and coalesced.  ·  latency: Event-to-tap can be sub-second after relay receipt, with no model round trip. Firmware queueing during playback adds at most a few seconds by policy.
- security: Haptic patterns reveal only that an event exists, not its content. Provide a silent/privacy mode and avoid encoding sensitive categories in easily observable patterns.
- depends on: A relay event taxonomy for job completion, failure, and reconnect; Authenticated pendant downlink/event delivery; A revised enclosure/PCB with a safe GPIO, driver transistor, and mechanical isolation
