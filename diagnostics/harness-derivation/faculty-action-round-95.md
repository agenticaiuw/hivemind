## Capabilities it proposed

### "When you do something for me, don't just say it was sent—keep working until the result is independently verified, tell me what actually changed on my pendant, and recover or ask me if it did not complete."
- **useful because:** Today a queued Mac job, a browser command, and a real-world outcome are easy to confuse. This gives the owner a closed loop: judgement's intended outcome is handed to action, perception checks the postcondition using the appropriate surface, the relay keeps the job alive if the voice session ends, and the pendant reports success, failure, or a precise request for help. It is specifically useful for cross-surface tasks such as changing a logged-in webpage and then confirming the account state, or creating a Mac reminder and confirming it exists.
- **path:** faculty-judgement emits a typed intent with an expected postcondition and sensitivity/approval requirement → faculty-action selects Mac AppleScript, Mac job execution, or authenticated browser bridge based on reachability and records stable action IDs → faculty-perception independently re-reads the target (browser DOM/account page, Calendar/Reminders/Mail state, or a relay receipt) and compares it with the expected postcondition → relay persists the job and retry/timeout state after the live voice turn ends, then publishes a concise signed result event → pendant receives success/failure/pending status over the relay and uses spoken audio plus LED patterns; if unavailable, the Mac dashboard shows the same evidence and next step
- **model tier:** Use realtime only for the owner's immediate acknowledgement and final short explanation; use a cheaper background planner/verifier for retries, polling, evidence comparison, and receipt summarization.
- **latency:** Acknowledge in under 1 second; reversible Mac actions usually complete within 10 seconds; allow up to 2 minutes for browser propagation and verification, with durable background continuation rather than blocking the conversation.
- **cost:** About $0.01–$0.05 for a normal background plan/verification cycle, dominated by browser/page extraction and retries; realtime cost is limited to two short utterances. Storage is small typed receipts plus bounded evidence snippets.
- **security:** Do not send page contents or credentials to the relay/model beyond the minimum redacted evidence. Never claim completion from an HTTP 200 or dispatch acknowledgement. Require explicit confirmation before irreversible external effects; redact secrets from receipts; expire evidence and provide cancellation. If verification disagrees, report 'unverified' rather than retrying a potentially duplicated action.
- **missing:** A typed postcondition schema shared by judgement, action, and perception; A durable state machine distinguishing planned, dispatched, observed, verified, failed, and unverified; A verifier route/worker that can poll the same tab/session and attach source/timestamp evidence; Relay push to the pendant for job state changes and a retry budget with idempotency enforcement

### "If I (or another device) change something while you are working on it, notice the conflict, show me both versions, and continue from the one I choose—never overwrite my newer change."
- **useful because:** The system currently treats an action as one agent's linear job. In real life the owner may edit the same calendar event, browser form, file, or message from an iPhone or another tab while the Mac is offline or retrying. Conflict-aware reconciliation prevents stale automation from erasing the owner's newer work and makes multi-device continuity trustworthy.
- **path:** faculty-action attaches a target version/fingerprint and pauses before any stale write → faculty-perception rereads the target across Mac APIs or authenticated browser tabs and identifies which fields changed, by whom if available, and when → faculty-judgement computes a field-level three-way diff against the original observation and asks the owner only about conflicting fields → relay keeps the suspended intent and conflict evidence durably after the voice session ends → Mac/browser perform only the selected non-conflicting merge; pendant speaks a compact conflict summary and accepts the owner's choice, while dashboard shows the full diff
- **model tier:** Use a cheap background model for field normalization and three-way diff; use realtime only to explain the conflict and capture the owner's selection.
- **latency:** Detect conflicts before the first write when possible; otherwise within one readback cycle (seconds). Suspend indefinitely without expiring into a silent overwrite.
- **cost:** Usually under $0.01 per conflict for normalization; storage is a small original/current/proposed field diff. Realtime cost is limited to the short spoken choice.
- **security:** Private field values must stay on the Mac/browser where possible; relay stores redacted diffs and hashes with short retention. Never infer ownership from timestamps alone. Require explicit choice for conflicting sensitive or irreversible fields; maintain an audit trail of the selected version.
- **missing:** A versioned target-observation contract with field-level fingerprints and provenance; Three-way merge/conflict representation shared by Mac, browser, and relay; A durable suspended-job state and owner-choice endpoint that cannot be mistaken for cancellation; Per-field merge adapters for Calendar/Reminders/files and authenticated browser forms


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Add a cross-surface completion ledger and verifier: every judgement action carries an idempotency key, expected postcondition (typed entity/field/operator/value), evidence scope, deadline, and approval class. The relay persists state planned→dispatched→observed→verified|unverified|failed; the Mac executor/browser bridge reports dispatch receipts only, while faculty-perception (or a cheaper verifier worker) must attach fresh, redacted evidence from the target surface before the ledger emits completion. On timeout, retry only idempotent reads or surface a pending/unverified state; never replay a write merely because verification is late. Emit state transitions to /pipeline/events and expose a compact owner-facing receipt linked to existing job receipts and undo.
- **owner gets:** The owner can trust 'done' again: a reminder, browser update, or message is confirmed at its destination instead of merely accepted by a local process. If the browser disappears or an action partially succeeds, they hear exactly that and get a safe next step rather than a false success or duplicate write.
- effort: Medium: shared JSON schema and D1/atomic-store state machine, adapters for existing /execute and browser command results, one verifier worker plus redaction tests, and pendant/dashboard event rendering. Start with Reminders and one browser read/write flow.  ·  risk: A stale or wrong verifier could falsely confirm; mitigate with source timestamps, expected-value comparisons, bounded freshness, and explicit unverified status. Duplicate writes remain possible for inherently non-idempotent actions, so those require approval and no automatic retry. Existing jobs must continue to parse their current receipts and be migrated as unknown/unverified rather than silently marked done.
- cost: Negligible storage and relay CPU; background verifier calls add roughly $0.005–$0.03 per task depending on browser extraction. No new hardware required.  ·  latency: Immediate acknowledgement remains unchanged; final completion adds typically 1–10 seconds, with longer waits moved to durable background polling.
- security: Improves auditability but creates more evidence records. Store hashes/snippets and field names rather than raw private pages, encrypt/expire evidence, and keep credentials confined to the Mac/browser session.
- depends on: A typed postcondition schema and action idempotency key emitted by faculty-judgement; A relay event/push path to the pendant; A faculty-perception verifier that can read Mac and authenticated browser state; Owner approval policy for non-idempotent effects
