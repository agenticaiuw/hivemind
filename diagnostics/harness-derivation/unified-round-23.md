# Harness derivation — unified — round 23

Model: `gpt-5.6-luna`  ·  probes against `http://localhost:8000`

## What it established

- **audio-path** — Current prototype captures at 15,625 Hz, uplinks Opus at 16 kHz/16 kbps, and decodes 24 kHz playback into a 31,250 Hz I2S wire clock; simultaneous encode/decode uses roughly 87% of one Cortex-M33 core. No negotiated packet metadata contract is evidenced yet.
  - evidence: get_hardware_spec(audio)
- **pipeline-audio-route** — The live Mac route table advertises POST /pipeline/audio, but describing it probes GET and receives 404 Cannot GET; the parameterized audio retrieval route exists and rejects invalid direction. Audio validation and route semantics need an explicit implementation test.
  - evidence: describe POST /pipeline/audio and GET /pipeline/:pipelineId/audio/:direction
- **retention** — Relay stack reports ~100 recordings in R2 with 30-day retention but sweeping disabled, so generated audio can accumulate beyond policy until a sweeper is enabled.
  - evidence: get_hardware_spec(stack)

## Capabilities it proposed

### "“If my pendant link gets bad, keep the conversation going through my Mac or phone and tell me only if I need to change anything.”"
- **useful because:** LTE-M is half-duplex and the current prototype spends most of a core decoding and encoding; a wearer should not lose an important answer because the pendant is in a dead spot or overheating. The system can preserve the live turn, then return to the pendant when its link is healthy.
- **path:** pendant detects packet loss, modem state, battery/thermal signals, and plays a short local status tone → relay owns the session epoch, buffers a bounded amount of audio, chooses the active transport, and emits a handoff receipt → Mac menubar app takes over realtime audio when reachable; iOS app is the secondary transport when the Mac is absent → browser is not used for raw audio, but can remain available for any authenticated action already in progress → dashboard shows transport, reason, and whether any words were lost
- **model tier:** Realtime for the active spoken turn only; no model call is needed to decide a transport handoff. Use a cheap background model only to summarize a turn if the owner explicitly asks after a disrupted session.
- **latency:** Detect degradation within 300 ms, switch transports within 1–2 seconds, and replay at most the last 2 seconds of assistant audio. The owner should hear no more than a brief earcon and one short sentence if manual action is required.
- **cost:** Near-zero incremental model cost; bandwidth and relay WebSocket duration dominate. A small amount of encrypted session metadata per handoff is negligible.
- **security:** Audio and session continuity must remain end-to-end scoped to the owner’s authenticated devices; never fall back to an unpaired browser. Buffer only a few seconds in memory, erase it after acknowledgement, rotate handoff tokens, and require confirmation before any queued browser/Mac side effect resumes after transport change.
- **missing:** A production pendant with a reliable second transport or a defined BLE/Wi-Fi path to the Mac/phone; Native Mac and iOS realtime audio clients with background/lock-screen behavior; Relay session migration protocol with sequence/timestamp continuity and bounded encrypted audio buffer; A user-visible pairing and emergency 'stay on pendant' control; The pending 24 kHz acceptance criteria and hardware constraints to set realistic thresholds

### "“While I’m in a meeting or driving, keep the pendant quiet, but let genuinely urgent things through as a one-sentence interruption—and give me everything else as a private queue afterward.”"
- **useful because:** The owner currently has scheduled briefs and spoken responses, but no coordinated interruption policy. This would prevent the pendant from becoming socially disruptive while preserving timely awareness of a critical calendar change, account warning, or explicitly urgent message.
- **path:** Mac planner reads the owner’s current calendar event, focus state, and local activity signals; it never infers driving from raw audio alone → browser extension reads only the authenticated notification sources the owner has explicitly enrolled → relay maintains the durable urgency queue, quiet hours, deduplication, and delivery receipts when the Mac is asleep → pendant receives only the approved one-sentence interrupt, a tactile/earcon severity cue, and a button action to defer or open the queue → dashboard and iOS show the queued items with source, reason for urgency, timestamp, and the policy that admitted or suppressed them
- **model tier:** Use a cheap background model to classify and deduplicate incoming items; use the realtime model only for the brief spoken interruption or when the owner asks follow-up questions. Deterministic calendar and policy rules should decide whether a source is eligible before any model call.
- **latency:** Urgency classification under 30 seconds for notifications and under 5 seconds for calendar changes. A permitted interruption should reach the pendant within 3 seconds; suppressed material can wait for the owner’s next review.
- **cost:** Low: background classification is the dominant API cost, roughly a few cents per active day depending on notification volume. The relay queue and receipts add negligible storage.
- **security:** The system must not silently read every notification or use microphone content to infer activities. Require per-source enrollment, explicit urgency rules, encrypted queue contents, and a local-only driving/focus signal where possible. Never speak secrets aloud in an interruption; redact or require a button-triggered private review. Sending, deleting, or changing anything remains confirmation-gated.
- **missing:** A cross-surface notification ingestion contract with source-specific permissions and webhooks/polling adapters; A durable urgency taxonomy and owner-editable policy language, including emergency contacts and quiet exceptions; Mac/iOS focus and calendar integrations that expose state without screen scraping; A pendant delivery protocol supporting severity, defer, acknowledgement, and replay receipts; A relay queue schema with deduplication, expiry, redaction, and audit history


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Ship an end-to-end negotiated audio contract rather than treating 24 kHz as a decoder setting: at conversation start the pendant advertises capture/playback clock rates, frame duration, Opus mode, and a monotonic stream epoch; each uplink/downlink packet carries epoch, sequence, capture/playout timestamp, sample count, and CRC. The relay validates and transcodes only when required, preserving timestamps; the Mac pipeline records per-hop latency, loss, underruns, resampler ratio, and codec CPU time. Add a loopback/conformance job that runs pendant→relay→Mac and Mac→relay→pendant, rejects drift or missing metadata, and stores a compact receipt for the dashboard. On packet loss, use bounded Opus PLC and a spoken-safe fallback (shorter frames / lower bitrate), then restore the negotiated mode only after a hysteresis window.
- **owner gets:** The owner gets natural, uninterrupted speech instead of occasional clipped, delayed, or pitch-shifted replies—and a truthful indication when the wearable link is degraded. A failed audio upgrade is caught before it reaches daily use, while the Mac and relay can diagnose whether the fault is radio, transcoding, clock drift, or the pendant.
- effort: Medium-high: firmware packet/header and clock instrumentation, relay negotiation/validation, Mac pipeline metrics and conformance runner, plus one dashboard receipt view. Requires hardware-in-loop access to the DK and ESP32 bridge.  ·  risk: A header or clock mismatch can make audio unintelligible; gate rollout behind protocol versioning and retain the current 16 kHz path as a fallback. PLC can hide loss while increasing latency, so cap concealment and end the stream cleanly after a threshold. Conformance artifacts must contain no raw audio by default; retain hashes and metrics only.
- cost: Negligible API cost; modest D1/R2 metadata (a few KB per session, no additional audio retention). Engineering/test hardware time dominates.  ·  latency: Adds one negotiation exchange at session start and microseconds of header validation; bounded PLC may add at most one frame. Avoiding unnecessary relay resampling should reduce steady-state latency.
- security: Authenticate the negotiated contract to the existing session, reject downgrade or unknown codec versions, and avoid storing PCM. Sequence/timing metadata can reveal usage patterns, so apply the existing retention policy and restrict receipts to the owner.
- depends on: A documented 24 kHz superwideband acceptance contract (still unavailable this round); Production pendant constraints beyond the current nRF9160 DK (still unavailable this round); Firmware and ESP32 bridge build/deploy path; A hardware-in-loop audio validation tool (audio_pipeline_validate request remains unresolved)

### `interaction` — Add a first-class, time-bounded disclosure mode shared by pendant, relay, Mac, and browser. The owner can say “private mode for this meeting” or press-and-hold the pendant; the relay issues a signed mode token with expiry and allowed destinations. Mac and browser action planners must attach the token to every read, spoken response, clipboard write, and outbound draft, applying field-level redaction rules before content leaves a surface. The pendant gives a local tone when a request is blocked or redacted. At expiry, the system produces a compact disclosure receipt listing what classes of data were accessed, spoken, copied, or withheld—without retaining the sensitive values.
- **owner gets:** The owner can use the hive mind around other people without having to remember which surface might reveal a private fact. A meeting, shared screen, or borrowed Mac becomes a deliberate privacy boundary rather than a trust gamble.
- effort: High: shared policy/token protocol, Mac and browser enforcement middleware, relay expiry and audit storage, pendant button/voice control, and adversarial tests covering retries, queued jobs, and offline devices.  ·  risk: An unenforced surface could leak data, so fail closed when a token is missing or stale and show a clear blocked-state cue. Clock skew and reconnects could cause premature expiry; use relay-issued monotonic deadlines with a short grace rule. Receipts themselves must be redacted and access-controlled.
- cost: Minimal runtime/API cost; engineering and security testing dominate. Audit metadata is small and can use D1 with short retention.  ·  latency: Adds local policy evaluation and token verification, expected under 20 ms; relay consultation is required only when a token is renewed or a high-risk action is attempted.
- security: Strongly improves confidentiality by making disclosure policy enforceable rather than prompt-based. It introduces a high-value policy token, so bind it to device identity, rotate it, reject replay, and make emergency disable local on the pendant.
- depends on: A shared typed context/policy service rather than hand-written per-surface context; Mac and browser action middleware that cannot be bypassed by queued jobs; Pendant-local mode state and button feedback; A durable, privacy-preserving audit/receipt schema
