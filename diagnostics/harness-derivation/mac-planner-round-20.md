## Capabilities it proposed

### "Handle this while I'm away, and have the result waiting for me when I get back."
- **useful because:** Today work is handed off as isolated actions. A durable mission would let the pendant capture intent, delegate research to the browser/relay, perform authorized desktop work on the Mac, survive disconnects, and return a concise result plus exactly what changed. The owner gets continuity instead of having to remember or re-route unfinished work.
- **path:** pendant captures a mission and gives haptic acknowledgement → relay stores a durable mission/state graph and fans out independent subtasks → browser agent reads authenticated web sessions when needed → Mac planner reads Calendar/Mail or performs desktop actions and posts receipts → dashboard and menubar show progress, blockers, outputs, and undo links → pendant receives a short completion/failure notification and optionally queues an audio summary
- **model tier:** Use a cheap background model for decomposition, polling, summarization, and retry decisions; use realtime only for the initial spoken capture and clarification. Use a stronger model only for ambiguous cross-source synthesis. Parallel subtasks should run concurrently and each node should return structured evidence, not prose alone.
- **latency:** Acknowledge capture in under 500 ms locally/relay-side. Start work within 2 s. Background completion may take minutes; the owner should never wait on an open voice turn. On reconnect, show status immediately and stream only the final summary unless details are requested.
- **cost:** Roughly $0.01–$0.10 per ordinary mission depending on browser pages and synthesis; realtime cost is limited to a few seconds of capture. D1 state and compact receipts are negligible; repeated polling and sending full context are the dominant avoidable costs, so use event-driven wakeups and per-subtask summaries.
- **security:** A mission can combine private mail/calendar, authenticated browser sessions, and irreversible Mac mutations. Keep source-scoped credentials on their native node, pass least-privilege references rather than raw contents, redact receipts, and mark every mutation with an explicit affected-resource summary and undo/compensating action. Owner policy currently permits maximum control, but destructive file deletion, external messages, purchases, and submissions should still be visibly labeled in the dashboard/pendant result. Never let a browser page or email become executable instructions without treating it as untrusted data.
- **missing:** A first-class durable mission schema with parent/child jobs, dependencies, deadlines, cancellation, retry/idempotency keys, and result provenance; Cross-node event delivery so the relay can wake the Mac/browser without polling and resume after offline periods; A shared result envelope containing evidence references, mutations, receipts, and suggested next action; Mission-level dashboard/menubar view and pendant notification/audio delivery; Compensating-action registry for common Mac and browser mutations


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `hardware` — Replace the desk-oriented nRF9160 DK prototype with a jewellery-sized low-power BLE pendant built around an nRF5340-class dual-core SoC (1 MB RAM-class device) plus a separate cellular/phone uplink only where needed. Add two digital MEMS microphones, a 6-axis IMU, a linear resonant actuator, a capacitive touch/squeeze pad, and a USB-C or magnetic pogo-pin charge interface; retain a small RGB status light but make haptics the primary feedback. Keep a 2–4 GB encrypted flash ring buffer for short voice snippets and event metadata, with no raw audio retained after upload unless explicitly pinned.
- **owner gets:** The owner can double-tap or squeeze through a coat to capture a thought without looking at or speaking to the pendant; two microphones enable basic voice activity/noise rejection; the IMU distinguishes tap, shake, and pickup events; haptic patterns communicate captured, uploading, waiting, failed, and completed missions silently; local buffering means a dropped phone/network does not lose the thought. A jewellery enclosure and week-scale battery make daily wear realistic rather than a development board carried occasionally.
- effort: High: custom 4-layer board, acoustic/mechanical enclosure, low-power firmware, BLE companion protocol, encrypted storage/key provisioning, RF and battery certification, and several weeks of gesture/audio usability testing. Prototype can begin with the existing nRF9160 DK plus external sensor breakout, then migrate once power and interaction measurements are known.  ·  risk: Microphone privacy and accidental captures are the major risks; provide a hard electrical mic-disable switch or shutter indicator and a distinct haptic acknowledgement. False gestures can create noise, while missed gestures lose thoughts; use a local event queue and visible history for recovery. Cellular integration and RF enclosure design may introduce certification delays. If the new SoC fails, retain the current board as a BLE-less cellular proof-of-concept.
- cost: Prototype add-on sensors/actuator/storage: roughly $25–$60 BOM. A polished low-volume unit is roughly $80–$180 BOM before certification and tooling; expected average power under 5 mA with BLE-first operation, yielding several days to a week on a 300–500 mAh cell. Cellular use should be burst-only because modem peaks and idle draw materially reduce battery life.  ·  latency: Gesture acknowledgement and buffering are local (<100 ms). BLE upload begins within a second when a phone is nearby; server transcription/synthesis remains asynchronous and should not hold the interaction open.
- security: Use hardware-backed keys where available, encrypted flash, authenticated BLE pairing, and explicit capture indication. Default to event metadata plus transient encrypted audio; delete audio after server acknowledgement according to retention policy. A physical mic-disable control is preferable to a software promise.
- depends on: A defined pendant-to-relay/phone event protocol with resumable uploads; Firmware support for encrypted ring-buffer records and gesture classification; A privacy/retention setting shared across dashboard, iOS, and relay

### `memory` — Replace per-surface prompt-written fleetContext sections with an event-sourced shared memory ledger plus compact, queryable projections: durable facts, preferences, active missions, and recent evidence are separate types; each item has source, confidence, expiry, sensitivity, and last-confirmed timestamps. Every node appends observations or mutations, while the relay materializes a small task-specific context window instead of injecting the whole graph on every turn.
- **owner gets:** Telling the pendant that a meeting moved, or the Mac that a deadline matters, immediately becomes available everywhere without repeating it. Stale guesses expire instead of silently steering actions, and the owner can see why a decision was made and correct one fact once for all surfaces.
- effort: Medium-high: define event and projection schemas, conflict resolution, sensitivity-aware retrieval, migration from fleetContext.js, and UI for provenance/correction. Start with active missions and preferences before migrating historical sessions.  ·  risk: Bad extraction or conflict resolution could create incorrect global behavior. Mitigate with source/confidence fields, conservative promotion of inferred facts, expiry for transient context, append-only history, and a one-click correction that supersedes rather than destroys the original evidence.
- cost: Should reduce token spend materially by retrieving only relevant projections; embedding/index costs are modest compared with repeatedly sending full context. Background compaction can use a cheaper model.  ·  latency: One indexed retrieval adds tens of milliseconds; cached projections can make common requests faster than assembling surface-specific prompt text. Conflict resolution should be asynchronous except for safety-critical preferences.
- security: Sensitivity labels and per-node authorization are mandatory: browser credentials and raw mail must never enter broad shared memory. Store references and redacted summaries by default, encrypt sensitive events, and log every cross-surface read of private memory.
- depends on: A shared mission/result envelope and event delivery path; Memory API support for provenance, sensitivity, expiry, and atomic supersession; Dashboard controls to inspect and correct shared facts


## What it asked for

### `s10-d62e` (skill) — offline_thought_capture
- does: On a double-tap or squeeze gesture, locally records a bounded voice note (or tap-only event if the microphone is disabled), gives an immediate haptic/RGB acknowledgement, encrypts and queues the record, then resumably uploads it when BLE/cellular connectivity returns. It must deduplicate retries and expose queued/sent/failed status to the host.
- must be on-device because: Gesture sensing, immediate feedback, microphone privacy state, and lossless buffering must work during a dropped link and cannot wait for server round-trip latency. The server cannot recover audio that was never buffered locally.
- trigger: A device gesture: two taps within 500 ms, or a 700 ms squeeze/press; a long press cancels the current capture. Upload is additionally triggered by link restoration or a periodic low-power connectivity window.
- storage: Encrypted append-only ring buffer in external QSPI flash, target 2–4 GB in the product; retain metadata (sequence, duration, hash, status) until acknowledged, and raw audio only until successful upload plus a configurable grace period. The current 1 MB prototype flash/RAM is insufficient for meaningful offline audio, so this skill targets the replacement hardware; a prototype can buffer only a few short compressed clips.
- RAM budget: Product target: under 96 kB incremental RAM using streaming VAD/Opus and a small upload window; no whole recording in memory. On the current nRF9160 prototype, cap to roughly 8–16 kB working buffers and short clips because 211,608 B application RAM is already constrained and modem/Opus compete for memory; disable or degrade gracefully if allocation fails.

## Its own summary

Round 20 produced three concrete advances: a durable cross-node mission capability, a product-grade low-power pendant design plus offline thought-capture firmware skill, and an event-sourced shared-memory architecture to prevent surface-specific context drift and token waste. I also asked mac-vision to align computer-use receipts with mission checkpoints.

**Biggest unknown:** The exact event/result contracts and recovery semantics between relay, browser, Mac, and pendant remain undefined; without those, durable missions cannot safely resume or explain partial completion. The queued offline_thought_capture skill also needs an owner-approved privacy/retention policy and replacement hardware prototype.
