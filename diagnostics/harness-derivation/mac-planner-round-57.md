## Capabilities it proposed

### "“If I ask you to do something on my Mac or in a logged-in browser, keep trying when the machine or browser is temporarily unavailable; tell me only if you need me to reconnect it or enable a permission, then finish and give me a proof of what happened.”"
- **useful because:** Today a plan can be accepted while the browser is offline, pending commands remain stranded, and Mac accessibility/screen-recording are missing. The owner should not have to diagnose which node failed or repeat the request.
- **path:** pendant: owner states the goal and receives a short blocked/completed voice update → relay-realtime: persists the job, classifies transient vs owner-actionable blockers, schedules bounded retries, and pushes status → mac-planner: performs a readiness handshake before execution, opens the exact System Settings pane or bridge app when repair is needed, and resumes the same idempotent job → browser-extension: heartbeats, acknowledges/clears stale commands, reconnects the selected tab/session, and returns typed receipts → dashboard: shows one timeline with blocker, retry schedule, and final evidence
- **model tier:** Use a cheap background model for retry classification and receipt summarization; use realtime only for the pendant conversation. No model is needed for permission/status checks or idempotency.
- **latency:** Readiness check under 1 second; retries use exponential backoff up to 15 minutes. A reconnect/permission repair may take tens of seconds and must resume without duplicating completed steps.
- **cost:** Usually <$0.01 per job for background classification/summary; dominant cost is relay storage and browser/Mac polling, not inference. Realtime voice cost occurs only when the owner asks or a final alert is spoken.
- **security:** Status and repair links leave the relay, but page contents stay on the Mac/browser unless explicitly summarized. Never expose page data in a blocker alert. Mutations remain under the owner's existing maximum-access policy, while each step gets an idempotency key and before/after receipt; browser reconnect must be bound to the paired extension and tab/session.
- **missing:** A cross-surface readiness/lease protocol reporting accessibility, screen recording, browser online state, bridge heartbeat, and queued-command age; Browser watchdog that expires or replays the 3 currently stranded pending commands safely; Idempotent job step ledger shared by relay, Mac executor, and browser bridge; A Mac repair helper that deep-links to the relevant System Settings permission and emits a post-repair retry event; Pendant/relay status event and dashboard timeline for blocked, retrying, repaired, and completed states

### "“Continue what I’m looking at.” The pendant should identify the owner’s current Mac document, browser page, selected text, and relevant window state, then let them continue that exact thread by voice or have the Mac prepare the next step without making them explain or share a screenshot."
- **useful because:** The owner often has the important context in front of them but cannot conveniently describe it through a pendant. Today the voice relay, Mac, and authenticated browser do not share a reliable, short-lived ‘current focus’ capsule, so the owner must repeat URLs, titles, selections, and intent.
- **path:** pendant: receives the spoken continuation request and reads back a compact confirmation of the captured context → mac-planner: captures the active app/document/window and selected text or accessibility-readable region, then performs the requested preparation → browser-extension: contributes the authenticated tab identity, URL, title, selection, and page-region evidence without exposing unrelated tabs → relay-realtime: binds the capsule to the conversation for a short expiry, routes the goal to the correct surface, and discards it afterward → faculty-perception: merges Mac and browser observations and labels uncertainty when the two disagree → faculty-judgement: decides whether the next step is read, draft, or mutation and returns a concrete plan → faculty-action: executes only the selected plan and records evidence → dashboard: shows the captured context, sources, expiry, and resulting artifact
- **model tier:** Use deterministic OS/browser inspection first. Use a small background model to normalize the context and resolve titles/entities; use the realtime model only for the spoken exchange and ambiguous owner clarification.
- **latency:** Capture and confirmation in 1–2 seconds; context capsule expires after 10 minutes or task completion. If page extraction is needed, return a first answer within 5 seconds while deeper evidence continues in the background.
- **cost:** Typically under $0.01 in non-realtime inference; the dominant cost is authenticated page extraction and optional screenshot/OCR, not the context normalization. Realtime cost is limited to the owner’s short voice interaction.
- **security:** The capsule may contain sensitive page text or private document content. Keep raw observations on the Mac, send only the minimum selected region and opaque tab/session identifiers to the relay, encrypt the capsule, apply a short TTL, never merge unrelated tabs, and visibly report exactly what was captured. No external submission should occur without the owner’s existing action policy.
- **missing:** A real read-only Mac focus/context inspector for active app, document, selection, and accessibility-readable regions; A browser extension context API that returns selected text and a stable tab/session identity on demand; A short-lived, source-addressed context-capsule schema shared by perception, judgement, and action; Cross-surface disagreement handling when the active Mac window and browser tab do not refer to the same task; Pendant phrasing and dashboard UI for confirming, redacting, or discarding captured context


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Implement a cross-surface execution lease and readiness protocol. Before accepting a Mac/browser plan, the relay obtains signed short-lived readiness snapshots from the Mac agent and browser bridge (accessibility, screen recording, bridge online/last heartbeat, selected tab/session, pending-command count). Each plan step receives an idempotency key and lease; completion receipts are persisted before advancing. Transient failures are retried with backoff, stale browser commands are expired or reconciled by request ID, and owner-actionable failures emit a repair URL plus a pendant event. On repair/heartbeat recovery, the same job resumes from the first uncommitted step.
- **owner gets:** A spoken request becomes reliable instead of silently stranded: the owner gets one concise explanation when a permission or reconnect is genuinely needed, and never has to repeat a partially completed task or wonder whether it ran twice.
- effort: Medium-high: relay schema/state machine, Mac readiness endpoint and repair deep links, browser heartbeat/ack cleanup, shared receipt/idempotency library, dashboard timeline, and integration tests for crash/reconnect at every step.  ·  risk: A bad reconciliation could duplicate a browser click or incorrectly mark a step complete. Mitigate by making reads and navigation idempotent, requiring explicit step fingerprints/before-after evidence for mutations, expiring leases, and exposing an undo/retry path. If the relay is down, the Mac can retain a local receipt and resume sync later.
- cost: Low inference cost; modest D1/local JSON growth from step receipts and snapshots (bounded retention). No new hardware. Polling/heartbeats add small network and battery use only while a job is active.  ·  latency: Adds about 0.5–1 s to initial planning for readiness and lease acquisition; retries improve total completion time by avoiding manual restarts. No added latency to ordinary pendant conversation.
- security: Readiness metadata is low sensitivity but should be authenticated and scoped to paired devices. Do not send browser page contents in status events. Keep session/tab identifiers opaque outside the paired Mac/extension; audit every resume and repair event.
- depends on: A durable browser job runner and command queue with request IDs/tab affinity; The existing Mac job/action receipts and async jobs store; A browser watchdog/reconnect implementation; A small authenticated readiness/status route on Mac and extension; Dashboard support for job timelines

### `context` — Add an ephemeral Focus Capsule protocol spanning Mac, browser bridge, relay, and the three faculties. A capture request creates a local capsule containing active-window/app identity, browser tab/session identity, selected text or bounded accessibility region, URL/document metadata, source timestamps, and confidence. The Mac/browser return only the requested bounded region; perception reconciles them, judgement produces a continuation plan, and action consumes the capsule by ID. Capsules are encrypted, redaction-capable, single-task scoped, and automatically destroyed on completion or after 10 minutes.
- **owner gets:** The owner can say “continue this” while looking at a document or logged-in page and get useful work done without narrating or screenshotting the context themselves. It turns the physical separation between pendant conversation and the Mac screen into a seamless handoff.
- effort: High: implement missing read-only Mac inspection, browser selection/context endpoint, authenticated capsule storage and TTL cleanup, faculty schemas, and a confirmation/readback UI. Test app switches, multiple tabs, stale selections, and private-page leakage.  ·  risk: Capturing the wrong window or too much private text could cause an incorrect or sensitive action. Bound captures by explicit foreground app/tab and selection, attach confidence and timestamps, require a spoken readback for ambiguous context, redact by default, and reject capsules older than their TTL. If capture fails, the system should ask for a URL or short description rather than guess.
- cost: Small relay storage and encryption overhead; transient capsules should be capped by size and retention. Inference remains low-cost except for difficult OCR or entity resolution.  ·  latency: Adds roughly 1–2 seconds for capture and readback, with deeper page extraction asynchronous. No effect on ordinary pendant conversations.
- security: Improves security over screenshot-based sharing by sending bounded selected regions rather than whole screens, but introduces a new sensitive transient artifact. Encrypt in transit and at rest, keep raw content local whenever possible, scope access to the originating job, and log destruction without retaining content.
- depends on: Implemented read-only Mac focus/UI inspection; Browser extension selection and tab-context API; Authenticated relay capsule store with TTL and deletion receipts; Faculty perception/judgement/action support for capsule IDs; A user-visible confirmation and redaction interaction
