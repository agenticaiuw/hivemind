## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Did that actually happen?” — for any task you carried out, verify it across the relevant surfaces and tell me in one short spoken sentence whether it is verified, contradicted, or still unknown, with the reason and the next safe step."
- **useful because:** Today a Mac action can return success while doing nothing, browser commands can remain pending, and pipeline runs can look terminal while staying processing. The owner should never have to infer reality from an optimistic receipt. This turns the hive into a trustworthy assistant: it checks the world after acting, not merely its own logs.
- **path:** faculty-action creates a typed action/receipt record with one request_id and expected postcondition → faculty-perception independently reads the Mac observation/permissions state, AppleScript-readable entities, browser session/tab state, and pipeline/job state, then compares observed facts to the expected postcondition → mac-planner uses granted AppleScript routes for Calendar, Mail, Finder, Notes, Messages, Safari, and Chrome where available; browser-extension reads authenticated tabs when online → relay-realtime delivers a compact spoken verdict to the pendant, with a cited detail available on the Mac workbench → unified preserves the verdict, evidence timestamps, contradiction, and retry/recovery recommendation as one conversation state
- **model tier:** Background/cheap model for polling, normalization, and evidence comparison; realtime model only to interpret an ambiguous user question or speak the final one-sentence verdict.
- **latency:** Routine reversible actions: 1–3 seconds after the action receipt, then up to 15 seconds for delayed surfaces; say “still checking” rather than block. High-impact actions require the verdict before any send/delete/purchase confirmation. No Accessibility or Screen Recording is required for the AppleScript/browser-readable path; GUI-only tasks remain explicitly unknown/blocked.
- **cost:** Roughly $0.001–$0.01 per verification depending on number of surfaces and whether an LLM is needed; most checks are typed comparisons and local reads. Audio delivery and durable evidence storage dominate operational cost, not inference.
- **security:** Evidence must be minimized and scoped to the request: store hashes/snippets and provenance, not whole private pages or mail bodies by default. Never treat an optimistic receipt as proof. Contradicted or unknown must not silently retry an irreversible action; ask for confirmation. Authenticated browser content stays on the local agent/workbench unless the owner explicitly requests a cloud summary.
- **missing:** A durable expected-postcondition schema attached to every action (entity, field, before, expected-after, deadline, and acceptable evidence sources).; A read-only cross-surface evidence reconciler that emits verified/contradicted/unknown with confidence, freshness, and contradiction reasons.; A delayed-observation watcher for jobs and browser commands so processing/pending states are revisited without another voice turn.; A small local workbench artifact and pendant receipt format for evidence citations and recovery actions.

### "“Learn what worked.” After I make a consequential choice through the pendant, Mac, or a logged-in browser, check the result later, ask me once whether it worked for me, and use that answer to improve future recommendations—without keeping the private source material."
- **useful because:** Assistants currently remember requests and receipts, but not whether their choices actually helped. The owner should not have to repeat durable preferences such as which meeting buffers, vendors, reply styles, or travel tradeoffs are genuinely good for them. This creates a personal feedback loop from real outcomes rather than guesses or surveillance.
- **path:** faculty-action records a compact prediction at decision time: goal, chosen option, expected benefit, expected downside, and review date; it does not retain full message/page contents → mac-planner and browser-extension later read only the minimum outcome signals from Calendar, Mail, browser tabs, or local files, using the surface that owns the state → faculty-perception compares outcome signals with the prediction and marks outcome evidence as positive, negative, mixed, or unavailable → relay-realtime asks the pendant one short follow-up at a suitable interruption window; the owner can answer satisfied, not satisfied, or skip → unified updates a preference model with the answer and provenance, while faculty-judgement uses it as a soft recommendation—not an automatic rule—on future cross-surface plans
- **model tier:** Background/cheap model for scheduled outcome checks and preference updates; realtime model only for the brief follow-up conversation or ambiguity resolution.
- **latency:** No blocking of the original action. Outcome checks run at the review date or when a relevant surface changes. The spoken follow-up is under 10 seconds and can be deferred indefinitely.
- **cost:** About $0.001–$0.02 per reviewed decision, mostly local extraction and occasional summarization; compact preference updates are far cheaper than resending source context.
- **security:** Store only structured predictions, outcome labels, and provenance hashes—not raw email, page, or calendar content. Never infer sensitive traits from outcomes. Ask before using a learned preference for a consequential action, and let the owner inspect, correct, or delete each learned preference.
- **missing:** A decision-outcome record linking an action to a future review date and predicted result; Outcome probes that can extract minimal signals without copying private source content; A preference ledger with confidence, expiry, evidence, and explicit owner correction; A quiet follow-up scheduler that avoids repeatedly interrupting the owner; A model-routing rule that keeps this asynchronous and cheap


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `integration` — Add an evidence-reconciliation stage between every terminal action receipt and its user-facing completion: persist an expected postcondition at plan time; schedule read-only probes against the owning surface; normalize observations into typed claims with source/timestamp/freshness; compare claims to the postcondition; and emit a signed verdict (verified, contradicted, unknown) plus recovery options. Fan this into the existing job/receipt model under the same request_id, expose it through /jobs/:jobId/receipts and relay_job_status, and send only the compact verdict to the pendant. For the 24 kHz spoken receipt path, queue the verdict as a short Opus audio item with sequence number and expiry so a dropped link cannot turn an old “done” into a current claim.
- **owner gets:** The pendant will tell the owner what is true, not what the automation hoped was true. Failed Mac permissions, offline browser tabs, and stuck processing jobs become visible and recoverable instead of silent false completion.
- effort: Medium: typed schema and comparator, probe adapters for AppleScript/browser/jobs/pipeline, durable retry scheduler, receipt UI/audio serialization, and tests for success/contradiction/timeout. Start with Calendar/reminders and browser navigation, then expand.  ·  risk: A stale or partial observation could produce a false contradiction; every verdict needs freshness and an explicit unknown state. Probes must be read-only and must never retry irreversible actions. If the verifier is unavailable, preserve the original receipt but label it unverified. Recovery is replaying only reversible steps after a fresh preflight.
- cost: Low background model/API cost; mostly local reads and small D1/R2 records. Audio adds negligible bandwidth for a one-sentence verdict. Storage grows with evidence metadata, so retain hashes and short snippets with bounded TTL.  ·  latency: Adds 1–3 seconds for local checks and up to 15 seconds for eventually consistent surfaces; the pendant should speak an immediate “checking” acknowledgement, then the verdict asynchronously.
- security: Evidence is least-privilege and surface-local by default. Persist provenance and hashes rather than private content; redact secrets; require owner confirmation before any recovery that sends, deletes, purchases, or changes account state.
- depends on: A durable expected-postcondition field in POST /plan and POST /execute; A cross-surface read-only observation/reconciliation primitive (not Accessibility-dependent); A delayed job/browser/pipeline watcher; A compact 24 kHz audio queue item and receipt expiry/sequence support

### `memory` — Create an outcome-linked preference ledger distinct from ordinary memories: at plan time, store a minimal predicted-outcome tuple (decision_id, goal, option, expected signal, review_at, sensitivity, expiry); at review time, attach only a normalized outcome label and evidence pointer; update preference weights only after explicit owner feedback. Make every learned preference inspectable, correctable, and automatically decaying unless reinforced.
- **owner gets:** The system gradually becomes better at choices that matter to this particular person—without pretending a one-off request is a permanent preference and without retaining their private correspondence. Future plans improve because they are measured against lived results.
- effort: Medium: schema and migrations, review scheduler, minimal-signal adapters for Calendar/Mail/browser/files, owner feedback interaction, preference retrieval and deletion UI, and regression tests preventing sensitive inference.  ·  risk: Outcome signals can be misleading or preferences can change. Keep confidence low until repeated feedback, represent mixed outcomes, expire stale entries, and never execute solely from a learned preference. If a source is inaccessible, record unavailable rather than guessing.
- cost: Small bounded metadata storage and low background inference cost; no raw-content archive. Occasional spoken follow-up adds negligible audio cost.  ·  latency: Zero impact on the original action; reviews are asynchronous. Preference retrieval adds only a small context lookup to later plans.
- security: Sensitive source content remains at its owning surface. The ledger contains only user-approved structured preferences, provenance, TTL, and outcome labels; deletion must remove both the preference and its evidence links.
- depends on: A new decision_id/predicted-outcome field in POST /plan; A review scheduler distinct from immediate job completion; Minimal-signal read adapters for Mac and authenticated browser surfaces; Owner-visible preference correction and deletion controls
