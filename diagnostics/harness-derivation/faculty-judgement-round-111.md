## Capabilities it proposed

### "“Read me the brief. If I say ‘why?’, show me the source and let me open it on my Mac.”"
- **useful because:** A spoken summary is otherwise a dead end: the owner can hear that something changed but cannot quickly judge whether it is real or act on it. Each audio item would carry a compact provenance token (source, capture time, freshness, confidence, and the exact supporting excerpt). Saying “why?” on the pendant retrieves the evidence, while “open it” uses the authenticated browser or Mac to reveal the original page without requiring the owner to remember which tab produced it. This is a genuinely joined behavior: the pendant supplies low-friction speech and playback, relay preserves the item identity, faculty-perception checks freshness, browser holds private sessions, and Mac presents the evidence.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → unified → faculty-perception → browser-extension → mac-planner → mac-vision
- **model tier:** Realtime only for the short spoken answer and disambiguation; background extraction, excerpt hashing, freshness checks, and briefing assembly use a cheaper background model. No model call for a direct cached-source lookup.
- **latency:** Initial “why?” response under 2 seconds from cached provenance; source opening under 5 seconds when the browser bridge is online. If the bridge is offline, speak the citation and queue the open request rather than claiming it opened.
- **cost:** Typically one small realtime turn for the spoken explanation; background extraction dominates but can use a cheap model. Direct provenance lookup is near-zero model cost. Browser/Mac execution cost is local/API latency, not inference.
- **security:** Private URLs, excerpts, and account names must remain scoped to the authenticated owner and never enter generic logs or model context unless needed. Opening a source is reversible navigation, but any follow-up mutation remains gated by the existing confirmation policy. Never report a source as opened without a verified browser/Mac receipt; stale or conflicting evidence must be spoken as such.
- **missing:** A stable briefing-item schema and provenance token that survives audio encoding, queueing, interruption, and reconnect; A read-only provenance lookup route returning source, excerpt hash, freshness, and confidence; A pendant utterance intent for why/source/open plus a resumable audio-item cursor; A browser/Mac verified-open operation with explicit offline/blocked outcome; A policy deciding which excerpt and URL fields may be spoken aloud

### "“When your sources disagree, don’t guess. Tell me what conflicts, ask the one question that resolves it, and remember the answer for next time.”"
- **useful because:** The owner currently gets plausible but unsafe answers when machine context, cached briefings, browser state, and remembered preferences disagree. This turns uncertainty into a tiny, actionable interaction: the pendant says the conflict in one sentence, asks only the minimum disambiguating question, and the relay records the owner’s answer with scope, source, and expiry. Future agents then stop re-opening the same ambiguity (for example, the authoritative timezone) and can explain which evidence they used.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → faculty-perception → faculty-judgement → faculty-action → mac-planner → browser-extension
- **model tier:** Realtime for stating the conflict and asking the one question; background model only for clustering repeated conflicts and suggesting a candidate resolution. Deterministic source comparison handles ordinary timestamps, reachability, and receipt states.
- **latency:** Conflict detection should add less than 300 ms to an answer when evidence is already present. The owner’s clarification is one short turn. Persist the resolution immediately; no Mac/browser round trip is required unless they are the conflicting source.
- **cost:** Near-zero for deterministic conflicts; one short realtime turn only when clarification is required. Background clustering is infrequent and cheap.
- **security:** Never expose secret values while explaining a conflict; say “a saved private fact” rather than reading it. Store only the owner-approved resolution, its scope, provenance, and expiry. Do not silently overwrite a higher-authority fact; retain both claims and mark one superseded. Time-sensitive actions must remain blocked until resolution.
- **missing:** A conflict object schema (claims, authorities, timestamps, scope, confidence, safe redaction); A deterministic resolver that compares machine-context, browser, briefing, receipt, and memory evidence before model narration; A one-question clarification policy and owner answer route; Durable supersession/expiry semantics in the shared context projection; A pendant-visible acknowledgement that the resolution was saved

### "“Why did we decide that? Reconstruct the short chain of evidence, assumptions, and approvals that led to it, and tell me what has changed since.”"
- **useful because:** Today the system can perform pieces of research, planning, and execution, but the owner cannot later audit a consequential personal or work decision as a coherent story. This capability would let them recover the actual decision context: what the pendant heard, which private page or Mac document supplied evidence, what was uncertain, what the owner approved, and which later facts invalidate the conclusion. It is not an action-history list; it is a time-bounded, evidence-linked explanation of a decision and its present validity.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → faculty-perception → faculty-judgement → faculty-action → browser-extension → mac-planner → mac-terminal
- **model tier:** Use deterministic event/provenance joins first; use the cheaper background model to summarize the chain. Reserve realtime for the owner’s spoken question and a concise answer, with drill-down only on request.
- **latency:** A cached decision packet should yield a spoken answer in under 3 seconds; reconstructing missing links may take up to 20 seconds and must be labeled incomplete rather than silently inferred.
- **cost:** Low per query when event joins and hashes are available; occasional background summarization dominates. No need to resend full page contents or audio—store compact excerpts/hashes and retrieve originals only when authorized.
- **security:** Decision chains may expose private mail, documents, or health/work context. Enforce per-source access at replay time, redact secrets and unrelated people, preserve immutable provenance, and distinguish owner approval from model suggestion. Never claim an approval that lacks a recorded owner event.
- **missing:** A durable decision entity with links to claims, source snapshots, model suggestions, owner approvals, and resulting actions; Immutable, privacy-filtered event snapshots for browser pages, Mac files, pendant utterances, and action receipts; A change detector that compares the original evidence against current accessible sources and reports invalidation; A spoken drill-down protocol that keeps the owner on one decision while fetching a specific source or receipt; Retention and deletion controls that can erase a decision chain without leaving orphaned sensitive artifacts

### "“Forget everything about that topic everywhere.”"
- **useful because:** The owner should be able to revoke a sensitive conversation as a single intent, rather than hunting through pendant audio, relay jobs, browser extracts, Mac drafts, and derived memory. The system would first enumerate what it found, then delete or cryptographically tombstone the source recordings, derived summaries, queued jobs, browser snapshots, and local notes across reachable surfaces, reporting any unreachable residue and retrying later. This is a real cross-surface privacy power that does not exist today.
- **path:** pendant → relay-realtime → unified → faculty-judgement → faculty-action → browser-extension → mac-planner → mac-terminal
- **model tier:** Realtime only to identify the topic and confirm scope; deterministic entity matching and deletion orchestration should run in the background. Use a cheap model only to resolve ambiguous references, never to decide deletion scope silently.
- **latency:** Show a deletion plan within 3 seconds from indexed records; complete reachable deletion within 30 seconds, with durable retries for offline Mac/browser and a concise receipt when finished.
- **cost:** Low model cost; storage/index scans and Mac/browser round trips dominate. Cost grows with the number of artifacts, not conversation length.
- **security:** This is destructive and must require explicit confirmation unless the owner has separately enabled a narrow auto-delete rule. Never delete unrelated records from a fuzzy topic match. Keep a minimal non-content tombstone (identifier, scope, time, completion state) so the system can prove what was removed, while allowing the owner to erase even that audit marker under a stronger confirmation.
- **missing:** A cross-surface artifact index linking raw audio, transcripts, summaries, context facts, browser snapshots, Mac files, and queued jobs; A deletion planner with exact-match/ambiguous-match classification and owner confirmation; Idempotent delete/tombstone APIs for relay storage, browser sessions, and Mac workspace artifacts; Offline retry leases and a verified deletion receipt that distinguishes deleted, unreachable, and not-found; A pendant intent and local pre-transmission cancellation path for newly captured audio


## Changes it proposed to its own stack

### `hardware` — Replace the prototype’s single multifunction button/LED with a dedicated, physically latched microphone mute switch plus a high-visibility two-color privacy LED, and expose its state as a signed device event. The relay must treat mute as an absolute input gate: stop capture, cancel pending speech transmission, mark queued work as locally muted, and tell Mac/browser surfaces not to start new capture or automation that depends on live speech until an explicit unmute event.
- **owner gets:** The owner can know, by touch and sight, that the pendant is not listening—especially in a meeting or around other people—without trusting a spoken claim from the same software being muted. The state follows the job across relay, Mac, and browser, preventing a stale queued command from surprising them after privacy mode is enabled.
- effort: Moderate hardware revision and firmware/pipeline integration: GPIO debouncing, LED state machine, signed mute/unmute events, relay enforcement, and tests for disconnect/reconnect and queued audio. No industrial design can be finalized until the pendant moves beyond the nRF9160 DK prototype.  ·  risk: A stuck switch or brownout could leave the owner muted; provide a clear LED fault pattern and a button-held recovery/self-test. If the event link is down, local mute still works and unmute should fail closed for capture until state is acknowledged. Do not infer privacy from network reachability.
- cost: One latching switch, LED/light-pipe, resistor, and small PCB change, roughly $1–$4 in BOM at volume; negligible power compared with the modem and audio path. No per-invocation API cost.  ·  latency: Local mute cutoff should be under one audio frame (<60 ms); relay propagation is best-effort and should not delay local privacy.
- security: Improves privacy materially: mute state is hardware-originated, authenticated, and fail-closed. Avoid logging raw audio or secret event payloads; retain only state transitions and timestamps.
- depends on: A signed pendant event envelope and reconnect state reconciliation; Relay enforcement that distinguishes hardware mute from software pause; Cross-surface cancellation of queued audio and speech-dependent jobs; A product pendant revision beyond the current nRF9160 development kit

## Its own summary

Round 111 produced three additions: (1) a provenance-aware spoken brief interaction (“why?” / “open that”) that preserves the audio item identity through relay, browser, and Mac; (2) a conflict-resolution contract that refuses to guess when machine, browser, briefing, or memory evidence disagree and asks one minimal clarifying question; and (3) a hardware privacy switch proposal that fail-closes capture locally and propagates mute state across every surface. I still need the implementation seams, not another broad capability: a durable briefing-item/provenance schema, deterministic evidence-conflict records with scoped supersession, and signed device mute events enforced by relay and queued-job cancellation. I also need authoritative owner confirmation of timezone before making time-sensitive claims, and a real pendant/audio acceptance run before treating the 24 kHz path as shipped. TCC/browser grants remain owner-side blockers; I did not re-request them.

**Biggest unknown:** Whether the current prototype can carry the required provenance/mute metadata and 24 kHz audio reliably under its 211,608 B RAM and ~87% single-core simultaneous codec load; this needs a measured end-to-end pendant test, not more route discovery.
