## Capabilities it proposed

### "“That didn’t work—tell me exactly why, try the safest recovery, and leave me a reviewable fallback if it still fails.”"
- **useful because:** Today failures are opaque across surfaces: the Mac journal shows 27 failed actions, repeated browser navigation has an 11/11 failure pattern, and 111 actions lack routing attribution. This capability would turn a dead end into a concise spoken diagnosis, a bounded retry that does not replay already-completed steps, or a browser/Mac fallback artifact the owner can inspect later.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-planner → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** background first for deterministic failure classification and known recovery recipes; escalate to planner only when the error and fallback are ambiguous. Realtime should only speak the short status to the pendant.
- **latency:** Under 2 seconds for receipt lookup and known-error diagnosis; under 20 seconds for one recovery attempt; otherwise enqueue durable work and notify through relay/pipeline when complete.
- **cost:** Usually $0–$0.01: receipt/journal lookup and known recipes are deterministic, with a small-model call only for unfamiliar errors. Planner escalation is the dominant cost (roughly 8–10k prompt tokens).
- **security:** Failure receipts can contain URLs, shell commands, paths, and authenticated-page snippets. Keep raw evidence on the Mac, send the relay only a redacted diagnosis and status, and require explicit owner confirmation before any fallback that sends, submits, deletes, or makes an irreversible shell change; retries of idempotent/read-only steps can remain automatic under the owner's maximum-access policy.
- **missing:** A failure taxonomy mapping executor/browser/permission/network errors to recovery recipes; Cross-surface correlation IDs linking pendant utterance, relay job, Mac job, browser command, and receipt; A retry planner that skips successful action IDs and emits a reviewable fallback bundle; A durable notification/acknowledgement state for recovery results

### "“Pause this exactly where it is, remember what I was looking at and what remains, and resume it when I’m back at my Mac—without losing the browser context.”"
- **useful because:** The owner can start a task by voice or in Safari, then walk away, but cannot currently preserve a coherent, resumable checkpoint across the pendant, relay, Mac job, and authenticated browser. Today a job receipt records actions after the fact; it does not capture the live task state, open-tab evidence, pending decisions, or the exact next safe step. This would make interruption normal rather than forcing the owner to reconstruct context.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-planner → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Use deterministic checkpoint capture and a small background model to compress the task state; use the realtime model only for the short pendant acknowledgement and a planner-tier call when resuming requires reconciling changed tabs or a new goal.
- **latency:** A pause acknowledgement and local checkpoint in under 2 seconds; resume inspection in under 10 seconds, with longer work continuing durably and a concise pendant notification when ready.
- **cost:** Usually under $0.01 per pause/resume: local browser snapshots, receipts, and structured state need no model; background summarization is the dominant routine cost. Planner escalation is reserved for changed or ambiguous authenticated pages.
- **security:** Checkpoints may contain authenticated URLs, page text, drafts, shell paths, and voice-derived private context. Store raw snapshots only on the Mac, encrypt or expire them, send the relay only a redacted task summary, and never resume a submit/send/delete step automatically. On resume, show changed evidence and require the owner's existing confirmation convention before irreversible actions.
- **missing:** A first-class resumable checkpoint schema containing goal, completed stable action IDs, next step, open browser tab/session IDs, evidence capsule references, pending decisions, and expiry; An atomic pause endpoint that snapshots Mac job state and authenticated browser state without executing a new command; Browser tab/session reattachment and stale-state detection on resume; A relay notification and dashboard review surface for paused, expired, and resumed checkpoints; A resume planner that compares checkpoint evidence with current tabs and produces a diff before continuing

### "“If something truly urgent happens, reach me on the pendant; otherwise wait until I’m free and put it on the Mac. Escalate only if I haven’t acknowledged it.”"
- **useful because:** The system can produce jobs, browser findings, and briefings, but cannot yet decide how to interrupt the owner based on live availability across the wearable and Mac. This would prevent nonurgent browser/account changes from interrupting the owner while making a genuinely time-sensitive item harder to miss, with an explicit acknowledgement trail rather than repeated alerts.
- **path:** pendant → relay → mac-planner → browser-extension → dashboard
- **model tier:** Deterministic policy for urgency, quiet hours, acknowledgement, and escalation; background model only to classify an unfamiliar finding's urgency. Realtime is used solely to speak an urgent alert and capture acknowledgement.
- **latency:** Urgent event delivered within 5 seconds of a confirmed finding; normal events batched at the next availability transition; escalation after a configurable deadline.
- **cost:** Near-zero for routing and acknowledgement state. Background classification is occasional and dominates model cost; realtime audio is only used for escalated alerts.
- **security:** Availability signals (active app, audio activity, pendant presence) are sensitive. Keep raw presence telemetry local or coarse-grained, transmit only urgency and delivery state, expire unacknowledged content, and do not speak sensitive page/email text aloud until the owner requests details.
- **missing:** A cross-surface availability state machine (pendant connected/away, Mac active/idle/locked, quiet hours, current call or focus mode); A normalized urgency contract for browser findings, Mac jobs, and relay routines; Durable notification delivery, acknowledgement, deduplication, and escalation records; A pendant-side alert/acknowledge event and a Mac presence adapter; Owner-configurable urgency rules and a dashboard showing why an alert was routed or deferred
